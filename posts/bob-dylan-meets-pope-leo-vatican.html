Bob Dylan Met Pope Leo XIV at the Vatican and Traded His Autobiography for a Rosary

By Editorial Team · 7 min read

Pope Leo XIV received Bob Dylan in a private audience at the Vatican on Wednesday, November 4, bringing together the 85-year-old Nobel laureate and the pope who quoted his lyrics in a book on disarmament earlier this year. The meeting ended with an exchange of gifts: a rosary from the pope and a copy of Dylan's autobiography from the singer.

The meeting

  • A brief audience. The meeting took place at the Apostolic Palace and was brief, consisting of greetings and a few lighthearted exchanges. Dylan wore a black hooded sweatshirt for the encounter, and the Vatican described the tone as warm rather than formal.
  • The gifts. The pope presented Dylan with a rosary, while Dylan reciprocated with a book: Chronicles: Volume One, his autobiography. The exchange matched the two men: a devotional object from the head of the Catholic Church and a life story from one of America's most written-about songwriters.
  • The two men. Dylan, born Robert Allen Zimmerman, is an American singer-songwriter and poet who won the Nobel Prize in Literature. Leo had already quoted his lyrics in July 2026, in the preface to a book published by the Vatican Publishing House, so the audience was a meeting between two men who had already spoken to each other through the printed page.

The song that connected them

  • Go Away You Bomb. The verses the pope cited come from "Go Away You Bomb," a protest song Dylan wrote in 1963, at the height of Cold War tensions. The song was never officially recorded or released on any album, and the original manuscript remained unpublished until its discovery in 2013.
  • The warning. In the preface to Disarmed and Disarming: Peace Is a Gift, the pope used Dylan's words to issue a stern warning against global rearmament and the nightmare of nuclear weapons. The lyric he quoted was addressed to the bomb itself: "I hate you cause yer man-made and man-owned, an' man-handled."
  • Why it mattered. The book was published by the Vatican Publishing House and edited by journalist Alessandro Banfi. A pope quoting an unreleased Dylan protest song in a book on disarmament gave the meeting its backstory: Leo knew Dylan's work well before the two men ever shared a room.

Dylan in Italy

  • The tour. Dylan is in Rome on the autumn leg of his "Rough and Rowdy Ways" tour, and he performs on Thursday, November 5, at the Palazzo dello Sport. The tour also includes stops in Milan and Bologna, and it is notable for its strict prohibition on smartphone recording at concerts.
  • Not his first pope. This is not the first time Dylan has met a pope: in 1997 he performed at the 23rd National Eucharistic Congress in Bologna, before Pope John Paul II and a crowd of approximately 300,000 young people. After performing three of his great classics, including "Knockin' on Heaven's Door," he removed his cowboy hat, climbed the steps to the papal stage, and exchanged a handshake and a few brief words with John Paul II.
  • A lyric answered. In his address to the young people at the 1997 event, John Paul II quoted the lyrics to "Blowin' in the Wind" and answered Dylan's question himself: "You ask me: how many roads must a man walk down before you can call him a man?" The Polish pope answered: "Only one. There is only one road for man, and that is the road of Jesus Christ." Nearly three decades later, Dylan walked into the Vatican again, this time to meet a pope who had already quoted him.
