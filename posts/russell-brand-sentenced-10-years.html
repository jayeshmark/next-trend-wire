Russell Brand Sentenced to 10 Years and Six Months in Prison for Rape and Sexual Assault

By Jayesh Marathe · November 9, 2026 · 7 min read

A judge at Southwark Crown Court sentenced Russell Brand to 10 years and six months in prison on November 9, four days after a jury convicted the comedian and actor of raping one woman and sexually assaulting three others in offences dating back to 2004.

The sentence

  • The headline term. Mr Justice Joel Bennathan sentenced Brand to 10 years and six months in prison, with nine years for the rape and a further 18 months for the three sexual assaults running consecutively.
  • How the total was built. The judge imposed nine months, 18 months and eight months for the three sexual assaults, with those terms running at the same time as each other, and added the 18 months on top of the nine year rape term to reach the total.
  • Time behind bars. Brand must serve two thirds of the sentence in custody before he can be released on licence, which amounts to about seven years, with the rest of the term served under supervision and strict conditions.
  • The register. The judge also ordered Brand to remain on the sex offenders register for life, a standard consequence of the convictions.
  • No bail. Brand, 51, had been in custody since the verdicts on November 5, when the judge refused bail and sent him to HMP Wandsworth, and he arrived at court for sentencing in a prison van.

What the jury decided

  • Four guilty verdicts. On November 5, a jury of eight men and four women convicted Brand of one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault involving four women.
  • Three acquittals. The same jury cleared him of two further rape charges and one charge of attempted indecent assault, after deliberating for more than eight hours over two days.
  • The time span. The offences he was convicted of took place between 2004 and 2009, during the years when Brand was rising to fame as a television and radio presenter and Hollywood actor.
  • The rape conviction. The rape involved a fan Brand met after a performance at the Hammersmith Apollo in 2009, who was attacked at his home in west London.
  • A long wait for justice. The women came forward after a joint investigation by The Sunday Times, The Times and Channel 4 Dispatches published allegations against Brand in September 2023, and the case reached trial after a four week hearing.
  • His position. Brand denied every charge throughout the trial and maintains that all of the encounters were consensual.

What the judge said

  • A predator behind the fame. "You were clever and in many people's eyes, funny and charismatic," Mr Justice Bennathan told Brand. "You had also become a sexual predator."
  • Fame as a shield. The judge said Brand believed that "fame meant you could act how you liked, when you liked, unhindered by the rules by which decent people live," and that his status had protected him until journalists published the allegations in 2023.
  • Contempt for consent. Bennathan said Brand showed a "casual and arrogant disregard" for a woman's right to say no, and told him: "Whatever women wear, wherever they go, indeed whatever they drink, they retain the right to say no and to demand justice from the criminal courts when sexual predators like you, Russell Brand, act without their consent."
  • Lies at trial. The judge said Brand had told a series of lies during the trial and displayed what he called "staggering arrogance" in the witness box.
  • How the sentence was set. The judge placed the rape in category 2B of the sentencing guidelines, which carries a starting point of eight years, added two years for aggravating features including the removal of protection without consent, then took a year off for mitigation.
  • The mitigation. That reduction reflected Brand's lack of significant previous convictions, the 17 years since the last offence, his charitable work and his help for people recovering from drug and alcohol addiction, and evidence from his wife about his family life.
  • No future risk finding. "Given how long ago these offences were committed and the evidence of your wife, I accept there is no sound evidential basis for me to conclude you pose a risk for the future," the judge said, which is why he did not order an extra report on dangerousness.
  • Only the convictions counted. The judge said he disregarded the allegations in the 2023 documentary and the charges the jury acquitted Brand of when deciding the sentence, and based it only on the four counts the jury found proved.

The case behind the verdict

  • A career built on screen. Brand first became a household name presenting television and radio in Britain, then crossed over to Hollywood with roles in Forgetting Sarah Marshall, Get Him to the Greek and Arthur, and as the voice of Dr. Nefario in the Despicable Me films.
  • The investigation. London's Metropolitan Police opened its Operation Actinolite inquiry after the 2023 media reports, and Brand was charged in April 2025, with two further charges added in December 2025.
  • The trial. Prosecutor Riel Karmy-Jones KC called Brand a "ruthless, calculating, manipulative" predator in a four week trial that heard from the four women and from Brand himself, with his wife Laura Gallacher giving evidence in his defence.
  • More investigations. The Metropolitan Police has said Brand remains under investigation over two other alleged rapes, one involving a then 16 year old girl, and has submitted files to prosecutors about those allegations.

What happens next

  • An appeal window. Brand has 28 days to apply to the Court of Appeal for permission to appeal against his convictions, the sentence, or both, and no appeal had been announced by the morning after sentencing.
  • The wider reckoning. The case has renewed scrutiny of how the entertainment industry handled allegations about Brand during his years in the spotlight, when his fame shielded him from consequences.
  • The victims. British law gives the women lifetime anonymity, and the judge opened his remarks by reminding everyone that naming the complainants is strictly prohibited.
