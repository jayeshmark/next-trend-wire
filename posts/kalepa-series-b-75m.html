Kalepa Raises $75 Million to Put AI Across Insurance Underwriting

By Editorial Team · 7 min read

Kalepa has raised $75 million in Series B funding at a $470 million valuation to expand its artificial intelligence software for insurers. The company announced the round on November 5, led by Updata Partners, five years after its last funding round. Kalepa has tripled its revenue and customer count in the past year while staying cash flow positive.

What happened

  • Updata Partners led the round. IA Ventures and Inspired Capital, both existing investors, joined the Series B. Kalepa has now raised $91 million in total, and the new money values the company at $470 million.
  • The last round was five years ago. Kalepa raised a $14 million Series A in 2021, led by Inspired Capital with IA Ventures participating. It waited five years between rounds while it built the business with customers instead of raising again.
  • Growth paid for the wait. Over the past year Kalepa tripled its revenue and its customer count, and it has been cash flow positive for more than two years. A company that funds itself from operations raises on very different terms from one that needs the money to survive.
  • The money funds expansion. Kalepa will use the funding to expand its AI product suite, move into new markets and double its headcount in 2027.

What Kalepa sells

  • AI that runs insurance work end to end. Kalepa's platform performs and orchestrates work across operations, underwriting, pricing and portfolio management, instead of automating one administrative task at a time. The pitch is that insurers need AI that carries connected workflows through to completion.
  • Agents with guardrails. An insurance-specific AI harness coordinates the agents behind those workflows, adapts as conditions change and matches the level of autonomy to each task and its risk. People step in where expertise or approval is needed, and every result is verified so it can be traced and audited.
  • Big-name customers. Its customers include major insurers and managing general agents such as W.R. Berkley, Bowhead Specialty and AmRisc. Those are the kinds of customers that do not switch software lightly, which makes the tripled customer count a meaningful signal.
  • The pitch is discipline, not just speed. Kalepa argues that insurers face climbing losses, a generation of experienced professionals retiring with decades of judgment, and AI-native competitors built for speed. Softening prices leave less room for error on every decision, so the company sells AI that strengthens risk selection while it moves faster.

The numbers behind the round

  • Customers report big gains. Insurers using Kalepa have reported 30 to 60 percent more premium per underwriter, a 58 percent reduction in quote time, a 960-basis-point improvement in combined ratio and double the quote-to-bind rate. The volume of work running through the platform grew fourfold in the past year. Those are customer-reported figures from the company's announcement, not audited results.
  • A new leadership bench. Kalepa added Garrett Schlesinger as chief technology officer after 12 years at Affirm, where he was the company's 12th employee and helped build its real-time underwriting system. David Meister joined as chief revenue officer from Addepar, Audrey Howell as head of product from Palantir and Butterfly Network, and Tim Khoo-Jones as head of talent from Block and Shopify.
  • The chief executive's case for the raise. "Insurance runs on the judgment of experienced professionals, and our Professional Grade AI is held to that same standard," said co-founder and chief executive Paul Monasterio. "This funding lets us bring Professional Grade AI to more of the work insurers do, so they have that choice across the entire value chain."

Why this round stands out

  • A profitable company raising on its own terms. A startup that has been cash flow positive for two years does not need the money to survive, which changes the negotiation. Kalepa is choosing how fast to grow rather than raising to stay alive, and the $470 million valuation reflects that position.
  • Five quiet years, then a big step up. The gap since the 2021 Series A suggests a company that built its product with customers before asking investors for more. That is a slower path than most AI startups take, and it is the opposite of the raise fast and figure it out later playbook.
  • Insurance AI is a crowded field. Kalepa's bet is that insurers will pay for AI that owns whole workflows and can be audited, not for tools that draft emails. The hiring plan for 2027 will show whether the market agrees, and whether the company can turn a strong year into a durable lead.
  • What to watch. Whether the doubled headcount turns into new markets and products on schedule, whether the customer-reported efficiency gains hold as the platform spreads to more insurers, and whether a company that waited five years between rounds moves faster now that it has the capital.
