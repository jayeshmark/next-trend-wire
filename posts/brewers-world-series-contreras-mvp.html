Brewers Won Their First World Series Behind Contreras

By Editorial Team · 7 min read

The Milwaukee Brewers beat the Tampa Bay Rays 4-1 in Game 5 of the World Series on October 28, 2026, to win the first championship in franchise history. Catcher William Contreras was named World Series MVP after batting .381 in the five game series, with an RBI double in the clincher.

What happened

  • A 4-1 clincher in St. Petersburg. Milwaukee beat Tampa Bay 4-1 in Game 5 at Tropicana Field on the night of October 28, 2026, closing out the series four games to one. It was the first World Series title for a franchise that began as the Seattle Pilots in 1969 and moved to Milwaukee the following year.
  • Contreras earned the MVP. Contreras batted .381 in the series, going 8 for 21 with four runs batted in, and his sixth inning double in Game 5 scored Yelich for an insurance run. He became the eighth catcher to win World Series MVP honors and the first World Series MVP in Brewers history.
  • A dominant October. Milwaukee finished the regular season with 103 wins, the best record in baseball and the first 100 win season in club history, then went 11-2 in the postseason. The Brewers beat San Diego in the Division Series, swept the Los Angeles Dodgers in the Championship Series, and took the World Series in five games.
  • A two title year for Contreras. Contreras was on Venezuela's roster for the 2026 World Baseball Classic, listed among the team's catchers, and Venezuela beat the United States 3-2 in the final on March 17, 2026, at loanDepot park in Miami, winning the country's first World Baseball Classic title. Seven months later he added a World Series ring.

Why it matters

  • The smallest market won. Milwaukee plays in the smallest market in the major leagues, and this title breaks the assumption that only large payroll teams can win in October. The Brewers did it with player development, a deep bullpen and a contact first style, not by outspending the Dodgers or the Yankees.
  • A 57 year wait ended. The franchise had reached the World Series only once before, in 1982, when it lost to the St. Louis Cardinals. Generations of Milwaukee fans had watched near misses, and the clincher in Tampa Bay turned a franchise defined by waiting into one defined by a trophy.
  • The first ABS Series. This was the first World Series played with the Automated Ball-Strike challenge system, and Contreras won multiple appeals during the series. The award therefore rewarded a new skill, the judgment to challenge a call at the right moment, alongside the traditional catching skills of blocking, throwing and calling a game.

Second order effects

  • Small market teams copy the blueprint. Every club with a limited payroll now has a fresh example of how to win: draft and develop pitchers, build the deepest bullpen in baseball, and trust young position players in October. Front offices will study Milwaukee's roster construction the way they once studied Tampa Bay's, and the copycat effect will show up in trades and draft strategies within a year.
  • Catchers gain value. If challenge judgment becomes part of what makes a catcher valuable, teams will scout and develop catchers differently. Framing a pitch for the umpire mattered less the moment a challenge could overturn the call, and the ability to read a pitch in real time and decide in seconds whether to challenge it becomes a skill worth paying for.
  • The Rays stay on the list. With Milwaukee's win, four franchises remain without a World Series title: Tampa Bay, Colorado, San Diego and Seattle. For the Rays, a second World Series loss extends a different kind of drought, and their front office now faces the question of whether a perennial contender needs a different October formula.

Third order effects

  • Replay keeps expanding. The ABS challenge system began as an experiment and ended its first season deciding a World Series MVP race. The third order effect is that every close call in every sport now gets measured against a standard where technology corrects the human eye, and leagues that resist replay will face more pressure from fans who have seen it work.
  • Challenge strategy becomes a coaching job. Teams already employ analysts for defensive shifts and bullpen matchups, and challenge decisions will get the same treatment. Expect a dedicated challenge coach or analyst role, with data on umpire tendencies, pitcher command and game situation feeding decisions that are currently made on instinct in a few seconds.
  • The strike zone itself changes. When catchers, pitchers and hitters all know that a borderline call can be challenged, behavior changes. Pitchers may attack the edges more confidently, hitters may take more close pitches, and the effective strike zone could shift even without any rule change, simply because the cost of a wrong call has fallen.

What to watch next

  • How Milwaukee keeps the core together. The Brewers now have to pay or trade a young core that just won a title, and the choices they make this winter will decide whether 2026 was the start of a run or a single peak. Watch extensions, arbitration decisions and whether the club adds payroll to defend the title.
  • Whether the ABS system expands. The challenge system worked through its first World Series, and the question now is whether it stays a challenge system or grows into full time automated calling. The competition committee's next decision on the system will shape how catchers are valued for a decade.
  • Contreras and the Venezuela connection. Contreras celebrated the title draped in the Venezuelan flag, and his two title year makes him a national figure in Venezuela as well as in Milwaukee. Watch how both the Brewers and Venezuelan baseball build on a player who now connects a World Series and a World Baseball Classic in the same calendar year.
