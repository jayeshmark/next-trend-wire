Traversal Raises $120 Million at a $1 Billion Valuation to Build the AI Site Reliability Engineer

By Jayesh Marathe · November 5, 2026 · 7 min read

Traversal, a New York startup building AI agents that diagnose, fix, and prevent software outages, has raised $120 million in a Series B round at a $1 billion valuation, the company announced on November 3, 2026. Kleiner Perkins, Redpoint Ventures, and Khosla Ventures co-led the round, which lifts Traversal's total funding to $173 million.

The round

  • The raise. Traversal announced $120 million in new funding at a $1 billion valuation, and said the money will support its vision of extending AI across the software development lifecycle by making production systems more resilient.
  • The co-leads. Kleiner Perkins, Redpoint Ventures, and Khosla Ventures co-led the round, with participation from Sequoia Capital, Battery Ventures, and Amex Ventures.
  • The returning backers. Kleiner Perkins, which led Traversal's Series A, increased its investment, while Sequoia, which led the seed round, deployed its growth fund to increase its stake well beyond its existing position, a step the company says the firm reserves for its highest conviction companies.
  • The funding history. Traversal came out of stealth in June 2025 with $48 million led by Sequoia and Kleiner Perkins, followed by a strategic investment from Amex Ventures in December 2025, and the new round brings its total funding to $173 million.
  • The hiring plan. On the heels of the raise, Traversal is hiring across research, engineering, and go-to-market roles as it builds out the team behind the product.

What Traversal builds

  • The product. Traversal describes itself as the AI site reliability engineer for the enterprise, helping engineering teams autonomously diagnose, remediate, and prevent production incidents instead of reconstructing failures by hand after they happen.
  • The engine. Its Production World Model and Causal Search Engine give AI a continuously updated understanding of production systems, their dependencies, and the root causes of incidents, which is what lets the agents work across different companies' software environments.
  • The gap it points to. On ORCA-bench, Traversal's production fidelity benchmark built with Cornell Tech and Columbia University, the best of five frontier agents achieved just 25.3 percent accuracy on medium difficulty incidents, dropping to 10 percent when incident reports were vague, across 1,079 root cause tasks, with hallucination rates running as high as 40 percent.
  • The customers. Traversal names Nubank, Harvey, PepsiCo, and DigitalOcean among the companies using the product, and says it is live inside Fortune 100 companies just 15 months after emerging from stealth.
  • The Nubank rollout. Nubank founder and CEO David Velez said that five weeks into the company's rollout, Traversal is already becoming part of how its on-call engineers work incidents, and that Nubank is expanding it wave by wave as those results hold up.
  • The usage growth. Traversal says its annual recurring revenue has grown ninefold year to date, while product usage is up nearly 100 times and monthly active users are up nearly 20 times over the same period, with customers running more than 150,000 investigations last quarter.
  • The incident scale. The product supported more than 4,000 engineers across more than 1,000 major incidents, 50,000 minor incidents, and 5,000 significant production changes, returning tens of millions of dollars in return on investment across customers, according to the company.

Why the company says the timing matters

  • The cost argument. "Site reliability engineering is one of the hardest problems in applied AI because every company's software environment is different, and with downtime costing companies as much as $600 billion a year, the stakes are enormous," said Anish Agarwal, CEO and co-founder of Traversal.
  • The cited research. Traversal cites a 2026 Splunk and Oxford Economics report finding that downtime costs have surged 50 percent in two years, with engineers losing seven hours a week to troubleshooting, and a Gartner projection that 85 percent of enterprises will adopt AI site reliability tooling by 2029, up from under 5 percent in 2025.
  • The AI code pressure. The company argues that teams now write 180 percent more code than they could two years ago, much of it with AI help, which means more software breaks in more ways that no single team can untangle by hand.
  • The American Express view. American Express CTO Matthew Liste said the company's work with Traversal is helping its engineering teams gain better insight into their production environment, giving them more context to identify and resolve issues at the scale and standards American Express demands.

The people and the pitch

  • The founders. Traversal was founded by causal machine learning researchers from MIT, Berkeley, Cornell, and Columbia: Anish Agarwal and Raj Agrawal earned their PhDs at MIT, Raaz Dwivedi earned his at UC Berkeley, and Ahmed Lone came from quant trading at Citadel Securities, while Agarwal and Dwivedi have kept faculty appointments at Columbia University and Cornell Tech.
  • The Khosla case. "Traversal is compressing hours of incident investigation into minutes, and the company is on a path to becoming the system of intelligence for how enterprise software runs and heals itself," said Jai Sajnani, partner at Khosla Ventures.
  • The Kleiner case. "Traversal gives engineering teams the intelligence they need to understand their systems, resolve incidents faster, and operate with confidence at a scale that simply wasn't possible before," said Aditya Naganath, partner at Kleiner Perkins.
  • The Redpoint case. Redpoint's Sai Senthilkumar and Scott Raney said Traversal has clearly cemented itself as the fastest growing and largest AI site reliability vendor by scale, and that internal usage has skyrocketed as large enterprises begin trusting it with their most critical incidents.
  • The end goal. Co-founder Raaz Dwivedi said Traversal is moving toward what the company calls superintelligent AI site reliability engineering, making the outer loop of the software development lifecycle resilient to complement the increasingly autonomous inner loop of AI coding agents, so the full lifecycle becomes autonomous end to end.
  • The vision in one line. "This funding brings us closer to our vision of letting engineers engineer," Agarwal said, framing the product as a way to hand incident reconstruction to machines and return nights and weekends to the people who build software.
