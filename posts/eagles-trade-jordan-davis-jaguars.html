Eagles Trade Jordan Davis to the Jaguars for a 2027 First Round Pick and a 2029 Second Rounder

By Editorial Team · 7 min read

The Philadelphia Eagles traded defensive tackle Jordan Davis to the Jacksonville Jaguars on November 9 for a 2027 first round pick and a 2029 second round pick, a blockbuster deal struck less than 24 hours before the NFL trade deadline.

The deal

  • The return. The Eagles agreed to send Davis, a former first round pick, Super Bowl champion and team captain, to Jacksonville in exchange for a first round pick in 2027 and a second round pick in 2029.
  • The timing. The teams agreed to the deal on Monday evening, with the NFL trade deadline set for 4 p.m. ET on Tuesday, November 10, and the Eagles announced the agreement that night.
  • A war chest. The trade leaves Philadelphia with 10 picks in the first two rounds of the next three drafts, including two first round picks in 2027, two first round picks and two second round picks in 2028, and multiple second round picks in 2029.
  • The offer. The Jaguars made an offer that Executive Vice President and General Manager Howie Roseman simply could not refuse, according to the Eagles' own announcement of the trade.

Why Philadelphia moved him

  • A lost season. The Eagles fell to 2-7 with a 19-11 loss to the New York Giants on November 8, their seventh consecutive defeat, a collapse that dashed any hope of remaining in the NFC's contending class.
  • A stunning reversal. Davis signed a three year extension worth as much as $78 million in March, with $65 million guaranteed, and was expected to anchor the defensive line for years alongside Jalen Carter, who signed a four year deal worth as much as $152 million in the summer.
  • The contract. The extension keeps Davis under contract through the 2029 season, so Jacksonville acquires far more than a rental, and the Jaguars will take on the deal.
  • The reset. Trading a 26 year old captain eight months after extending him signals that Roseman is eyeing a far more ambitious overhaul of the roster over the next several years, stockpiling premium picks while the team sits last in the NFC East.
  • His final game. Davis got his first sack of the season in the loss to the Giants, playing 38 of Philadelphia's 67 defensive snaps, and afterward was asked about the trade deadline in the context of teammates leaving, never expecting he would be the one to go.
  • The line after him. With Davis gone, the Eagles have Carter, Moro Ojomo, Byron Young and Ty Robinson on the interior defensive line, and the team has told other clubs that Ojomo, who is due for a new contract after the season, is not available.

What Jacksonville gets

  • A contender loading up. The Jaguars sit at 6-2 and lead the AFC South, and Davis is their third defensive addition of the day, following Pro Bowl safety Jessie Bates III from Atlanta and defensive tackle Davon Godchaux from New Orleans.
  • A championship defense. Davis joins Arik Armstead in the middle of the line between Travon Walker and Josh Hines-Allen, giving coordinator Anthony Campanile a proven inside disruptor for a unit the Jaguars are building to championship level.
  • The player. Davis, 6 foot 6 and 336 pounds, was the 13th overall pick in the 2022 draft out of Georgia, won Super Bowl LIX with Philadelphia, and has recorded 31 tackles and 1.5 sacks in nine games this season after a career best 4.5 sacks last year.
  • The price of ambition. Sending a first round pick two drafts out and a second rounder three drafts out is what a team does when it believes its roster is close, and Jacksonville has now spent its 2027 first and second round picks on this deadline's defensive haul.

What comes next

  • The deadline. The trade deadline arrives at 4 p.m. ET on Tuesday, November 10, and the Jaguars, who have been the most aggressive buyers in the league, may not be done.
  • A debut in teal. Davis is expected to join the Jaguars immediately, with Jacksonville's next game offering his first chance to show what its new defensive front can do.
  • The Eagles' future. Philadelphia now holds two first round picks in 2027, its own, currently projected at No. 5 overall, and Jacksonville's, currently projected at No. 25, giving Roseman the capital to reshape the roster in a hurry.
