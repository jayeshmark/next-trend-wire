Yes, Dear Star Anthony Clark Dies at 62

By Jayesh Marathe · November 12, 2026 · 7 min read

Anthony Clark, the comedian and actor best known for playing Greg Warner on the CBS sitcom Yes, Dear, died on Tuesday. He was 62. His former co-star Mike O'Malley announced the death on Wednesday, remembering Clark as a hilarious guy and a one of a kind comic.

The announcement

  • The news. O'Malley shared the news on X on Wednesday, November 11. "Anthony Clark died yesterday. He was a hilarious guy," he wrote. No cause of death was shared.
  • The tribute. O'Malley wrote that when he and his wife Lisa were dating in the mid nineties, Clark was already famous in Boston, regularly selling out comedy shows. "The first time we saw him at The Comedy Connection we laughed and laughed and laughed," he wrote.
  • The friendship. "He was so unique, one of those comics who is laughing along with you at the absurdity of his stories and observations," O'Malley wrote. "I was a fan before I was a friend."
  • The last call. O'Malley said he and Clark last spoke three weeks ago. "We spoke on the phone three weeks ago. He had us laughing loudly once again," he wrote. He attached snapshots from the last episode of Yes, Dear and signed off: "Rest in peace, Tony."
  • The first word. O'Malley's post was the first public word of the death, and the news spread from his announcement.

The career

  • The Boston years. Clark was born April 4, 1964, in Lynchburg, Virginia, and graduated from Emerson College before building a career as a stand-up comedian in Boston. He was already a draw in the city's clubs before television found him.
  • Yes, Dear. Clark starred as Gregory "Greg" Warner, an uptight television studio executive, on the CBS sitcom Yes, Dear, alongside O'Malley, Jean Louisa Kelly and Liza Snyder. The show, created by Greg Garcia and Alan Kirschenbaum, ran for six seasons on CBS between 2000 and 2006 and totaled 122 episodes.
  • Boston Common. Before Yes, Dear, Clark starred in NBC's short lived sitcom Boston Common, another showcase for the Boston comic playing a version of his own world.
  • The Young Comedians. Clark was featured alongside Garry Shandling, Dave Chappelle and Dave Attell on HBO's 1995 Young Comedians special, a credential that marked him as one of the promising comics of his generation.
  • The films. His film credits included 1993's The Thing Called Love and 2012's My Uncle Rafael. He also served as the season four host of Last Comic Standing, the NBC stand-up competition.
  • The quiet years. Clark stepped back from regular television work after Yes, Dear ended about twenty years ago. He kept a low public profile, and his death was announced by the co-star who had known him since their Boston days.

Why he mattered

  • The straight man. Greg Warner was the uptight half of Yes, Dear's two couple household, the foil to O'Malley's looser Jimmy Hughes. Clark played the role without winking at it, which is what made the jokes land.
  • The Boston comic. Clark came up in the Boston scene that produced a generation of stand-ups, and O'Malley's tribute is a reminder that his reputation there was built on live rooms, not on television first.
  • The 122 episodes. Six seasons and 122 episodes is a long run for any sitcom, and Yes, Dear reruns kept Clark's face familiar long after the show ended.

What to watch next

  • The tributes. More of Clark's contemporaries from the Boston comedy scene and the Yes, Dear cast are expected to share memories in the coming days.
  • The cause. No cause of death has been shared by the family or by O'Malley, and any report of one should be treated as unconfirmed until the family says so.
