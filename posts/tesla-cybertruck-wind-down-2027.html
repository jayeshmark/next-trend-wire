Tesla Winds Down Cybertruck Production to Make Room for Cybercabs

By Editorial Team · 7 min read

Tesla will wind down production of the Cybertruck around the middle of 2027, chief executive Elon Musk said on Friday, November 6. The electric pickup will give up its line at Gigafactory Texas so the plant can build more Cybercabs, Tesla's robotaxi. The move ends a troubled run for a truck that never came close to its sales targets.

What Musk announced

  • One post ended the speculation. Musk wrote on X: "Tesla will be winding down production of the Cybertruck at Gigafactory Texas around the middle of next year to make room for increased Cybercab production." He gave no firm end date, no production total and no word on outstanding orders.
  • He called it the end of an era. Replying to a user on X, Musk called the Cybertruck "the best vehicle Tesla has ever made" and agreed that the wind-down marked the "end of an era." The praise sat oddly next to the decision, and it captured how personally Musk had backed the truck.
  • The truck's own engineer said goodbye in the past tense. Cybertruck lead engineer Wes Morrill wrote that the truck "was an incredible technology development platform and lives on through Cybercab." He also indicated that parts and service support will continue for the trucks already on the road, which matters to owners who bought into a vehicle with unique parts.
  • Tesla has not said what happens next. The company has not said whether Cybertruck production will move to another plant or stop altogether, and its Austin factory is the only plant known to be equipped to build the truck's specialized parts. Tesla did not respond to a request for comment sent outside regular business hours.

Why sales stalled

  • Buyers never showed up in the numbers Tesla planned. Tesla built the Cybertruck line for 250,000 trucks a year. Kelley Blue Book estimates the company sold 20,237 Cybertrucks in the United States in 2025, down 48 percent from 38,965 in 2024.
  • This year looks worse. Cox Automotive estimates Tesla sold 11,586 Cybertrucks in the first nine months of 2026, down 28 percent from the same period a year earlier. The truck now accounts for a tiny slice of the American electric vehicle market, about 1.6 percent of United States EV sales by Cox's count.
  • Tesla's own reports tell the same story. Tesla groups the Cybertruck with the Model S, Model X and Semi under "Other Models," and that category delivered 8,295 vehicles in the third quarter of 2026, down 48 percent from a year earlier. Tesla does not break out the truck on its own, but the bucket it sits in has fallen to its lowest level since the second quarter of 2021.
  • The factory was built for far more. Tesla's latest quarterly update still lists Cybertruck installed capacity above 125,000 units a year at Gigafactory Texas, far above the roughly 20,000 trucks it sold in the United States last year. The capacity table in the update shows the scale of the bet: a factory built for a mass market truck that stayed a niche product.
  • The truck has been recalled 11 times. The National Highway Traffic Safety Administration counts 11 recalls for the Cybertruck since it went on sale in late 2023, a steady drip of fixes for a vehicle that was supposed to showcase Tesla's manufacturing skill.
  • The first deliveries were only three years ago. Tesla handed over the first Cybertrucks in November 2023, two years behind its original schedule, after unveiling the angular stainless steel truck in 2019. The wind-down will come about three and a half years after those first deliveries, a short life for a vehicle Musk once pitched as a rival to America's best-selling pickups.

What takes its place

  • The Cybercab is a purpose-built robotaxi. It has butterfly doors and no steering wheel, pedals or mirrors, and Musk has made it central to his plan to turn Tesla into an AI and robotics company rather than just a carmaker.
  • Production started this year. Tesla began building Cybercabs at Gigafactory Texas in April, and started providing paid Robotaxi rides using Cybercabs on September 3 in Austin. The update lists the Austin launch among the quarter's highlights, alongside record production in Berlin and the company's 10 millionth vehicle.
  • The robotaxi fleet is still small. About 570 of the more than 1,200 Tesla robotaxis operating in Texas are Cybercabs, according to the state's Department of Motor Vehicles, with the rest mainly Model Y vehicles. The program is months old, not years, and it has yet to prove itself commercially.
  • Tesla wants to sell the Cybercab to the public. The company aims to begin selling the two-seat Cybercab to customers by the end of 2026. That would turn a robotaxi into a retail product, and it is the bet the Cybertruck's factory floor is now being cleared for.

What to watch next

  • Whether production ends or moves. Tesla has not named another plant for the Cybertruck, and no other factory is known to be tooled for its stainless steel body and castings. If no new home appears, the wind-down is a shutdown.
  • What happens to orders and inventory. Musk said nothing about how long Tesla will keep selling trucks from inventory or what happens to open orders, and owners are waiting to hear how long parts and service will last.
  • Whether the Cybercab bet pays off. The wind-down frees the line for a vehicle that has yet to prove itself commercially, and Tesla is betting its future on autonomy rather than a second act for the pickup.
  • How many trucks are left to build. With no firm end date, the total number of Cybertrucks Tesla will ever build is still unknown, and the final count will say a lot about how far short of Musk's ambitions the truck fell.
