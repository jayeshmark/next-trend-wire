Samsung Is Shutting Down Samsung News in India and South Korea on December 21

By Editorial Team · 7 min read

Samsung has told Galaxy owners in India and South Korea that Samsung News will be discontinued on December 21, 2026, ending the built in news feed that has sat on the home screen of Galaxy phones and tablets since 2023, and users there who rely on it now have seven weeks to find a replacement.

What Samsung announced

  • The date. Samsung said in an in-app notice that Samsung News will be discontinued as of December 21, 2026, at 00:00 UTC, and the notice, dated October 26, 2026, has begun reaching users inside the app.
  • The exact wording. The notice reads: "Thank you for using Samsung News. We would like to inform you that Samsung News will be discontinued as of December 21, 2026, 00:00 (UTC). Following the discontinuation of the service, the Samsung News widget will no longer receive content updates. We kindly recommend removing the widget from your Home screen."
  • What goes away. The shutdown covers the Samsung News app itself, the widget, and the dedicated media page on the leftmost panel of the Galaxy home screen, so the whole feed disappears rather than just one part of it.
  • The data deletion. Samsung says user data, profiles and personalization information tied to Samsung News will be deleted after the service ends, except where local law requires retention for a set period, after which that data will also be erased, so any tailored feed setup tied to the service will not survive the cutoff.
  • No reason given. Samsung has not given a reason for the shutdown, and it has not announced a replacement service for Samsung News.

Where the shutdown is confirmed, and where it is not

  • The confirmed markets. So far the discontinuation notice has appeared only for users in India and South Korea, and those are the only markets where Samsung has confirmed the shutdown.
  • The open question. Samsung has not confirmed whether the shutdown reaches other markets, including the United States, so Galaxy owners elsewhere cannot yet tell from Samsung's own statements whether their feed will stop on the same date.
  • How to check. The only reliable check for now is to open Samsung News on your own device and look for the banner.
  • The planning date. The December 21 date applies to the confirmed markets, so owners elsewhere should watch for a notice on their own device rather than assume the same cutoff.

What Samsung News was

  • The launch. Samsung launched Samsung News in 2023 as an update to Samsung Free, offering morning and evening briefings, custom feeds and podcasts in one place on Galaxy devices.
  • The pitch. Samsung presented the service as a curated selection of news tailored to each user's preferences, with editors picking the top headlines for a morning briefing and an evening briefing each day.
  • The slot. Samsung News occupied the leftmost panel of the Galaxy home screen, and Galaxy owners could also place its widget on the home screen.
  • The footprint. Samsung expanded Samsung News to Canada in July 2023, and the service is now offered in multiple markets, which is why a shutdown confirmed so far only in India and South Korea still matters to Galaxy owners elsewhere.

What Galaxy owners should do

  • Check for the notice. Open Samsung News and look for the discontinuation banner, which confirms the shutdown for that device and account.
  • Switch the feed. Galaxy phones already offer Google Discover as a feed option for the home screen panel, and owners can switch to it before December 21 so the panel can keep showing news.
  • Remove the widget. Samsung recommends removing the Samsung News widget from the home screen after the shutdown, since it will stop receiving content updates.
  • Save what matters. Owners who follow specific topics or sources in Samsung News should note them down before the cutoff, since the feed and its personalization will be deleted with the service.
  • Watch for wider confirmation. Owners outside India and South Korea should watch for a notice on their own device or a Samsung statement for their market, since Samsung has not yet confirmed the shutdown beyond the two markets.
