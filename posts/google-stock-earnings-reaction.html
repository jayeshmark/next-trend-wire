Alphabet Grew Revenue 25 Percent While AI Spending Outran Cash Flow

By Editorial Team · 7 min read

Alphabet reported revenue of $127.6 billion for the third quarter, up 25 percent, while operating income rose 30 percent to $40.6 billion. But the company spent $51.4 billion on property and equipment against $42.3 billion of operating cash flow, leaving free cash flow at negative $9.1 billion. Google stock drew more than 200,000 searches on Google Trends.

What happened

  • Revenue beat, profit fell. Alphabet reported third quarter revenue of $127.6 billion, up 25 percent from a year earlier, in its earnings release filed with the Securities and Exchange Commission on October 28, 2026. Operating income rose 30 percent to $40.6 billion, yet net income available to common stockholders fell to $26.2 billion, or $2.12 per diluted share, down from $2.87 a year earlier.
  • An investment loss did the damage. Other income swung to a net expense of $8.2 billion, from income of $12.8 billion a year earlier, because of a net loss of $8.7 billion on equity securities. That loss reduced net income by $6.7 billion and diluted earnings per share by $0.55, which explains how profit fell while the operating business grew.
  • Cloud more than doubled. Google Cloud revenue reached $30.7 billion, up 103 percent, and Cloud operating income more than tripled to $11.1 billion from $3.6 billion a year earlier. Alphabet attributed the growth to demand for enterprise AI infrastructure and AI solutions, plus sales of its own Tensor Processing Unit systems.
  • Cash flow went negative. Alphabet generated $42.3 billion of operating cash flow in the quarter while purchases of property and equipment reached $51.4 billion, producing free cash flow of negative $9.1 billion. It was the second consecutive quarter of negative free cash flow, after the same measure turned negative in the second quarter of 2026.
  • Search interest spiked. Google stock appeared on Google Trends in the United States with a search volume band above 200,000 in the past 24 hours. The spike in curiosity, rather than any single price print, is the observable market reaction in this post, because the search data is public and the after hours price reports conflicted with each other.

Why it matters

  • The beat was not the story. Revenue grew 25 percent and Cloud doubled, yet the conversation after the release centered on cash, not growth. That is because the quarter showed a company whose core business is still expanding while its spending is expanding faster, and investors have to decide which trend matters more.
  • Cash flow is the constraint. Demand is not the problem in this report. The problem is arithmetic: $51.4 billion of quarterly capital spending against $42.3 billion of operating cash flow means the AI buildout is currently funded from the balance sheet and from outside capital, not from the cash the business generates in the same quarter.
  • Buybacks stopped. Alphabet bought back no stock in the first nine months of 2026, against $39.8 billion of buybacks in the same period of 2025, according to the cash flow statement in its filing. When a company stops shrinking its share count, it is a quiet signal that cash has a higher priority use, and in this case that use is data centers, servers and chips.

Second order effects

  • AI becomes a capital allocation story. When every large technology company reports the same pattern of rising capital spending and falling free cash flow, the competitive question shifts from who has the best model to who can fund the largest buildout the longest. Balance sheet strength becomes a product feature, and smaller rivals without it fall behind on infrastructure even if their research keeps pace.
  • Chip and power suppliers win first. The money Alphabet spends lands first with chip makers, server builders and energy providers, long before it returns as Cloud revenue. That front loads profits for suppliers while Alphabet carries the risk that demand arrives slower than the capacity it is building.
  • Advertising faces a monetization question. Search query growth is at a five year high, yet the open question is whether AI generated answers earn as much per query as the traditional results page. If they earn less, Search can grow in usage while growing slower in revenue, which would tighten the cash flow math further.

Third order effects

  • Energy demand becomes a local political issue. Data center construction on this scale turns electricity supply, water use and permitting into community debates in the towns where facilities are built. The AI race therefore stops being only a technology story and becomes a land use and utility story, with local opposition able to delay projects that headquarters approved years earlier.
  • A correction would hit suppliers first. If AI revenue growth ever disappoints, the companies that feel it first will not be the model builders but the suppliers whose order books depend on continued capital spending. That reverses the usual pattern, where suppliers are seen as the safe way to invest in a boom, and it makes the whole supply chain more cyclical than it appears during the buildout.

What to watch next

  • Fourth quarter capital spending. The next release will show whether Alphabet stays inside its $195 billion to $205 billion full year range or pushes beyond it. A move above the range would confirm that the cash flow gap is widening, while a move below it would suggest the buildout is being managed more conservatively than planned.
  • A return to positive free cash flow. Watch whether operating cash flow grows fast enough to cover capital spending again. Two negative quarters in a row is a pattern, and a third would change the story from a temporary investment surge to a structural funding shift.
