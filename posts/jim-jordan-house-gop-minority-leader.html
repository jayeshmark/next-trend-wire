Jim Jordan Beats Scalise 106-73 to Lead House Republicans Into the Minority

By Jayesh Marathe · November 10, 2026 · 7 min read

House Republicans elected Rep. Jim Jordan of Ohio as their next leader on November 10, choosing the combative Judiciary Committee chairman over Majority Leader Steve Scalise in a 106-73 secret ballot vote one week after midterm losses cost the party control of Congress.

The vote

  • The result. Jordan won 106 votes to 73 for Scalise in a closed door vote of the House Republican Conference, according to lawmakers in the room, held in the Capitol Visitor Center on Tuesday afternoon.
  • The job. Jordan will serve as minority leader in the 120th Congress, which convenes on January 3, 2027, and he will lead the party's opposition after Democrats take control of the House.
  • The opening. The post came open when Speaker Mike Johnson announced on November 4 that he would not seek the minority leader role, saying the toll of the job on his family had led him to step aside.
  • The rematch. Jordan and Scalise faced off once before, in the 2023 race for speaker after Kevin McCarthy was ousted, when Scalise won the internal vote 113-99 before neither man could claim the gavel.
  • The rest of the team. House Republicans also voted to keep Rep. Tom Emmer of Minnesota as their whip, turning back a challenge from Rep. August Pfluger of Texas, who leads the Republican Study Committee.

Who Jordan is

  • The veteran. Jordan, 62, has served in the House for about 20 years and has chaired the Judiciary Committee for the last four years, but he has never held a senior leadership post in the party.
  • The founder. He co-founded the House Freedom Caucus and served as its first chairman, building his national profile as a confrontational questioner in Oversight and Judiciary hearings.
  • The third attempt. The victory came roughly eight years after Jordan first launched a bid to become the top House Republican, and it marks his third attempt at landing the role.
  • The pitch. In a letter to colleagues before the vote, Jordan called himself battle tested on oversight and said he knows the Democrats' playbook, arguing that Republicans need a leadership team that is active, engaged, forward thinking and straight shooting.
  • The rival. Scalise, 61, has held senior leadership posts since 2014 and has served as the No. 2 House Republican since 2019, pitching continuity and experience against Jordan's call for a shake up.

What Jordan said

  • The promise. "Over the next two years House Republicans will fight for American families," Jordan told reporters after the vote, without taking questions. "When Democrats offer more government, we'll offer more freedom. We will bring that fight in committee, on the floor and on the stump every single day."
  • The backing. House Freedom Caucus Chairman Andy Harris called Jordan "a fierce fighter, uniter, principled conservative, and relentless advocate for the American people" and said he is exactly the right man to fight back against the Democrats' agenda.
  • The ally. Jordan has been one of President Donald Trump's most vocal allies in Congress through both terms, and his rise puts a Trump loyalist at the top of the House Republican Conference.

Why the choice matters

  • The scale of the loss. Republicans lost more than 40 House seats in the November 3 midterms, along with the Senate, and Jordan inherits a historically small conference that is only beginning to grapple with its loss of power.
  • The opposition. Democrats are expected to launch investigations into the Trump administration when they take control in January, and Jordan's oversight experience is the reason his supporters say he is the right leader for the moment.
  • The other side. Rep. Hakeem Jeffries of New York is in line to become speaker, the first African American to hold the job, with Rep. Katherine Clark of Massachusetts expected to become majority leader.
  • The task. As minority leader, Jordan will be responsible for trying to win back seats, raising money for the party and negotiating with Democrats on bills that must pass, including government funding measures.

What comes next

  • The handover. Johnson remains speaker until the new Congress is sworn in on January 3, when Democrats take control of the chamber and Jordan formally becomes minority leader.
  • The Democrats. Democrats hold their own leadership elections next week, and the new majority will set the agenda Jordan must answer.
  • The test. Jordan's first job is to hold together a conference that just lost a midterm rout and disagrees over how much to fight and how much to compromise, with the 2028 campaign to win back the House already in view.
