Berkshire Hathaway Buys Back $9.6 Billion of Stock as Operating Profit Falls

By Jayesh Marathe · November 7, 2026 · 7 min read

Berkshire Hathaway bought back $9.6 billion of its own stock in the third quarter, its biggest quarterly repurchase of the year, even as operating earnings fell 5.8 percent. The results, released on Saturday, show the conglomerate leaning harder on buybacks in Greg Abel's first year as chief executive.

The numbers

  • Operating earnings fell 5.8 percent. Operating earnings fell to $12.705 billion in the third quarter from $13.485 billion a year earlier, a drop of 5.8 percent.
  • Net earnings fell about 28 percent. Net earnings fell to $22.112 billion from $30.796 billion a year earlier, as investment gains shrank.
  • Investment gains shrank sharply. Investment gains were $9.407 billion in the quarter, down from $17.311 billion a year earlier, and that swing explains most of the drop in net earnings.
  • The nine months still look strong. Operating earnings for the first nine months rose 8.0 percent to $37.034 billion from $34.286 billion, so the quarter broke from a stronger year to date.
  • Buybacks more than doubled. Berkshire bought back about $9.6 billion of its shares in the third quarter, more than the roughly $4.8 billion it spent in the entire first half, taking nine-month repurchases to about $14.4 billion.
  • Berkshire also bought more in October. Berkshire bought about $2 billion more of its stock in October, Reuters reported, pushing the year's buyback total higher still.
  • Earnings per share fell. Net earnings came to $15,502 per Class A share, down from $21,413 a year earlier.
  • Revenue rose 11 percent. Revenue rose to $105.65 billion, up 11 percent from a year earlier, Reuters reported.

Where the quarter went wrong

  • Insurance underwriting dropped 43 percent. Insurance underwriting profit fell to $1.352 billion from $2.369 billion a year earlier, a drop of about 43 percent.
  • GEICO led the insurance decline. GEICO's underwriting profit fell about 45 percent as claims costs and advertising spending rose, according to the company's quarterly filing.
  • Investment income also slipped. Insurance investment income fell to $2.734 billion from $3.181 billion.
  • A currency swing hurt. Berkshire took $343 million of foreign currency exchange losses on non-dollar debt in the quarter, against gains of $331 million a year earlier.

What grew

  • The railroad ran ahead. BNSF earned $1.729 billion, up from $1.449 billion a year earlier, a rise of about 19 percent.
  • Energy earnings rose. Berkshire Hathaway Energy earned $1.612 billion, up from $1.489 billion a year earlier.
  • Manufacturing and retail grew. Manufacturing, service and retailing earned $4.042 billion, up from $3.616 billion a year earlier.
  • The cash pile shrank. Berkshire ended September with about $356.9 billion of cash, down from $380.2 billion six months earlier, according to its quarterly filing.
  • Insurance float grew. Insurance float was about $178.5 billion at September 30, up about $2.1 billion since the end of 2025.

What the buybacks signal

  • Abel is spending faster than Buffett did. The pace marks a shift from the Buffett era: Reuters noted that Abel has shown a greater willingness than Buffett to put Berkshire's cash pile to use.
  • Buybacks are the main outlet for the cash. With the cash pile still near $357 billion, repurchases are the fastest way to return money to shareholders while Berkshire hunts for large acquisitions.
  • The policy stays discretionary. Berkshire buys back stock only when it judges the price attractive and its liquidity safeguards allow, and the program has no required minimum or maximum, according to the company's filing.
  • The market reacts on Monday. Markets were closed on Saturday, so the first trading reaction to the results comes on Monday.

Abel's first year in charge

  • Abel took over from Buffett in January. Greg Abel succeeded Warren Buffett as chief executive in January 2026, and this is his third quarterly report in the job.
  • Buffett moved to Chairman Emeritus in September. Berkshire announced on September 18 that Warren Buffett had become Chairman Emeritus while remaining a director, and Howard Buffett was elected chairman of the board.
  • Buffett praised the handover. In a letter to shareholders, Buffett wrote that Abel "has taken hold of the Chief Executive Officer job in every respect" and that the company is in excellent hands.
  • The buyback pace is the clearest change so far. The cash pile stood near $380 billion at midyear, and Abel has now spent $14.4 billion on repurchases in nine months.

What to watch next

  • Whether buybacks keep accelerating. October added about $2 billion more, and the fourth quarter will show whether Abel keeps spending at this pace.
  • Whether GEICO recovers. GEICO's claims costs will be the key line to watch when Berkshire reports next.
  • Whether insurance underwriting rebounds. The underwriting drop drove the quarter's decline, so a recovery there would lift operating earnings fast.
  • What Abel does with the remaining cash. With about $357 billion still on hand, investors will watch for a large acquisition alongside the buybacks.
