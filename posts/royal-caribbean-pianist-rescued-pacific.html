Fishermen Pull Royal Caribbean Pianist From the Pacific After Nine Hours in the Water

By Jayesh Marathe · November 8, 2026 · 7 min read

Two fishermen from Newport Beach pulled a Royal Caribbean crew member from the Pacific Ocean about 20 miles off the California coast on Friday after he spent roughly nine hours treading water, and the man told them he was a pianist on the cruise ship Ovation of the Seas. The man was taken to a hospital and is expected to survive, and the United States Coast Guard is investigating how he went overboard.

The rescue

  • The discovery. Sean Norton and Grant Garrett, both of Newport Beach, were heading home from a morning of marlin fishing near Catalina Island on Friday when their boat's captain spotted a man in the water around noon, nearly 20 miles off the coast.
  • The pullout. Norton and Garrett grabbed an arm each and lifted the man out of the water and onto their boat. The man appeared to be in total shock when they reached him, Norton said.
  • The first words. After the man regained consciousness, he told the fishermen he was a pianist on the 14 deck Royal Caribbean cruise ship Ovation of the Seas. "Thank you. I thought I was gonna die," he told Norton, according to CBS Los Angeles.
  • The call for help. The fishermen sent out an emergency maritime call, and Newport Beach lifeguards responded and transported the man to shore, where he was taken to a hospital.
  • The distance. "It was approximately 15 to 20 miles offshore, pretty much in the middle of the ocean," Battalion Chief Adam Yacenda of the Newport Beach lifeguards said.

The man in the water

  • The timeline. Newport Beach lifeguard officials believe the crew member went overboard at approximately 2:30 a.m. on Friday, nearly nine hours before the fishermen found him around noon.
  • The ship. The Ovation of the Seas docked in Los Angeles on Friday morning around 7 a.m., hours before the man was found, and disembarked its passengers without disruption.
  • The identity. The man has not been publicly identified, and the Coast Guard has not released his name. Royal Caribbean confirmed he is a crew member but said it will share no further details to respect his privacy.
  • The survival. Norton said the man likely survived so long in the water because warmer than usual ocean temperatures this year kept the water from turning dangerously cold.

What is still unknown

  • How he went overboard. It is not clear what happened or how the man ended up in the ocean, and no official account of the fall has been released.
  • The ship's alert. It is not known whether the ship raised an alarm when the crew member went missing, and Royal Caribbean has not said when it learned he was gone.
  • The investigation. The United States Coast Guard is investigating the circumstances of the incident, and Royal Caribbean said it is providing support and assistance to its crew member during a difficult time.
  • The statement. "We are providing support and assistance to our crew member during this difficult time," Royal Caribbean Group said in a statement. "To respect the privacy of our employee, we have no additional details to share."
