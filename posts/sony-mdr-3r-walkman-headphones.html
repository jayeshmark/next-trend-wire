Sony Brings Back Its 1979 Walkman Headphones as the $60 MDR-3R

By Jayesh Marathe · November 7, 2026 · 7 min read

Sony has revived the headphones that shipped with the first Walkman, announcing the MDR-3R on November 5 at $59.99. The wired USB-C model copies the 1979 original down to its 23mm drivers and orange ear pads, and a Stranger Things edition follows at $79.99.

What Sony announced

  • The headphones are on sale now. The MDR-3R is available now with a suggested retail price of $59.99 in the US, at Sony, Amazon, Best Buy, and other authorized dealers. Sony Electronics announced the model on November 5 from San Diego.
  • The design reaches back 47 years. The MDR-3R takes its design from the original MDR-3 model that debuted alongside the first Walkman TPS-L2 player in 1979. Introduced in 1979, the Walkman and the MDR-3 helped create a new way of enjoying music freely on the go, and Sony calls the new model a faithful modern take on that first pair.
  • The sound stays close to the original. Sony re-engineered the headphones with the same 23mm driver units as the MDR-3 to reproduce sound quality close to the original. Subtle refinements tune the sound for modern listening, while the natural, well balanced signature carries forward through generations of Sony headphones.
  • The look is the point. The MDR-3R carries forward the original aesthetic with its iconic blue and orange ear pads, which owners can swap to suit their mood. Even the packaging and the instruction manual inherit the design of the original model, and the lightweight open ear design is built for long listening sessions.

What changed from 1979

  • USB-C replaces the headphone jack. With USB-C support, the headphones connect directly to compatible smartphones and other devices, with no charging, pairing, or updates required. Plug them in and the music starts, which is the whole pitch: wired headphones never need a charge and never drop a Bluetooth connection.
  • A microphone and remote join the cable. The MDR-3R adds a built-in microphone for calls and an in-line remote for playback and call controls. The original MDR-3 did neither, so the new model works as a headset for calls and video as well as a music player.
  • One quirk survives in the fine print. Sony notes that the headphones are compatible with USB Audio Class 1.0 devices, and that use with a Walkman is not recommended. The company that invented the Walkman now ships headphones that officially do not work with one, which says something about how far the product line has moved on.
  • The materials got greener. Recycled plastic is used in a portion of the headphone body, and the packaging is plastic free. The environmental touches are small, but they mark the difference between a straight reissue and a product built for today's buyers.

The Stranger Things edition

  • A limited edition ties the headphones to Netflix. Sony will also launch an MDR-3R Stranger Things Edition at the same time, celebrating the spirit of the 1980s. The edition pairs the portable music culture of the Walkman era with the world of Netflix's Stranger Things.
  • It copies Max's headphones. The special edition takes its style from Max's signature headphones in the series, with red and black ear pads, an engraved Stranger Things logo, and custom packaging. The Upside Down inspires the exclusive design details, which extend the experience beyond the headphones themselves.
  • It costs $20 more and it is pre-order only for now. The Stranger Things Edition is available for pre-order now at $79.99 in the US, at Sony, Netflix, and Amazon US. The standard model is already on shelves, while the limited edition is still taking orders.

Why wired again

  • Wired headphones are having a moment. Sony says the value of wired headphones is being rediscovered for their simplicity, ease of use, and freedom from charging or pairing. Younger listeners have driven the revival, wearing wired earbuds and headphones as a style choice as much as a practical one, and Sony is the first major brand to answer with a retro flagship rather than a budget model.
  • The timing is deliberate. The launch lands as the final season of Stranger Things brings 1980s nostalgia back to the top of streaming, and the Walkman is the most recognizable piece of 1980s tech Sony owns. Pairing the two gives the headphones a story that a spec sheet alone could never carry.
  • The price makes it an impulse buy. At $59.99, Sony is pricing the headphones as an everyday purchase rather than a collector's item, and buyers get the retro look with a warranty, a microphone, and a USB-C cable. The low price is part of the strategy: the headphones are cheap enough to buy on nostalgia alone, and useful enough to keep using after the novelty fades.

What to watch next

  • Whether the Stranger Things edition sells out. Limited edition tie-ins live or die on scarcity, and the pre-order window will show whether the $20 premium buys real demand or just curiosity. A quick sellout would invite more Sony and Netflix collaborations.
  • Whether other brands follow. If the MDR-3R sells, expect rival brands to dig through their own archives for wired classics, since every major headphone maker has a famous design from the cassette era. The wired revival has been a grassroots trend so far, and Sony just gave it a corporate endorsement.
  • Whether the sound holds up. The 23mm drivers match the original on paper, but modern listeners judge headphones against today's tuning, not 1979's. Early reviews will decide whether the MDR-3R is a daily listener or a shelf piece that looks better than it sounds.
