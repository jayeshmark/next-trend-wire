Shield AI Buys Radar Maker Echodyne for $5.3 Billion and Raises $2.5 Billion at a $28.8 Billion Valuation

By Jayesh Marathe · November 6, 2026 · 7 min read

Shield AI has agreed to acquire radar maker Echodyne in a transaction valued at $5.3 billion and is raising $2.5 billion to fund the deal, a package that values the defense technology company at $28.8 billion. Shield announced both moves on November 5, 2026, pairing its largest acquisition with its largest financing in a single day.

The acquisition

  • The deal. Shield has entered into a definitive agreement to acquire Echodyne Corp., an industry leading radar company based in Kirkland, Washington, in a transaction valued at $5.3 billion.
  • The payment. Of the $5.3 billion transaction value, $4.8 billion will be paid and issued at closing and up to an additional $500 million is contingent on Echodyne meeting agreed performance targets.
  • The closing. The acquisition is expected to close in early 2027, subject to receipt of applicable regulatory approvals and satisfaction of other customary closing conditions.
  • The strategy. The deal builds out Shield's Mission Systems business with software defined radars that enable counter unmanned aircraft systems, which detect, track, and defeat drones, and it gives Shield an in house sensor business to pair with its autonomy software and aircraft.
  • The chief executive's case. "We are building a new-generation defense technology company consisting of three fast-growing, high-margin, mutually supporting business units: autonomy software, autonomous systems, and mission systems," said Gary Steele, chief executive of Shield. "The acquisition of Echodyne dramatically accelerates our efforts to meet growing customer demand for multi-mission capabilities, provides increased access to a rapidly expanding counter-drone industry, and further diversifies our revenue streams with sustained, compelling growth for the combined company."

The $2.5 billion financing

  • The package. Shield is raising $2.5 billion to support the transaction, including $1 billion in Series H funding at a $28.8 billion post-money valuation, a $1 billion structured equity investment from funds managed by Blackstone, and $500 million in debt financing led by JPMorgan Chase Bank as sole arranger.
  • The Series H leads. The Series H round is co-led by Disruptive and Innovation X Capital, with participation from Advent, the Strategic Investment Group of JPMorganChase's Security and Resiliency Initiative, Snowpoint Ventures, Apandion, Center15 Capital, and others, and the proceeds will be used principally to fund the acquisition.
  • The scale. The $1 billion Series H alone ranks among the largest private defense technology rounds ever raised, and the combined $2.5 billion package is nearly half the value of the acquisition it funds.
  • The advisers. J.P. Morgan Securities served as exclusive financial adviser to Shield, Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen and Katz served as legal adviser, and Covington and Burling advised Shield on regulatory and business diligence, while Morgan Stanley and Robert W. Baird served as financial advisers to Echodyne and Fenwick and West served as its legal adviser. Sullivan and Cromwell served as legal adviser to Shield on the financing.

What Shield is buying

  • The technology. Echodyne designs and manufactures compact, solid-state radars using its patented Metamaterials Electronically Scanned Array technology, known as MESA, which uses standard materials and manufacturing processes to cut the cost of high performance radar.
  • The customers. Echodyne radars are built into hundreds of counter-drone and security systems used by defense, government, and commercial customers in more than 40 countries.
  • The military work. In September, Echodyne received a contract with a $250 million ceiling from Joint Interagency Task Force 401 to supply counter-drone radar to United States and allied forces, and its radars are integrated onto remote weapon stations, high-energy lasers, and high-power microwave systems, where they cue interceptors toward incoming drones.
  • The civilian work. Civilian operators use the same radars to protect stadiums and airports from unauthorized drones and to let police and fire departments fly drones safely in shared airspace.
  • The factory. Echodyne recently opened an 86,350 square foot manufacturing facility in Washington, a $40 million investment that will be able to produce about 30,000 radars per year.
  • The growth. Both companies expect triple-digit revenue growth this fiscal year, according to the announcement.

What happens after the deal closes

  • The structure. Following the closing, Echodyne will become part of Shield's Mission Systems business and will accelerate its existing strategy of supplying high performance radar systems to integrators and end customers across defense, government, and commercial sectors.
  • The team stays. Echodyne's team will remain in place, its MESA radars will remain available as standalone products, and chief executive Eben Frankenberg will report directly to Steele and remain responsible for Echodyne's products, manufacturing operations, and partner and customer engagement.
  • The Washington footprint. The deal extends Shield's presence in Washington, where Echodyne is based and where Shield named the state in September as the production home for X-BAT, its AI piloted fighter jet.
  • The combined company. Shield already builds Hivemind autonomy software, Aechelon simulation for training and testing, and the V-BAT and X-BAT autonomous systems, and the acquisition adds the sensors those systems need to see the battlefield, closing the loop between sensing and acting.
  • The Echodyne view. "Hundreds of customers rely on our radars to detect and track drones, protect people and infrastructure, secure borders, and ensure safe airspace operations," said Frankenberg. "Shield shares our view that autonomous systems can only act on what they can sense, and superior sensors lead to superior systems and outcomes. By joining Shield, we're creating a company with the size and breadth to deliver even more value to our broad range of defense, government, and commercial customers around the globe."
