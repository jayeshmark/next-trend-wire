Senator Chris Murphy Hospitalized After a Stroke, His Office Says He Is Awake and Alert

By Editorial Team · 7 min read

Connecticut Democratic Senator Chris Murphy was hospitalized after suffering a stroke on Saturday afternoon, his office said on Sunday, November 1, and he is now monitored at Johns Hopkins Hospital in Baltimore. His chief of staff said doctors are encouraged, he is awake and alert and responding well, and the office is optimistic he will make a full recovery.

What happened

  • The first sign. Murphy, 53, began feeling numbness in his arm on Saturday afternoon while attending his son's hockey game, according to a statement from his office issued on Sunday.
  • The hospital trip. Murphy was taken to a local hospital, where doctors determined he had experienced a stroke, and he was then transferred to Johns Hopkins for further monitoring.
  • The statement. His chief of staff, Allison Herwitt, issued the statement on Sunday, giving the first official account of what happened and of his condition.
  • The condition, in the office's words. Herwitt said: "The doctors are encouraged by his progress, he is awake and alert, and we are optimistic he will make a full recovery," adding that Murphy is responding well to treatment.

A campaign schedule interrupted

  • Not on Tuesday's ballot. Murphy is not on the ballot in this year's midterm elections, so his hospitalization does not change any race he is running in, but it removes an active campaigner from the final days of the campaign.
  • Weeks on the road. Murphy had spent recent weeks traveling to campaign for fellow Democrats, with stops in Michigan, Minnesota, and Florida, as the party fought to win seats in Tuesday's elections.
  • A New Hampshire trip planned. Murphy had been due to campaign in New Hampshire on Sunday alongside Representative Chris Pappas, the Democratic nominee for Senate there, an appearance that came as he remained in hospital under monitoring.
  • Campaigning pauses. His office's announcement on Sunday put his campaign travel on hold, with his treatment and monitoring at Johns Hopkins now taking priority over the final weekend of the campaign.

Who Murphy is

  • A Connecticut senator since 2013. Murphy, a Democrat, has represented Connecticut in the United States Senate since 2013, after serving Connecticut's 5th Congressional District in the House of Representatives.
  • A high profile Senate Democrat. Murphy is one of the more prominent Democrats in the Senate and a member of the party's leadership in the chamber, known for his work on foreign policy and gun safety.
  • A name in 2028 talk. Murphy has also been mentioned as a potential candidate for the Democratic presidential nomination in 2028, a conversation that makes his health a matter of national political interest as well as personal concern.
  • A colleague's reaction. Pennsylvania Democratic Senator John Fetterman, who suffered a stroke during his own 2022 campaign, posted on Sunday that he hoped Murphy would enjoy a full and speedy recovery, saying he understood all too well how much a stroke impacts a life.

What remains unknown

  • No details on severity. Murphy's office has not released specifics about the type of stroke, its severity, or how long he will remain at Johns Hopkins, and this report does not speculate on any of those questions.
  • No timeline for a return. The office has not said when Murphy might return to work in the Senate, and any timeline will depend on his doctors' advice as his monitoring continues.
  • A developing story. The office described a patient who is communicating and responding to treatment, but the story is still developing, and further updates from his office, rather than from outside speculation, will say more about his recovery.
  • What to watch next. The next official update from Murphy's office will matter most: whether he remains at Johns Hopkins, how his treatment progresses, and when his doctors clear him to leave hospital and resume his duties.
