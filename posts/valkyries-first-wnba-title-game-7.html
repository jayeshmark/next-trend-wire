Gabby Williams Scored 28 in the Second Half as the Valkyries Won Their First WNBA Title

By Jayesh Marathe · November 1, 2026 · 7 min read

Gabby Williams scored 30 points and the Golden State Valkyries beat the Atlanta Dream 68-63 in Game 7 on October 31, winning the franchise's first WNBA championship in only its second season. Williams scored 28 of her points after halftime and was named Finals MVP.

How Game 7 turned

  • The slow start. Williams scored only two points in the first half as Golden State trailed 30-24 at halftime, and Atlanta looked ready to complete a comeback series win after forcing the deciding game with an 80-69 victory in Game 6 at home.
  • The third quarter surge. Williams scored 15 points in the third quarter, including three three pointers, as Golden State outscored Atlanta 21-14 in the period and took a 45-44 lead into the fourth, turning a nine point deficit into a lead in front of a roaring home crowd.
  • The finishing run. The game was tied at 53 midway through the fourth quarter when Williams scored nine straight points for Golden State, including a deep three pointer that made it 60-57 with 2:18 left, and the Valkyries never trailed again.
  • The final minute. Kiah Stokes tipped in a Williams miss in the final minute to stretch the lead to seven, and Veronica Burton added free throws in the closing seconds as Golden State closed out a 68-63 win at Chase Center in San Francisco.
  • The crowd behind it. The win came before a sellout crowd of 18,064 at Chase Center, a building Valkyries fans call Ballhalla, where the team has sold out every game in franchise history.

A second half for the record book

  • The headline number. Williams finished with 30 points on 10 of 23 shooting, with four blocks, three rebounds and two assists, and her 28 points in the second half set a record for the most points in a half of a WNBA Finals game.
  • The company she joined. Williams joined Penny Taylor as the only players in WNBA history to score 30 or more points in a winner-take-all Finals game, and she did it after a first half in which she made only one of four shots.
  • The series behind it. Williams averaged 19.6 points, 3.6 rebounds and 2.7 assists across the seven game series, and her 137 total points set a record for a WNBA Finals series, passing the 114 points A'ja Wilson scored in the 2025 Finals.
  • The supporting cast. Tiffany Hayes scored 11 points and Veronica Burton added 10 points and five assists for Golden State, while Stokes grabbed a game high 13 rebounds and anchored a defense that held Atlanta star Angel Reese to eight points.
  • The Dream's night. Jordin Canada led Atlanta with 18 points and Allisha Gray added 16, but the Dream shot poorly all night against a physical Golden State defense and could not find a late answer once Williams took over.

Why this title is historic

  • The speed of it. The Valkyries won a championship in just their second season, making them the fastest expansion team in major North American sports to win a title, and the team's own announcement celebrated the Game 7 win as the franchise's first championship.
  • The first Game 7. The series was the first WNBA Finals to reach a seventh game since the league adopted a best of seven format in 2025, and Golden State won it after trailing in the series and losing Game 6 on the road.
  • The roster behind it. The Valkyries became the first team in league history to win a title without a player selected to the All-WNBA team, a roster built from the expansion draft, international signings and one key free agent addition in Williams.
  • The promise kept early. Owner Joe Lacob declared in October 2023, before the franchise had played a game, that the Valkyries would win a championship within five years, and they did it in two, after reaching the playoffs in their inaugural season and then beating Dallas in the first round and sweeping Las Vegas in the semifinals.
  • The personal breakthrough. The title was Williams' first in the WNBA after stints in Chicago and Seattle, and it followed painful near misses with France, including a one point loss to the United States in the gold medal game at the Paris Olympics in 2024. "I wasn't gonna lose on Halloween," Williams joked after winning Finals MVP.

What comes next

  • The celebration. Golden State will celebrate a championship that arrived far ahead of schedule, with a Finals MVP in Williams, a coach in Natalie Nakase who built a defensive identity in two seasons, and a home crowd that never stopped filling the building.
  • The standard set. The Valkyries now enter next season as defending champions with the league's attention on them, a very different position from the expansion team that began play in 2025 with no established star and modest outside expectations.
  • The Dream's wait. Atlanta, also chasing a first title, heads into the offseason after a fourth Finals defeat in franchise history, with a core that pushed the champions to seven games but could not survive Williams' second half.
