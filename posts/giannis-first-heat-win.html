Giannis Wins First Game as a Heat Player as Bam Passes Haslem

By Editorial Team · 5 min read

Giannis Antetokounmpo won his first game as a Miami Heat player on October 28, 2026, scoring 24 points with 18 rebounds as Miami beat the Atlanta Hawks 129-117 in Atlanta. The win ended Miami's 0-4 start, and Bam Adebayo passed Udonis Haslem to become the Heat's all-time leading rebounder.

What happened

  • The breakthrough. Miami beat Atlanta 129-117 at State Farm Arena, earning its first win of the season at the fifth attempt after an 0-4 start, and giving Giannis Antetokounmpo his first victory in a Heat uniform.
  • Giannis delivered. Antetokounmpo finished with 24 points, 18 rebounds and three assists on 11-of-18 shooting, picking up two fouls in the first four minutes and sitting for much of the first quarter before finishing with a season-high rebound total.
  • Bam made history. Adebayo scored 24 points with nine rebounds, three assists and three steals on 8-of-16 shooting, passing Udonis Haslem's franchise record of 5,791 career rebounds and ending the night with 5,794, taking the game ball in a postgame presentation.
  • The game flow. Miami shot 15 of 23 in the first quarter, 65 percent, to lead 34-25, took a 69-56 halftime lead after Nikola Jovic hit a half-court shot at the buzzer, and stretched the lead to 95-77 on a Tim Hardaway Jr. three-pointer late in the third quarter.
  • The Hawks rally. Atlanta cut the lead to 118-112 on a Dyson Daniels tip-in with 3:33 left, but Antetokounmpo answered with four consecutive points and Miami closed on an 11-5 run to secure the win.
  • The support. Andrew Wiggins scored 18 points, Pelle Larsson had 17 points, eight assists and six rebounds, Hardaway scored 14 off the bench, Bobby Portis Jr. had 11, and Davion Mitchell added 10 points and 10 assists, as Miami shot 53.1 percent from the field and outscored Atlanta 70-52 in the paint.
  • Atlanta's side. Rookie Kingston Flemings scored a season-high 23 points on 9-of-12 shooting, Nickeil Alexander-Walker added 23 with five three-pointers, Jalen Johnson had 12 points, 11 assists and nine rebounds, and CJ McCollum scored 13, as the Hawks fell to 2-3 with a third straight loss after a 2-0 start.

Why it matters

  • The pressure valve. An 0-4 start with a new superstar was becoming the story of Miami's season, and one win does not fix the record, but it stops the losing streak from defining the opening month.
  • The fit question. Antetokounmpo and Adebayo combined for 48 points and 27 rebounds, and Adebayo's early-season struggles beside his new frontcourt partner got a one-night answer.
  • A franchise milestone. Haslem held the rebounding record since November 2012 across a 20-season career, and Adebayo, who took over the captaincy when Haslem retired, passed him in his 10th season after arriving as the 14th pick in the 2017 draft.
  • Giannis is settling. Antetokounmpo's 138 points across his first five games are the most by any player in his first five games with the franchise, and his 1,000th career steal came in the same game, which suggests the production is translating even while the wins catch up.

Second order effects

  • Foul trouble as a feature. Miami won the first quarter largely without Antetokounmpo after his two early fouls, which says something about the bench and about how the offense moves when the ball is not going through one star.
  • Atlanta's interior problem. The Hawks were outrebounded 64-45 and gave up 70 points in the paint, with Onyeka Okongwu limited by foul trouble, which left Atlanta without an answer inside all night.
  • Spoelstra's steadiness. Head coach Erik Spoelstra praised his players for staying positive through the 0-4 start, and the win rewarded that approach before frustration could harden into something worse.
  • The Hawks' slide. Three straight losses after a 2-0 start is not a crisis in October, but it is a trend, and Atlanta's defense has now been exposed inside twice in the same week.

Third order effects

  • The ceiling question. If the Antetokounmpo-Adebayo frontcourt keeps producing like this, Miami's ceiling in the Eastern Conference rises quickly, but if it was one hot night, the 1-4 record is still the reality.
  • Adebayo's words. "I can't really put it into words," Adebayo said after the game, adding that he wished he could have set the record at home, which tells you how much the Haslem connection means to him.
  • Atlanta's bright spot. Flemings' 23-point night off the bench gives the Hawks something real from a loss, and rookie production is the kind of thing that matters more in October than one defeat.
  • The East is unforgiving. Miami sits at 1-4 while Philadelphia has already moved to 4-1, so the Heat have ground to make up even after breaking through.

What to watch next

  • Heat host Indiana. Miami hosts the Indiana Pacers on Friday, October 30, with a chance to make it two wins in a row.
  • Hawks host Cleveland. Atlanta hosts the Cleveland Cavaliers on Thursday, October 29, with tip pushed back an hour so Game 6 of the WNBA Finals can be played at State Farm Arena during the afternoon.
  • The foul watch. Whether Antetokounmpo avoids the early foul trouble that sidelined him in the first quarter against Atlanta.
  • The streak test. Whether Miami can build on its first win and whether Atlanta can stop its slide at three losses.
