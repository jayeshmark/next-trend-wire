House Blocks Hegseth Impeachment 211-204 as Massie Stands Alone

By Jayesh Marathe · November 10, 2026 · 7 min read

The House voted 211-204 on November 10 to send Rep. Thomas Massie's eight articles of impeachment against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth to the Judiciary Committee, blocking an immediate floor vote and handing Hegseth a reprieve with only weeks left in the current Congress.

The vote

  • The count. The House approved the referral motion 211-204 on Tuesday evening, sending Massie's resolution to the Republican led House Judiciary Committee instead of voting on the charges themselves.
  • The motion. Rep. Darrell Issa of California offered the referral motion shortly after 5:30 p.m. ET, and the House floor record shows lawmakers approved it at 6:02 p.m. ET.
  • The lone Republican. Massie was the only Republican to vote against referring his own resolution to committee, while 210 Republicans and one independent backed the referral.
  • The Democrats. 203 Democrats joined Massie in opposing the referral, and seven Republicans and 11 Democrats did not vote, according to the official roll call.
  • The effect. The referral kills any immediate vote on impeaching Hegseth, and the articles are now in the hands of a committee controlled by Trump allies.

What the articles charge

  • The filing. Massie, a Kentucky Republican, refiled the eight articles as a privileged resolution on November 9, forcing the House to act within two legislative days.
  • The Iran war. The first three articles charge Hegseth with waging war against Iran in violation of the War Powers Resolution, without a declaration of war, without authorization from Congress and without an imminent threat.
  • The boat strikes. A fifth article charges extrajudicial killings in lethal strikes on suspected drug trafficking vessels in the Caribbean Sea and the eastern Pacific since September 2, 2025, which the articles say killed more than 200 people.
  • The other charges. The remaining articles cover weakened civilian casualty protections, retaliation against Sen. Mark Kelly of Arizona for protected speech, the January operation that captured Venezuelan President Nicolas Maduro, and a bombing campaign in Yemen.
  • The history. Massie first filed the same eight articles on September 15, but Speaker Mike Johnson sent the House home for a seven week campaign recess the next day, and the resolution expired without a vote.

How Hegseth answered

  • The press conference. Hegseth held his first Pentagon press conference in six months on Tuesday morning, using it to attack the impeachment push rather than to discuss the war in Iran.
  • The words. Hegseth called the effort a "farce" and a "political spectacle" and compared himself with President Donald Trump, who was impeached twice by the House.
  • The president. Trump welcomed the vote on social media, writing that the attempt to impeach Hegseth had "miserably FAILED" and that every Republican except Massie had voted in favor of Hegseth.
  • The stakes. Hegseth also used the briefing to press Congress to pass the Pentagon's historic $1.5 trillion budget request.

Why the referral ends it for now

  • The calendar. Only a few weeks remain in the current Congress, and the Judiciary Committee is expected simply not to take up the articles before the two year session ends on January 3.
  • The expiry. The articles will formally expire when the current Congress ends, so Democrats would need to produce a new version if they want to launch their own impeachment effort in January.
  • The Senate bar. Even if the House had voted to impeach, the Senate would need 67 votes to convict and remove Hegseth, far more than the 54 to 56 seats Democrats are projected to hold in the next Senate.
  • The precedent. If Hegseth had been impeached, he would have become only the third cabinet secretary ever impeached by the House, after Secretary of War William Belknap in 1876 and Homeland Security Secretary Alejandro Mayorkas in 2024.

What comes next

  • The new Congress. Democrats take control of both chambers in January and are expected to revive the impeachment effort, with House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries in line to become speaker.
  • The lame duck. Massie, who lost his re-election primary to a Trump backed challenger, leaves Congress at the end of the year, so the lame duck session was his last chance to force the vote himself.
  • The war. The impeachment fight grew out of the war in Iran and the boat strikes, and both continue while the political battle over who authorized them moves to the next Congress.
