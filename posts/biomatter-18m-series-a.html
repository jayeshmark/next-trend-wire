Biomatter Raises $18M to Scale AI-Designed Enzymes

By Jayesh Marathe · November 12, 2026 · 7 min read

Biomatter, a Lithuanian company that designs custom enzymes with artificial intelligence, has closed an oversubscribed $18 million Series A financing. The money will fund a new design and testing facility in Vilnius and grow the company's commercial team and United States presence.

The round

  • The size. Biomatter announced the closing of an oversubscribed $18 million Series A financing on Thursday, November 12, 2026, in a company announcement.
  • The lead. The round was led by Astanor, with participation from existing investors UVC Partners, Inventure and Metaplanet.
  • The customers. Biomatter has delivered more than 50 custom enzymes to more than 25 enterprise customers spanning Europe, the United States and Japan, including AstraZeneca, BASF and Sanofi. Roughly half of its customers are based in the United States.
  • The proof in products. The company's work is already in products, including Thermo Fisher Scientific's Invitrogen SuperScript V Reverse Transcriptase, an enzyme used to convert RNA into DNA in molecular biology labs and mRNA research.
  • The science. Biomatter's founders published one of the first demonstrations that generative models can produce functional enzymes far from anything found in nature, in Nature Machine Intelligence in 2021. The company designs catalysts that solve real world problems across industries.

What the money funds

  • The facility. The financing will fund a new 2,000 square meter design and testing facility in Vilnius, opening in 2027, which will bring more of the build and test loop in house.
  • The speed. The new facility accelerates the loop between AI design and the lab, cutting cycles from weeks to days, so the company can test more enzyme designs faster.
  • The team. Proceeds will grow the commercial team and the company's United States presence, where about half its customers are based, and continue investment in the computational platform and the enzyme dataset behind it.

Why it matters

  • The AI biology wave. Enzyme design is one of the clearest commercial uses of generative AI in biology: a model proposes a protein, the lab builds and tests it, and the results train the next round. Biomatter's 50 delivered enzymes show the loop works beyond the lab.
  • The customer list. AstraZeneca, BASF and Sanofi are not pilot customers. They are large industrial buyers, and their presence on the customer list is the strongest signal that AI designed enzymes have moved from research into production.
  • The small round, big signal. $18 million is a modest Series A by AI standards, but it is oversubscribed, and it comes with a new facility and a United States expansion attached. The company is scaling the business, not just the science.
  • The Vilnius bet. Building a 2,000 square meter facility in Lithuania, rather than moving the lab to Boston or San Francisco, keeps the company's research base where its team is while the commercial push targets the United States.

What to watch next

  • The facility opening. The Vilnius site opens in 2027, and its first test will be whether bringing the build and test loop in house actually cuts design cycles from weeks to days.
  • The United States hires. The commercial expansion will show how fast Biomatter can convert its American customer base into a larger share of revenue.
  • The next round. An oversubscribed Series A with a new facility and a growing customer list sets up a larger Series B if the 2027 facility delivers on its speed claims.
