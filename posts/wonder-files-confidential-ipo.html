Wonder Files Confidentially for an IPO After Building a Food Empire on Delivery and Restaurants

By Editorial Team · 7 min read

Wonder Group has confidentially filed for an initial public offering, taking the first formal step toward the public markets after years of aggressive expansion. The food technology company announced on November 5, 2026 that it has submitted a draft registration statement on Form S-1 to the Securities and Exchange Commission for a proposed offering of its Class A common stock.

The filing

  • The move. Wonder confidentially submitted a draft Form S-1 to the SEC, a step that lets the company begin the regulator's review process privately, without publishing its financials, while it prepares for a possible public offering.
  • The unknowns. Wonder has not yet determined how many shares it plans to offer or the price range for the offering, and it has not disclosed a proceeds target, an exchange, a valuation, or a timetable.
  • The conditions. The proposed offering remains subject to market and other conditions and the completion of the SEC's review process, so the filing starts the process rather than guaranteeing an IPO.
  • The timing. Wonder had previously said it expects to be IPO ready by early next year, and the confidential filing puts that plan into motion on schedule.

What Wonder has built

  • The founder. Wonder was founded in 2018 by Marc Lore, the serial entrepreneur who founded Diapers.com and Jet.com and sold them to Amazon and Walmart, and who has made no secret of his plans to take Wonder public.
  • The platform. Wonder has grown from a food delivery concept into a vertically integrated food technology business spanning restaurants, delivery, meal kits, and automated kitchen technology, operating about 170 locations across the United States.
  • The acquisitions. The company owns delivery app Grubhub and meal kit company Blue Apron, and it has raised more than $3 billion in venture funding while spending at least $1 billion acquiring businesses and restaurant concepts.
  • The funding. In July, Wonder raised more than $650 million in a Series D round at a valuation of around $9 billion, with backing from investors including Accel, GV, AllianceBernstein, and ARK Invest.
  • The DoorDash ties. In September, DoorDash made a $125 million long-term investment that added to the Series D and agreed to acquire Grubhub Campus Dining, Wonder's campus dining business, for $300 million, in a transaction that remains unfinished as Wonder enters the IPO review process.

What comes next

  • The new bet. Starting November 17, Wonder plans to roll out Wonder Create, a program that lets influencers, entrepreneurs, and brands pay $10 a month to start their own restaurant business out of a Wonder location and earn a share of the revenue from orders.
  • The pitch. The program turns Wonder's kitchens into a platform for other people's restaurant ideas, a move that would add a new revenue stream on top of its own restaurant concepts and delivery business.
  • The scrutiny. As a public company, Wonder would face investor scrutiny of a business that has burned through billions in private capital to assemble its food empire, and the IPO will test whether the market believes the model can turn scale into profit.
  • The watch item. The next milestone is the SEC review itself, which will decide when Wonder can publish its financials and move toward a priced offering, and until then the company has said little beyond the filing.
