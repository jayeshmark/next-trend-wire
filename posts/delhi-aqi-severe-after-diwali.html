Delhi Air Crosses Into Severe Range the Morning After Diwali as Emergency Curbs Stay in Force

By Editorial Team · 7 min read

Delhi woke on Monday to thick smog and air quality in the severe range at monitoring stations across the capital, a day after Diwali firecrackers added to already foul air. The city's overall reading stayed in the "very poor" band while emergency restrictions imposed last week remained in force.

The morning after Diwali

  • The readings. Delhi's overall air quality index stood at about 366 to 370 on Monday morning, in the "very poor" band, while 15 of the city's 59 monitoring stations recorded readings above 400, the threshold for "severe," according to Central Pollution Control Board data.
  • The worst spots. Sonia Vihar recorded 449, while Burari and Vivek Vihar each recorded 441, and the station at ITO crossed into the severe range before dawn with a reading of 406.
  • The Diwali day. On Diwali itself, Sunday, the city's average reading settled at 376, also "very poor," after firecracker smoke joined vehicle exhaust and seasonal stubble burning in still autumn air.
  • The gap in the numbers. Private monitors showed far higher readings than the official figure, a difference that reflects different measurement standards rather than different air, and the official reading remains the basis for government action.
  • The forecast. The Air Quality Early Warning System forecast that Delhi's air would stay in the severe category from Monday for the next few days.

What Stage III restricts

  • The decision. The Commission for Air Quality Management invoked Stage III of the Graded Response Action Plan across Delhi-NCR on November 6, as a precaution before the air crossed into the severe range.
  • The vehicles. Older, more polluting four wheelers are barred: BS-III petrol and BS-IV diesel vehicles cannot operate in Delhi and designated NCR districts, including Gurugram, Faridabad, Ghaziabad, Gautam Buddha Nagar and Sonipat.
  • The construction halt. Non-essential dust generating construction and demolition work is banned across the region, with exemptions for essential infrastructure.
  • The schools. Primary schools must run hybrid classes for children up to Class 5, letting families choose between physical and online attendance.
  • The four stages. Under the revised GRAP schedule issued on September 29, Stage I applies when the index is 201 to 300, Stage II at 301 to 400, Stage III at 401 to 450, and Stage IV above 450, with measures growing stricter at each step.

The measures already in place

  • The October order. In an order dated October 30, the commission directed precautionary measures for November 5 to 20, noting that early to mid November has repeatedly brought severe air to the region in past years.
  • The construction curb. The order restricts dust generating demolition and outdoor construction across the entire NCR, except for projects such as railways, metro services, airports, hospitals and national security work.
  • The office rule. State government and private offices in Delhi and five NCR districts must operate with up to 50 percent of staff attending physically, with the rest working from home, except where the arrangement is not feasible for industrial establishments and essential services.
  • The firecracker limits. The Supreme Court allowed only certified green firecrackers in Delhi-NCR on November 7 and 8, between 6 am and 7 am and 8 pm and 10 pm, with the complete ban restored from November 9. Firecrackers were nevertheless used widely on Diwali night.

Why the air turned

  • The seasonal pattern. The commission's order notes a recurring pattern of elevated pollution in early to mid November, driven by local emissions, seasonal farm residue and biomass burning, and episodic events, while low wind speeds and a shallow atmospheric boundary layer limit how fast pollutants disperse.
  • The worst pre-Diwali air in years. Delhi's 24 hour average index hit 382 on November 5, its worst pre-festival air in eight years, with fine particulate matter peaking at about 280 micrograms per cubic metre in the run up to Diwali.
  • The festival push. Firecracker emissions on Diwali night added to the load, pushing stations that were already close to the severe threshold over it by Monday morning.
  • The scale. The official scale rates 0 to 50 as good, 51 to 100 satisfactory, 101 to 200 moderate, 201 to 300 poor, 301 to 400 very poor, and 401 to 500 severe.

What happens next

  • The review. Stage III stays in force until the commission reviews the readings and forecasts and decides otherwise, and Stage IV, the "severe plus" step, would follow if the index crosses 450 or forecasts point that way.
  • The wider reach. Stage IV would tighten truck entry and extend hybrid teaching to older school classes, among other emergency steps.
  • The watch. With the early warning system forecasting severe air for the coming days, the next decision point is whether the commission tightens the response or holds at Stage III.
