Jack Campbell Hospitalized Overnight After Neck Injury in Lions Win Over Vikings

By Jayesh Marathe · November 2, 2026 · 7 min read

Detroit Lions linebacker Jack Campbell was taken to a local hospital on Sunday, November 1, after suffering a neck injury during the Lions 19-15 win over the Minnesota Vikings, and the team said he would remain in the hospital overnight for continued evaluation and monitoring.

What happened

  • The play. Campbell was hurt while making a tackle on the first play of the fourth quarter, the Lions said, and game reports identified the player he was tackling as Vikings tight end T.J. Hockenson.
  • The scene on the field. Medical personnel attended to Campbell on the field, teammates gathered around him as he was placed on a cart to leave the field, and the game paused while he received treatment.
  • The hospital trip. Campbell was taken to a local hospital to be evaluated for a neck injury, the Lions said.
  • The result. Detroit held on to win 19-15 at Ford Field, a victory that came at a heavy cost after the sight of its defensive leader leaving on a cart.
  • Three tackles before the injury. Campbell had recorded three tackles in the game before he was hurt, and his absence left Detroit to finish a division game without one of its central defenders.

The team's update

  • Resting overnight. A couple of hours after the game, the team said Campbell was resting and would remain in a local hospital overnight for continued evaluation and monitoring.
  • The coach's words. Lions head coach Dan Campbell said immediately after the game: "Thoughts and prayers are with Jack and his family. He's being taken care of. We don't have any new information. We'll keep you updated as this thing goes."
  • Breathing and alert. Dan Campbell told the Lions Radio Network after the game that Jack Campbell was breathing and alert when he left the stadium, but that he had no further information at that point.
  • A teammate's reaction. Lions quarterback Jared Goff called the moment scary, said it brought home that Campbell is a husband and a father, and said the team was praying for him and hoping he would be all right.
  • No diagnosis announced. The Lions have not announced a diagnosis or a timetable for Campbell's return in their latest update, and this report does not speculate on either question.

Who Campbell is

  • A first team All Pro. Campbell, 26, was a first team All Pro last season and has grown into one of the leaders of the Detroit defense in his fourth season with the team.
  • A central defender. Campbell entered the game as a player Detroit builds its defense around, which is why his injury drew an immediate reaction from teammates on both sidelines and from fans watching at Ford Field.
  • A division win without him. Detroit finished the game and secured the win after Campbell left, but the team now waits on medical news about a player it can ill afford to lose for long.

What remains unknown

  • The severity. The Lions have not said how severe the neck injury is, and no medical detail beyond the hospital evaluation has been released.
  • How long he will be out. No return date has been given, and any timeline will depend on what doctors find as his evaluation and monitoring continue.
  • The next update. The team said it will provide further updates as it has them, so the next confirmed word on Campbell's condition should come from the Lions rather than from outside speculation.
