Onodrim Raises $300 Million at a $1.3 Billion Valuation to Buy and Modernize European Defense Suppliers

By Editorial Team · 7 min read

Onodrim Industries announced on November 9 that it has raised a $300 million Series A led by Lux Capital, valuing the Amsterdam company at $1.3 billion. The European defense and industrial platform will use the money to buy established manufacturers and modernize them with technology.

The round

  • The headline numbers. Onodrim announced a $300 million Series A led by Lux Capital, with General Catalyst, Lakestar, Altimeter, Strike Capital, 137 Ventures, 8090, Expeditions, and more taking part, valuing the company at $1.3 billion.
  • The running total. The financing follows a $45 million seed round announced earlier this year, bringing Onodrim's disclosed funding to $345 million.
  • The model. Onodrim takes controlling stakes with permanent capital and applies technology, operational, and business development expertise to accelerate the growth of the companies it buys.
  • The focus. The company targets dual-use businesses in critical industries across supply chains, services, and products that solve major bottlenecks for the European defense industry.

A buyer, not a builder

  • The pitch. Onodrim buys established aerospace, defense, and industrial manufacturing businesses instead of starting new ones, betting that Europe's next defense gains come from upgrading suppliers it already has.
  • The gap it sees. "Europe has no shortage of exceptional companies," co-founder and chief executive Aistis Šimaitis said. "The challenge has been access to the resources needed to help them grow at the scale and speed the moment demands."
  • The first deals. Onodrim expects to announce its first acquisitions in the coming months, and it is opening offices in Munich and Turin alongside its existing offices in Amsterdam, London, and Vilnius.
  • The mission. "We are bringing permanent capital, technology and world-class operators to Europe's best companies, giving them the resources to expand, compete and strengthen the continent's defence industrial base," Šimaitis said.

The team

  • The chief executive. Šimaitis is a former adviser to the Lithuanian Government and a technology leader at Palantir Technologies, and he holds a doctorate from the University of Oxford.
  • The technology chief. Co-founder and chief technology officer Alexander Blessing was a Forward Deployed Engineer at Palantir Technologies, with degrees from Stanford University and the University of Cambridge.
  • The third co-founder. Christian Garrett, a partner at 137 Ventures, is also a co-founder of the company.
  • The investor case. "Europe needs a stronger industrial foundation to meet the security challenges ahead," Brandon Reeves, a partner at Lux Capital, said. "We see an enormous opportunity to bring a new model of ownership and investment to the continent's most important businesses."

What comes next

  • The test. Onodrim has announced no acquisitions yet, and the first deals will show whether established manufacturers can grow faster under Onodrim's ownership.
  • The welcome mat. "We welcome approaches from owners of defence, aerospace, manufacturing and industrial businesses who share this ambition," Blessing said, alongside a call for engineering and operational talent.
  • The wider bet. "Every country needs to be in control of its own destiny, and that starts with what it can build," Jeannette zu Fürstenberg, president and managing director at General Catalyst, said. "Europe's established manufacturers already have the expertise, customers, and trust. What they need is technology that helps them evolve and take on larger programmes."
