Agility Robotics Starts Trading on Nasdaq as the First Pure-Play Humanoid Robot Stock

By Jayesh Marathe · November 5, 2026 · 7 min read

Agility Robotics said on November 4 that it had completed its merger with Churchill Capital Corp XI, and its shares begin trading on the Nasdaq today, November 5, under the ticker AGLT. The listing makes Agility the first US-listed company devoted entirely to humanoid robots with machines already working for paying customers.

The deal

  • The close. Agility said on November 4 that the business combination had closed, and the combined company will operate as Agility Robotics, Inc. Churchill XI's common stock, warrants and units stop trading under their previous ticker symbols, and the combined company's common stock and warrants are expected to begin trading on Nasdaq under the ticker symbols AGLT and AGLTW.
  • The money. The gross capital raise in connection with the merger and related financings is expected to total approximately $465 million, before transaction expenses. The total breaks down into about $164 million from Churchill XI's trust account, about $201 million from a common stock PIPE committed at $10 per share and led by Foxconn, and $100 million of equity funding already received after the business combination was announced.
  • The vote. Churchill XI shareholders approved the business combination at a special meeting on November 2, with more than 84 percent of votes cast in favor. The vote cleared the last major hurdle before the November 4 closing.
  • The structure. The combination also moved Churchill XI's incorporation from the Cayman Islands to Delaware, and the continuing company was renamed Agility Robotics, Inc. All existing Agility shareholders are rolling 100 percent of their equity into the combined company, and their shares are subject to a 180-day lock-up after closing.

The robot

  • Digit at work. Agility's humanoid robot, Digit, is a general-purpose machine built to work alongside people in warehouses and factories, and it is already commercially deployed. The company says Digit has logged more than 65,000 hours of real-world operation, which is the experience Agility points to when it calls itself a pure-play humanoid robotics company rather than a research project.
  • Digit 5. Digit 5, unveiled on September 15, 2026, is designed to work safely alongside people and support a broader range of industrial workflows. Agility is on track to begin deliveries to early-access customers in the first half of 2027, with general availability targeted for the end of 2027.
  • The order book. Agility has disclosed more than $300 million in committed multi-year orders for Digit 5. That figure represents potential multi-year value expected to be realized over time, subject to contractual milestones and product specifications, and it does not represent current-period revenue.
  • The customers. Recent commercial momentum includes an expansion of Agility's relationship with GXO to deploy Digit at two additional facilities, a commercial agreement with AGCO for a manufacturing pilot expected to begin in 2027, and three additional manufacturing customers participating in paid programs. The active pipeline holds more than 30 prospective customers across manufacturing, automotive, aerospace, defense, logistics and consumer products.
  • The backers. Agility counts NVIDIA, Amazon, SoftBank Vision Fund 2, Schaeffler, Foxconn, Abico, DCVC and Playground Global among its strategic investors and partners. Foxconn's role goes beyond the PIPE: the electronics manufacturer led the $201 million private placement and is one of the industrial partners Agility needs to scale production.

Why it matters

  • A first. Agility expects to become the only US publicly listed pure-play humanoid robotics company with proven, active commercial deployments. Rivals in humanoid robotics are either private or part of much larger companies, so AGLT gives public investors their first direct way to bet on humanoid robots doing real work.
  • The test. The listing answers the question of whether a humanoid robotics company can reach the public markets, and the answer is yes. The harder test starts now: turning a pipeline of pilots and paid programs into repeat orders, and turning Digit 5 deliveries in 2027 into the revenue that justifies a public valuation.
  • The funding. Agility says it enters the public markets well capitalized, with the resources to expand commercially and support growing customer demand. The $465 million raise is meant to fund that expansion, and how fast the company spends it will show whether the public debut was a starting line or a peak.
