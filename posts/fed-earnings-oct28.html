The Fed Held Rates While Alphabet and Microsoft Paid Heavily for AI Growth

By Editorial Team · 7 min read

The Federal Reserve held its benchmark rate at 3.75 to 4 percent on October 28, 2026, while Alphabet and Microsoft reported strong operating growth in the same quarter alongside heavy spending on artificial intelligence infrastructure, and Amazon still to report.

What happened

  • A hold, with two dissents. The Federal Reserve maintained the target range for the federal funds rate at 3.75 to 4 percent, by a 10 to 2 vote, while Lisa D. Cook and Beth M. Hammack preferred to raise the range by a quarter percentage point at this meeting.
  • Strong activity, elevated inflation. The Federal Reserve described activity as expanding at a strong pace, with resilient spending, strong productivity, robust investment, steady jobs, and little change in unemployment. Inflation remains elevated, and more information will help determine the stance for a timely return to 2 percent.
  • Alphabet grew fast and spent faster. Alphabet reported third quarter revenue of $127.6 billion, up 25 percent, for its 13th consecutive quarter of double digit growth, while operating income rose 30 percent to $40.6 billion. Operating cash flow was $42.3 billion, but purchases of property and equipment reached $51.4 billion, leaving free cash flow at negative $9.1 billion.
  • Cloud doubled while profit fell. Google Cloud revenue rose 103 percent to $30.7 billion, with Cloud operating income up to $11.1 billion from $3.6 billion, while Google Services rose 11 percent to $96.3 billion, including Search at $63.4 billion and YouTube ads at $11.2 billion. Net income to common stockholders fell to $26.2 billion, or $2.12 per share, from $34.979 billion, or $2.87 per share.
  • An investment swing explains the gap. Alphabet reported other income and expense as a net expense of $8.2 billion, driven by an $8.7 billion net loss on equity securities, against a $10.7 billion gain a year earlier. That loss cut net income by $6.7 billion and earnings per share by $0.55.
  • Microsoft cleared its own bar. Microsoft reported fiscal first quarter revenue of $92.3 billion, up 19 percent, with operating income up 21 percent to $45.9 billion and GAAP net income up 32 percent to $36.6 billion, or $4.92 per share. Chief Financial Officer Amy Hood said results exceeded expectations, while Cloud rose 30 percent to $63.9 billion and Azure rose 48 percent to $33.2 billion.
  • Amazon is still to come. Amazon had not released third quarter results at the time of writing, with its report scheduled for after the market close on October 29, so this post contains no Amazon numbers.

Why it matters

  • The Fed kept both directions open. The Federal Reserve promised no next move, tying its judgment to more information while repeating that it will deliver price stability. Strong activity and elevated inflation argue against easing, while two dissents for a hike show patience itself is contested.
  • AI demand is no longer the question. Alphabet Cloud revenue more than doubled, Microsoft Azure grew 48 percent, and Microsoft commercial remaining performance obligation rose 73 percent to $677 billion. The debate shifts from whether anyone wants AI infrastructure to whether builders can earn enough, fast enough, to pay for it.
  • The cost is now visible in cash. Alphabet spent $51.4 billion on property and equipment against $42.3 billion of operating cash flow, while Microsoft added $39.4 billion against $58.0 billion of operating cash flow. Those lines turn an abstract AI race into a funding question.

Second order effects

  • Building stays urgent while money stays pricey. A Federal Reserve hold at 3.75 to 4 percent leaves the price of money unchanged, while Alphabet and Microsoft keep adding capacity that takes quarters to build and years to pay back. That rewards funding from operating cash flow and pressures companies that must borrow to keep up.
  • Cloud becomes the scoreboard. Google Cloud operating income more than tripled to $11.1 billion, and Microsoft Cloud reached $63.9 billion in quarterly revenue, so cloud is no longer a side business. It is where AI spending either turns into recurring revenue or piles up as capacity waiting for customers.
  • Headline profit gets harder to read. Alphabet operating income rose 30 percent while profit to common stockholders fell, because an equity securities swing overwhelmed the operating gain, while Microsoft reported GAAP net income up 32 percent but non-GAAP net income up 18 percent excluding its OpenAI impact.

Third order effects

  • Capacity becomes a bet on other people's business. Data centers, chips, and power cannot be unbuilt quickly, so Alphabet spending $51.4 billion in a quarter and Microsoft spending $39.4 billion lock in a view that customers will keep buying AI capacity for years. If demand slows, the largest builders face the largest fixed bill.
  • Pricing power moves to infrastructure owners. When Google Cloud more than doubles in a year and Microsoft reports $677 billion in commercial remaining performance obligation, sellers of capacity gain leverage over startups and enterprises that depend on it. That can appear as higher prices or tighter terms.
  • Talent and suppliers follow the spending. A build of this size pulls engineers, chip supply, and power contracts toward the largest spenders, while Alphabet points to a half trillion dollar revenue run rate and Microsoft points to accelerating Cloud growth.

What to watch next

  • The next Fed statement, not just the next rate. Watch whether the Federal Reserve still calls inflation elevated and still says more information will guide its stance, or whether that language softens or hardens. The October statement gave no commitment, so a wording change will carry more information than any single rate forecast.
  • Amazon after the close on October 29. Watch for the Amazon release when it arrives, and apply the same cash test to its cloud and retail businesses. Until then, no conclusion about all three hyperscalers can be drawn from two reports alone.
  • Whether cloud profit keeps pace with spending. Watch Alphabet Cloud operating income against its property and equipment purchases, and Microsoft Azure growth against its $39.4 billion of property and equipment additions. If profit grows faster than spending, the build starts to pay for itself.
