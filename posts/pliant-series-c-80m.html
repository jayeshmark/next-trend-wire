Pliant Raises $80 Million to Open Its B2B Payments Platform to AI Agents

By Jayesh Marathe · November 5, 2026 · 7 min read

Pliant, a Berlin fintech that builds payment infrastructure for businesses, has raised $80 million in a Series C round led by Seaya Nova and Speedinvest, the company announced on November 4, 2026. The round follows three straight years in which Pliant doubled its business, and it comes shortly after the company passed $100 million in annual recurring revenue.

The round

  • The raise. Pliant announced an $80 million Series C funding round led by Seaya Nova and Speedinvest, with participation from Illuminate Financial, Motive Partners, and Molten Ventures. The company described the round as oversubscribed.
  • The timing. The funding follows three consecutive years in which Pliant doubled its business, and it comes shortly after the company surpassed $100 million in annual recurring revenue. The raise turns a fast growing payments business into a bet on the next shift in how companies pay.
  • The backers. Seaya Nova and Speedinvest lead the round together. Seaya Nova is backed by the European Investment Fund through the European Tech Champions Initiative, alongside other institutional investors.
  • The company behind it. Pliant employs more than 300 people across eight offices and processes billions of dollars in annual payment volume for businesses and partners across Europe and North America. Its customers span travel, insurance, and AI native companies, as well as banks, fintechs, and software platforms.

The AI agents bet

  • The pitch. Pliant is opening its commercial payments infrastructure to AI agents, so software can move money on a company's behalf instead of waiting for a person to approve every payment. The new capital goes toward deepening that capability.
  • How agents plug in. Third party agents, from assistants like Claude and ChatGPT to a company's own automations, connect to Pliant through the Model Context Protocol and embed payment workflows into business processes. The platform's own AI agents already help customers automate recurring finance work at scale.
  • The guardrails. Pliant's platform gives AI agents access to regulated payment infrastructure with permissions, controls, and auditability built in. That matters because an agent that can spend needs limits, a paper trail, and a licensed party behind it.
  • The founder's case. "We built Pliant as an API first, modular B2B payments platform from day one, and we have adopted AI deeply, in our product and in how we run the company," said Malte Rau, CEO and co-founder of Pliant. "Whether an established business wants AI to automate its payment processes with the right guardrails, or an AI native company needs a licensed partner that moves as fast as it does, Pliant is built for that."

What the money funds

  • North America first. The investment will support Pliant's continued expansion in the United States and Canada, and prepare its entry into further international markets. The Berlin company is pushing into the market where agentic payments will be decided.
  • The AI roadmap. The capital will accelerate Pliant's AI roadmap as businesses connect AI agents directly to their financial infrastructure. The work covers agentic payments and workflows, not a side product.
  • The licensed layer. Pliant is a principal member of Visa and Mastercard, and it issues commercial cards in 13 currencies across 32 countries under its European e-money license and through regulated partners in the UK and US. That license is the moat: agents can only move money through a party regulators already trust.

Why investors say infrastructure wins

  • The Seaya view. "Every wave of software has made the application layer cheaper and the infrastructure underneath more valuable, and AI is no exception," said Vinoth Jayakumar, General Partner at Seaya Nova. "When agents move money, someone still has to be licensed, auditable and liable, and that is the layer Pliant owns."
  • The full stack in one place. Jayakumar pointed to principal member card issuing, payment accounts, and foreign exchange built into a single modular platform that businesses, banks, and now AI agents can build on, and said Seaya backs infrastructure companies with network effects in regulated markets.
  • The platform beneath the product. Pliant combines commercial card issuing, payment accounts, foreign exchange, and spend management for businesses and AI agents in one modular system. Fintechs, software platforms, and banks use the same infrastructure to build and run their own commercial payment offerings.
  • The founding story. Founded in 2020 and headquartered in Berlin, Pliant serves businesses and partners across Europe and North America. Six years in, the company has grown from a startup fixing business payments into a licensed platform that wants to be the rails AI agents pay on.

What to watch next

  • The North America test. The round's real test is whether a Berlin built platform can win the default position in US and Canadian business payments before card networks and domestic spend platforms set it themselves.
  • The agent economy. If AI agents start paying suppliers, booking travel, and settling invoices on their own, the companies that own the licensed rails collect the toll. Pliant just raised $80 million to be one of them.
  • The revenue bar. Passing $100 million in annual recurring revenue before this round means Pliant is no longer selling a promise. The next numbers to watch are how fast the agent driven volume grows, and whether the North America push turns into the company's largest market.
