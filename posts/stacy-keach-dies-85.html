Stacy Keach, TV's Mike Hammer, Dies at 85 After Heart Battle

By Jayesh Marathe · November 11, 2026 · 7 min read

Stacy Keach, the actor who played private detective Mike Hammer on television and built one of the most respected stage careers in America, has died at 85. He died Tuesday at his family home in Magdalenka, Poland, after what his wife called a long and difficult battle with a serious heart condition and heart failure.

What happened

  • The announcement. Keach died Tuesday, November 10, at his family home in Magdalenka, Poland, his wife, Malgosia Tomassi Keach, said in a statement. He was 85.
  • The cause. The statement said he died after a long and difficult battle with a serious heart condition and heart failure, and that he departed peacefully.
  • The confirmation. His wife shared the statement with Variety and with People, and the news was confirmed by The Hollywood Reporter, The Guardian, and Reuters within hours.
  • A life split between two countries. Keach, born in Savannah, Georgia, had lived in Poland with his wife, a Polish-born actress, and held Polish citizenship alongside his American citizenship since 2015.
  • His manager's farewell. His manager of 30 years said he had worked with Keach for three decades and would miss him very much.

The Mike Hammer years

  • The role that defined him on television. Keach played Mickey Spillane's hard-drinking private detective Mike Hammer in the CBS series "Mickey Spillane's Mike Hammer" from 1984 to 1987, then returned to the part in "The New Mike Hammer" and in the syndicated "Mike Hammer, Private Eye" in 1997 and 1998.
  • The films around it. His best-known screen work included the boxing drama "Fat City" in 1972, directed by John Huston, "The New Centurions" the same year, "The Long Riders" in 1980, in which he played Frank James opposite his brother James as Jesse, and "The Ninth Configuration" in 1980.
  • The later screen roles. Younger audiences knew him as the voice of Skipper Riley in "Planes," as a white supremacist in "American History X," as a warden in "Prison Break," and as Archbishop Kevin Kearns on "Blue Bloods" from 2016 to 2024. He also narrated the CNBC documentary series "American Greed" from 2007 to 2023.
  • The last role. His final screen performance came in the 2025 Netflix film "Jay Kelly," starring George Clooney, a quiet coda to a career that ran from 1962 to 2026.

A stage actor first

  • The classical foundation. Before television made him famous, Keach built his reputation as one of America's finest classical stage actors, with a Master of Fine Arts from the Yale School of Drama and a Fulbright scholarship at the London Academy of Music and Dramatic Art.
  • The awards. He won four Drama Desk Awards and was nominated for a Tony Award for Best Actor in a Play for playing Buffalo Bill in Arthur Kopit's "Indians" in 1970.
  • The Hamlet. His Hamlet for the New York Shakespeare Festival in 1972 drew praise from New York Times critic Clive Barnes, who called it one of the great Hamlets of the time and Keach the finest American classical actor since John Barrymore.
  • The range. His stage work ran from "MacBird!" and "Long Day's Journey into Night" to "King Lear" and "Richard III," and he was inducted into the Theater Hall of Fame in 2015.
  • The Hemingway turn. He played Ernest Hemingway in the 1988 miniseries "Hemingway," winning a Golden Globe Award and earning an Emmy nomination, and he returned to Hemingway on stage in the one-man play "Pamplona" at Chicago's Goodman Theatre.
  • The heart attack on stage. On the opening night of "Pamplona" in May 2017, Keach suffered a mild heart attack on stage, had bypass surgery the next day, and saw the run canceled so he could recover. He returned to the role a year later, in July 2018.

The man behind the roles

  • Savannah beginnings. Walter Stacy Keach Jr. was born June 2, 1941, in Savannah, Georgia, the son of theatre director Stacy Keach Sr., and grew up in Southern California.
  • Born with a cleft lip. He was born with a cleft lip and a partial cleft palate, underwent numerous operations as a child, and wore his trademark mustache in part to hide the scars. He served as honorary chairman of the Cleft Palate Foundation and advocated for insurance coverage for corrective surgeries.
  • The honors. He received a star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame in 2019, capping a career that earned him a Golden Globe, four Drama Desk Awards, and two Helen Hayes Awards.
  • The family. He was married four times and is survived by his wife, Malgosia Tomassi, whom he married in 1986 and who appeared with him in several of the Hammer productions.

Tributes

  • Jamie Lee Curtis. His "Road Games" co-star from the 1981 Australian thriller mourned him on Instagram, calling him a talented actor and a lovely man and saying it was another big loss in a hard year.

Why it matters

  • Two careers in one life. Few actors ever anchor both a classical stage career and a network television franchise. Keach did both, playing Hamlet and Buffalo Bill with the same conviction he brought to a private eye, and he carried the same seriousness into every medium.
  • The bridge between stage and screen. He came to television as a Shakespearean, and his performances argued, without ever making the argument out loud, that a detective series and a boxing film deserved the same craft as a king.
  • A working actor for six decades. His career ran from his film debut in "The Heart Is a Lonely Hunter" in 1968 to "Jay Kelly" in 2025, a span of 57 years in which he rarely stopped working, on stage, on screen, or behind a microphone.

What to watch next

  • The memorial plans. No public memorial details had been announced by Wednesday morning, and his family had not said whether services would be held in Poland or in the United States.
  • The screen legacy. "Jay Kelly" remains his final performance, and the Hammer series, "Fat City," and his stage recordings stand as the record of a career that crossed every corner of American acting.
