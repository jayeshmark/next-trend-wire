Baby Found Alive in Buried Dog Crate After 24 Hours Underground

By Jayesh Marathe · November 11, 2026 · 7 min read

An 11-month-old boy was found alive in South Carolina on Tuesday after spending about 24 hours buried in a dog crate in the woods. Koah Sharpe was wrapped in a towel inside the crate, buried in a shallow hole, and deputies say the cold weather overnight probably slowed his breathing and gave them more time to find him.

What happened

  • The discovery. Koah Sharpe was found at about 1:45 p.m. on Tuesday, November 10, in the 300 block of Parthenon Road in Lexington County, South Carolina. A search party spotted freshly disturbed dirt in the woods about 50 yards from his grandfather's house, and dug up a 2-by-2-foot dog crate wrapped in a towel, buried about a foot under the surface.
  • The condition. The infant was alive when deputies pulled him from the ground. He was taken to a hospital, where he remained in serious condition on Tuesday afternoon. Lexington County Sheriff Jay Koon said the boy was clinging to life but that he was hopeful the baby would make a full recovery.
  • The time underground. Sheriff Koon said deputies believe the baby was buried for about 24 hours. He said the cold weather overnight likely helped slow the baby's breathing, giving authorities more time to find him. "By the grace of God, the crate probably provided enough air for him to survive our estimation of the last 24 hours under that dirt," Koon told reporters on Tuesday.
  • The search. Koah's relatives filed a missing person report on Monday afternoon after his father took him. Deputies and other authorities searched on Tuesday for the missing infant, and the search party found him hours later.

The father

  • The arrest. The infant's father, Michael Comer, 25, was arrested on Monday night on charges of burglary and resisting arrest, while Koah was still missing. He remained behind bars on Tuesday afternoon.
  • The taking. The Lexington County Sheriff's Department said Comer took the boy on the afternoon of Monday, November 9. Relatives filed the missing person report later that day.
  • The charges ahead. Additional charges related to Koah's disappearance were still to be determined on Tuesday afternoon, the sheriff said. No Amber Alert was issued, Koon said, because Comer does not have access to a car.

Why it matters

  • A rescue against the odds. A baby buried a foot underground for a day, in a closed crate, in the cold, is not supposed to survive. Deputies credit the towel, the crate and the cold weather with keeping him alive long enough to be found.
  • The search that worked. The case turned on a search party noticing freshly disturbed dirt in the woods near the family home. The find came hours into the search, and deputies say the timing mattered: every hour underground made survival less likely.
  • The questions now. The sheriff's department has not said what led Comer to take the boy or to bury him, and the investigation into the disappearance is still open. The boy's recovery is the first priority, and the charges against his father will follow.

What to watch next

  • The hospital. Koah remains in the hospital, and his condition in the coming days will tell whether the sheriff's hope of a full recovery holds.
  • The charges. The sheriff's department said additional charges related to Koah's disappearance were to be determined, and Comer remained in custody on Tuesday afternoon.
  • The investigation. Deputies have not released further detail about what happened between the time Comer took the boy on Monday afternoon and the time he was arrested that night.
