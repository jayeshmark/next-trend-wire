Primordial Labs Raises $18M to Let Soldiers Command Machines by Voice

By Jayesh Marathe · November 10, 2026 · 7 min read

Primordial Labs announced on November 10 that it has raised $18 million in Series A funding to scale Anura, its natural language command and control platform for military systems. Ballistic Ventures led the round, with Squadra Ventures and Lockheed Martin Ventures taking part, and the company says it is already delivering licenses to the Army and special operations units.

The round

  • The headline numbers. Primordial Labs announced an $18 million Series A led by Ballistic Ventures, with participation from existing investors Squadra Ventures and Lockheed Martin Ventures.
  • The money's job. The funding will accelerate deployment of Anura across Department of War programs and advance the company's mission to enable human-machine teaming.
  • Licenses already shipping. "We're delivering licenses to the Army and SOCOM today, and this round lets us meet the demand we're seeing across domains, from ground vehicle crew stations to the cockpit to the dismounted soldier," co-founder and chief executive Lee Ritholtz said.
  • What the money buys. The company will expand its engineering, product, and growth teams, deliver Anura across Department of War programs already under contract, and extend it into new platforms, domains, and allied markets.

What Anura does

  • The problem. Each sensor, radio, and robotic system added to a platform brings its own interface and demands on the operator.
  • The virtual teammate. Anura serves as a virtual teammate, allowing crews to direct onboard sensors, communications, displays, and connected uncrewed systems just as they would a human.
  • How it works. Operators task systems with natural language goals and constraints, and Anura decomposes that intent into tasks, orchestrates them, carries them out, confirms the outcome, and adjusts when conditions change.
  • The shared picture. Anura brings an understanding of the real-time operating environment to machines, so crews and their systems work from the same picture of the world.

Where it runs

  • More than a dozen platforms. Anura is integrated on more than a dozen crewed and uncrewed platforms across the land and air domains.
  • The Abrams deal. Primordial Labs announced a master teaming agreement with General Dynamics Land Systems on Oct. 7 to bring Anura to ground combat vehicles across the GDLS portfolio, including the Abrams tank.
  • Small drones. The company is delivering licenses for small uncrewed aircraft systems to multiple Army and special operations units and sells them through the Army's UAS Marketplace.
  • Helicopters and MQ-9. Two 2026 Small Business Innovation Research Phase III awards extend that work to Army rotary-wing aircrews and the Air Force's MQ-9 ground control station.

The founders and the bet

  • Skunk Works roots. Ritholtz and co-founder and CTO Adrian Pope met at Lockheed Martin's Skunk Works, where they built and deployed small uncrewed aircraft systems and payloads, and went on to help found the Lockheed Martin Artificial Intelligence Center.
  • The investor's case. "The military doesn't need another proprietary interface for every new system. It needs technology that makes existing and future capabilities work together," said General Timothy Haugh, USAF (Ret.), Strategic Advisor to Ballistic Ventures.
  • Why Ballistic backed it. "At Ballistic, it always starts with the team, and Lee and Adrian are exceptional technical founders who have spent years solving the hardest problems in autonomous systems," said Roger Thornton, co-founder and general partner at Ballistic Ventures.
  • Beyond the battlefield. The same interface burden is emerging wherever humans work alongside intelligent machines, from mining and energy to logistics and public safety.
