Nvidia CEO honey-trapped by alleged Chinese spy

By Editorial Team · 7 min read

Nvidia's board opened an independent investigation on Sunday into reports that founder and CEO Jensen Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence effort, hours after Huang told employees he had been unfaithful to his wife. The board said it has no reason to believe national security or company secrets were compromised.

The board's investigation

  • The decision. Nvidia's board said on November 8 it is initiating an independent investigation in light of recent reporting that founder and CEO Jensen Huang has been the target of a foreign intelligence effort.
  • Who runs it. A committee of independent directors, assisted by outside independent counsel, will oversee the investigation, the board said.
  • The board's position. The board said it has no reason to believe that either national security or Nvidia secrets were compromised, while adding that its inquiry will examine all available information.
  • Confidence and cooperation. The board said Huang fully supports the review and has committed to cooperating with it, and that it has confidence in Huang and his leadership team, with further updates to come as appropriate. The statement names no committee members, no outside counsel, and no date for the investigation to conclude.

What Huang told his staff

  • The admission. In a message to employees published on Nvidia's newsroom on November 8, Huang wrote, "I was unfaithful to my wife. My actions hurt her and our children. There is no excuse for what I did. I am deeply sorry."
  • Full responsibility. Huang said he takes full responsibility for what he did and the hurt he caused, accepts the criticism that comes with it, and told colleagues, the board, partners and shareholders that "you deserve better from me."
  • The security question. Huang wrote that the board has initiated an independent investigation, that he supports it and will cooperate fully, and that he is committed to protecting Nvidia and supporting America's national security.
  • What he did not say. The message names no one, does not mention any other person, and does not use the word spy. It asks for privacy for his family, saying his family bears no responsibility for his choices.
  • Staying in charge. A company spokesperson said the news will not slow Nvidia and that Huang is committed to continuing to lead the company as CEO without interruption.

The reports behind the probe

  • The October report. Reports in late October said US intelligence agencies had assessed Alice Huang, a former executive at the Singapore based data center company Megaspeed who is also known as Huang Le, as an asset gathering information for Beijing, and that she had developed a close relationship with Jensen Huang. The two are not related.- The November report. A further report on November 6 said Huang was believed to have been in a romantic relationship with Alice Huang. Huang told Bloomberg in late October that his dealings with her were connected to Megaspeed and "completely normal and not unusual."- Unproven claims. The intelligence claims remain unproven in public. Alice Huang has not been charged with any crime, and China's government has rejected the claims. Singapore's home affairs ministry said on October 31 that its authorities had not investigated her over the alleged intelligence connections or the reported relationship.
  • The Megaspeed question. Megaspeed buys Nvidia's AI chips and has faced scrutiny over suspected diversion of advanced chips to China in breach of US export controls. Trade records cited in news reports showed its Malaysian affiliate bought at least $4.8 billion of advanced AI equipment containing Nvidia chips between June 2024 and February 2026.- The chip rules. The United States bars shipment of its most advanced AI chips to China, which is why any question about where Nvidia's chips end up carries national security weight. Nvidia has told reporters its regional cloud customers receive ordinary business information, not sensitive trade secrets.

Congress wants a briefing

  • The letter. Three Republican House committee chairs wrote to Huang on November 4, asking him to appear for a joint closed door briefing on November 18 about Nvidia's security protocols against foreign adversary espionage and his interactions with Alice Huang, whom the letter calls "a publicly reported People's Republic of China (PRC) intelligence asset."
  • The signatories. The letter was signed by Foreign Affairs Chairman Brian Mast, Select Committee on China Chairman John Moolenaar, and Intelligence Chairman Rick Crawford, and it asks Huang to accept the November 18 date or propose an alternative session day by November 11.
  • The concern. The chairs wrote that recent reports raise significant national security concerns about China's access to sensitive US technology and information, and that it is vital for Congress to determine the extent to which US national security has been placed at risk.
  • A request, not a summons. The letter asks for a voluntary briefing. It is not a subpoena, and Nvidia has not said whether Huang will attend.

The reaction in Washington and the market

  • Trump's defense. President Donald Trump told reporters on Sunday in West Palm Beach, Florida, that he had not spoken to Huang about the matter, called him "a fine person" and "an honourable guy," and said he would "certainly take him" on his trip to China for the APEC summit. Asked whether Huang should stay as CEO, Trump said, "I think so. Oh yes, sure."
  • The shares. Nvidia shares closed at $186.64 on Friday. Out of hours trading on Monday pointed to a fall of about 3.4 percent, to around $180.30, in after hours and perpetual futures markets.
  • The earlier briefing. Reports also said Trump was briefed on the intelligence findings about a year ago and took no action, a decision reports linked to a wish not to disrupt Nvidia's business and US China trade relations.

What happens next

  • November 11. Huang is due to tell the House chairs by November 11 whether he will attend the November 18 briefing or propose another date.
  • November 17. Nvidia reports third quarter results on November 17, the day before the requested briefing, in its first earnings call since the reports began.
  • November 18. The closed door briefing is requested for November 18 in the Rayburn House Office Building, if Huang accepts.
  • The open questions. The board has given no timeline for its investigation and said only that it will provide further updates as appropriate. What the investigation finds, and whether Congress hears from Huang, will decide how long this stays a governance story rather than a security one.
