One Dead, 12 Hurt as Brooklyn Building Falls Into Construction Pit

By Jayesh Marathe · November 12, 2026 · 7 min read

A three story building in Flatbush, Brooklyn, collapsed into an open construction pit on Wednesday afternoon, killing one person and injuring 12 others, six of them critically, according to a Fire Department official. Firefighters dug through the rubble into the night as the search continued for people believed to be trapped beneath the wreckage.

The collapse

  • The time and place. The building at 1321 Flatbush Avenue collapsed shortly after 4 p.m. on Wednesday, November 11, falling into an open construction site at 2625 Bedford Avenue next door. The first 911 call came in at 4:01 p.m.
  • The building. The structure was a three story mixed use building, built in 1930, with a shop at street level and apartments above. City records list four residential units and five units in total.
  • The first alert. The Fire Department said in a statement on Wednesday afternoon, "FDNY units are currently operating at a building collapse on Flatbush Avenue in Brooklyn."
  • The dead and injured. One person was pronounced dead at the scene, and 12 people were injured, six of them critically, according to a Fire Department official. The identity of the person who died had not been released.
  • The scale of the response. About 250 Fire Department and EMS personnel were operating at the scene by 7 p.m., the department said, using drones and flashlights as daylight faded and a search dog to comb the rubble.

The rescue

  • People trapped. Rescue workers did not have a full account of how many people may be trapped or missing, a Police Department official said, but they believed it is several.
  • Pulled from the rubble. Firefighters pulled survivors from the wreckage through the evening, including a woman rescued from the rubble just after 6:30 p.m. Two people had been removed from the rubble within the first hour.
  • The work. Firefighters cut through debris with saws and removed it by the bucketful, working by hand through piles of brick, wood and household items that had fallen into the pit, including a stove, a toilet and a door seen in drone footage.
  • Help for residents. New York City Emergency Management set up a Red Cross help center for residents affected by the collapse, and warned of traffic delays and road closures around the scene.
  • The mayor. Mayor Zohran Mamdani said first responders "are working quickly to secure the location and assist New Yorkers," and asked residents to "join me in holding our neighbors and the members of this community in our hearts." Flatbush Avenue was closed from Clarendon Road to Bedford Avenue.

The construction next door

  • The neighboring lot. The building fell into a pit on the lot next door, where a one story building had been demolished for a construction project. City records show the demolition permit was first issued on March 13, 2026, and signed off on June 8, 2026.
  • The cause. Officials have not announced a cause for the collapse and have not said whether the construction work next door contributed to it.
  • The inspectors. The city's Department of Buildings said its inspectors were on the scene assisting with recovery operations and conducting structural stability inspections of nearby buildings.
  • The investigation. The department is investigating the collapse, and the permit and violation history of the neighboring site is expected to figure in the inquiry. Council Member Farah Louis, who represents Flatbush, said she was in direct communication with Fire Commissioner Lillian Bonsignore about the rescue effort.

What happens next

  • The search. Firefighters continued searching the rubble into the night for anyone still trapped, and officials said the casualty figures could change as the rescue continued.
  • The buildings nearby. Inspectors will determine whether neighboring buildings are safe to occupy after the collapse exposed the structure of the homes beside the pit.
  • The questions. What brought the 96 year old building down, and whether the excavation next door played a role, will be the focus of the investigation in the days ahead.
