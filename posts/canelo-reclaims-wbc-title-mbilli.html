Canelo Alvarez Reclaimed the WBC Title by Beating Christian Mbilli in Riyadh

By Editorial Team · 7 min read

Canelo Alvarez beat Christian Mbilli by unanimous decision over 12 rounds on October 31 in Riyadh, reclaiming the WBC super middleweight title more than a year after losing his undisputed championship to Terence Crawford. The win handed Mbilli his first professional defeat and made Alvarez a world champion again at 36.

How the fight went

  • The early rounds. Mbilli, the reigning champion, started as the busier fighter and pressed forward behind his jab, but Alvarez's tighter defense and sharper counters kept the early rounds close and stopped the champion from building a lead.
  • The middle rounds. Alvarez took control in the second half of the fight, pinning Mbilli to the ropes in the seventh and landing the cleaner, harder punches as the champion's output faded and his pressure lost its sting.
  • The decision. All three judges scored the fight for Alvarez by unanimous decision after 12 rounds, giving the Mexican star the WBC super middleweight title and improving his record to 64-3-2 with 39 knockouts, while Mbilli fell to 29-1-1.
  • The title at stake. The World Boxing Council's preview had billed the bout as its super middleweight championship, with Mbilli entering as champion and Alvarez as the challenger ranked number one by the WBC.

The turning point

  • The eighth round deduction. Referee Hector Afu took a point from Mbilli in the eighth round for punches to the back of the head, a penalty that widened the gap on the cards and left the champion needing a finish he could not find.
  • The veteran's patience. Alvarez, fighting for the first time since his September 2025 loss to Crawford, boxed patiently behind his jab and uppercut rather than chasing a knockout, trusting that the cleaner work would win rounds as Mbilli grew more desperate.
  • The closing rounds. Mbilli caught Alvarez with a good right hand in the final round and Alvarez waved him on, but the Mexican finished the fight in control and celebrated as the decision was read. "It feels like my first title," Alvarez said afterward. "Sixteen years ago I won my first title. Now I won the title again."

What the win means

  • The comeback complete. Alvarez lost his undisputed super middleweight championship to Crawford in September 2025 and spent more than a year out of the ring, so the win answers the central question of his return: whether a 36 year old with 21 years as a professional could still beat an unbeaten champion.
  • The champion's fall. Mbilli entered the fight unbeaten and was elevated to full WBC champion in January 2026, but he leaves with his first defeat after a night in which his pressure never broke Alvarez's defense or timing.
  • The backdrop. The card went ahead in Riyadh even as the conflict between Saudi forces and Yemen's Houthi movement escalated, with other sporting events in the region called off, and Alvarez's early start time was set so children could go trick-or-treating on Halloween before the main event.

The rest of the card

  • The co-main event. Jaime Munguia survived a sixth round scare to retain his WBA super middleweight title, beating Nikita Zon by unanimous decision with scores of 117-111, 117-111 and 116-112.
  • Another title kept. Rafael Espinoza retained his WBO featherweight title on the same card, stopping replacement opponent Ricardo Blandon and keeping the belt in Mexican hands on a night billed as Mexico versus the World.
  • What comes next. Alvarez said he will take a couple of weeks off before returning to the gym, and with the WBC belt back around his waist, the division's biggest fights, including a possible unification, run through him again.
