Bob Heinz, Two Time Super Bowl Champion From the Dolphins' Undefeated 1972 Team, Dies at 79

By Editorial Team · 7 min read

Bob Heinz, the defensive tackle who started on the Miami Dolphins teams that won back to back Super Bowls and completed the NFL's only undefeated season in 1972, died on October 31, 2026, at his home in Fresno, California. He was 79.

The death

  • The passing. Heinz died of natural causes at his home in Fresno on Saturday, October 31, according to his obituary, and his son John confirmed the death to reporters. He was 79.
  • The health history. His son said Heinz had been diagnosed with heart failure four years earlier. The family has planned a private service, and in lieu of flowers it asks for donations in his memory to the Miami Dolphins Foundation.
  • The team's statement. The Dolphins posted a statement on Tuesday, November 3, saying: "We are saddened to learn of the passing of former Dolphins defensive tackle Bob Heinz, who played for Miami from 1969-77. Bob was a member of the 1972 Perfect Team, a two-time Super Bowl champion and a key part of Miami's legendary No-Name Defense. We extend our deepest sympathies to his family, loved ones and former teammates."
  • The family. Heinz is survived by his wife, Carol, his children Julie, Joe, and John, his daughter-in-law Laura, his grandchildren Matthew and Samantha, and his sister-in-law Claire Covington. His obituary said he will be remembered for his friendship, his sense of humor, and his deep love for those closest to him.

The 1972 perfect season

  • The undefeated run. Heinz started at defensive tackle on the 1972 Dolphins, the only team in NFL history to finish a season undefeated and untied, at 17-0, and win the Super Bowl. The team repeated as champions the next season.
  • The No-Name Defense. Heinz was a key part of the unit known as the No-Name Defense, a group with no single star that shut teams down as a whole. The nickname stuck because the defense had no household names, only players who did their jobs.
  • The record that still stands. More than five decades later, the 1972 Dolphins remain the league's only unbeaten champion. Heinz died on October 31, one day before Detroit's win over Minnesota secured that record for another year, when the Vikings took their first loss of the season.
  • The shrinking roster. Heinz is the 22nd player from that 1972 roster to die. Each passing brings the football world a step closer to the end of an era that no team has matched since.

A career in Miami

  • The draft pick. Born on July 25, 1947, in Milwaukee, Wisconsin, Heinz moved with his family to Stockton, California, while in high school. He played college football at Delta College and the University of the Pacific, and the Dolphins took him in the second round of the 1969 NFL Draft, 37th overall.
  • Nine seasons with the Dolphins. Heinz spent nine seasons with Miami, appearing in 111 games, and he missed the entire 1975 season with an injury before returning for 1976 and 1977. He finished his career with one season in Washington in 1978.
  • The honors. Heinz was inducted into the University of the Pacific's athletic Hall of Fame in 1988. He also enjoyed a good cigar and bourbon, the music of Jimmy Buffett, cheering on the San Francisco Giants, and rooting against any NFL team attempting perfection, according to his obituary.
  • The farewell toast. Hall of Fame running back Larry Csonka marked the occasion with a social media post and a drink, toasting all those who experienced the perfect season together.

What his death means

  • The end of an era, one player at a time. The 1972 Dolphins are remembered as a team, not as a collection of stars, and Heinz embodied that idea: a second round pick who did unglamorous work in the middle of the line for nine seasons.
  • The record lives on. The perfect season is the reason the team is still celebrated every year when the league's last unbeaten team loses. Heinz's death, one day before that ritual played out again, ties his name to the record one last time.
  • The team's goodbye. The Dolphins' statement called him a two time Super Bowl champion and a key part of the No-Name Defense. That is how a franchise says goodbye to a player who helped build its greatest team.
