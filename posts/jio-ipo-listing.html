Jio Listed at a 21% Premium. India's Biggest IPO Ever.

By Editorial Team · 7 min read

Jio Platforms listed on October 28, 2026, opening at Rs 1,358 on the BSE and NSE, a 21.36% premium over its Rs 1,119 issue price, and closed at Rs 1,417.50 on the NSE and Rs 1,417.35 on the BSE, up about 26.7%. The Rs 30,213-crore IPO is India's largest ever, and the debut made Jio the country's second-most valuable listed company.

What happened

  • A blockbuster debut. Jio Platforms began trading on the BSE and NSE on October 28, 2026, opening at Rs 1,358, a premium of Rs 239, or 21.36%, over its Rs 1,119 issue price, then hit an intraday high of Rs 1,423.80 on the BSE (Rs 1,423.90 on the NSE) before closing at Rs 1,417.50 on the NSE and Rs 1,417.35 on the BSE, up about 26.7%.
  • India's largest IPO ever. The offering raised Rs 30,213 crore through a fresh issue of 27 crore shares and no offer for sale, beating Hyundai Motor India's 2024 record of Rs 27,870 crore. The price band was Rs 1,065 to Rs 1,119, in lots of 12 shares, and Reliance Industries' October 24 filing fixed the issue price at Rs 1,119.
  • Demand broke records. The issue drew bids for 22.19 times the shares on offer, with qualified institutional buyers at 70.55 times, non-institutional investors at 14 times, and retail at 2.72 times. Total demand crossed Rs 7.4 lakh crore, applications approached 1.39 crore, and the issue filled within 100 minutes of opening on October 21. The October 19 anchor book raised Rs 8,110 crore from 212 investors, on bids above Rs 2.5 lakh crore.
  • The exchanges admitted it formally. The BSE's October 27 circular admitted 9,209,030,830 shares to trading from October 28 under symbol JPL and scrip code 545004, while the NSE's circular the same day admitted them to its Capital Market segment as Group 1 securities, eligible for margin trading from day one. Reliance's October 14 filing recorded the red herring prospectus, reserving up to Rs 500 crore for employees and up to Rs 2,500 crore for Reliance shareholders, and its October 27 filing confirmed allotment completed on October 26 with final listing approval in hand.

Why it matters

  • A public price for India's digital rails. Jio's listing gives the market its first standalone price for the network carrying a large share of India's mobile data, with 533.3 million customers as of June 2026, and turns a Reliance subsidiary into a separately valued company.
  • The second-most valuable company in India, overnight. At the close, Jio's market capitalisation reached Rs 13.05 lakh crore, behind only parent Reliance at about Rs 15.7 lakh crore, ahead of Bharti Airtel, HDFC Bank and ICICI Bank, making Jio India's most valuable telecom company, with 7.98 million shareholders, the highest for any Indian company at debut.
  • A $100 billion debut club. Valued at about Rs 10.3 lakh crore at the issue price and Rs 13.05 lakh crore at the close, Jio joined Meta and Alibaba among founder-led businesses valued above $100 billion around their public debut.
  • Fresh money, not an exit. Because the issue contained no offer for sale, every rupee raised went to the company rather than to existing shareholders, with up to Rs 27,500 crore earmarked to repay or prepay borrowings of telecom arm Jio Infocomm.

Second order effects

  • Reliance's sum of parts is now visible. With Jio priced separately, investors can value Reliance's remaining retail, oil-to-chemicals and new energy businesses without guessing at the telecom arm's worth, giving the holding-company discount a market-tested number.
  • A new anchor for Indian indices. A Rs 13 lakh crore company is an immediate candidate for benchmark index weight, and any Nifty or Sensex inclusion would force index funds to buy the stock, creating demand unrelated to anyone's view on Jio.
  • Tariffs are now a public debate. Jio's earnings, average revenue per user and 5G monetisation will be judged every quarter in public, and any tariff hike, broadband push or enterprise deal will move the stock, giving pricing decisions a market audience they never had inside Reliance.

Third order effects

  • India's capital market grew up a notch. An offering that drew 1.39 crore applications and more than Rs 7.4 lakh crore of demand, then listed cleanly, shows domestic demand can absorb the country's largest deal without foreign buyers, with the NSE's total market capitalisation now at Rs 455 lakh crore.
  • A template for the next giant listing. Jio proved a mega issue can fill within 100 minutes, list at a premium and hold its gains on a weak market day, and the next large Indian listing will be judged against that playbook.
  • The AI bet gets a public scoreboard. Jio pitches itself as a technology and AI company, not just a telecom operator, and a listed price means that claim will now be tested quarterly against cloud, enterprise and digital earnings rather than accepted on management's word.
  • Concentration risk rises with the reward. Two companies from one group now top India's market-value rankings, so a shock to Reliance or Jio would ripple through indices, mutual funds and retail portfolios at a scale no single group has commanded.

What to watch next

  • The October 30 results. Jio Platforms reports September-quarter results on October 30, two days after listing, and the first numbers as a public company will set the tone for the debut premium.
  • Whether Rs 1,358 holds. Watch whether the stock defends its Rs 1,358 listing price, as listing-pop buyers may sell into strength.
  • Tariff and ARPU signals. Watch for any move on tariffs or average revenue per user, since earnings are highly sensitive to small changes in monthly wireless ARPU.
  • Broadband and enterprise growth. Watch home broadband additions, enterprise deals and cloud and AI revenue, Jio's growth lines as mobile growth slows.
  • Index decisions. Watch whether index providers add Jio to the Nifty or Sensex, since inclusion triggers automatic buying by index funds.
