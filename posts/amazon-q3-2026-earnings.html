Amazon posts $201.9 billion in third quarter sales as AWS growth jumps to 39%

By Editorial Team · 7 min read

Amazon reported third quarter net sales of $201.9 billion, up 12%, and operating income of $28.1 billion, its highest operating margin ever at 13.9%. AWS sales grew 39% to $46.0 billion, while net income slipped to $20.7 billion as last year's Anthropic gains did not repeat. The company guided fourth quarter sales of $240 billion to $247 billion.

What happened

  • The headline result. Amazon reported net sales of $201.9 billion, up 12% from $180.2 billion a year earlier, and operating income of $28.1 billion, up from $17.4 billion, for a 13.9% operating margin that Amazon called its highest ever. Net income fell to $20.7 billion, or $1.89 per diluted share, from $21.2 billion, or $1.95 per share, because the year-ago quarter included $9.5 billion of pre-tax gains from Amazon's investments in Anthropic that did not repeat.
  • AWS growth. AWS sales grew 39% to $46.0 billion, up from $33.0 billion a year earlier, for a $184 billion annualized revenue run rate, while AWS operating income rose to $17.8 billion from $11.4 billion.
  • Stores result. North America sales rose 6% to $112.9 billion with operating income of $8.7 billion, up from $4.8 billion, while International sales rose 5% to $43.1 billion with operating income of $1.6 billion, up from $1.2 billion.
  • The cash and spending picture. Operating cash flow rose 29% to $168.3 billion for the trailing twelve months, while free cash flow swung to a $42.4 billion outflow from a $14.8 billion inflow a year earlier, driven by a $94.8 billion increase in purchases of property and equipment, net of proceeds from sales and incentives, mostly for artificial intelligence.

Why it matters

  • Against its own guidance. Net sales of $201.9 billion landed inside Amazon's guided range of $197.0 billion to $202.0 billion, near the top, while operating income of $28.1 billion came in above the guided range of $22.5 billion to $26.5 billion. The beat came on profit, not sales, which means margin did the work this quarter.
  • AWS as the profit engine. AWS operating income of $17.8 billion was nearly two thirds of Amazon's $28.1 billion total, while AWS sales of $46.0 billion were less than a quarter of the $201.9 billion total. North America contributed $8.7 billion of operating income and International contributed $1.6 billion, so the cloud business remains the profit center that funds everything else.
  • The spending question. Operating cash flow reached $168.3 billion for the trailing twelve months, yet free cash flow is now a $42.4 billion outflow because purchases of property and equipment, net of proceeds, reached $210.7 billion for the same period, up 82% year over year. Amazon confirmed it still expects about $220 billion of cash capital expenditure in 2026, so the AI build is still outrunning operating cash flow.
  • The one-off warning. Net income fell even as operating income hit a record, because the year-ago quarter included $9.5 billion of pre-tax Anthropic gains and this quarter's other income was a $0.7 billion expense. The $1.89 of diluted earnings per share, down from $1.95, says more about investment accounting than about trading in the quarter.

Second order effects

  • Capacity against demand. The release lists new AWS agreements with Meta, NVIDIA, OpenAI, Salesforce, Oracle and others, says Bedrock token volume grew nearly 17 times year over year, and quotes chief executive Andy Jassy saying AWS revenue growth is projected at over 40% in both 2027 and 2028. That is a demand picture, not a demand problem, and the open question is whether Amazon can build capacity fast enough to serve it.
  • Advertising and stores. Advertising services revenue reached $19.6 billion, up 11% year over year, or 12% excluding foreign exchange, with Amazon blaming timing anomalies mostly tied to Prime Day, and it expects advertising growth in the high teens in the fourth quarter. Inside stores, online stores grew just 2% to $68.5 billion, while third-party seller services grew 9% to $46.5 billion and subscription services grew 11% to $14.0 billion.
  • The Prime Day timing effect. Amazon repeated that Prime Day fell in the second quarter this year against the third quarter last year, and that excluding the shift, third quarter growth would have been about 400 basis points higher. Reported growth of 12% therefore understates the underlying trend by roughly that amount, which is why the company keeps restating the adjustment.

Third order effects

  • The AI infrastructure bet. With purchases of property and equipment, net of proceeds, at $210.7 billion for the trailing twelve months and cash capital expenditure still expected at about $220 billion for 2026, Amazon is funding the largest infrastructure build in its history from operating cash flow plus debt. Long-term debt rose to $173.1 billion at September 30 from $65.6 billion at December 31, 2025, and the quarter included $48.1 billion of long-term debt proceeds. The bet is that AWS growth above 40% in 2027 and 2028 pays for it.
  • Retail margin against cloud margin. AWS operating margin was 38.7% in the third quarter, against 7.7% in North America and 3.7% in International. The company keeps getting more profitable as the mix shifts toward cloud, even as the stores business grows more slowly, which is the structural story underneath any single quarter.
  • The holiday quarter setup. Fourth quarter guidance of $240 billion to $247 billion in sales and $28.5 billion to $33.5 billion in operating income would make it Amazon's largest sales quarter on record, with advertising expected to grow in the high teens and more than 300,000 seasonal workers hired for the holidays. Whether record margins survive that peak is the question the quarter will answer.

What to watch next

  • Q4 guidance. Whether fourth quarter sales land in the $240 billion to $247 billion range, up 12% to 16% from a year earlier, and operating income in the $28.5 billion to $33.5 billion range, against $25.0 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025.
  • AWS growth against capacity. Whether AWS growth holds near 39% or moves toward the over 40% rates Jassy projected for 2027 and 2028, and whether the new agreements with Meta, NVIDIA, OpenAI and others convert into reported revenue in the next two quarters.
  • Capex and free cash flow. Whether the about $220 billion cash capital expenditure expectation for 2026 holds, and whether free cash flow stops deteriorating after the trailing twelve month outflow widened from $7.6 billion at the end of the second quarter to $42.4 billion at the end of the third.
  • The holiday season. The holiday quarter itself: more than 300,000 seasonal hires, the fastest third quarter delivery speeds on record with more than 1.5 billion items arriving same-day or overnight, and whether advertising growth rebounds to the high teens Amazon expects.
