Vikings Bring Back Kirk Cousins After Bills Expose Quarterback Crisis

By Jayesh Marathe · November 10, 2026 · 7 min read

The Minnesota Vikings acquired quarterback Kirk Cousins from the Las Vegas Raiders on November 10 for a 2027 third round pick, bringing back the quarterback who led them for six seasons less than 24 hours after their offense collapsed in a 23-6 loss to the Buffalo Bills.

The deal

  • The return. The Vikings sent a 2027 third round pick to Las Vegas for Cousins, and the team announced the trade on Tuesday just ahead of the league trade deadline. The deal is pending Cousins passing a physical.
  • The price. Minnesota sent its own 2027 third round pick and kept the extra third round pick it received from Philadelphia in the Jonathan Greenard trade, according to reports, so the cost was a single mid round selection for a starting quarterback.
  • The contract. Cousins is playing this season on a one year deal worth $11.3 million, and the Vikings take on the rest of his $1.3 million base salary plus a $10 million roster bonus due in the spring, according to reports.
  • The timing. The trade landed hours before the 4 p.m. ET deadline on Tuesday, November 10, and it came one day after the Vikings managed 178 total yards against Buffalo on Monday night.
  • The trigger. Carson Wentz turned in what was described as the worst performance by a Vikings quarterback since 2006, and the Vikings moved within a day to fix a position that had threatened to sink a 6-2 season.

Why Minnesota had to act

  • A season at risk. The Vikings started 6-0 and then lost back to back games, and the offense fell apart in the second loss as Wentz struggled to move the ball at all against the Bills.
  • No answer on the roster. Kyler Murray, who signed a one year deal with Minnesota after his release by the Cardinals, failed to grab hold of the starting job, and Wentz could not hold it either, leaving the Vikings with no dependable passer.
  • A familiar fix. Cousins knows head coach Kevin O'Connell's offense from their two seasons together in 2022 and 2023, so he can step in without learning a new system in the middle of a playoff race.
  • The schedule. The Vikings visit the Green Bay Packers on Sunday, November 15, at Lambeau Field, the same stadium where Cousins tore his Achilles in his last game in purple in October 2023, and a win there would steady a season that wobbled in a single week.
  • The defense. Minnesota's defense, led by coordinator Brian Flores, has kept the team in contention all season, and the front office decided a proven quarterback was the missing piece rather than a reason to wait for the offseason.

What the Vikings get

  • The production. Cousins has completed 202 of 315 passes this season for 2,340 yards with 20 touchdowns and eight interceptions in nine starts, ranking second in the league in touchdown passes.
  • The history. Cousins, 38, played for Minnesota from 2018 through 2023, made four Pro Bowls, and left for the Atlanta Falcons in free agency in 2024 before joining the Raiders.
  • The record. He went 4-5 as the Raiders starter this season, and his departure opens the door for Las Vegas to hand the offense to its rookie quarterback.
  • The reunion. Cousins returns to the coach and the playbook he knows best, and to a receiving group built around Justin Jefferson, who has waited all season for steady quarterback play.

What Las Vegas gets

  • The pick. The Raiders receive a 2027 third round pick for a 38 year old quarterback on a one year deal, a solid return for a player they signed to bridge the gap to their rookie.
  • The rookie. No. 1 overall pick Fernando Mendoza will take over as the Raiders starter, according to ESPN's Adam Schefter, and he will make his first career start on Sunday against the Seattle Seahawks.
  • The test. Mendoza, the Heisman Trophy winner and national champion at Indiana, faces the defending Super Bowl champions in his debut, with the Denver Broncos waiting the week after, two of the toughest defenses in the league.
  • The resume. Mendoza has played in only one game this season, completing six of eight passes for 48 yards in the fourth quarter of a 45-17 loss to the New England Patriots on October 11.
  • The future. The Raiders sit at 4-5, and the trade signals that Las Vegas is ready to find out now whether the quarterback it drafted first overall is ready to lead the franchise.

What comes next

  • The debut. Cousins could make his Vikings return on Sunday at Lambeau Field, five days after the trade, in a game that will test how quickly a veteran can rejoin a team midseason.
  • The physical. The deal still needs Cousins to pass his physical with Minnesota before it becomes official, the standard final step for a deadline trade.
  • The race. The Vikings sit third in the NFC North at 6-2, and the next month will decide whether the Cousins reunion saves their season or only delays a rebuild.
  • The rookie watch. Mendoza's first two starts come against Seattle and Denver, and how he handles that stretch will shape the Raiders' offseason more than anything else they do this year.
