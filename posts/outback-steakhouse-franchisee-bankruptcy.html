Outback Steakhouse Franchisee Files for Bankruptcy After Closing 10 Restaurants, and the Chain's Parent Moves to Buy the Rest

By Jayesh Marathe · November 5, 2026 · 7 min read

Out West Restaurant Group, the franchisee that runs 61 Outback Steakhouse restaurants across the western United States, filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection in Delaware on November 2, 2026, after closing 10 restaurants in October. Bloomin' Brands, the chain's parent company, moved the same week to buy the remaining restaurants for $26.3 million.

The filing

  • The Chapter 11 petition. Cerca Trova Restaurant Group Holdings, which operates as Out West Restaurant Group, filed voluntary petitions for relief under Chapter 11 in the United States Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware on November 2, 2026. The company is an independently owned franchisee of Bloomin' Brands, not part of the parent company itself.
  • The scale of the business. Out West runs 61 Outback Steakhouse restaurants in the western United States, and the filing says the business will continue as a debtor in possession under the court's supervision. Court documents put the workforce at about 3,669 people.
  • No immediate change for guests. The filing states that the Chapter 11 case has no immediate impact on any Out West or company owned Outback Steakhouse guests, restaurant team members, or day to day operations. The 61 restaurants stay open while the case moves through the court.
  • Outback itself is not bankrupt. The bankruptcy covers the franchisee only. Outback Steakhouse, the brand, has filed nothing, and Bloomin' Brands has announced no nationwide shutdown of its own restaurants.

The 10 closed restaurants

  • The October closures. Court documents show Out West closed 10 underperforming restaurants between October 11 and October 25, 2026, and then asked the court to let it walk away from their leases. The closures span four states.
  • The Arizona closures. The closed restaurants include the Outback at 4871 E. Grant Road in Tucson and the one at 1860 E. McKellips Road in Mesa, according to the court documents.
  • The California closures. Three California restaurants closed in October: 7575 Beach Blvd. in Buena Park, 25261 N. The Old Road in Valencia, and 26652 Portola Parkway in Foothill Ranch.
  • The Colorado closures. Four Colorado restaurants closed: 7065 Commerce Center Drive in Colorado Springs, 15 W. Springer Drive in Highlands Ranch, 2066 S. Abilene St. in Aurora, and 8601 W. Cross Drive in Littleton.
  • The Nevada closure. The tenth closed restaurant is at 3645 S. Virginia St. in Reno, Nevada.
  • Three more closed earlier in the year. Court documents show Out West had already closed three California restaurants earlier in 2026, in Brea and Pittsburg in March and Westminster in June, bringing its total closures this year to 13.

The $26.3 million buyout

  • The stalking horse bid. On November 1, 2026, Bloomin' Brands signed an asset purchase agreement to buy substantially all of Out West's assets for $26.3 million, acting as the stalking horse bidder in the court run sale. A stalking horse bid sets the floor price, and rival buyers can try to beat it.
  • The price mechanics. The $26.3 million price drops by whatever Out West owes under the bankruptcy loan, and rises dollar for dollar if the vendor liabilities Bloomin' takes on come in below $6.1 million. Bloomin' also takes on certain liabilities, including lease obligations for future periods, unredeemed gift cards, and specified vendor debts.
  • The bankruptcy loan. Bloomin' agreed to lend Out West up to $5.5 million in interest free debtor in possession financing to keep the restaurants running while the case proceeds. Whatever Out West borrows comes off the purchase price.
  • The deadline. The outside closing date under the purchase agreement is January 31, 2027, after which the agreement ends unless it is extended. The sale still needs bankruptcy court approval, and the bidding process must run first.

What stays open

  • The 61 restaurants. The restaurants covered by the case sit in Arizona, California, Colorado, Nevada, and New Mexico, according to the bankruptcy filing, and all 61 are expected to keep serving guests while the sale process runs.
  • The parent takes direct control. If the sale closes, the 61 restaurants move from franchisee ownership to direct ownership by Bloomin' Brands, which already owns the Outback brand and runs most of the chain's restaurants itself.
  • Why the parent wants them. Bloomin' said it believes taking over the restaurants gives it direct operational insight, lets it invest in the units, and helps it limit disruption for guests and employees. The filing frames the purchase as a way to steady the restaurants rather than as a rescue of the brand.
  • The message to regulars. For guests, the practical point is simple: the filing changes who owns the restaurants in five western states, not whether the doors stay open, and the parent company has said operations continue without interruption.

What happens next

  • The court runs the sale. Rival buyers get a chance to top Bloomin's $26.3 million offer, with a court supervised auction to follow if anyone else bids, and a judge must approve the final sale.
  • Gift cards stay valid. Because Bloomin' agreed to take on unredeemed gift cards as part of the deal, guests holding Outback gift cards should see them honored at the affected restaurants.
  • The numbers to watch. The case turns on whether a rival bidder emerges with a higher offer, and on the court's view of the bidding rules, which will decide whether Bloomin' ends up owning the 61 restaurants outright.
  • The search that started it all. People searching for a closures list this week were looking for exactly this answer: 10 restaurants closed in October, three more earlier in the year, 61 still open, and a parent company stepping in to buy them.
