Britain Backs Fractile With £500M in AI Chip Race

By Jayesh Marathe · November 12, 2026 · 7 min read

The British government has joined a funding round worth more than £500 million for Fractile, a startup building the chips that run artificial intelligence, in a bet that Britain can build its own AI hardware instead of buying it from abroad. The Series C values the London and Bristol company at more than £5 billion.

The round

  • The size. The round is worth more than £500 million, the government said in an announcement on Wednesday, November 11. It is a Series C financing, and it values Fractile at more than £5 billion.
  • The bigger numbers. Fractile told Reuters it raised $720 million in the round, at a $7 billion valuation. The figures differ because the government stated them in pounds and as minimums, while the company stated the dollar total.
  • The leaders. The round was led by Thrive Capital, Founders Fund, Lightspeed and Redpoint, Fractile told Reuters. CapitalG, BlackRock, DST Global, Elad Gil, Balderton, SV Angel and the British Business Bank also joined, along with the government's Sovereign AI fund.
  • The public money. The UK government's Sovereign AI Venture Fund and the British Business Bank backed the round. The government did not say how much either one put in, calling the figures commercially sensitive.
  • The last round. The Series C follows a $220 million round Fractile announced in May 2026 to build next generation inference hardware.

What Fractile builds

  • The product. Fractile designs chips for AI inference, the process of running a trained AI model to produce answers. Its chips are meant to run AI faster, use less energy and cost less than the hardware in use today.
  • The approach. The company is rethinking the full stack that AI rests on, from chips to software to fundamental research into machine learning, the government said. It was founded in the summer of 2022 and is based in London and Bristol.
  • The first chips. Fractile plans to ship its first chips and systems next year, its chief executive Walter Goodwin wrote on LinkedIn, calling the funding the means to get the company's first products out the door.
  • The competition. Fractile is one of a small group of startups building purpose built AI chips, alongside Axelera, Etched and OLIX, as the largest AI companies pour money into hardware that can run models more cheaply.

Why the government is in

  • The ambition. The investment is another step in the government's ambition to make Britain a world leader in the technologies that will shape the future of AI, the announcement said, ensuring that British scientific excellence translates into British companies, jobs and industrial strength.
  • The sovereignty argument. The government said the backing will strengthen Britain's sovereign capabilities in critical technologies, reducing dependence on fragile overseas supply chains and giving the UK a greater stake in the hardware that will underpin its future economy.
  • The minister. AI Minister Kanishka Narayan said Britain has produced some of the world's best scientific ideas for too long, "only to see too much of the technology, industry and wealth they create built elsewhere," and that the country needs to build more of the technology that powers AI.
  • The business secretary. Business Secretary Jonathan Reynolds called Fractile "a fantastic British success story" and said the company plans to expand its presence in Bristol, putting the government's plans for reindustrialization into action.
  • The investors. Sovereign AI Investment Partner Josephine Kant said Fractile's team is taking on an ambitious challenge with the potential to compete with the very best globally. British Business Bank chief investment officer Leandros Kalisperas said the team is building chips designed to work faster and at greater capacity.

The wider package

  • The Budget. The news comes two weeks after the Budget, in which the Chancellor confirmed a further £140 million for Sovereign AI, with £60 million to support the fund's investments and £80 million for its research and development procurement scheme.
  • The expansion. The Chancellor also said Sovereign AI would expand into physical and industrial AI, covering robotics, autonomous systems and advanced manufacturing, and that the UK will invest up to £2 billion in the coming years to build and deploy the compute infrastructure for the AI era.
  • The bank. The Budget expanded the British Business Bank's investment capacity by £5 billion to £30.6 billion, giving it more firepower to back fast growing British firms. The package is expected to support more than 24,600 existing jobs and create more than 15,800 new ones.

What to watch next

  • The first chips. Fractile's first commercial chips are due next year, when the company will have to show its hardware can compete with the established chip makers on speed, energy use and cost.
  • The valuation test. A valuation above £5 billion for a company that has not shipped a product sets a high bar, and the next test is whether the hardware works as promised when it reaches data centers.
  • The policy signal. The round puts two state backed institutions alongside some of the biggest names in venture capital, a sign that Britain is willing to spend public money to keep its AI hardware companies at home.
