Michael Sanft, Lawyer Who Defended Keefe D in Tupac Murder Trial, Dies

By Jayesh Marathe · November 7, 2026 · 7 min read

Michael Sanft, the Las Vegas lawyer who defended Duane "Keefe D" Davis in the murder trial over Tupac Shakur's death, has died. The Clark County coroner's office confirmed his death on Friday, November 6, and his cause of death is pending.

What happened

  • Sanft was found dead in Las Vegas. Sanft was found dead in Las Vegas, and a spokesperson for the Clark County Office of the Coroner confirmed his death on Friday, November 6.
  • No cause of death has been released. His cause of death is pending, and no further details have been released.
  • Police referred questions to the coroner. The Las Vegas Metropolitan Police Department referred questions about Sanft's death to the Clark County coroner's office.
  • His firm has not commented publicly. No statement had been released by Sanft's law firm, and the coroner's office said it would release more information when it is available.

The Tupac trial

  • Sanft defended Keefe D pro bono. Sanft represented Duane "Keefe D" Davis without charging legal fees in the trial over the 1996 shooting of Tupac Shakur.
  • A jury convicted Davis in August. A jury found Davis guilty of first-degree murder with the use of a deadly weapon for facilitating the fatal 1996 shooting of Shakur.
  • Davis was the only person charged. Davis, 63, was the first and only person charged in the 30-year-old case, and a judge sentenced him to 40 years to life in prison in October.
  • The sentence came in two parts. Davis received 20 years to life for first-degree murder and an additional 20 years to life for the deadly weapon enhancement.
  • An appeal was already filed. Davis's defense team filed a notice of appeal on October 30, a week before Sanft's death, and Sanft had planned to challenge the conviction.

Who Michael Sanft was

  • He led trials at a Las Vegas firm. Sanft served as lead trial attorney at SOS Injury Lawyers in Las Vegas.
  • He tried more than 150 cases. His professional biography credited him with trying more than 150 cases to jury verdict.
  • He took the Tupac case for free. Sanft accepted Davis's defense pro bono and said he believed his client was innocent.
  • The trial made him nationally known. The Tupac case brought Sanft national attention, as the trial closed a case that had gone unsolved for nearly 30 years.

What happens to the appeal

  • The appeal now needs a new lawyer. Sanft's death leaves an open question about who will handle Davis's appeal, and no replacement attorney had been identified.
  • The record is large. Any new lawyer will need to absorb a long trial record and the arguments Sanft intended to raise.
  • Davis keeps the right to appeal. Davis still has the right to pursue post-conviction review, but a change of counsel will take time.
  • No briefing schedule exists yet. No briefing schedule for the appeal was available, and it is unclear when the court will set one.
