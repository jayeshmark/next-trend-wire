Binance Says $6.3 Billion Was Drained in a Security Incident, the Largest Crypto Theft on Record

By Editorial Team · 7 min read

Binance, the world's largest cryptocurrency exchange, says about $6.3 billion in crypto was drained from its wallets on Thursday in what would be the largest theft in the industry's history. The exchange says about $3 billion has been frozen, that customer losses will be covered, and that withdrawals are suspended while it investigates.

What happened

  • The first alarm. On Thursday afternoon, on-chain watchers flagged crypto moving out of Binance wallets into newly created addresses, and the exchange's first public response came at about 5:15 p.m. ET, when its official account said it was aware of reports of a potential security issue and was actively investigating.
  • The scale. In a late Thursday update, Binance put the estimated total impact at around $6.3 billion, with about $3 billion frozen so far, including $2.3 billion in USDT and USDC stablecoins. That would make it the largest crypto theft on record, nearly four times the $1.5 billion taken from Bybit in February 2025.
  • The wallets. The transfers began at 4:24 p.m. ET, when blockchain records show 167,464 ether moving from a wallet labeled Binance 14 to a newly created address, followed by 28,005.6 ether from Binance 16, and then about 195,460 ether, worth roughly $442 million, sent on to a second address. In total, roughly 204,500 ether moved.
  • A second breach the same day. Prediction market provider Polymarket said a third-party provider of its own had been breached and suspended international trading after hackers drained more than $2.6 million from user accounts.

How it happened

  • Binance's early finding. Binance's initial investigation suggests the incident may involve a compromised laptop belonging to a DevOps engineer, which allowed an attacker to gain access to a service container and submit unauthorized withdrawal transactions.
  • No key leak found. The exchange said it has found no evidence of any wallet private key leak, and it revoked the affected address authorization.
  • A technical twist. Analysts say Ethereum's EIP-7702 feature, which lets a wallet hand control to a piece of software, was the mechanism that turned the wallet into a sweeper forwarding incoming funds to the attacker's address.
  • Who is blamed. Blockchain intelligence firm TRM Labs said it traced the attack to TraderTraitor, an elite North Korean hacking group, and other investigators, including ZachXBT and Blockaid, pointed the same way. Binance itself has not named an attacker.

The freeze and the cover

  • Tether and Circle moved first. Tether said it froze more than $1.567 billion in USDT linked to the incident, and Circle said it froze more than $800 million in USDC in coordination with law enforcement.
  • Others joined. Ripple froze roughly $200 million, and World Liberty Financial, working with custodian BitGo, froze $27 million in stablecoins and about $2 million worth of its own tokens.
  • Customers covered. Binance co-founder and co-CEO Yi He said Binance will cover all hacking losses, from its Secure Asset Fund for Users, which holds roughly $1 billion in bitcoin, and from company funds. Binance says user funds are safe.
  • Withdrawals paused. Binance temporarily suspended deposits and withdrawals while its team conducts security checks, freezes funds and works with law enforcement, and it temporarily suspended trading on its Binance Alpha platform. Yi He said withdrawals will resume as soon as the security scan is complete.
  • The US arm is separate. Binance.US, a separate company, said it was not affected and that its customer funds and services were operating normally.

Why it matters

  • The record it breaks. At $6.3 billion, the theft dwarfs every previous crypto heist, including the $1.5 billion Bybit attack in 2025 that was also linked to North Korean hackers, and it lands on the world's largest exchange, not a small platform.
  • The reserve question. The drained wallets are the same ones Binance lists in its monthly proof of reserves as evidence that customer crypto is fully backed. According to CoinDesk's review of that data, the stolen ether would leave Binance's ether backing near 94 percent, down from 100 percent on October 1.
  • The market reaction. Bitcoin fell more than 3 percent over 24 hours to about $77,400 on Friday morning, briefly dipping toward $76,000, and more than $1 billion in leveraged futures bets were liquidated, according to market coverage.
  • Trust is the product. An exchange sells custody. When the largest one loses $6.3 billion in an afternoon, even with losses covered, the question every customer asks is whether their funds were ever as segregated as the marketing said.

What to watch next

  • The November 1 snapshot. Binance's next proof-of-reserves snapshot, due November 1, will show whether it has replaced the missing assets.
  • The post-mortem. Binance promised a detailed technical post-mortem once the facts are fully verified, which should settle how the laptop compromise worked and whether the EIP-7702 mechanism was the key step.
  • Withdrawals. The practical test for customers is when deposits and withdrawals resume, and whether the freeze total climbs above $3 billion as tracing continues.
