Nasdaq Plunges 5.5 Percent in Worst Day in 19 Months as AI Selloff Deepens

By Jayesh Marathe · November 6, 2026 · 7 min read

US stocks tumbled on Thursday, November 5, as the Nasdaq Composite fell 5.52 percent in its worst day in 19 months. A ferocious selloff in chip and data center stocks erased the entire post election rally in a single session and pushed the Nasdaq into correction territory.

What happened

  • The Nasdaq led the fall. The Nasdaq Composite dropped 5.52 percent to close at 24,315.00, its biggest one day decline since April 2025, and it ended the day more than 10 percent below its record closing high from October 6, confirming a technical correction.
  • The broader market fell too. The S&P 500 fell more than 4 percent, while the Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 3.44 percent to 51,523.06, a drop of more than 1,800 points that wiped out the roughly 1,500 point rally from the day before, and all 30 Dow stocks finished lower.
  • Every sector fell. All 11 sectors of the S&P 500 ended lower, with technology down 7.3 percent, the deepest loss of any sector, while consumer staples and utilities lost less than 1 percent each.
  • The Nasdaq 100 fell hardest. The Nasdaq 100 dropped 6.36 percent, its worst single day since March 2020, and it lost $2.3 trillion in market value in one session, the largest one day loss on record for the index, leaving it about 16 percent below its peak.
  • Chip stocks cratered. The Philadelphia semiconductor index fell 12 percent, its second biggest drop on record, as SanDisk plunged 22 percent and Micron, AMD and Marvell each fell 17 percent, while Nvidia dropped 9 percent.
  • The slide extends a rough stretch. The Nasdaq had already fallen 2.86 percent on October 30, its worst day since June at the time, and Thursday turned a two day wobble into a full blown correction in technology shares.

What triggered it

  • Investors repriced the AI buildout. The selling gathered force as investors reassessed the Democrats' midterm sweep and what Democratic control of Congress could mean for regulation of artificial intelligence and the data center buildout, turning a trade that had powered the market all year into the market's biggest liability.
  • Qualcomm disappointed on phones. Qualcomm reported fourth quarter fiscal 2026 results on November 4, with handset revenue down 26 percent to $5.14 billion and total revenue down 8 percent to $10.32 billion, and its shares fell about 9 percent even though automotive revenue jumped 89 percent and the company repeated its $5 billion data center revenue target for fiscal 2027.
  • Chip stocks fell for a second day. The semiconductor slide ran for a second straight session, with memory and data center names leading the losses as investors questioned whether the massive spending on AI infrastructure will pay off fast enough.
  • The post election rally reversed in a day. Stocks had surged on Wednesday as the easing of political uncertainty lifted risk appetite, with the Dow posting its biggest one day percentage gain in three months, and Thursday gave back all of it and more.
  • Gold caught a bid. Investors moved toward safety as the selloff deepened, with gold rising while Treasury yields fell, the classic pattern of a market rushing out of risk.

Why it matters

  • The AI trade is being repriced. For two years investors paid up for any company spending on AI, and now they are asking when that spending turns into profit, with results that merely meet forecasts no longer enough to hold a stock up.
  • The damage is concentrated in tech. The Dow finished the day down about 6 percent from its peak while the Nasdaq 100 sat about 16 percent below its own, which shows the selloff is a technology story first, not a whole economy story.
  • A correction changes the conversation. With the Nasdaq more than 10 percent below its record, the question for fund managers shifts from whether to buy the dip to whether the AI boom has further to fall.
  • Politics now drives the tape. The market spent Thursday trading the midterm results rather than earnings, which means Washington's next moves on AI regulation and data center policy matter as much as the next earnings report.
  • The losses are real money. Roughly $2 trillion in Nasdaq market value vanished in a single session, a scale of destruction that forces even long term holders to rethink how much of their portfolio sits in a handful of AI names.

What to watch next

  • Friday's jobs report. The October nonfarm payrolls report lands Friday, and a hot or cold reading could either steady the market or deepen the selloff, with traders also watching whether the Federal Reserve changes course on rates.
  • Whether the selling spreads. The next test is whether the rout stays in chips and data centers or bleeds into the rest of the market, which held up better on Thursday.
  • The next wave of earnings. Whether other big technology companies reassure investors about returns on AI spending, or confirm the worries that drove Thursday's selling.
  • The Nasdaq's next test. Whether the index stabilizes near its current level or extends its slide further below the October 6 record, which would confirm a deeper correction in technology shares.
  • Washington's next move. What the new Democratic Congress signals about AI regulation and the data center buildout, and whether the industry gets clarity or a long fight.
