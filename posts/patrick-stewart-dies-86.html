Patrick Stewart, Captain Picard and Professor X, Dies at 86

By Editorial Team · 7 min read

Patrick Stewart, the Yorkshire-born Shakespearean actor who became Captain Jean-Luc Picard on Star Trek and Professor Charles Xavier in the X-Men films, has died at 86. He died Wednesday at his home in Los Angeles, his family announced Thursday, after what it called a brief journey with Alzheimer's disease and Lewy body dementia.

What happened

  • The announcement. A statement shared Thursday on Stewart's official social media account by his spokesperson, Kelly Bush Novak, said he died Wednesday at his home in Los Angeles, in the loving care of his wife, Sunny Ozell, after a brief and poignant journey with Alzheimer's disease and Lewy body dementia.
  • Diagnoses kept private. The statement said his diagnoses had not been previously disclosed.
  • Family at his side. The statement said he was held in the warm embrace of family and friends, surrounded by live music, prayer, tears, laughter, and the kind of profound emotion that only he could inspire, and that his family would forever cherish the peaceful ease and reflection on his face.
  • In lieu of flowers. The family asked for memorial contributions to the Johns Hopkins Alzheimer's Disease Research Center Fund and the Alzheimer's Foundation of America Caregiver Resources.
  • A week of loss for Star Trek. Stewart died three days after Walter Koenig, who played Ensign Pavel Chekov on the original Star Trek series, died Sunday at 90 of old age at his home in Los Angeles.

A career on stage and screen

  • Yorkshire beginnings. Stewart was born in 1940 in Mirfield, in northern England, to a textile worker mother and a soldier father, and built his career as a classical stage actor with the Royal Shakespeare Company before television made him famous worldwide.
  • Knighted for drama. He was knighted in 2010 by Queen Elizabeth II for his contributions to drama.
  • Memoir. In 2023 he published the memoir "Making It So," reflecting on his life and career, including the painful end of his first marriage.

The Picard years

  • The Next Generation. Stewart played Captain Jean-Luc Picard in "Star Trek: The Next Generation," which ran from 1987 to 1994, leading the crew of the starship Enterprise through seven seasons.
  • The films. He returned to the role in feature films, including "Star Trek Generations" in 1994, where Picard met William Shatner's Captain James T. Kirk.
  • Professor X. To a second generation of fans he was Professor Charles Xavier, the telepathic mentor of the X-Men, a role he played across the franchise's films.
  • One more Picard run. His most recent turn as Picard came in the Paramount+ series "Star Trek: Picard," which ran from 2020 to 2023.
  • A Marvel return still to come. He had filmed a return as Professor X for "Avengers: Doomsday," due in theaters in December, according to reports, and Marvel Studios paid tribute to him on Thursday, calling him a singular talent, a generous collaborator and a true gentleman.

The family he leaves

  • Three marriages. Stewart was married three times: to actress and dancer Sheila Falconer in 1966, to television producer Wendy Neuss in 2000, and to singer Sunny Ozell in 2013.
  • Two children. He is survived by Ozell and by his two children from his first marriage, Daniel, born in 1967, and Sophie, born in 1972.
  • A recent anniversary. He and Ozell had celebrated their 13th wedding anniversary a month before he died.

Tributes

  • Ian McKellen. His longtime friend and collaborator Ian McKellen paid tribute on Instagram, reflecting on a friendship that began nearly half a century earlier when they were young actors with the Royal Shakespeare Company.
  • The Enterprise crew. William Shatner, LeVar Burton and Jonathan Frakes honored their Star Trek colleague, and X-Men co-stars including Hugh Jackman and Famke Janssen remembered him. Jackman wrote, "We lost one of the greats of stage and screen today."

Why it matters

  • Two franchises, two generations. Few actors ever anchor one beloved universe. Stewart anchored two, playing Picard for a generation that grew up on syndicated television and Professor X for a generation that grew up on superhero films, and he carried the same gravity into both.
  • A stage actor in genre roles. Stewart came to both parts as a Shakespearean, and his performances argued, without ever making the argument out loud, that a starship captain and a comic book mentor deserved the same seriousness as a king.

What to watch next

  • Doomsday in December. "Avengers: Doomsday" arrives in December with Stewart's filmed return as Professor X, giving audiences one more performance to see.
  • The memorial funds. The two funds his family named will carry his name into Alzheimer's research and caregiver support, the causes closest to the illness that took him.
