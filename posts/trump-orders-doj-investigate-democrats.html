Trump Orders Justice Department to Investigate Democrats Over Alleged Threats to Donors

By Jayesh Marathe · November 8, 2026 · 7 min read

President Donald Trump has ordered the Department of Justice to investigate Democratic lawmakers, accusing them of threatening administration officials and Republican donors with impeachment and imprisonment. Trump issued the order in two posts on Truth Social late on Saturday, calling the alleged threats extortion at the highest level and saying those responsible should go to jail.

The order

  • Trump issued the order in a late Saturday post. Trump wrote that Democrats are going around threatening a lot of people, including the Attorney General of the United States, with impeachment and many other forms of punishment, even though they admit they have no reason to do so and no impeachable acts have been committed.
  • He called it extortion at the highest level. "This is extortion at the highest level, and they should be held accountable for this," Trump wrote in the post, which appeared at 11:15 p.m. on Saturday.
  • He said donors face threats of imprisonment. Trump wrote that Democrats are threatening donors, some of the most respected people anywhere in the world, with imprisonment and many other levels of punishment just because they happen to be Republicans or Republican supporters.
  • He called it weaponization and lawfare. Trump described the alleged threats as weaponization and lawfare at another level, and said threatening letters have been sent to many innocent people from Democratic political leaders.
  • The posts name no letter and offer no evidence. Trump did not name any letter, say who sent it or show its contents, and the posts contain no evidence of a crime.
  • He ordered the department to act at once. "By copy of this statement, I am ordering the Department of Justice to look into this criminal act, immediately!" Trump wrote.

The second post

  • An hour later, Trump told donors to report the threats. In a second post just after midnight, Trump told Republican donors and party members to report the alleged threats to the Department of Justice immediately, or send them directly to him at the White House.
  • He said those responsible should go to jail. "These SLEAZEBAGS should go to jail for what they are doing," Trump wrote, calling the alleged threats absolutely unprecedented and totally illegal.
  • He called on Congress to act as well. "The Senate must act, and stop being pushed around to the point of the Republican Party losing Elections, and worse!" Trump wrote. "Likewise, the DOJ and House of Representatives."

Slotkin singled out

  • Trump named one Democrat in the first post. Trump wrote that Senator Elissa Slotkin of Michigan is saying she is going to go after all young people who believe in Republican values and give even the slightest inclination to be a Republican.
  • He said the imprisonment should fall on the other side. "The imprisonment should be against these EXTORTIONISTS, and Republicans should get smart, fast!" Trump wrote.
  • Slotkin answered the same day. Slotkin said anyone who breaks the law or uses taxpayer dollars to enrich themselves should be held accountable, adding, "If you didn't break the law, then it's all good."
  • She pointed to the failed grand jury case. "Mr. President, your threats of legal action and worse didn't work when you tried to criminally indict me in front of a grand jury last February," Slotkin wrote in her response.
  • She also criticized the Miller appointment. Slotkin criticized Trump's decision to name Stephen Miller as White House chief of staff, and said the administration should instead address the Iran war, tariffs and health care costs.
  • Trump appeared to be reacting to October remarks. CNBC reported that Trump appeared to be reacting to remarks Slotkin made on October 13 at the University of Chicago, where she said she wanted graduates weighing jobs in the Trump administration to understand it could bring them problems later, and that she wanted that deterrence message sent.
  • The two clashed before over an orders video. In November 2025, Slotkin and five other Democratic lawmakers released a video telling military personnel they could refuse unlawful orders. Trump called the video seditious behavior punishable by death, and a federal grand jury declined to indict the lawmakers in February 2026.

Why now

  • The posts came four days after the midterms. Democrats won control of both the House of Representatives and the Senate in the November 3 midterm elections, ending Republican control of Congress.
  • Democrats have signaled sweeping investigations. Democratic leaders have said the new majorities will investigate the administration once the new Congress takes office on January 3.
  • The same day brought another headline offer. Trump also offered the White House press secretary job to UFC chief Dana White on Saturday, in a separate Truth Social post.
  • Miller took the top staff job on Friday. Trump named Stephen Miller as White House chief of staff on Friday, so the order came one day after the biggest staffing change of the week.

What happens next

  • No investigation has been announced. CNBC reported that the Justice Department had not announced an investigation as of Saturday afternoon, and the department has not said whether it will open one.
  • It is unclear what a post can order. The order came in a social media post rather than a formal directive, and it remains unclear what action, if any, the department will take in response.
  • Donors may now send in reports. Trump asked donors and party members to report any alleged threats to the department or directly to him, so the department could receive complaints in the coming days.
  • The new Congress takes office on January 3. The standoff between the White House and the incoming Democratic majorities will sharpen when the new Congress is seated, with oversight hearings and investigations on both sides.
