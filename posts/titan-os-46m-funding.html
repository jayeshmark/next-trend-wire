Titan OS Raises $46 Million to Take Its Smart TV System Beyond Europe

By Jayesh Marathe · November 5, 2026 · 7 min read

Titan OS, the Barcelona company whose software powers the home screens of Philips, Sharp, JVC, and other television brands, has raised $46 million in new financing, the company announced on November 4, 2026. The round combines fresh equity from existing investors with a credit facility from funds managed by BlackRock, and it lifts Titan OS's total funding to $110 million.

The round

  • The raise. Titan OS announced a new $46 million financing round comprising equity provided by existing investors Highland Europe and Mangrove Capital Partners as well as a credit facility from funds and accounts managed by BlackRock. The company did not say how the $46 million splits between equity and debt.
  • The total. The round brings Titan OS's total funding to date to $110 million. The company was founded in 2023, so the total covers three years of fundraising.
  • The backers. Highland Europe and Mangrove Capital Partners return as the equity investors, while funds managed by BlackRock join as the credit provider. The release calls the backing a strong vote of confidence from the world's largest asset manager and two of Europe's leading growth investors.
  • The timing. The announcement landed on November 4, 2026, three years after Titan OS was founded, and it positions the company to expand beyond the European and Latin American markets where it already operates.

What Titan OS does

  • The product. Titan OS builds the operating system that runs on smart TVs, deciding what viewers see when they switch the set on and how they find shows, apps, and channels. Its software blends content, technology, and advertising from many providers into one home screen.
  • The brands. Titan OS has licensed its technology to major global TV brands including Philips, JVC, Vestel, Skyworth, and Sharp, reaching millions of viewers across Europe and Latin America. The company sells to manufacturers, not to viewers directly.
  • The business model. TV makers license the system and earn a share of the revenue their screens generate for years after the sale, through advertising and content partnerships, while advertisers reach millions of homes across many TV brands at once.
  • The independence pitch. Titan OS presents itself as the independent alternative to the big platform owners. Its argument is that manufacturers prefer a partner that does not compete with them for viewers, and that independence is what keeps new brands signing on.

The growth so far

  • The revenue climb. Operating for over three years, Titan OS has scaled revenues by 15 times. The company gives no revenue figure, so the multiple stands on its own.
  • The founders. Titan OS was founded in 2023 by Jacinto Roca, previously the founder of Wuaki.tv, which Rakuten acquired, and his co-founders Timothy Edwards, Miquel Barba, and Tobias Pfalzgraff. Roca's last company taught him the streaming business from the inside.
  • The team. Titan OS employs more than 200 people, headquartered in Barcelona. The company describes itself as a technology, entertainment, and advertising company, not just a software vendor.
  • The market it wants. The company's goal is to become a global player in a market now dominated by US and Asian companies, and the new money is meant to fund the push outside its home region.

What the money funds

  • The expansion. With the new round, Titan OS will strengthen its position as the leading independent TV operating system from Europe, expand its global partnerships with TV makers, and invest further in product innovation and advertising technology. The priority is more brands, in more countries.
  • The investor's view. "Since our first investment, we've watched the Titan team build a rare flywheel," said Laurence Garrett, General Partner at Highland Europe. "TV makers earn a share of the revenue their screens generate for years after the sale. Advertisers reach millions of homes across many TV brands. And unlike the walled gardens, Titan is a partner, not a competitor. That attracts more TV brands, which brings more viewers and more advertisers."
  • The company's view. "Three years after launch, Titan OS is already in millions of homes and continues to deliver strong year on year growth as Europe's leading independent operating system for connected TVs," said Hyoung-Ung Kim, CFO of Titan OS. "Our goal is clear: to become a true global player, built from Barcelona, in a market currently dominated by US and Asian companies."

Why this round matters

  • The BlackRock signal. A credit facility from BlackRock managed funds is not a venture bet. It is a lender's judgment that the business generates enough cash to service debt, and it arrives alongside fresh equity from the investors who already know the company best.
  • The independence test. Every new TV brand that picks Titan OS over a platform owned by a rival strengthens the pitch that independence wins. The money funds the sales push that proves it, brand by brand.
  • The next milestone. The company now has $110 million in total funding and a product in millions of homes. The next test is whether it can win manufacturers outside Europe and Latin America, where the biggest TV brands and the toughest competition live.
