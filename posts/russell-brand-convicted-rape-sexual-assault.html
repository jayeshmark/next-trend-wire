Russell Brand Found Guilty of Rape and Sexual Assault After Trial in London

By Editorial Team · 7 min read

A jury at Southwark Crown Court in London convicted comedian Russell Brand on November 5, 2026, of one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault. The verdicts ended a four week trial over offences against four women between 2004 and 2009, and the judge remanded Brand in custody ahead of sentencing on Monday, November 9.

The verdict

  • The convictions. Brand, 51, was found guilty of one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault following a four week trial at Southwark Crown Court, the Crown Prosecution Service said. The offences concerned four women and took place between 2004 and 2009, a period when Brand's career grew from stand up comedy into television and acting.
  • The acquittals. The jury cleared Brand of two further counts of rape and one count of attempted indecent assault, after deliberating for more than eight hours over two days. The trial had covered seven charges involving six women in total.
  • The custody decision. Judge Joel Bennathan refused Brand bail after the verdicts and sent him to the cells, telling him he faced a significant custodial term when he is sentenced on Monday. Brand had been free on bail throughout the trial.
  • The plea. Brand had pleaded not guilty to every charge and told the jury he had always had consensual sex, saying that although he had been promiscuous in the past, he had never forced himself on anyone. He gave evidence in his own defence during the trial.

What the prosecution argued

  • The pattern. Prosecutors told the jury that Brand used his growing celebrity status to create situations in which he could pursue women sexually regardless of what they wanted, and that his tactics varied from woman to woman while the abuse of power at the centre stayed the same.
  • The prosecutor's words. "Russell Brand was predatory and used his celebrity status to commit sexual crimes," said David Roberts, senior crown prosecutor in the Crown Prosecution Service London rape and serious sexual assault unit. "He was a manipulator with an insatiable desire to get what he wanted regardless of whether consent was given or not."
  • The abuse of power. Roberts said that although Brand's tactics varied, at the centre of every assault was an abuse of power and sexual greed, with a complete disregard for the impact of his actions on the women involved.
  • The behaviour evidence. The prosecution's opening described Brand as someone who was childlike in his expectation that his every desire should be met, and it drew on witness testimony to show how he used his increasing power and fame to manipulate situations in order to carry out his crimes.
  • The friend's account. One of Brand's own friends told the court that Brand described himself as a womaniser and an expert pick up artist who had wanted to bed 5,000 women in his lifetime.
  • The suspect centred case. Specialist lawyers in the Crown Prosecution Service London rape and serious sexual offences unit built the case around Brand's actions and behaviour rather than assumptions about how his victims should have acted or responded, an approach that sits under Operation Soteria, the national initiative that shifts the focus in rape cases from the victim onto the perpetrator.

How the case reached court

  • The 2023 programme. The women did not report the offences to police at the time, which the Crown Prosecution Service said is not unusual. A television programme about Brand broadcast in September 2023 prompted them, independently and for their own reasons, to come forward.
  • The investigation. The joint investigation by The Times, The Sunday Times and Channel 4's Dispatches programme published allegations from several women in September 2023, and police began investigating Brand that month. He was interviewed by detectives on four occasions between 2023 and 2025.
  • The charges. The Crown Prosecution Service authorised charges against Brand in April 2025, covering five counts of rape and sexual assault against four women, and added a further count each of rape and sexual assault in December 2025, bringing the total to seven charges.
  • The consent message. The Crown Prosecution Service said the case sends a clear message that fame and status do not change the fundamental issue of consent, and that it hopes the convictions give other victims the confidence to come forward when they are ready, no matter who the offender is or how long ago the offence took place.

What happens next

  • The sentencing date. Brand will be sentenced on Monday, November 9, at Southwark Crown Court, when the judge will decide the length of the custodial term. The judge has already warned that the sentence will be significant.
  • The custody status. Brand remains in custody until sentencing, after the judge rejected his bail application because of the possibility that he might flee.
  • The further investigations. London's Metropolitan Police said it is still investigating rape allegations made by two other women, separate from the seven charges decided by the jury. Brand has previously been interviewed under caution about both complaints, and files have been submitted to the Crown Prosecution Service while detectives continue their enquiries.
  • The police statement. Detective Superintendent Andy Furphy of the Metropolitan Police said Brand had used his influence, status and fame to exploit and sexually offend against women, and that he hoped the conviction sends a clear message that no one is above the law, no matter how famous or powerful they may be.
  • The wider fallout. Brand, once one of Britain's most high profile broadcasters and a star of several Hollywood films, has in recent years remade himself as a conservative religious online commentator with a large social media following. The sentencing on Monday will determine how long he spends in prison.
