Sentra Raises $150 Million to Police What AI Agents Can Reach in Corporate Data

By Editorial Team · 7 min read

Sentra, a data security startup built for the age of AI agents, has raised $150 million in a Series C round led by Capital One Ventures. The company says the money will expand software that maps and limits the sensitive corporate data that employees, apps and AI agents can touch.

What happened

  • Capital One Ventures led the round. Sentra announced on November 4 that Capital One Ventures led a $150 million strategic investment, with existing backers Bessemer Venture Partners, Zeev Ventures, Standard Investments, Munich Re Ventures and Key1 Capital also taking part.
  • A bank's venture arm is now on the board. A Capital One representative will join Sentra's board of directors as part of the investment, a sign of how seriously a major bank takes the problem of agents reaching into its own data.
  • The round follows rapid growth. Sentra said the investment follows a year of more than 300 percent year over year growth, as enterprises turn to the company to understand their data and control which identities, human and AI, can access it.
  • Total funding passed $250 million. The new round brings Sentra's total raised to more than $250 million since its founding in 2021, and the company did not disclose the valuation at which the investment was made.
  • The company is still small. Sentra employs about 130 people, split between New York and Tel Aviv, and the new money will fund product work and an expansion of its sales and marketing teams.

What Sentra does

  • It guards data at rest. Sentra's platform finds and classifies sensitive information where it sits, across cloud services, business apps, data warehouses, office file shares and the knowledge stores that AI tools draw from.
  • It maps who can reach what. The software builds a map of the people, service accounts and AI agents able to open each dataset, including access an agent has inherited from elsewhere in the system.
  • It enforces least privilege. Sentra continuously trims access down to the minimum each identity needs, for human and non human identities alike, and strips risky permissions automatically when an agent reaches for unusual data.
  • It watches agents in real time. The platform gives companies visibility into the agents, copilots and models touching their sensitive data, and can contain an agent quickly when it behaves unexpectedly.
  • It was built for the agent era. Sentra argues that each AI agent is a new identity, accessing data continuously and at machine speed, with no central control plane, and that old data security tools were never designed for that.

Why investors are paying up

  • Agents are the busiest data consumers. AI copilots and autonomous agents now pull corporate data at a scale and speed no human team matches, and every new agent is another door into the company's most sensitive files.
  • Regulation is catching up. Companies adopting AI quickly still need to stay governed and compliant, and a tool that shows exactly what each agent touched is becoming a requirement rather than a luxury.
  • The founders know the problem. Sentra was founded in 2021 by chief executive Yoav Regev, president Asaf Kochan, chief technology officer Ron Reiter and vice president of product Yair Cohen, with Regev and Kochan both previously holding senior roles in Israel's Unit 8200.
  • A bank led the round for a reason. Capital One runs one of the largest data operations in American banking, so its venture arm backing a data security company is both an investment and a signal about where financial services sees the risk.
  • The timing fits the market. The funding landed the same week CrowdStrike agreed to buy Fiddler AI, another startup policing AI systems, showing that the biggest security companies are racing to own the agent problem.

What to watch next

  • How the money gets spent. Whether Sentra's expansion into agent visibility and rapid containment wins large enterprise deals, or whether bigger security platforms build the same features themselves.
  • The valuation question. Whether Sentra's next round pushes it past a $1 billion valuation, after its chief executive said the valuation has already tripled since the previous funding round.
  • The competition. How CrowdStrike, Palo Alto Networks and other incumbents respond as agent security becomes one of the hottest corners of cybersecurity.
  • Customer proof. Whether Sentra can show that its growth comes from paying enterprise customers rather than pilot projects, as AI budgets come under closer scrutiny.
  • The next funding cycle. Whether the $150 million buys Sentra a clear run at the market, or whether the agent security space consolidates around a handful of winners first.
