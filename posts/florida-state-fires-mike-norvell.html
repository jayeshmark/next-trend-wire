Florida State Fires Mike Norvell After Clemson Loss, Taking On a Buyout Near $50 Million

By Jayesh Marathe · November 2, 2026 · 7 min read

Florida State fired head football coach Mike Norvell on Sunday, November 1, one day after a 36-24 home loss to Clemson dropped the Seminoles to 3-5 on the season. The decision ends Norvell's tenure after more than six seasons in Tallahassee, with former Seminole quarterback Rick Stockstill named interim head coach and a national search for a successor already underway.

What happened

  • The decision. Florida State president Richard McCullough informed Norvell on Sunday afternoon that the university was making a change in the leadership of its football program, effective immediately, roughly 12 hours after the loss to Clemson.
  • The statement. McCullough said in a statement: "We have made the decision to change the leadership of our football program. We thank Mike Norvell for his commitment and service to Florida State and wish him and his family the very best."
  • The interim coach. Stockstill, a Florida State Athletics Hall of Fame member who played quarterback for the Seminoles and served as the team's director of scouting, will lead the team for the rest of the season.
  • The wider change. Florida State also dismissed defensive coordinator Tony White, making Sunday a full reset of the program's on-field leadership rather than a single change at the top.
  • The next game. The Seminoles play next on Saturday, November 7, on the road at Boston College, giving Stockstill less than a week to prepare the team for his first game in charge.
  • The search. McCullough said Florida State will conduct a thorough national search for its next head coach, someone who embraces the program's tradition of championship athletics and its standard of excellence.

The buyout

  • The price of the change. Reports put Norvell's buyout at roughly $50 million, which would rank among the largest buyouts in college football history, behind only Jimbo Fisher's payout from Texas A&M and Brian Kelly's payout from LSU.
  • The contract terms. Under the contract Norvell signed after the 2023 season, he was owed 85 percent of his remaining salary through the end of a deal that runs through the 2031 season, and he had been scheduled to make $10.3 million in 2026.
  • The mitigation clause. The contract includes a duty to mitigate, which means any salary Norvell earns in a future coaching job would reduce what Florida State owes him, the same kind of clause that sharply reduced Penn State's payout to James Franklin after he took the Virginia Tech job.
  • The full cost. Buying out Norvell's assistants and covering transition costs would push the total cost of the coaching change well past the head coach's buyout alone, which is why the decision required approval at the highest levels of the university.
  • The timing. Florida State acted now, with five games still to play, rather than waiting until the end of the season, a sign that the university's leadership had decided the program needed a new voice immediately.

The slide that ended it

  • The peak. Norvell led Florida State to a 13-1 season in 2023 and an Atlantic Coast Conference championship, a run that earned him a major contract extension and made him one of the highest paid coaches in college football.
  • The record. Norvell finished at Florida State with a 41-39 overall record and a 23-30 record in ACC play, numbers that show a program that never sustained the 2023 level after that season.
  • The 2026 collapse. The Seminoles fell to 3-5 overall and 1-4 in ACC play in 2026, with the loss to Clemson marking their 17th defeat in their past 21 conference games.
  • The fan pressure. Before the Clemson game, a plane flew over Doak Campbell Stadium carrying a banner calling for Norvell's firing, a public sign of how far fan patience had fallen during the slide.
  • The leadership vacuum. Florida State had already fired athletic director Michael Alford in mid-September, which meant the decision to fire Norvell was made without a permanent athletic director in place, with McCullough and interim leadership authorizing the buyout.
  • The patience that ran out. McCullough had publicly backed Norvell through earlier struggles, but the combination of on field regression, falling home attendance, and donor pressure left the university's leadership convinced that continuing with Norvell would cost more than the buyout.

What comes next

  • The search begins now. Florida State said its search for a permanent head coach will commence immediately, and the job is expected to attract major candidates because of the program's history, its three national championships, and its place in the ACC.
  • The interim test. Stockstill, who spent 18 seasons as Middle Tennessee's head coach before joining Florida State's staff, gets his first audition on Saturday at Boston College, where a win would steady a team that has lost its way.
  • The money question. The buyout will hang over the search, since Florida State must pay Norvell while also funding a new coach's salary and staff, a financial squeeze that will shape how aggressive the university can be in the market.
  • Norvell's future. Norvell, who built his reputation at Memphis before arriving in Tallahassee in December 2019, now enters the coaching market with a mitigation clause that gives him a strong incentive to take another job quickly, which would also reduce Florida State's bill.
