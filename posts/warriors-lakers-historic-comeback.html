Down 25, the Warriors Beat the Lakers 112-95 and Made NBA History

By Editorial Team · 7 min read

Stephen Curry scored 31 points and the Golden State Warriors erased a 25 point deficit to beat the Los Angeles Lakers 112-95 on October 30 in their NBA Cup opener at Chase Center. The comeback set a shot clock era record, and Curry passed Dominique Wilkins for 18th place on the NBA's all time scoring list along the way.

The hole

  • The Lakers' start. Luka Doncic scored 21 points in the first quarter, shooting 7 for 12 with five three pointers, as Los Angeles raced to a 38-15 lead after one quarter and stretched it to 40-15 early in the second.
  • The mirror quarter. Golden State answered with a 38-15 second quarter of its own, tying the game 53-53 by halftime, which made the Warriors the first team in the shot clock era, including the playoffs, to trail by 23 or more points after the first quarter and be tied or better at the break.
  • The record. The 17 point final margin also set a shot clock era record for a team that trailed by 20 or more points after the first quarter, topping the previous best from March 4, 1976, when Golden State trailed Chicago 41-18 after one quarter and won by 14.

The comeback

  • Curry led it. Curry finished with 31 points, six assists and four rebounds, shooting 11 for 23 from the field and 8 for 14 from three point range, including a third quarter stepback three over Doncic after a crossover that sent Doncic to the floor.
  • The bench decided it. Golden State's reserves outscored the Lakers' bench 50-7, with Brandin Podziemski scoring 21 points with six assists and five rebounds, De'Anthony Melton adding 16 off the bench and Georges Niang 11.
  • The quarter by quarter finish. Golden State won the third quarter 32-27 and the fourth 27-15, turning a game the Lakers led by 25 into a 112-95 win, and avenging a 127-105 loss to Los Angeles in the season opener on October 21.
  • Doncic's night, in defeat. Doncic led all scorers with 37 points, adding seven rebounds and five assists, but the Lakers scored only 15 points in both the second and fourth quarters as the comeback took hold.

Curry's milestone

  • The pass. In the fourth quarter, Curry passed Dominique Wilkins, who scored 26,668 career points, for 18th place on the NBA's all time scoring list, reaching the mark with a three pointer.
  • The form. The performance followed a 44 point game at Minnesota, giving Curry back to back statement nights as Golden State won its third straight game to reach 3-3, while the Lakers fell to 4-2.

What it means

  • The Cup race. The win opened Golden State's NBA Cup group play at 1-0 and dropped Los Angeles to 0-1, with every group game counting toward the knockout rounds.
  • The lesson. The Lakers led for most of the night and still lost by 17, a reminder that a 25 point lead built on one quarter's shooting rarely survives a bench gap of 50-7.
  • What comes next. Both teams play again Sunday, with the Lakers hosting Toronto and the Warriors hosting the Clippers, and Golden State will try to stretch its winning streak to four.
