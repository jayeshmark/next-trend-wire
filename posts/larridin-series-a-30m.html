Larridin Raises $30 Million to Show Companies What Their AI Spending Actually Produces

By Editorial Team · 7 min read

Larridin, a South San Francisco startup that measures the business value of enterprise AI, has raised $30 million in Series A funding led by Pelion Ventures. The round brings the company's total funding to $47 million as businesses demand proof that their AI budgets are producing real work.

The funding round

  • The raise. Larridin announced on November 2 that it has raised $30 million in Series A funding, bringing its total funding to $47 million.
  • The lead investor. Pelion Ventures led the round, joined by existing investors Andreessen Horowitz and Gradient Ventures.
  • The new backers. GFT, W23, Thayer, DG Daiwa Ventures, Alumni Ventures and Cougar Capital joined as new investors in the round.
  • The use of funds. Larridin said it will accelerate product development and expand its teams to build out what it calls the industry's most complete AI intelligence platform.

What Larridin measures

  • The problem. Larridin is an enterprise wide AI measurement and intelligence platform that connects AI adoption, productivity and spending data to the work of people and agents across a business.
  • The pitch. The platform helps leaders see where AI is delivering value, where to invest next and which workflows to automate, moving the conversation from who uses AI to what AI produces.
  • The scale so far. Larridin said it has measured more than 2 billion AI sessions and tracked more than 82,000 workflows end to end.
  • The reported results. Across its customer base, Larridin reports a 47 percent average increase in measurable team output and a median $2.4 million in AI spend optimized within the first year.
  • The spending gaps it finds. At one customer, 92 percent of engineers were active AI users, yet a single engineer accounted for roughly 65 percent of the team's entire AI spend in one week, and at another, Larridin found a 66 fold difference in cost per shipped engineering change between two AI tools doing comparable work.

Why investors backed it

  • The accountability shift. "Enterprise AI has moved quickly from innovation budget to financial accountability, and our investors share our conviction that companies must be able to prove tangible business value from skyrocketing AI spend," said Russ Fradin, CEO of Larridin.
  • The investor case. Jeff Kearl, General Partner at Pelion Ventures, said Larridin fills a vital gap for enterprise leaders by delivering real transparency into AI and tying every dollar spent directly to measurable business outcomes.
  • The customer base. Larridin customers include SurveyMonkey, Vertiv, Klaviyo, Gainsight, Globality, TigerConnect, EcoVadis and Sundt, spanning a broad range of industries.
  • The founders. CEO Russ Fradin and President Jim Larrison built the foundation for digital measurement at Comscore, while CTO Ameya Kanitkar built enterprise technical foundations and AI infrastructure at LinkedIn, Coinbase and Groupon.

The report behind the raise

  • The new benchmark. Alongside the funding, Larridin introduced its quarterly Engineering AI Impact Report, starting with the Q3 2026 edition, to give enterprises ongoing data on AI adoption, productivity and return on investment.
  • The data set. The report draws on proprietary data from more than 1,000 engineers across Larridin's customer base, tracking how AI changed engineering output, spending, code quality and team structures from April to September 2026.
  • The headline finding. AI assisted pull requests surged from 35 percent of merged code changes in April to 62 percent in September, while median wait times for initial human code reviews increased by 50 percent over the same period.
  • The bottleneck. The findings show that faster code creation now waits on slower human review, which is why Larridin argues companies must measure cost, quality and efficiency of AI work, not just how much work AI helps produce.
  • The company. Founded in 2024 in South San Francisco, California, Larridin helps enterprises measure, manage and optimize AI adoption, performance and spend across teams, workflows and tools.
