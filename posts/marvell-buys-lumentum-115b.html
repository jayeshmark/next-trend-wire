Marvell to Buy Lumentum in a $115 Billion All-Stock Bet on Optical AI Connections

By Editorial Team · 7 min read

Marvell Technology has agreed to acquire optical components maker Lumentum in an all-stock deal worth an implied enterprise value of $115 billion, the two companies announced November 2. The combination joins Marvell's chip and electrical interconnect business with Lumentum's lasers and optical parts, betting that the wiring inside AI data centers is shifting from copper to light.

What the companies agreed

  • The price. Lumentum stockholders will receive a fixed exchange ratio of 4.500 shares of Marvell common stock for each share of Lumentum common stock they own, with the transaction carrying an implied enterprise value of $115 billion for Lumentum, or approximately $1,132 per Lumentum share.
  • The premium. The per-share value represents a premium of approximately 23.9 percent based on each company's unaffected closing share price as of October 28, 2026, which the companies describe as the last trading day before market speculation about a potential transaction began.
  • The ownership split. Current Marvell stockholders will own approximately 67 percent of the combined company on a fully diluted basis after closing, while Lumentum stockholders will own approximately 33 percent.
  • The board votes. Both companies' boards of directors unanimously approved the agreement, and the deal is structured as a definitive agreement rather than a preliminary understanding, which means the terms are set and the remaining steps are approvals and closing conditions.
  • The timeline. The transaction is expected to close in the first half of calendar year 2028, subject to approval by Marvell and Lumentum stockholders, receipt of regulatory approvals, and other customary closing conditions, so the two companies will keep operating separately until then.
  • The leadership. Matt Murphy will continue as chairman and chief executive of the combined company, while three members of Lumentum's board, including Lumentum chief executive Michael Hurlston, will join Marvell's board, which currently consists of seven directors.

Why optical, and why now

  • The copper problem. Rapidly growing bandwidth requirements and the physical limitations of copper-based connectivity are accelerating the transition toward optical technologies, the companies said, because AI infrastructure keeps scaling to more complex architectures that copper connections struggle to serve.
  • The architectural shift. As AI infrastructure scales, particularly in what the industry calls scale-up and scale-in designs, the shift runs from discrete, modular components toward highly integrated optical solutions that require much closer co-design of electrical and optical technologies to deliver greater bandwidth, performance, and energy efficiency.
  • The once-in-a-generation framing. Marvell chairman and chief executive Matt Murphy said in the announcement: "AI infrastructure is undergoing a fundamental architectural shift toward optical connectivity, creating an opportunity that comes along once in a generation," adding that the companies that move first and help define the transition will set the standard for decades to come.
  • The market prize. The combination is expected to expand the combined company's total addressable market to $550 billion, and Hurlston said joining Marvell creates a company uniquely positioned to capitalize on a market opportunity of that size and generate significant, sustainable stockholder value.
  • The customer need. Surging AI data traffic requires rapid hardware innovation, and the combination enhances the companies' ability to deliver next-generation solutions at scale, meeting customers' complete interconnect needs from today's scale-across and scale-out systems to tomorrow's scale-up and scale-in domains.

What each side brings

  • Marvell's side. The combination brings Marvell's strengths in electrical and optical interconnect, network switching, custom XPU and XPU attach, and advanced semiconductor technologies, the part of the stack that moves data electrically through chips and switches inside AI systems.
  • Lumentum's side. Lumentum contributes expertise in high-performance lasers, optical transceivers, optical circuit switching, and specialized compound semiconductor manufacturing, the part that moves data as light, which the companies say delivers greater bandwidth, performance, and energy efficiency than copper-based connectivity as AI systems scale.
  • The manufacturing edge. Lumentum brings advanced compound semiconductor manufacturing and process technologies, including indium phosphide, as well as high-speed and high-power laser capabilities, and after closing the combined company plans to bring together leading-edge semiconductor design, advanced packaging, and specialized manufacturing for deeper heterogeneous integration.
  • The combined portfolio. Following closing, the combined company is expected to offer a highly complementary portfolio spanning leading-edge CMOS, silicon photonics, compound semiconductors, advanced packaging, and manufacturing capabilities, covering connections from distributed data centers down to links within advanced semiconductor packages.
  • The co-design pitch. By bringing together complementary technologies and engineering expertise across the signal chain, the combined company expects to develop highly optimized, pre-validated optical solutions designed to improve bandwidth, performance, and power efficiency, accelerating customer time to market and supporting high-volume production.

The financial case the companies make

  • The synergies. The transaction is expected to provide line of sight to more than $1 billion in annual run-rate cost synergies, the savings the combined company expects to reach on a yearly basis once the two operations are integrated.
  • The earnings claim. The deal is expected to be accretive to Marvell's non-GAAP earnings per share in year one after closing, meaning it should add to, rather than dilute, Marvell's adjusted per-share profit in its first full year as a combined company.
  • The margin case. The transaction is expected to enhance the combined company's long-term revenue growth, non-GAAP gross margin, and non-GAAP operating margin profile.
  • The category claim. Combining Marvell's electrical and optical interconnect, switching, and custom silicon technologies with Lumentum's lasers, optical transceivers, and optical circuit switching will create a comprehensive connectivity portfolio spanning scale-across, scale-out, scale-up, and scale-in, positioning the combined company to address customer connectivity requirements across a broad range of distances and topologies.
  • The revenue update alongside the deal. Marvell also reported preliminary revenue for the third quarter of fiscal 2027 consistent with previously provided guidance, maintaining the midpoint of its prior guidance while narrowing the expected range to plus or minus 2 percent, compared with the original range of plus or minus 5 percent, and Lumentum separately reported its financial results for the first quarter of fiscal 2027.

What the combined company will sell

  • End-to-end co-design. The combination is expected to create a broad full-stack connectivity portfolio intended to enable end-to-end extreme co-design, high-volume production of fully optimized, pre-validated optical solutions, and faster time to market for customers, meaning one company designs the electrical and optical pieces together instead of customers stitching them together from separate suppliers.
  • A portfolio across distances. The combination is intended to enable deeper integration and co-design across the electrical and optical signal chain, positioning Marvell to deliver highly optimized connectivity solutions across a wide range of distances, from distributed data centers to connections within advanced semiconductor packages.
  • What Lumentum is. Lumentum is a global leader in optical and photonic technologies that power the networks and infrastructure behind AI, cloud computing, and next-generation communications, delivering high-performance lasers, modules, and optical subsystems that enable scalable, energy-efficient data center connectivity, advanced telecom networks, industrial manufacturing, and sensing applications.
  • What Marvell is. Marvell builds semiconductor solutions that move, store, process, and secure the world's data, trusted by the world's leading technology companies for over 30 years, and the deal extends that data infrastructure franchise from electrical connections into the optical layer that AI's growth now demands.

What has to happen before it closes

  • The stockholder votes. The deal requires approval by both Marvell and Lumentum stockholders, and Marvell intends to file a registration statement with the Securities and Exchange Commission that will include a joint proxy statement and prospectus for those votes.
  • The regulatory review. The transaction also requires receipt of regulatory approvals and other customary closing conditions.
  • The advisors. Qatalyst Partners is serving as lead financial advisor and J.P. Morgan Securities and Goldman Sachs are serving as financial advisors to Marvell, with Wilson Sonsini Goodrich and Rosati as legal advisor, while Citigroup Global Markets and Evercore are advising Lumentum financially and Davis Polk and Wardwell is serving as its legal advisor.
  • The test to watch. The announcement answers the strategy question, but the deal itself is the test, and the next markers are the proxy filing, the two stockholder votes, and the regulatory verdicts that will decide whether a $115 billion optical bet closes in the first half of 2028 as planned.
