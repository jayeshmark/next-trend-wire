FBI Arrests Thomas Markle, Meghan's Father, on Child Sexual Abuse Material Charge

By Editorial Team · 7 min read

The FBI has arrested Thomas Wayne Markle, the estranged father of Meghan, Duchess of Sussex, on a federal charge of possessing child sexual abuse material. Agents took the 82-year-old into custody at his senior living home in Lakeland, Florida, on Friday.

The arrest

  • Agents arrested Markle on Friday. FBI agents took Markle into custody at the Maravida Senior Living facility in North Lakeland, Florida, on Friday, November 6.
  • He faces one federal count. Markle is charged with one count of possession of child pornography, the Justice Department said in a statement on Friday.
  • He appeared in court the same day. Markle made his initial appearance in federal court in Tampa on Friday, and a magistrate judge ordered him released on house arrest.
  • His travel is restricted. As a condition of his release, Markle may not travel outside the Middle District of Florida and the Central District of California, and he must have no contact with minors.
  • He faces up to 20 years. If convicted, Markle faces a statutory maximum sentence of 20 years in federal prison.
  • He has denied the allegations. Markle has denied the accusations, and he has yet to enter a plea to the charge.

What prosecutors allege

  • The material was in storage units. Prosecutors allege Markle kept child sexual abuse material in two Los Angeles-area storage units he rented for nearly two decades.
  • He rented the units since 2008. Markle has rented both units since 2008, and his most recent payments on both were made last month by auto-pay, according to the Justice Department.
  • Agents seized about 78 items. FBI agents searched the units under court authorized warrants and seized approximately 78 items, including slides, film negatives and books containing suspected abuse material.
  • The material includes photographs of Markle. Prosecutors say the material includes photographs depicting Markle with minor girls, according to the department's statement.
  • A witness identified him. The affidavit filed with the complaint says a witness identified Markle as the adult in some of the photographs.
  • The complaint is only an accusation. A criminal complaint contains allegations, and every defendant is presumed innocent until proved guilty beyond a reasonable doubt, the Justice Department said.

How the case surfaced

  • A tip came from the Philippines. In October 2026, law enforcement learned that an individual had signed an affidavit about the discovery of suspected abuse material among Markle's possessions in the Philippines.
  • His son raised the alarm. Markle's son, Thomas Markle Jr., reported to authorities in Cebu City in the Philippines that he had found a stash of suspected abuse material belonging to his father.
  • A second tip led to the units. Law enforcement also interviewed another person who said Markle rented a storage unit in Los Angeles, and information from the first person later led agents to a second unit.
  • The FBI moved in last week. FBI agents executed search warrants at the two storage units last week, seizing the material before arresting Markle in Florida on Friday.
  • Meghan said she was distressed. Meghan said last month she was deeply distressed by the accusations, and she had already blocked contact between her father and her children over earlier troubling allegations.

What the FBI and prosecutors said

  • The US attorney called the allegations extremely serious. First Assistant US Attorney Bill Essayli said Markle is accused of possessing child sexual abuse material, including photographs depicting himself with minor girls, and added that the allegations are extremely serious and the government will prosecute Markle to the fullest extent of federal law.
  • The FBI director spoke about the arrest. FBI Director Kash Patel said the FBI and its partners arrested Markle after an investigation revealed he was in possession of child sexual abuse material, and he thanked FBI offices in Los Angeles and Tampa and law enforcement attaches in Mexico, Manila and the Marshall Islands.
  • The FBI urged the public to speak up. Patrick Grandy, the assistant director in charge of the FBI's Los Angeles field office, said the case shows why family members and friends should speak up if they suspect someone is preying on children.
  • Three prosecutors will handle the case. Assistant US Attorneys Daniel H. Weiner, Kevin J. Butler and Kathy Yu are prosecuting the case for the Central District of California.

Who Thomas Markle is

  • He is Meghan's estranged father. Thomas Wayne Markle, 82, is the father of Meghan, Duchess of Sussex, the wife of Prince Harry, and the two have been estranged for years.
  • He worked in television. Markle worked as a television lighting director before retiring.
  • He lived in the Philippines. Markle moved to Cebu in the Philippines in 2025 and remained there until May, when he returned to the United States for medical care.
  • He suffered a stroke this year. Markle suffered a stroke earlier this year and had his left leg amputated.
  • He has been in a Florida care home. Markle has been recovering at a rehabilitation center and senior living facility in Lakeland, Florida, where FBI agents arrested him.

What happens next

  • The FBI investigation continues. The FBI said it is still investigating the matter, and the case will proceed in federal court in California, where the charge was filed.
  • Markle has yet to enter a plea. Markle has not yet entered a plea to the charge, and his next court dates have not been announced.
  • The charge carries a long sentence. A conviction on the single possession count carries a statutory maximum of 20 years in federal prison.
  • The case turns on the seized material. Prosecutors will need to prove the material in the storage units belonged to Markle and meets the legal definition of child sexual abuse material, while the defense will have the chance to challenge the evidence.
