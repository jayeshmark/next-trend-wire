Nvidia Board Prepares to Oust Huang After Second Affair Report

By Jayesh Marathe · November 12, 2026 · 7 min read

The New York Times reported on Wednesday that Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang had an alleged extramarital affair with Kimberly Powell, the company's vice president of healthcare and one of his direct reports. Minutes later, the Wall Street Journal reported that Nvidia's board is preparing to oust Huang. Nvidia shares fell more than 10 percent in after hours trading.

The report about Kimberly Powell

  • Five sources. The Times said five people who worked at Nvidia told the paper Huang had an extramarital relationship with Powell, and that the relationship had not been reported before.
  • The video. Two of the sources said Huang and Powell were filmed being affectionate at a hotel in San Jose, California, during Nvidia's developer conference in March. The Times said it has not seen the video.
  • Who she is. Powell, 48, joined Nvidia in 2008 and has served as its vice president of healthcare since 2017. She reports directly to Huang. Both Huang, 63, and Powell are married to other people.
  • No confirmation. Neither Huang nor Powell has publicly confirmed the alleged relationship, and Nvidia declined to comment on the report.
  • The phone call. When a Times reporter reached Huang by phone and asked about his relationship with Powell, Huang said, "I don't think you should be talking to me directly," and hung up, the paper reported.

The board moves toward an exit

  • The Journal report. The Wall Street Journal reported on Wednesday that Nvidia's board is preparing to oust Huang and is preparing to meet as soon as Wednesday to determine his future.
  • The investigation. The board opened an independent investigation on November 8, led by the law firm Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen and Katz, after reports that Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence effort.
  • The board's position. In its November 8 statement, the board said it has no reason to believe national security or Nvidia secrets were compromised, and that a committee of independent directors will oversee the inquiry with outside independent counsel.
  • Huang's standing. The board said Huang fully supports the review and has committed to cooperating with it. A company spokesperson said the news will not slow Nvidia and that Huang is committed to continuing to lead the company as chief executive without interruption.
  • The market verdict. Nvidia shares fell more than 10 percent in after hours trading on Wednesday after the two reports landed within minutes of each other, according to market reports.

What Huang admitted on Sunday

  • The message. In a message to employees published on Nvidia's newsroom on November 8, Huang wrote, "I was unfaithful to my wife. My actions hurt her and our children. There is no excuse for what I did. I am deeply sorry."
  • Full responsibility. Huang said he takes full responsibility for what he did and the hurt he caused, accepts the criticism that comes with it, and told colleagues, the board, partners and shareholders that "you deserve better from me."
  • The security question. Huang wrote that the board has initiated an independent investigation, that he supports it and will cooperate fully, and that he is committed to protecting Nvidia and supporting America's national security.
  • What he did not say. The message names no one, does not mention any other person, and does not use the word spy. It asks for privacy for his family, saying his family bears no responsibility for his choices.

The spy allegation behind the probe

  • The October report. Reports in late October said US intelligence agencies had assessed Alice Huang, a former executive at the Singapore based data center company Megaspeed who is also known as Huang Le, as an asset gathering information for Beijing, and that she had developed a close relationship with Jensen Huang. The two are not related.
  • The November report. A further report on November 6 said Huang was believed to have been in a romantic relationship with Alice Huang. Huang told Bloomberg in late October that his dealings with her were connected to Megaspeed and "completely normal and not unusual."
  • Unproven claims. The intelligence claims remain unproven in public. Alice Huang has not been charged with any crime, and China's government has rejected the claims. Singapore's home affairs ministry said on October 31 that its authorities had not investigated her over the alleged intelligence connections or the reported relationship.
  • The Justice Department. The Justice Department has since taken over the federal investigation into the company, the Times reported.

Congress and the market

  • The letter. Three Republican House committee chairs wrote to Huang on November 4, asking him to appear for a joint closed door briefing on November 18 about Nvidia's security protocols and his interactions with Alice Huang, whom the letter calls "a publicly reported People's Republic of China (PRC) intelligence asset."
  • The signatories. The letter was signed by Foreign Affairs Chairman Brian Mast, Select Committee on China Chairman John Moolenaar, and Intelligence Chairman Rick Crawford, and it asks Huang to accept the November 18 date or propose an alternative session day by November 11.
  • A request, not a summons. The letter asks for a voluntary briefing. It is not a subpoena, and Nvidia has not said whether Huang will attend.
  • Earnings next week. Nvidia reports third quarter results on November 17, the day before the requested briefing, in its first earnings call since the reports began.

What happens next

  • The board meeting. The board is preparing to meet as soon as Wednesday to determine Huang's future, according to the Journal report. No decision has been announced.
  • November 17. Nvidia reports third quarter results, when analysts are expected to press the company on the investigation and the board's plans.
  • November 18. The closed door briefing is requested for November 18 in the Rayburn House Office Building, if Huang accepts.
  • The open questions. The board has given no timeline for its investigation and said only that it will provide further updates as appropriate. What the investigation finds, and whether Congress hears from Huang, will decide how long this stays a governance story rather than a security one.
