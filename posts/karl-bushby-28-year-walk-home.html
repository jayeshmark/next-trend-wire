Karl Bushby Walks Home After 28 Years and About 31,000 Miles

By Editorial Team · 7 min read

Karl Bushby walked up to his mother's front door in Hull, England, on Monday, November 2, 2026, and finished a walk he started in Chile 28 years earlier. The 57-year-old former paratrooper covered about 31,000 miles across 30 countries without using motorized transport to move forward, and crowds welcomed him home.

The homecoming

  • The finish. Bushby walked up to his mother's front door in Hull on November 2, 2026, completing one of the longest unbroken journeys on foot ever attempted and closing the Goliath Expedition he began in 1998.
  • The welcome. Crowds gathered in Hull city center before Bushby walked the final stretch home, and his mother, Angela, and his son, Adam, welcomed him at the house where he grew up.
  • The family reunion. Bushby met his granddaughter in person for the first time at the homecoming, after nearly three decades away from the home he left as a young man.
  • The cart. Bushby arrived pushing the handcart that carried his supplies along the route, a cart he called The Beast, which reached its final destination with him in Hull.

The two rules

  • No motorized transport. Bushby set two rules for the journey: he could never use transport to move forward on his route, and he could not return home until he arrived on foot.
  • No shortcuts across water. The rules meant that water crossings had to be swum rather than sailed or flown, which shaped the hardest legs of the route.
  • A longer road than planned. Bushby once expected the expedition to take about 12 years, but border problems, funding setbacks, and the pandemic stretched it to 28 years.

The route

  • The start. Bushby left Punta Arenas, Chile, on November 1, 1998, at age 29, carrying a tent, a few hundred dollars, and a cheap plastic camera, and turned north toward home.
  • The Americas. He walked the length of the Americas, hacking through the Darien Gap between Colombia and Panama, where he spent 18 days in jail in 2001 after crossing a restricted border zone.
  • The frozen crossing. In 2006, Bushby crossed the frozen Bering Strait on foot from Alaska into Siberia, and he was detained by Russian authorities for entering the country illegally.
  • Asia and Europe. He continued through Central Asia and Europe, swimming the Caspian Sea in stages rather than taking a boat, and spent years slowed by Russian visa problems.
  • The final water. After Eurotunnel refused him permission to walk the Channel Tunnel service corridor, Bushby swam the English Channel and landed on Shakespeare Beach near Dover on October 5, 2026, leaving about 400 kilometers still to walk to Hull.

What comes next

  • A documentary. Filmmaker Werner Herzog accompanied Bushby on the homecoming and is making a feature length documentary about the journey called Walk of Life.
  • A speaking tour. Bushby plans to begin a speaking tour around England in the coming weeks, after spending nearly half his life on the road.
  • A second book. Bushby is also planning a sequel to his memoir Giant Steps, telling the story of a journey that outlasted every plan he made for it.
