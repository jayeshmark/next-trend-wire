Dataline Raises $10.6 Million to Build a Data Network for AI Agents

By Jayesh Marathe · October 31, 2026 · 7 min read

Dataline, a decentralized network that sells data to AI agents, has raised $10.6 million in a round co-led by Lattice and Samsung-backed Dispersion Capital, the company announced October 31. The raise arrives with a partner cohort spanning prediction markets Polymarket and Kalshi and blockchains Base and Robinhood Chain.

What happened

  • The round. Dataline raised $10.6 million in a round co-led by Lattice and Samsung-backed Dispersion Capital, with participation from AI fund Symbolic Capital. Strategic backers include Coinbase Ventures, Anagram, a former Head of the CFTC, and founders and partners from Robinhood, Venmo, Anchorage, NEA, General Catalyst, Jump Capital, and FalconX.
  • The launch cohort. The AI Data Launch cohort brings together Kite, Aerodrome, B3OS, Sentient, Sahara, Hyperliquid, Polymarket, Kalshi, Primus, Cluster, Apro, Base, Robinhood Chain, BNB, Sui, TON, and others. As part of the launch, Dataline said builders on Base and Robinhood Chain will get free Dataline access.
  • The traction claim. The company says it has powered 19.4 million onchain transactions with a 96.4% agent task success rate and grown a community of more than 300,000 people. The protocol was also named a winner of the 2025 BNB Chain AI Hackathon.
  • The founder's framing. Cofounder Nuo Shi said in the announcement: "After building AI agents ourselves, we realized that clean and reliable data is the lifeblood of AI. Today's AI is capable enough to know what data it needs, sometimes better than we do."
  • What comes next. In November, Dataline plans to hold a Uniswap Continuous Clearing Auction on Base and on Robinhood Chain.

Why it matters

  • Bad data means bad agent actions. An AI agent that books travel, trades, or moves money acts on whatever data it is fed, so a wrong price or a stale listing becomes a wrong decision. Dataline's pitch is a confidence score on every API response, weighing source reliability, freshness, and consistency, so agents can flag or refuse bad data before acting.
  • Pay per call instead of subscriptions. Dataline runs a two-sided marketplace where anyone can publish a data feed and any agent can pay per call with x402, the payments protocol for machine-to-machine transactions. Payments settle instantly, and sellers' credentials never leave the gateway.
  • A bounty model for data agents cannot reach. When an agent needs data no feed carries, a requester posts a bounty, any fulfiller in the network claims it, supplies the data, and gets paid. Fulfillers stake tokens to submit responses, so good data earns the bounty and bad data gets the stake slashed.
  • The OpenRouter comparison. Dataline describes itself as the routing layer for AI data, the way OpenRouter routes AI tokens: one integration for all markets, with cross-verified sources and custom-fetched data requests on top.

Second order effects

  • Prediction markets become agent infrastructure. With Polymarket and Kalshi in the launch cohort, live market odds could become a standard feed that agents check before acting on news, prices, or election outcomes, turning prediction markets into a data utility rather than just a betting venue.
  • Chains compete on agent tooling. Base and Robinhood Chain offering free Dataline access shows blockchains now competing to be the place agents build, with data access as the perk rather than just low fees.
  • A reputation market for data sellers. If confidence scores become a default filter, sellers will compete on verified reliability instead of price alone, and a seller with a history of slashed stakes will find fewer buyers.

Third order effects

  • Agents as a customer class. If agents routinely pay per API call, data becomes a machine-to-machine economy with its own pricing, reputation, and settlement layers, and the biggest buyers of data may soon be software rather than people.
  • Provenance becomes a feature. An optional zero-knowledge attestation tier lets providers attach a verifiable proof bundle to every response, which points toward a future where the proof behind a number matters as much as the number itself.
  • Regulators follow the money. A former CFTC head among the backers is a reminder that agent-driven trading on market data will draw the same questions about manipulation and fairness that human trading already faces.

What to watch next

  • The November auction. Whether the planned Uniswap Continuous Clearing Auction on Base and Robinhood Chain draws real demand or passes quietly.
  • Whether the cohort ships feeds. Announcements are cheap; the test is whether Kite, Sentient, Sahara, and the rest publish live feeds that agents actually pay to call.
  • The success rate under load. Whether the reported 96.4% agent task success rate holds as more agents, feeds, and bounty requests join the network.
