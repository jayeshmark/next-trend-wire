Accel Leads $30M Bet on Drones That Fly Without GPS

By Jayesh Marathe · November 12, 2026 · 7 min read

SE3, a Munich startup building spatial intelligence software for autonomous systems, has raised $30 million in Series A funding led by Accel. The company says its software lets drone fleets operate without satellite navigation, a capability European defense buyers now want as electronic warfare spreads.

The round

  • The size. SE3 announced on Thursday, November 12, 2026, that it has raised $30 million in Series A funding.
  • The lead. Accel led the round, with participation from Keen, Lakestar and Seedcamp.
  • The investor view. Accel said it is delighted to lead the round as SE3 builds sovereign spatial intelligence for Europe.
  • The founders. SE3 was founded by chief executive Lukas Köstler, chief technology officer Simon Klenk and chief science officer Professor Daniel Cremers, spinning out of Cremers' computer vision lab at the Technical University of Munich.
  • The prize. Professor Cremers holds the university's chair for computer vision and AI and is a recipient of the Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz Prize, Germany's most prestigious research award.

What the software does

  • The gap. SE3 says AI can now automate much knowledge work but still struggles to understand and operate in three dimensions, and it was created to close that gap and bring AI to the physical world.
  • The layer. Its platform-agnostic technology acts as the spatial intelligence software layer for unmanned systems, fusing sensor data from drones and ground vehicles into a shared 3D picture of the environment.
  • The swarm. The software operates the swarm intelligently, optimizing behavior, routes and positions for each system based on its understanding of the terrain, and operators can command all functions by voice.
  • The jammed GPS problem. The software runs entirely on edge devices and keeps systems working when satellite navigation is disrupted, which SE3 says is now the norm in contested environments.
  • The neutral pitch. SE3 built its technology for a defense procurement landscape focused on modularity, so its software integrates across manufacturers' platforms without competing with their vehicles.

The defense focus

  • The market. The company's primary focus is European defense, where investment in sovereign technology is rising as geopolitical shifts accelerate.
  • The European spend. The EU aims to mobilize 800 billion euros in defense spending by 2030, with plans for a European Drone Wall to defend its eastern flank.
  • The Ukraine deployment. SE3 says its technology is already deployed in Ukraine, where large drone fleets stretch the limits of human supervision while electronic warfare disrupts satellite navigation.
  • The contracts. In the last two years SE3 has shipped thousands of systems and is working on multiple contracts with Ukraine and with Germany's armed forces, the Bundeswehr.
  • The partners. SE3 is an AI partner to defense primes such as Rheinmetall and HENSOLDT, and to fast-growing scale-ups such as Auterion and ERC System.

What the money funds

  • The teams. The funding will grow SE3's engineering, commercial and operations teams, ready to fulfill further contracts as the company scales.
  • The map. SE3 will strengthen its presence in Germany and Ukraine, open an office in the UK and expand further across Europe.
  • The civilian turn. Defense remains the immediate focus, with plans to explore robotics, emergency response and other civilian applications for demanding environments from 2027.
  • The quote. Köstler said the funding will get the company closer to providing the spatial intelligence technology needed across Europe to protect a free and secure future.

Why it matters

  • The GPS-free bet. SE3's pitch is that autonomy only counts if it holds up when satellite navigation is jammed, and its on-edge software is built for exactly that failure mode.
  • The sovereign wave. European defense startups are raising fast as the EU mobilizes hundreds of billions, and SE3's Accel-led round puts a top-tier Silicon Valley firm behind a Munich defense bet.
  • The Ukraine proof. Deployment in Ukraine and contracts with the Bundeswehr give SE3 something most AI startups lack: systems already tested where electronic warfare is real.
  • The platform play. By staying neutral across manufacturers, SE3 aims to become the software layer for every drone fleet rather than another hardware maker.

What to watch next

  • The UK office. The new London base will test whether a Munich defense startup can sell into British procurement as well as German.
  • The civilian step. The 2027 move into robotics and emergency response will show whether spatial intelligence travels beyond the battlefield.
  • The next round. Further contracts with Ukraine and the Bundeswehr will decide how fast SE3 needs to raise again.
