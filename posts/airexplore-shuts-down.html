Slovak Airline AirExplore Shuts Down After 16 Years

By Jayesh Marathe · November 12, 2026 · 7 min read

Slovak charter airline AirExplore has permanently stopped flying after 16 years of operations, grounding its Boeing 737s and filing to surrender its air operator's certificate. The Bratislava based carrier, owned by Avia Solutions Group, said a brutal cargo market and heavy losses left it with no commercially sustainable path forward.

What happened

  • The shutdown. AirExplore permanently ceased all flight operations on November 10, 2026, and submitted a formal request to surrender its air operator's certificate. The airline has also commenced insolvency proceedings.
  • The company's words. "The Slovakian ACMI and charter airline Air Explore has ceased operations on 10 November 2026 and has submitted a request to surrender its Air Operator Certificate (AOC)," the airline said in a statement. "We thank all our employees, customers, partners, and team for their support over the years."
  • The reason. A spokesperson said the decision was extremely difficult. "Challenging conditions in the cargo market, the difficulty of securing contracts for the winter season, and the company's significant financial losses have left us without a commercially sustainable path forward," the spokesperson said.
  • The cargo losses. The company's cargo operations recorded a loss of 25.5 million euros since January 2025, as the freight business it had tried to build never turned profitable.
  • The creditors and staff. The company said claims from creditors will be addressed in accordance with applicable law, and that all October salaries, social security contributions and taxes have been paid in full. Slovak media reported plans to lay off 109 of roughly 160 employees.
  • The flights. The airline was operating only a limited number of charter contracts at the time of the shutdown, so no immediate disruption to the winter flight schedule is expected. AirExplore flew mostly wet lease and charter work rather than scheduled services under its own name.

The airline

  • The start. AirExplore was founded in 2008 and began ACMI operations in 2010 with a single Boeing 737-400, later expanding its fleet with Boeing 737-800s.
  • The business. ACMI stands for aircraft, crew, maintenance and insurance. AirExplore rented its jets, with crews, to larger airlines that needed extra capacity in peak seasons or cover during fleet outages.
  • The owner. Avia Solutions Group acquired AirExplore in June 2023, adding the carrier to its portfolio of wet lease and charter airlines across Europe.
  • The integration. In February 2026, Avia Solutions Group put AirExplore and fellow subsidiary KlasJet under a shared management structure, with AirExplore keeping its identity and its focus on ACMI work while KlasJet concentrated on VIP and private charter.
  • The wind down. The airline had already been shrinking its fleet through 2026 as part of that integration, and it ended operations with its remaining Boeing 737-800s.

Why it matters

  • The pattern. AirExplore is the latest European carrier to fall to the same pressures: high jet fuel costs, weak demand and rising corporate debt. Spanish charter airline AlbaStar halted operations on the same day, November 10, though AlbaStar called its grounding temporary while it looks for new investors.
  • The cargo bet. AirExplore tried to diversify into air freight by converting Boeing 737-800 passenger aircraft into freighters. The cargo business lost money instead, and the winter season brought no new contracts to save it.
  • The group question. Avia Solutions Group has been restructuring its international airline operations, and AirExplore is now the latest name in its portfolio to disappear. The shutdown raises the question of which of the group's remaining carriers are next.
  • The airport. For Bratislava Airport, the closure means the loss of a regional player and its headquarters jobs, though the airline's limited charter schedule means no immediate disruption to the winter flight program.

What to watch next

  • The insolvency. The proceedings will decide how creditors are paid and what happens to the airline's remaining aircraft and its owned 737.
  • The staff. The reported layoffs of most of the workforce will show whether Avia Solutions Group absorbs any of the pilots and crew into its other airlines.
  • AlbaStar. The Spanish carrier's temporary halt is the next test of whether November's two shutdowns are the end of the wave or the start of one.
