Path Robotics Raises $300 Million to Take Its Robots Beyond Welding

By Jayesh Marathe · November 10, 2026 · 7 min read

Path Robotics announced $300 million in Series E financing on November 10 at a post money valuation of $2.45 billion, a bet that the same artificial intelligence that taught its robots to weld can automate the rest of the factory floor.

The round

  • The money. The $300 million Series E was led by Georgian, an AI focused growth equity firm, at a $2.45 billion post money valuation.
  • The backers. Existing investors Addition, Drive Capital, IronArc Ventures, Tiger Global and Yamaha Motor Ventures took part, alongside new investors D. E. Shaw Ventures and NAVER Ventures.
  • The board. Emily Walsh, a partner at Georgian, will join Path Robotics' board of directors as part of the financing.
  • The total. The round brings Path's total capital raised since its founding in 2018 to more than $610 million.
  • The growth. Path said its annual recurring revenue grew 17 fold over the past 18 months as it scaled deployments with major industrial customers in North America, though the announcement gives no absolute revenue figure.

What Path builds

  • The model. Path's Obsidian model is a physical AI system that lets robots find joints, plan welds and adapt to changing conditions on the factory floor, rather than following fixed programming.
  • The machine. In May 2026 the company introduced Rove, a mobile welding system that pairs its Obsidian model with a quadruped robot, showing its software can work across different robotic forms.
  • The customers. Path names large manufacturers HII, Lockheed Martin and Saronic among its customers, as well as mid sized manufacturers EK Machine, Millerbernd Manufacturing, TransAmerican Power Products and TYCROP.
  • The shipyard deal. In August 2026 Path announced a $600 million performance based production agreement with HII to accelerate physical AI deployment across US Navy shipbuilding programs.

Where the money goes

  • Beyond welding. Path plans to use the capital to extend its physical AI beyond welding into more manufacturing tasks and robotic form factors, and to deploy its platform in industries it describes as critical to US industrial capacity.
  • The goal. Co-founder and chief executive Andy Lonsberry said the company will scale its welding business while investing further in the data, compute and model development needed for the tasks before and after welding, with the goal of automating the entire factory.
  • The bet. The round values a Columbus, Ohio company that started with one hard manufacturing problem, welding, at $2.45 billion, on the strength of revenue growth and a customer list that now includes the US Navy's biggest shipbuilder.

What comes next

  • The expansion. Path will push its platform into defense and energy infrastructure work, the sectors Lonsberry has pointed to as the next step beyond commercial welding.
  • The test. The company must now prove that a model built for welding can learn the many other jobs on a factory floor, and the $300 million buys it the time and compute to try.
