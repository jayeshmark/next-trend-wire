Rachel Apa, 24, Dies After Collapsing Near the Finish of Her First New York City Marathon

By Jayesh Marathe · November 4, 2026 · 7 min read

Rachel Apa, a 24 year old runner from Lewisberry, Pennsylvania, died on Sunday, November 1, after collapsing near mile 24 of the New York City Marathon, about two miles from the finish line in Central Park. She was running her first marathon, in memory of her grandfather, when she collapsed at about 1:30 p.m. local time.

The collapse

  • The final miles. Apa collapsed in Central Park near the 24 mile mark of the 26.2 mile course, with about two miles left to run, after spending months preparing for her first marathon.
  • The rescue effort. According to accounts from her family and the race organizer, spectators began CPR, and New York Road Runners medical staff then took over and used a defibrillator before paramedics took her by ambulance to Mount Sinai.
  • The death. Her father, Steve Apa, said doctors worked for hours to save her, and she was pronounced dead at about 9:40 p.m. on Sunday.
  • The police account. New York police told reporters that a 24 year old woman had a medical episode near mile marker 24 and was taken to a hospital in critical condition.
  • The cause, as her family described it. Her father said doctors attributed her death to rhabdomyolysis, a condition he described as a rare muscle injury, and he called her death unfathomable for a runner he described as being in perfect health and perfect shape.
  • The official record. No hospital, medical examiner or race official has publicly confirmed a cause of death, and the account of rhabdomyolysis comes from her family.

A first marathon for her grandfather

  • The reason she ran. Apa ran as part of the Parkinson's Champions team, raising money in memory of her grandfather, who died from Parkinson's disease.
  • The athlete. She was a lifelong athlete and a former Bucknell University field hockey player, and she was pursuing a master's degree at Charleston Southern University.
  • The milestone. The race was her first marathon, a personal goal she had trained for over months before lining up in New York on Sunday.

What rhabdomyolysis is

  • The condition. Rhabdomyolysis is a condition in which muscles break down and release their contents into the bloodstream, where they can damage the kidneys.
  • The triggers. Cleveland Clinic lists high intensity exercise, including pushing too hard without resting, among the causes, along with trauma, severe dehydration and overheating, and says the condition can be life threatening if it isn't treated quickly.
  • The warning signs. The clinic lists muscle swelling, weak muscles, and tender and sore muscles, and dark urine that looks brown, red or tea colored among the signs, and says symptoms usually develop one to three days after a muscle injury.

The organizer responds

  • The statement. New York Road Runners, which organizes the marathon, said in a statement: "We are deeply saddened by Rachel's passing following running in Sunday's race. Our thoughts and sincere sympathies are with her family, friends, and the entire running community."
  • The scale of the race. The marathon course runs through all five boroughs before entering Central Park for its final miles.
  • What remains unknown. The precise circumstances that triggered the condition in Apa's case have not been publicly established, and the organizer's statement stayed on her family, her friends and the running community she joined for one race.
