FDA Orders Eye Warning on Ozempic, Zepbound and Every GLP-1 Drug

By Jayesh Marathe · November 9, 2026 · 7 min read

The Food and Drug Administration on November 9 ordered every maker of GLP-1 diabetes and weight loss drugs to add a warning about a rare eye condition that can cause sudden, permanent vision loss in one eye. The warning covers Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro, Zepbound, and every other drug in the class.

The warning

  • What changed. The FDA is requiring application holders for all GLP-1 RA medications to add information about the potential increased risk of NAION to the Warnings and Precautions section of their labels.
  • The condition. NAION, or non-arteritic anterior ischemic optic neuropathy, is a rare condition that can result in acute, painless, decreased vision in one eye, including permanent vision loss. It is caused by decreased blood flow to the front of the optic nerve.
  • The scope. NAION cases have been reported for all currently marketed GLP-1 RA products, so the requirement covers the whole class rather than a single brand.
  • Not a recall. The action is a labeling change, not a recall or a ban, and the FDA has not told patients to stop taking their medicine.

What the FDA found

  • The trigger. In July 2024, Hathaway and colleagues published a study on the risk of NAION in patients prescribed semaglutide, and the FDA identified several limitations in that study but determined that further assessment was warranted.
  • The trial data. A meta-analysis of 126 trials covering 134,253 patients found 15 probable NAION events, 10 in patients taking GLP-1 drugs and 5 in patients taking a placebo, with an incidence of 6.4 events per 100,000 person-years against 3.8 in the comparison groups.
  • The real-world data. A Sentinel study of adults with type 2 diabetes who started a GLP-1 drug or another common diabetes medicine between 2019 and 2024 found NAION rates of 30 to 32 events per 100,000 person-years for GLP-1 users, against 31 for SGLT-2 inhibitors and 26 for DPP-4 inhibitors.
  • The split result. The Sentinel study found no statistically significant increase in NAION risk for GLP-1 drugs compared with SGLT-2 inhibitors, but it did find an increased risk compared with DPP-4 inhibitors, with a hazard ratio of 1.43.
  • No mechanism found. FDA review of the available animal data did not identify a mechanism for how GLP-1 RA use could increase the risk of NAION.
  • The conclusion. The FDA concluded that there is a potential increased risk of rare NAION events in patients taking GLP-1 RAs. The agency's word is potential, not proof: it has not determined that the drugs cause NAION.

What to watch for

  • The symptoms. Symptoms include sudden, painless vision loss in one eye that patients may notice upon waking, blurred or missing portions of vision such as blind spots or tunnel vision, distortion of colors, and reduced central sharpness.
  • What patients should do. Patients should contact their health care professional right away if they have any of these symptoms.
  • After an event. If a patient has had a NAION event, they should talk with their health care professional about whether to continue taking their GLP-1 RA medication or to stop it.
  • The risk factors. Risk factors may include smoking, diabetes, high blood pressure, sleep apnea, anemia, and kidney disease, and NAION in one eye raises the risk that it will occur in the other eye.
  • What doctors should do. Health care professionals should tell patients to report sudden vision changes immediately and promptly refer suspected NAION to an ophthalmologist for evaluation.

Why it matters

  • The scale. GLP-1 drugs are widely used to treat people with type 2 diabetes and to reduce excess body weight and maintain weight reduction long term, so a class-wide warning reaches a large share of the medicine cabinet.
  • The evidence is thin but consistent. The trial signal rests on 15 probable cases in more than 134,000 patients, and the real-world data point in different directions depending on the comparison drug, yet the FDA found enough across its data sources to require the warning.
  • The practical point. The warning gives patients a specific symptom to act on fast, and it tells doctors to send suspected cases straight to an eye specialist rather than waiting to see if vision returns.
