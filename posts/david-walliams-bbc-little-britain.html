BBC Pulls Little Britain as Walliams Faces Sexual Misconduct Allegations

By Jayesh Marathe · November 10, 2026 · 7 min read

The BBC began removing programmes featuring David Walliams from iPlayer on November 10, including Little Britain, hours after a joint investigation by the Guardian and ITV News named the comedian in sexual misconduct allegations involving multiple women. Walliams denies all of the allegations against him.

What the BBC did

  • The removal. The BBC said it is removing some programmes featuring and written by Walliams, including Little Britain, while it reviews all material featuring him on its platforms.
  • The statement. "These are serious allegations," a BBC spokesperson said. "As such, the BBC is looking carefully at all material featuring and written by David Walliams on our platforms. We are in the process of removing some programmes, including Little Britain, whilst this review is underway."
  • The scale. The entire run of Little Britain is being taken down, along with episodes of The Wheel and Would I Lie To You, a 2011 episode of Doctor Who in which Walliams played Gibbis, an episode of Who Do You Think You Are? focused on Walliams, and adaptations of his children's books such as Gangsta Granny.
  • The show. Little Britain, which Walliams co-created with Matt Lucas, ran on the BBC from 2003 until 2006 and made Walliams one of Britain's best known comedians.

The investigation

  • Three years. The allegations come from a three year joint investigation by ITV News and the Guardian into Walliams, who is 55.
  • The claims. Multiple women accuse Walliams of sexual misconduct and of using his position to groom teenagers as young as 15, with the alleged conduct dating back to 2003. Two of the women say they met Walliams when they were 15.
  • The court fight. Walliams brought legal action against Independent Television News and Guardian News and Media, publisher of the Guardian, ahead of publication, seeking an injunction to block the reporting. The bid was dismissed, his anonymity was lifted, and he did not appeal before the deadline, clearing the way for publication on November 10.
  • The denial. In a statement to ITV News, Walliams denied all of the allegations against him.
  • The film. ITV News will air a documentary about the investigation, David Walliams: Power and Silence, on ITV1 at 10:50 p.m. on Tuesday, and it is already available to stream on ITVX and YouTube.

The fallout

  • The party. The Labour Party suspended Walliams as a member on Tuesday after the allegations were published.
  • The agent. Walliams was dropped by his literary agency, the Independent Talent Group, on the same day the investigation was published.
  • The broadcaster. The BBC review covers every programme featuring or written by Walliams, so more removals may follow as the review continues.
  • The status. No charges have been reported against Walliams, and the allegations remain claims made in a journalistic investigation, which he denies.

What comes next

  • The review. The BBC has not said how long its review of Walliams material will take or whether the removed programmes will return.
  • The documentary. The ITV film airs on Tuesday night, and its reception will shape how far the story travels beyond the BBC's own platforms.
  • The question. Walliams built a second career as one of Britain's most successful children's authors, and the investigation now puts that work, his television career and his public standing all under review at once.
