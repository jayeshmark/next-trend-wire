Anthony Edwards to Miss Up to Two Months After Knee Procedure

By Editorial Team · 4 min read

Minnesota Timberwolves star Anthony Edwards will undergo an arthroscopic procedure on his right knee on October 29, 2026, and is expected to miss up to two months. The procedure cuts short the best start of his career, with Edwards averaging 33.3 points through four games for the 3-1 Timberwolves.

What happened

  • The procedure. Edwards will undergo an arthroscopic procedure on his right knee on Thursday, October 29, and is expected to miss up to two months, according to ESPN's Shams Charania, with team reporting putting a December return as the hope.
  • The team's word. Timberwolves president of basketball operations Tim Connelly confirmed the procedure on Wednesday, said it is not a meniscus tear, said it would happen within 24 hours, and said Edwards will be "good as new," adding that the team is not concerned about the long-term outlook.
  • How it started. Edwards began grabbing at his right knee during Sunday's home opener against Toronto, a 117-94 win, and coach Chris Finch called it a "daily situation" before Monday's game, saying at the time he was not worried about a long-term problem.
  • The Cleveland night. Listed as questionable before Monday's game at Cleveland, Edwards was cleared after pregame warmups, played a season-high 41 minutes, and scored 47 points on 19-of-34 shooting in a 127-125 overtime loss, his second game of at least 45 points in the team's first four games.
  • The start he loses. Through four games Edwards averaged 33.3 points, 4.8 rebounds, 3.5 assists and 2.0 steals on 48.1 percent shooting and 40 percent from three-point range, including 45 points in the season opener at Miami, a 131-106 win, and he entered the week as the early league leader in field goals made and attempted.
  • The history. The right knee is not new: Edwards dealt with runner's knee in the same leg last season, missed nine of the final 22 regular-season games managing it, then hyperextended his left knee and sustained a bone bruise in the first-round playoff series, missing 21 games in all, the most of his career, while Minnesota went 12-9 without him.

Why it matters

  • The best start, interrupted. Edwards had opened the season playing at an MVP level, and losing him four games in takes the league's early scoring leader off the floor before the season has found its shape.
  • A proactive call. Rather than let Edwards keep playing through discomfort, Minnesota chose surgery now, betting that a December return leaves most of the season for seeding and chemistry before the playoffs.
  • The recurring knee. Last season's rest-first approach did not settle the right knee, and two months off this summer did not either, which is why the team moved from management to a procedure.
  • A new roster's first test. Minnesota overhauled its core in the offseason, trading away Julius Randle and Naz Reid and bringing in LaMelo Ball and Jonathan Kuminga, and the 3-1 start now meets its first real test without its best player.

Second order effects

  • Who scores now. Ball, who had been playing more as a facilitator, plus Jaden McDaniels and Kuminga, will have to carry more of the offense, with Ayo Dosunmu also in line for a bigger role.
  • Last season's evidence. Minnesota went 12-9 without Edwards last season, and there is confidence inside the organization that the roster has enough depth to keep winning while he recovers.
  • The league-wide injury wave. Edwards joins a long early-season list that includes Jalen Williams, Cason Wallace and Kawhi Leonard, which has made health, not talent, the first storyline of the season.
  • The timetable question. ESPN reports up to two months, while other team reporting puts a December return as the hope and notes a return could come sooner depending on what doctors find during the procedure, so the first update after surgery matters more than the first report.

Third order effects

  • The long-term bet. The front office framed the surgery as protecting Edwards' long-term health rather than chasing wins in October, which is the right priority for a 25-year-old franchise player and the wrong one only if the team slides out of contention while he is gone.
  • Durability and the record. Edwards had not missed a game this season, and last season's 21 missed games were already the most of his career, so his availability is now a season-long question rather than a one-week concern.
  • The trust test. Edwards wanted to keep playing, by Connelly's own account, and the team overruled its star for his own good, which only works if the December timeline holds.

What to watch next

  • The post-procedure update. A more precise timetable is expected after Thursday's operation.
  • The scoring load. Who starts and who takes the late shots in the first games without Edwards.
  • The December window. Whether the recovery tracks toward a December return or stretches longer.
  • The record test. Minnesota's record during the absence, measured against last season's 12-9 mark without him.
