Nine Killed as Learjet 60 Crashes in a Palm Beach County Neighborhood

By Editorial Team · 7 min read

All nine people aboard a Learjet 60 were killed on Sunday, November 1, when the business jet crashed in a residential area of Palm Beach County, Florida, after departing Austin, Texas, for the Bahamas. United States Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy confirmed the deaths, and the National Transportation Safety Board will lead the investigation into what caused the crash.

What happened

  • The crash. A Learjet 60 crashed in a residential area near Cresthaven, Florida, around 12:50 p.m. local time on Sunday, November 1, according to a statement from the Federal Aviation Administration.
  • The location. The Palm Beach County Sheriff's Office placed the crash near Military Trail, between Cresthaven Boulevard and 10th Avenue North, in an unincorporated area near the communities of Greenacres and Palm Springs.
  • The flight. The aircraft had departed Austin-Bergstrom International Airport in Texas and was headed to North Eleuthera Airport in the Bahamas, the FAA said.
  • The fire. The jet erupted in flames after hitting the ground near a busy roadway, scattering debris across a wide area and closing lanes in both directions while emergency crews worked at the scene.
  • The death toll. Duffy confirmed that all nine people aboard were killed, calling the crash a horrific tragedy in a statement and saying the FAA was sending a team to support the investigation.
  • The people aboard. Officials had not released the identities of the nine people aboard, and had not said how many were passengers and how many were crew.

The investigation

  • The lead agency. The FAA said it and the National Transportation Safety Board will investigate, with the NTSB in charge of the investigation and providing all updates.
  • The go team. The NTSB said it was launching a go team to investigate the crash, with investigators arriving at the site on Sunday and on Monday morning.
  • A briefing planned. The NTSB said it would hold a media briefing in West Palm Beach on Monday, with the time and location to be announced.
  • The final descent. Flight tracking data showed the aircraft entering a steep, rapid descent shortly before tracking cut off, but no official cause has been given and the tracking data alone does not explain why the jet went down.
  • No cause yet. The cause of the crash remains unknown, and investigators will examine the wreckage, review available flight data, and analyze air traffic communications as part of the inquiry.

Why this crash stands out

  • The second deadliest this year. The crash is the second deadliest in the United States this year, according to National Transportation Safety Board records, following a skydiving plane crash in Butler, Missouri, in June that killed 12 people.
  • The worst for its type. The crash is the worst involving a Learjet 60, according to data from the Flight Safety Foundation, after a 2008 crash on takeoff from Columbia, South Carolina, that killed four people aboard a Learjet 60 bound for Los Angeles.
  • A neighborhood impact. The jet came down in a residential area rather than at an airport or in open ground, which is why the crash drew a large emergency response and closed roads around the site even though the aircraft itself was a small business jet.
  • A busy corridor. The crash site sits about four miles south of the airport in West Palm Beach, in a densely built part of Palm Beach County where a falling aircraft had little open ground beneath it.

What remains unknown

  • The cause. Nothing official has been said about what caused the steep final descent, and the investigation will take time before the NTSB reaches any finding.
  • Injuries on the ground. Officials had not confirmed whether anyone on the ground was injured or killed, and Florida Governor Ron DeSantis said there were no immediate reports of anyone on the ground injured or killed.
  • The identities. The names of those aboard had not been released, and further details are expected to come from the NTSB and local authorities rather than from speculation about who was on the flight.
  • The next update. The NTSB, as the lead agency, will provide the next official update at its West Palm Beach briefing, and this report will be updated when that briefing gives investigators' first account of the scene.
