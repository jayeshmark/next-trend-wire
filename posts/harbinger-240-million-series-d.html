Harbinger Raises $240 Million to Take Its Electric Truck Technology Into Defense and Energy Storage

By Editorial Team · 7 min read

Harbinger, the electric truck maker building an industrial technology platform, has raised $240 million in a Series D funding round as it expands beyond commercial vehicles into defense, energy storage, power generation, and autonomy software. The company announced the round on November 5, 2026, and said it has now raised more than $650 million since its founding in 2021.

The round

  • The deal. Harbinger announced that it has raised $240 million in Series D funding to fuel the company's next phase of growth, with the round co-led by Tiger Global, Capricorn's Technology Impact Fund, and Ridgeline.
  • The backers. Additional investors include crossover investors Fidelity Management and Research Company, Squarepoint Capital, and Xantium, returning investors Maniv and Greycroft, Australian Gulf Capital, PanaceaVC, ArcTern Ventures, and Leitmotif, and strategic investors FedEx and THOR Industries through TechNexus, as well as BuildGroup in data centers.
  • The placement agents. Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley served as placement agents on the financing round, a role that signals how large the deal is for a company of Harbinger's age.
  • The funding history. The round comes almost one year to the day after Harbinger announced a $160 million Series C, which included an initial truck order from FedEx, and lifts the company's total raised since its 2021 founding above $650 million.
  • The Tiger Global case. "Harbinger excels at electrification, and electrification has become one of the most pressing bottlenecks of the AI era," said Griffin Schroeder, partner at Tiger Global. "We led this financing because we see tremendous value in bringing Harbinger's technical expertise and manufacturing capabilities to new markets."

What the money is for

  • The five directions. The new funding will support Harbinger's expansion across commercial vehicles, energy storage systems, power generation, autonomy software, and defense applications, as well as the continued ramp up of its medium duty platform manufacturing capacity.
  • The platform idea. Harbinger describes itself as an American industrial technology platform company reinventing the critical systems the world depends on across transportation, power, defense, autonomy, and components, rather than as a truck maker alone.
  • The first principles pitch. The company says it engineers systems from first principles, using deep vertical integration to control product development, performance, cost, and scale, an approach it first demonstrated through its medium duty electric vehicle platform and is now applying across industries.
  • The AI economy angle. Harbinger says the new funds will support its growth as a manufacturing platform powering the AI economy and electrification, a framing that connects its battery and power work to the data center buildout as well as to vehicles.
  • The CEO's case. "Harbinger exists to challenge what industry has learned to accept, and reinvent the critical systems the world depends on," said John Harris, co-founder and CEO of Harbinger. "By rethinking critical systems from first principles, we aim to unlock better performance, greater reliability, safer operations, and more room to grow. Electrification is at the core of everything we do, and increasingly, electrification is at the core of industry, defense, mobility, and AI."

The FedEx relationship

  • The big order. FedEx recently placed an order for 2,000 all-electric Harbinger trucks valued at more than $300 million, with delivery planned by the end of 2027 for FedEx pickup and delivery operations in the United States and Canada.
  • The board seat. FedEx senior vice president of safety and transportation Paul Melander has joined Harbinger's board of directors, to support the company's focus on enhanced driver safety, delivery quality, and customer execution.
  • The repeat investment. FedEx also invested again in the Series D after leading last year's round, a sign that the logistics giant's commitment to Harbinger goes beyond the truck order.
  • The manufacturing test. The FedEx order is the largest single test of Harbinger's ability to build at scale, and the company says the new funding will help it ramp the manufacturing capacity needed to deliver the full order within about 18 months.

Why this round matters

  • The defense move. Harbinger's push into defense applications puts it alongside a growing group of startups selling electrification and autonomy technology to military buyers, a market that has drawn heavy venture investment this year.
  • The energy storage play. The company's battery technology, first built for trucks, is now being offered as standalone energy storage and auxiliary power products, which opens a second market that does not depend on vehicle sales.
  • The crowded field. Harbinger is expanding at a moment when electric truck makers face intense competition and thin margins, so the breadth of its platform, spanning vehicles, batteries, power, autonomy, and defense, is the bet that sets it apart from single product rivals.
  • The scale question. With more than $650 million raised and a 2,000 truck order to fill, Harbinger's next test is execution: turning a well funded platform story into delivered trucks, installed storage, and signed defense contracts.
