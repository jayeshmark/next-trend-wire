Irma P. Hall, Soul Food's Big Mama, Dies at 91

By Jayesh Marathe · November 12, 2026 · 7 min read

Irma P. Hall, the actress who played Big Mama Joe in Soul Food and won a Cannes prize for The Ladykillers, died on Tuesday in Dallas. She was 91. Her family announced her death on Wednesday, saying she died surrounded by her loved ones. No cause was given.

The announcement

  • The family statement. "It is with profound sadness that the family of acclaimed actress Dr. Irma P. Hall announces her passing," the family said in a statement. It called her an award winning actress, a beloved Dallas resident and a longtime member and cherished Church Mother of Inspiring Body of Christ Church.
  • The details. Hall died on Tuesday, November 10, in Dallas, surrounded by her loved ones. She is survived by her son, her daughter Irma Hall Crayton, and four grandchildren. Funeral arrangements will be announced later.
  • The honors. Hall had been inducted into the Texas Film Hall of Fame and was recently named a 2026 Dallas History Makers Award winner by the Dallas Historical Society. The awards luncheon is set for November 19, nine days after her death.

A late start

  • The teacher. Hall spent two decades as a schoolteacher in Dallas before turning to acting full time. She did not step in front of a camera until her late 30s, and her acting career took off after she turned 60.
  • The beginnings. Born Irma Dolores Player on June 3, 1935, in Beaumont, Texas, Hall was raised in Chicago and returned to Texas for college, attending Texas College in Tyler.
  • The breakthrough. Hall was 60 when A Family Thing, the 1996 film starring James Earl Jones and Robert Duvall, brought her a major film role. The next year she played Big Mama Joe in Soul Food, the matriarch whose warmth and strength anchor the story of a Chicago family.
  • The song. Her performance in Soul Food inspired "A Song for Mama," the Boyz II Men track written for the film's soundtrack.

The roles that defined her

  • The Ladykillers. In 2004 Hall played Marva Munson, a Bible reading landlady, opposite Tom Hanks in the Coen brothers' comedy The Ladykillers. The performance won her a Jury Prize at the Cannes Film Festival, a Black Reel Award and a NAACP Image Award nomination.
  • The range. The same year she appeared in Collateral, opposite Tom Cruise, and in 1997 she played the root doctor Minerva in Midnight in the Garden of Good and Evil. She returned as Mama Joe in the Soul Food television series in 2000.
  • The stage. Hall was a pioneer of Dallas theater and helped build the city's Black theater community, including support for the Dallas Minority Repertory Theatre. She remained a Dallas resident all her life.

What she leaves behind

  • The legacy. Hall's Big Mama Joe became one of the most loved screen matriarchs in Black cinema, a role that made her a household name in her 60s and kept her working into her 80s.
  • The family. Her daughter remembered her mother's influence as both an educator and a mentor, and the family said details about her services will be announced later.
