Virginia Madsen's Son Arrested in Fatal Alaska Hit-and-Run

By Jayesh Marathe · November 11, 2026 · 7 min read

Jack Sabato, the 32-year-old son of actors Virginia Madsen and Antonio Sabato Jr., has been arrested in Anchorage, Alaska, after a fatal hit-and-run that killed a pedestrian early Monday morning. Sabato faces two charges, was arraigned on Tuesday and remains in custody on a $5,000 bond, with a judge ordering him not to drive.

What happened

  • The crash. Police say Sabato was driving a white Chevrolet Tahoe when he hit a pedestrian near the intersection of Airport Heights Drive and DeBarr Road in Anchorage at about 5:35 a.m. on Monday, November 9. Officers responded to a report of an unresponsive person at about 5:48 a.m., and the pedestrian was pronounced dead at the scene.
  • The victim. The pedestrian was identified in charging documents as Carl Simmonds, 42. He was pronounced dead at the scene, and police have not released further detail about him.
  • The arrest. Police arrested Sabato on Monday. According to Alaska Public Media, he called police shortly after the crash and reported that he had hit an animal. Police traced the Tahoe through surveillance footage and found damage described as consistent with a pedestrian strike.
  • The charges. Sabato was arrested on two charges: leaving the scene of a crash involving injury or death, and leaving the scene of a crash without assisting an injured person. These are charges, not findings of fault, and the reports did not describe a cause of the crash.
  • The arraignment. Sabato was arraigned on Tuesday afternoon in the Anchorage Jail Courtroom. A judge set his bail at $5,000, issued a no-driving order and warned of another $5,000 penalty for missed court dates. He remained in custody at the Anchorage Correctional Complex as of Tuesday evening.

The family

  • The parents. Sabato is the son of Antonio Sabato Jr., a model and former General Hospital actor, and Virginia Madsen, an Oscar-nominated actress known for Candyman, Sideways and A Prairie Home Companion. The two were married from 1993 to 1998, and Jack, born in August 1994, is Madsen's only child.
  • The father's statement. Antonio Sabato Jr. told TMZ the family had just learned the news and asked for privacy. "We all just found out the terrible news and our family is digesting this horrible news and asking for privacy in this devastating news. Thank you," he said. He also said he has not yet spoken to his son since the incident.
  • The delivery. Police allege Sabato was delivering food at the time of the crash, according to reports of the charging documents.

The Anchorage context

  • A wider problem. Alaska Public Media noted that Anchorage has an ongoing problem with pedestrians being struck, and that this incident marks the ninth such incident this year.
  • The investigation. The Anchorage Police Department has not released further detail about the crash itself, and the charging documents obtained by local television station KTUU remain the fullest public account of what happened.

Why it matters

  • A death, not a scandal. The story is not about who Sabato's parents are. A 42-year-old man is dead, and the case turns on whether the driver who hit him stopped, helped and called for aid. The charges say he did none of those things.
  • The call. If the report that Sabato called police to say he hit an animal is accurate, it will be central to the case: what he knew, when he knew it, and what he told officers in the hours after the crash.
  • The family in the spotlight. The Sabato family learned of the arrest the same way the public did, and the father's statement makes clear they have had no contact with their son since the incident.

What to watch next

  • The next hearing. Sabato remains in custody, and his next court appearance will set the course of the case. The $5,000 bail, the no-driving order and the penalty for missed dates are already in place.
  • The charges. Prosecutors may add charges as the investigation continues, and the charging documents so far describe only the two counts of leaving the scene.
  • The family. Neither Madsen nor Antonio Sabato Jr. has said anything further, and both have asked for privacy while the case moves forward.
