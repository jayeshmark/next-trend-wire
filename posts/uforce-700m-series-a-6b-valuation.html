UFORCE Banks $700M to Build Drones That Sank Warships

By Jayesh Marathe · November 12, 2026 · 7 min read

UFORCE, the Ukrainian-founded defense technology company, has raised a $700 million Series A financing at a $6 billion valuation. The round was co-led by Valor Equity Partners and the UK National Wealth Fund, and the company says the money will scale production of the combat-proven drone fleets Ukraine has used in the Black Sea.

The round

  • The size. UFORCE announced a $700 million Series A financing on Thursday, November 12, 2026, at a valuation of $6 billion.
  • The co-leads. Valor Equity Partners and the UK National Wealth Fund co-led the round, with participation from the US-Ukraine Reconstruction Investment Fund.
  • The wider syndicate. Menlo Ventures, Xantium and Hiro Capital joined as new investors, while existing backers Shield Capital, Ballistic Ventures, Lakestar and Squadra increased their stakes.
  • The board seat. General (Ret.) Christopher G. Cavoli, the former NATO Supreme Allied Commander Europe and now a partner at Valor Equity Partners, joins the UFORCE board.
  • The company. UFORCE was founded in Ukraine and is headquartered in London, with major hubs in Kyiv and Washington, DC. It builds autonomous uncrewed platforms across sea, land and air, plus the AI software, orchestration and command and control layer that operates them.

What the money buys

  • The production spread. The funding will expand and distribute production capacity across the United States, Europe, Asia-Pacific and the Middle East, manufacturing platforms close to the theaters where they are needed.
  • The Ukraine commitment. The capital will also support operations, staffing and research and development in Ukraine, including new product development backed by the US-Ukraine Reconstruction Investment Fund.
  • The product lines. The round will scale production of the MAGURA uncrewed surface vessel, NEMESIS unmanned aerial vehicle, LIUT unmanned ground vehicle and PREDATOR turret families.
  • The US beachhead. UFORCE recently announced a partnership with ReconCraft to scale US manufacturing of MAGURA uncrewed surface vessels, giving global markets access to its combat-proven maritime capabilities.
  • The alliances. The company is a founding member of the EU-Ukraine Drone Alliance and says it will deepen partnerships developed within NATO and the Joint Expeditionary Force networks.

The combat record behind the valuation

  • The warships. The MAGURA family of uncrewed surface vessels has contributed to the destruction of more than a dozen Russian warships in the Black Sea and has shot down multiple helicopters and fighter aircraft in combat.
  • The flying base. Ukrainian armed forces have used MAGURAs as a mobile maritime base, launching NEMESIS drones for long-range strikes against targets such as air defense systems.
  • The sortie count. NEMESIS Class 1 heavy strike drones have flown more than 300,000 combat flights and are now used across more than 100 units, according to the company.
  • The doctrine. UFORCE says its goal is to make defense a hundred times cheaper than offensive operations, using cost-effective autonomous platforms that are ready to field now.

The carrier demonstration

  • The pilot. On November 12, aboard the aircraft carrier HMS Prince of Wales in Norway, UK defense secretary Wes Streeting piloted a UFORCE MV7 uncrewed surface vessel from the MAGURA family, located more than 1,500 miles away in the Atlantic Ocean near Portugal.
  • The audience. Streeting was joined by National Wealth Fund chief executive Oliver Holbourn and UFORCE chief executive Oleg Rogynskyy for the demonstration.
  • The message. Streeting said the technology being developed in Ukraine is changing warfare before our eyes, and that Britain and its allies must move just as quickly.
  • The jobs case. Holbourn said the investment will create high-skilled jobs and strengthen supply chains in both Britain and Ukraine, calling it a testament to the strength of Ukrainian innovation.

What they said

  • The chief executive. Rogynskyy said the war in Ukraine has shown that effective defense requires systems that can keep improving as threats change, and that the investment will help UFORCE scale production for Ukraine and bring allies solutions they can field at the speed of modern combat.
  • The general. Cavoli said UFORCE turns hard-won frontline experience into unmanned systems and the operational know-how to use them effectively, and that he is proud to join the board.
  • The reconstruction fund. Conor Coleman, speaking for the US-Ukraine Reconstruction Investment Fund, called the deal its largest investment in the emerging technology sector to date and a significant step toward mobilizing investment that strengthens Ukraine.

Why it matters

  • The scale. A $700 million Series A at a $6 billion valuation is an extraordinary first institutional round, and it makes UFORCE one of the most valuable defense startups in Europe.
  • The sovereign signal. The co-lead from the UK National Wealth Fund, a state-backed institution with 43.2 billion pounds of finance to deploy, puts a government balance sheet behind a Ukrainian-founded weapons maker.
  • The US link. The reconstruction fund's participation ties American reconstruction money to Ukrainian defense production, with the fund calling UFORCE the kind of bold Ukrainian company it was built to support.
  • The wartime test. Few defense startups arrive with a combat record. UFORCE's platforms have already been used at scale in Ukraine, which is the core of the investment case.

What to watch next

  • The production map. The test of the round is whether UFORCE can stand up factories in the United States, Europe, Asia-Pacific and the Middle East while keeping supply flowing to front-line troops.
  • The board. Cavoli's arrival gives the company a former NATO supreme commander in the boardroom as it sells to allied governments.
  • The next raise. A $6 billion valuation on a Series A sets a high bar, and the next round will be judged on production volumes and allied contracts rather than battlefield headlines.
