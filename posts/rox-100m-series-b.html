Rox Raises $100M at $1.3B to Put AI Agents on Sales Teams

By Jayesh Marathe · November 11, 2026 · 7 min read

Rox, a two-year-old startup building an AI agent that does the work of enterprise sales teams, has raised $100 million at a $1.3 billion valuation. The Series B, announced Tuesday, was co-led by General Catalyst and March Capital, and the company says its revenue grew more than tenfold in its first year in the market.

The round

  • The money. Rox announced a $100 million Series B financing at a $1.3 billion valuation on Tuesday, November 11. The round is the company's first priced financing since its seed and Series A, which it announced in November 2024 and which totaled $50 million.
  • The backers. General Catalyst and March Capital co-led the round. Existing investors Sequoia Capital and Factory doubled down on their investment, and new investors joined in, including Databricks Ventures and Prosperity7 Ventures.
  • The use of the money. Rox will use the capital to advance its AI research, build custom models and evaluation infrastructure, and expand its work with large enterprises across healthcare, industrial, financial services, energy and technology.
  • The founders. Rox was founded in 2024 by Ishan Mukherjee, Shriram Sridharan and Diogo Ribeiro, and is headquartered in San Francisco. Mukherjee is the chief executive, Sridharan the chief technology officer and Ribeiro the chief growth officer.

What Rox sells

  • The product. Rox researches and develops an AI revenue agent for the Global 2000, the world's largest companies. The agent generates pipeline, qualifies leads, books meetings, manages deals and expands accounts inside a company's existing systems, and it is built on each customer's individual data warehouse.
  • The pitch. Enterprise sales follows a power law, the company says: the top 15 percent of sales representatives bring in roughly 90 percent of revenue, while the average representative spends 40 percent of the time on administrative work, according to Salesforce. Rox was built to raise the ceiling for those teams, automating pipeline generation, deal execution and upsells so representatives can manage complex accounts and build relationships.
  • The results it claims. For a global IT services firm, Rox says it delivered $330 million in qualified pipeline in eight weeks, and representatives handed off pursuit preparation to the agent and got 1,000 hours back for selling. For a global medical technology company, it says it identified more than $200 million in new funding opportunities at medical facilities and helped the team close $24 million in revenue in five months.
  • More of the same. For a leading global engineering and professional services firm, Rox says it identified more than $310 million in potential opportunities. For a market-leading DevSecOps platform, it doubled outbound volume. For a leading payroll provider, it opened up 4 million prospects the sales team could not economically reach, turning them into meetings at 20 percent of the cost of traditional outreach. For a global industrial software company, it doubled sales team capacity and delivered more than 50 meetings in six weeks. For a large German engineering and technology company, it delivered 24.3 times return on investment in 90 days.
  • The headline numbers. In its first year in the market, Rox says it grew annual recurring revenue by more than ten times, and it is now deployed by leading public and private enterprises in non-technology industries including IT services, energy, commodities, medical technology, industrial manufacturing and automotive. It reports net revenue retention above 700 percent across enterprise customers, with some seeing about 25 times return on investment within 90 days.

The people behind it

  • The chief executive. "Rox is the product I wish I had," said Ishan Mukherjee, the co-founder and chief executive. "We built Rox for the Global 2000 because that is where the problem is the hardest and the stakes are the highest. Our own go-to-market runs entirely on Rox, with no other tools, and our customers are already generating hundreds of millions of dollars of pipeline at a fraction of the traditional cost."
  • The investors. Konstantine Buhler, a partner at Sequoia Capital, said Rox is a clear example of the real return on investment of AI, delivering measurable results for Global 2000 sales teams and chief revenue officers in under a quarter. Marc Bhargava, a managing director at General Catalyst, said Mukherjee is as obsessed with revenue as other founders are with rockets, and that boards are now telling revenue leaders to try something new rather than renew the incumbent. Sumant Mandal, a co-founder and managing partner at March Capital, said the team's combination of deep technical talent and real domain expertise showed up in conversations with delighted Global 2000 customers.
  • The customers. Manu Narayan, the chief information officer at GitLab, said Rox's agents help the revenue team find and research prospects and fit into the systems and governance standards the company already runs. Tanay Tiwary, the global head of digitization at Puma Energy, said the company would rather set the pace than wait, and is combining Rox with customer data and market intelligence to identify high-potential prospects.

Why it matters

  • The validation. Rox was named a Visionary in the 2026 Gartner Magic Quadrant for Revenue Action Orchestration and a Major Player in the 2026 IDC MarketScape for Worldwide Unified Revenue Orchestration Platforms, two analyst rankings that put it on the map for large buyers.
  • The hard problem. Revenue is the next frontier for AI agents and one of the hardest, the company says, because unlike code, where an agent can test its work immediately, sales outcomes take weeks or months to materialize. Rox says it is solving that with custom models and original research, including a revenue-agent benchmark to measure performance on real-world revenue tasks.
  • The price signal. Rox recently cut its outbound prospecting pricing by 50 percent, which it says was driven by research-led improvements, a sign that the cost of running its agents is falling fast enough to pass on to customers.

What to watch next

  • The build-out. The company says the new capital goes into AI research, custom models and evaluation infrastructure, and into expanding its enterprise customer base across healthcare, industrial, financial services, energy and technology.
  • The benchmark. Rox is launching a revenue-agent benchmark to measure performance on real-world revenue tasks, which will test whether its claims about pipeline and returns hold up against rivals.
  • The valuation test. A $1.3 billion valuation for a company founded in 2024 sets a high bar, and the next test is whether the tenfold revenue growth of its first year repeats in its second.
