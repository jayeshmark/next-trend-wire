Nooks Lands $500 Million to Build AI Sales Agents and Launches Node

By Editorial Team · 7 min read

Nooks has secured $500 million in growth capital from General Catalyst and launched Node, an AI personal assistant for sales, alongside a new Revenue Agent Platform that covers the full sales cycle. The company announced the package on November 4, pairing the funding with a product launch aimed at putting AI agents on every sales team.

What happened

  • General Catalyst committed $500 million. The money comes from General Catalyst's Customer Value Fund and is growth capital, not a traditional equity round. The fund takes a share of the returns its capital helps generate rather than buying a stake in Nooks, and Nooks did not disclose the terms.
  • The announcement came with two launches. Nooks introduced the Revenue Agent Platform, where sales reps and AI agents work the whole revenue lifecycle together, and Node, which the company calls the first AI personal assistant for sales.
  • The funding follows fast growth. Nooks says it is growing about three times year over year, with net revenue retention of 135 percent, and that more than 2,000 companies now run on its platform, including Glean, Notion, Deel, Factory and Replit.
  • The money funds hiring and expansion. Nooks said it will use the capital to bring new products to market, scale into the enterprise and internationally, and hire across the company, with more than 100 open roles.
  • The investor praised the business model. "AI changed how software gets built. Nooks is changing how it gets sold," said Pranav Singhvi, partner and co-head of the Customer Value Fund at General Catalyst, in the announcement.

Meet Node

  • Node works the deal before the rep does. Node is designed to work every deal in the background and come to the rep first, rather than waiting to be asked, which is how the company describes the shift from prompting AI to being prompted by it.
  • Its first job is deal management. Node remembers what was promised on the last call, drafts the follow up, and sends it once the rep approves. It flags it when someone on the buying committee goes quiet and drafts the move most likely to restart a stalled deal.
  • Every rep gets their own Node. Each member of the deal team gets a Node, and the Nodes share what is working across the team, so a tactic that wins one deal can be reused on the next.
  • Reps still approve every send. Nooks says 96 percent of emails drafted by its agents go out without edits, but a rep approves each one before it is sent.
  • General availability comes in early 2027. Node will be generally available in early 2027, and teams can sign up for early access now.

What the platform does

  • It covers the full revenue cycle. The Revenue Agent Platform runs Deal, Outbound, Expansion and Coaching Agents today, with Inbound Agents arriving in the first quarter of 2027.
  • Its agents learn from real sales work. Nooks says its agents train on more than 1 billion real sales interactions and rehearse each play against a simulated buyer before reaching a real one.
  • A true revenue agent owns an outcome. Nooks defines its agents by three traits: they are goal oriented, they hold durable context across months or years of calls and emails, and they get better with every interaction.
  • Customers build more pipeline. Nooks says customers typically build three times more pipeline with its platform, and that more than half of its customers use more than one Nooks product.
  • The company eats its own cooking. Nooks runs its own sales operation on its own platform, which the company says is why it knows what each dollar of sales and marketing spend turns into.

Why this funding is different

  • It is not a valuation event. Because the Customer Value Fund provides growth capital rather than equity, the commitment does not set a new valuation for Nooks the way a priced round would.
  • The fund backs proven economics. The Customer Value Fund invests in sales and marketing for companies with proven, predictable customer economics, and it is repaid from the customer value that spending creates.
  • The structure fits a sales company. Nooks argues the model works for it because its own go to market runs on Nooks, so the company can show exactly what the funded spending produces.
  • The timing matches the AI agent wave. Sales software is moving from tools that draft emails to agents that run deals, and the funding gives Nooks room to build for that shift while rivals race to do the same.
  • The bet is that AI adds salespeople. Nooks argues AI will make companies want more salespeople, not fewer, because agents take the follow ups and admin while reps spend their time on strategy, creativity and earning a buyer's trust.

What to watch next

  • Whether Node delivers on the demo. The test is whether an AI assistant that prompts the rep actually moves deals faster than the copilots and drafting tools sales teams already use.
  • How the $500 million gets spent. Whether the money buys enterprise deals and international growth, or whether it mostly funds a hiring spree in a crowded AI sales market.
  • The competition. How Salesforce, HubSpot and the wave of AI sales startups respond as agents move from drafting emails to owning parts of the sales cycle.
  • The retention number. Whether Nooks holds its 135 percent net revenue retention as it scales past 2,000 customers and into larger enterprise accounts.
  • The next round. Whether the growth capital lets Nooks delay a priced equity round, or whether a big valuation round follows once Node is generally available in early 2027.
