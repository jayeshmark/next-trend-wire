Coframe Raises $25 Million to Build Websites That Improve Themselves

By Jayesh Marathe · November 9, 2026 · 7 min read

Coframe has raised $35 million in total funding, including a $25 million Series A co-led by Khosla Ventures and Hanabi Capital, to build websites, landing pages and ads that continuously generate, test and improve themselves instead of waiting for the next redesign.

The round

  • The headline. Coframe announced $35 million in total funding, including a $25 million Series A co-led by Khosla Ventures and Hanabi Capital, with participation from leaders at OpenAI, Anthropic and Google DeepMind.
  • The backers. The company is backed by Khosla Ventures, Hanabi Capital, Nat Friedman, Antler and other technology investors.
  • The valuation. Coframe did not disclose its valuation after the Series A.
  • The founder. Coframe was founded in 2023 by Josh Payne, who previously co-founded Autograph with Tom Brady, which reached a valuation of more than $2 billion, and created GPT-Migrate, an early autonomous coding agent that became the top-ranked project on GitHub globally.
  • The team. Coframe has acquired companies founded by two early Optimizely executives, Bo Mohazzabi, now vice president of go-to-market, and Steven Ebin, now general manager of revenue optimization.

What Coframe builds

  • The pitch. Coframe calls its vision "the living internet." Instead of every visitor seeing the same static website, Coframe plugs into a company's website or advertising campaigns and continuously generates, tests and deploys new variations, personalizing what each visitor sees and improving performance in real time.
  • The engine. Behind it is a specialized transformer model trained on user interactions, and a team of AI agents, a strategist, a designer, a software engineer, a QA specialist and a data scientist, that can ideate, build, test and optimize hundreds of website and ad variations in minutes.
  • The speed. At Dropbox, an experiment that previously required four weeks of engineering, design and QA work was built and deployed in 30 minutes with Coframe.
  • The win rate. Across thousands of variants, the platform has achieved a 39 percent experiment win rate, more than three times the industry average, while increasing customers' testing velocity ten times or more.
  • The next step. Apps, email and other digital experiences are next on the roadmap, after websites, landing pages and ads.

The customer results

  • The headline number. Coframe says it has generated more than $300 million in incremental revenue for its customers, with conversion rates improving by more than 20 percent on average.
  • The standouts. The company reports conversion rate increases of 158 percent for Dropbox, 410 percent for Replit and 60 percent for Cash App.
  • The fast result. For one Fortune 500 customer, Coframe launched dozens of variations and delivered a 70 percent lift in conversion within 30 days of contract signature.
  • The customers. The customer base includes Dropbox, BCG, Block, Backcountry, Sur La Table and other leading brands.
  • The caveat. The revenue and conversion figures are company reported, and Coframe has not supplied an independent audit of them.

The founders' case

  • The vision. "We're building an internet that improves itself," said Josh Payne, founder and chief executive of Coframe. "A digital experience should understand what it is trying to accomplish, adapt to the person interacting with it and learn from every result. Instead of waiting months for the next redesign, companies should have websites and applications that get better every day."
  • The investor's view. "For decades, the web has been frozen in place, showing every visitor essentially the same page," said Vinod Khosla, founder of Khosla Ventures. "Coframe is building the living internet, where two people can visit the same website and have completely different experiences, personalized to who they are and what they need. These interfaces continuously learn and improve themselves. This is a huge leap beyond traditional A/B testing and the next iteration of the internet."
  • The engineering view. "The way most companies build and manage their user interfaces today is wrong, and in a year or two it will be obvious why that is," said Pavlo Razumovskyi, co-founder and vice president of engineering of Coframe. "User interfaces should improve themselves, and be personalized to every user."

What comes next

  • The AI agent web. Coframe is also building for a future where a growing share of web traffic comes from AI agents that research, compare and buy on people's behalf, and it is baking agent best practices directly into every website it builds.
  • The OpenAI work. That work is already underway through a collaboration with OpenAI on its ChatGPT ads offering, enabling customers to optimize the ad and the landing page as one continuously learning experience, building on an earlier partnership between the companies on training a specialized model for generating on-brand code and creative assets.
  • The funding. The new capital will accelerate product development, expand the company's AI agent capabilities and grow its operations.
