FDA Adds 21st Cinnamon Product to Lead Alert as Wise Wife Recall Reaches 14 States

By Jayesh Marathe · November 8, 2026 · 7 min read

The Food and Drug Administration has added a 21st ground cinnamon product to its public health alert on lead contamination, after a Connecticut laboratory found elevated lead in Wise Wife brand cinnamon and the distributor recalled the product in 14 states. SLR Food Distribution announced the recall on October 29, 2026, and the FDA posted the company's announcement the same day.

The recall

  • The product. The recall covers Wise Wife Brand Ground Cinnamon in a 110 gram clear plastic container with a black screw top cap, carrying UPC 0688474302754 and a best before date of April 25, 2027.
  • The distributor. SLR Food Distribution Inc. of Hicksville, New York, voluntarily recalled the cinnamon after a sample collected and analyzed by the Connecticut Agricultural Experiment Station revealed elevated levels of lead.
  • The states. The cinnamon was distributed to retail stores and grocery stores in Connecticut, Indiana, Kansas, Massachusetts, Michigan, Minnesota, North Carolina, Nebraska, New York, Ohio, Pennsylvania, Texas, Virginia, and Wisconsin between September 8, 2025, and June 28, 2026.
  • The illnesses. No illnesses have been reported in connection with the recalled cinnamon, according to the company announcement.
  • The advice. Consumers should not eat the recalled product and should either throw it away or return it to the place of purchase for a full refund.

The alert grows

  • The update. The FDA updated its public health alert on ground cinnamon to add the Wise Wife product, bringing the list to 21 products with elevated lead levels ranging from 2.03 to 7.68 parts per million.
  • The level. The FDA's alert lists the Wise Wife sample at 2.118 parts per million of lead, a level the agency says makes prolonged exposure potentially unsafe.
  • The pattern. This is the third public health alert the FDA has issued this year for ground cinnamon, and the agency said it issued the alert to protect cinnamon used in foods that babies and young children eat often.
  • The history. The FDA has issued earlier alerts on ground cinnamon on March 6, 2024, and July 25, 2024, and the agency has been testing cinnamon at retail with state partners ever since.
  • The repeat. This is the second time in about 13 months that a Wise Wife ground cinnamon product has been recalled over lead. In September 2025, the company recalled a smaller 50 gram jar after FDA testing found elevated lead.

Why lead in cinnamon matters

  • The risk. The FDA says consuming these products could contribute to elevated levels of lead in the blood, and long term exposure over months to years could contribute to adverse health effects, especially for people who already have elevated blood lead levels from other exposures.
  • The children. The very young are particularly vulnerable to the harmful effects of lead because of their smaller body sizes and rapid metabolism and growth, and high levels of exposure in early childhood can lead to learning disabilities, behavior difficulties, and lowered IQ.
  • The scale. The lead levels in this alert are far lower than the levels found in the cinnamon behind the 2023 WanaBana apple puree and applesauce recall, where the cinnamon contained between 2,270 and 5,110 parts per million and hundreds of babies and young children were found to have unsafe blood lead levels.
  • The check. Ground cinnamon has a long shelf life, so the FDA advises consumers to check their spice cupboards for the listed products and throw away any jar that matches, even if it was bought months ago.
