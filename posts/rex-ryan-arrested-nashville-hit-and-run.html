Rex Ryan Arrested After Allegedly Running Over Two Pedestrians' Feet

By Jayesh Marathe · November 11, 2026 · 7 min read

Rex Ryan, the former NFL head coach and current ESPN analyst, was arrested in Nashville on Wednesday after police say he ran over the feet of two pedestrians with his pickup truck and drove away. Ryan, 63, turned himself in on six misdemeanor warrants and was released early Wednesday morning on a $6,000 bond.

What happened

  • The arrest. Ryan turned himself in to the Metropolitan Nashville Police Department on Wednesday, November 11, on six outstanding misdemeanor warrants, police said in a statement. He was booked in the Davidson County Jail and released from custody early Wednesday morning after posting a $6,000 bond.
  • The incident. Police say Ryan struck two pedestrians with his pickup truck just before midnight on Thursday, November 5, on Broadway, the city's famed honky tonk street. The pedestrians were crossing Broadway from 4th Avenue with a walk sign when the truck turned right from 4th Avenue, running over both victims' feet and making contact with one of their legs.
  • The drive away. The pedestrians told police the vehicle kept going but stopped for the red light at Broadway and Representative John Lewis Way, where they waved down the driver. They said he stepped out of the truck to argue with them, got back in and drove away. They took pictures of the truck's license plate, which police said is registered to Ryan, and of the driver.
  • The charges. Ryan faces two counts of leaving the scene of an accident, two counts of failure to give information or render aid, and two counts of failure to obey a traffic control signal. He is scheduled to appear in a Nashville courtroom on December 18.

Who Rex Ryan is

  • The coach. Ryan was the head coach of the New York Jets from 2009 to 2014, taking the team to back-to-back AFC championship games in his first two seasons, and of the Buffalo Bills from 2015 to 2016. He is the son of the late Buddy Ryan, the architect of the Chicago Bears' famed 46 defense.
  • The analyst. Ryan has appeared on ESPN as an NFL analyst since 2016, and he remains a regular on the network's Sunday and Monday programming. ESPN said in a statement that it is aware of the arrest and is in touch with Ryan, and the network has not announced any change to his television assignments.
  • The personality. Ryan built his reputation as one of the loudest and most quotable coaches in the league, famous for guaranteeing a Super Bowl before his first season with the Jets and for his brash, confident style on the sideline and on television.

Why the charges matter

  • The pattern. Leaving the scene of an accident is the charge that carries the weight here. Police say Ryan did not stop to check on the two people he is accused of hitting, and instead argued with them at a red light and drove off, which is what turned a traffic incident into a criminal case.
  • The evidence. The case rests on the pedestrians' account and on the photographs they took of the license plate and the driver. Police said the plate is registered to Ryan, and the warrant followed.
  • The stakes. All six charges are misdemeanors, not felonies, and Ryan was released on bond within hours. But a conviction on leaving the scene of an accident would put a criminal record on a broadcaster whose job is built on credibility with an audience of football fans.
  • The status. The allegations against Ryan remain unproven. No plea has been entered, and the case will be heard in a Nashville courtroom on December 18.

Why it matters

  • A familiar face in trouble. Ryan is one of the most recognizable voices in NFL television, and his arrest puts a serious allegation in front of the football world on the same week his network is covering the stretch run of the season.
  • The detail that made it news. The arrest drew attention because of the strange specificity of the allegation: two pedestrians run over at the feet, on Broadway, by a former head coach driving a pickup truck through downtown Nashville.
  • The question for ESPN. The network has acknowledged the situation and said it is in touch with Ryan. Whether he stays on the air while the case moves through court is now the question hanging over his assignments.

What to watch next

  • The court date. Ryan is scheduled to appear in a Nashville courtroom on December 18, when the case will move to its next step.
  • The network. ESPN has not announced any change to Ryan's role, and any decision about his assignments will be watched closely in the weeks ahead.
  • The victims. Police have not released the names or the condition of the two pedestrians, and no further detail about their injuries has been made public.
