Paul George Drops 36 on 76ers in Revenge Game Rout

By Jayesh Marathe · November 11, 2026 · 7 min read

Paul George scored 36 points against his old team as the Boston Celtics beat the Philadelphia 76ers 123-101 on Tuesday night, spoiling Jaylen Brown's revenge game and taking first place in the Eastern Conference. George, traded to Boston in the summer blockbuster that sent Brown to Philadelphia, torched the team that moved him in his first game back.

What happened

  • The headline number. George finished with 36 points on 14-of-22 shooting, including 6 of 11 from three-point range, with five rebounds and one assist in just over 30 minutes, and he turned the ball over only once.
  • The fourth-quarter burst. He scored 14 points in a four-minute stretch of the fourth quarter, pushing a tight game open as the lead ballooned to 111-87, and Boston led by as many as 29 points down the stretch.
  • The fast start. George set the tone early with 17 points on 7-of-11 shooting in the first half, as Boston took a 62-51 lead into the break and never trailed again.
  • The game inside the game. Jayson Tatum added 21 points, six rebounds, and four assists for Boston, including a poster dunk over LeBron James in the third quarter, while Payton Pritchard scored 18 points off the bench.
  • The Philly rally that fell short. Tyrese Maxey scored 24 points for the 76ers and tied the game at 79-79 with a three-pointer late in the third quarter, but Boston closed the period on a 12-3 run, Pritchard opened the fourth with two more threes, and the game was gone.
  • The team numbers. Boston shot 22 of 49 from three-point range, eight Celtics hit at least one three, and the Boston bench outscored Philadelphia's 49-29 in a road win at Xfinity Mobile Arena.
  • The standings flip. The win moved Boston to 9-2 with a fourth straight victory, lifting the Celtics past the 9-3 76ers into first place in the Eastern Conference.

The trade that set it up

  • The July blockbuster. In July, Boston sent Jaylen Brown to Philadelphia in exchange for George and draft picks, a trade that shocked the league because Brown had spent a decade in Boston and won the 2024 championship alongside Tatum.
  • The forgotten man. All the talk around the trade centered on Brown, the younger star and All-NBA guard, while George arrived in Boston as the afterthought, a 36-year-old veteran coming off two seasons in Philadelphia.
  • The other reunion. Tuesday was also Brown's first game against the team he called home for a decade, and the pregame greeting between Brown and Tatum was barely acknowledged, with reports of a beef between the two former title-winning partners hanging over the night.
  • Brown's quiet night. Brown never found his rhythm, finishing with 13 points and four turnovers in under 30 minutes as early foul trouble kept him on the bench, and the revenge game he wanted never materialized.
  • The history between the teams. The matchup carried extra weight because Philadelphia had rallied from a 3-1 deficit to beat Boston in the first round of last spring's playoffs, so the Celtics arrived in Philadelphia with a score to settle of their own.
  • The crowd noticed. NBC's broadcast team of Noah Eagle and Reggie Miller could not ignore the reaction in the building as George kept scoring, with the Philadelphia crowd left to watch the player it traded away put on a show in green.

Why it matters

  • George answered the only question that mattered. The trade was framed as Boston getting older while Philadelphia got a star in his prime, and one game does not settle that argument, but 36 points on 63.6 percent shooting in his old building is the loudest possible reply.
  • Boston's depth is the real story. The Celtics won by 22 on the road with eight players hitting threes and a 49-29 bench edge, which says the roster around George and Tatum is deeper than the team that lost to Philadelphia last spring.
  • The East has a new leader. Boston now sits alone at the top of the conference at 9-2, and the team that owned first place on Tuesday morning, Philadelphia, now chases it.
  • Brown's adjustment is real. The 76ers built their season around Brown joining Embiid, Maxey, and LeBron James, and his 13-point night in the biggest regular-season game so far shows the fit is still a work in progress.

Second order effects

  • The Tatum and Brown story is not over. The frosty pregame moment and the reports of tension between the two former teammates will follow both teams until they meet again, and the next matchup is January 21 at TD Garden.
  • Philadelphia's defense has a problem. The 76ers left George open on the perimeter repeatedly, and a team with championship ambitions cannot give a shooter of his caliber that many clean looks.
  • The age question flips. George is 36 and was supposed to be the declining asset in the trade, but on Tuesday he looked like the best player on a floor full of stars, which complicates every judgment made about the deal in July.
  • Boston's road form. The Celtics finished a four-game road trip with a statement win, and the victory showed they can win big in a hostile building without their best basketball for three quarters.

What to watch next

  • Celtics at Toronto. Boston visits the Toronto Raptors on Wednesday, November 11, to close out a back-to-back with a chance to stretch the winning streak to five.
  • 76ers host Toronto. Philadelphia hosts the same Raptors on Thursday, November 12, with a chance to answer a bad loss before the noise around Brown grows louder.
  • The rematch. The teams meet again on January 21 in Boston, and the first game set the terms: George owns the building in Philadelphia, and Brown has a revenge game of his own still to play.
  • The East race. Boston and Philadelphia are now separated by half a game at the top of the conference, and the next month will show whether Tuesday was a statement or just a hot shooting night.
