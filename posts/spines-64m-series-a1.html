Spines Raises $64 Million as AI Publishing Platform Hits a $50 Million Revenue Run Rate

By Editorial Team · 7 min read

Spines secured $64 million in growth capital on November 9, combining a $29 million Series A-1 equity round with a $35 million non-dilutive growth facility, as the AI publishing platform reported a $50 million revenue run rate and 400 percent year over year growth.

The round

  • The headline. Spines announced $64 million in growth capital to accelerate its expansion, with a $29 million Series A-1 equity round led by Zeev Ventures and a $35 million non-dilutive growth financing facility from Twelve.
  • The backers. Aleph, NordStar, M-Fund and LionTree joined the equity round, alongside angel investor Dan Senor, the chief public affairs officer at Elliott Investment Management.
  • The lead. The round was led by Oren Zeev of Zeev Ventures, an early investor in Audible and a longtime investor in category defining technology companies.
  • The history. Aleph led Spines' seed round and went on to participate in both the Series A and the current Series A-1, staying with the company from its earliest days.
  • The growth. Spines also announced that it has hit a $50 million revenue run rate, with more than 8,000 American authors publishing books through the platform and around 400 new authors joining every month.

What Spines does

  • The pitch. Spines is an agentic AI publishing platform built to help authors turn written manuscripts into professionally published books and reach readers around the world.
  • The workflow. Authors arrive with a manuscript and use the platform to manage the entire publishing journey, with specialized AI agents performing tasks like editing, proofreading, cover design, formatting, metadata, translation, audiobooks, distribution, royalty management and marketing.
  • The human layer. Authors can also work with Spines' in-house human editors, proofreaders, designers and native speaking language professionals to add human expertise and support.
  • The speed. The platform compresses a process that traditionally took 12 to 18 months into a matter of weeks, while giving authors greater visibility, flexibility and control throughout.
  • The reach. Spines distributes books through more than 100 global distribution channels, tracking sales across platforms and giving authors a centralized royalty management system that shows what their books are earning across channels and markets.

The founder's case

  • Empower, not replace. "We don't believe the future of publishing is AI writing books. We believe the future is AI giving authors superpowers," said Yehuda Niv, founder and chief executive of Spines. "The writing, the story and the knowledge come from the author. Our job is to build the technology and infrastructure around them."
  • The gatekeepers. "For most of publishing history, gatekeepers decided who was worthy of becoming an author and which stories deserved to reach the world," Niv said. "Anyone with a story, an idea or knowledge to share deserves the opportunity to publish it."
  • The investor's view. "Spines has all the ingredients to become the category leader in the next generation of publishing," said Oren Zeev, Founding Partner of Zeev Ventures. "I've seen it first-hand; a close friend of mine published his book with Spines and had a great experience."
  • The author's view. "Spines' growth since our seed investment has been extraordinary, and it's clear that authors are hungry for this kind of AI-powered publishing solution," said Michael Eisenberg, Co-Founder and General Partner at Aleph, who is himself the author of nine books.

What comes next

  • New tools. Spines is expanding its audience development and marketing tools, with campaign management features that help authors create, launch and manage campaigns designed to reach relevant readers.
  • More languages. The platform currently supports translation from English into seven target languages, Spanish, Italian, French, German, Portuguese, Polish and Hebrew, combining AI translation with native speaking human review, and plans to expand to 20 target languages over the next six months.
  • Audiobooks and illustration. Authors can create professionally narrated audiobooks using synthetic voices, or create a model of their own voice from a short recording and use it to narrate their book, even in other languages, while children's book authors get specialized AI illustration technology designed to maintain consistent characters across an entire book.
  • The expansion. The funding will accelerate Spines' growth as it pushes further into global markets, with Tel Aviv and New York as its home bases.
