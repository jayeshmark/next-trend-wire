Curry Exits After Six Minutes; Warriors Await MRI Verdict

By Jayesh Marathe · November 12, 2026 · 7 min read

Stephen Curry left the Golden State Warriors' game against the Houston Rockets six minutes in with right abdominal soreness, went for an MRI during the second half, and left the Warriors waiting on the test results with their eight game winning streak over. Houston won 115-108 at Toyota Center on Wednesday night.

What happened

  • The exit. Curry hit a 28 foot three pointer with about seven and a half minutes left in the first quarter, grabbed at his right side, and checked out at the 6:25 mark. He walked to the locker room with Drew Yoder, the team's director of sports medicine, and did not return.
  • The ruling. The Warriors ruled Curry out for the rest of the game with right abdominal soreness. He finished the night with three points on 1 of 3 shooting in six minutes, his only basket the three pointer he hit just before the pain started.
  • The fall before it. Curry had fallen to the floor on a drive just before he made the three pointer. He stayed down for a couple of possessions, then got up and kept playing until the next stoppage, when he headed for the locker room.
  • The MRI. Curry left the arena during the second half to undergo an MRI. Head coach Steve Kerr confirmed the scan after the game and said the team had not received the results when he spoke to reporters.
  • The words from Kerr. Kerr said Curry told him he had tweaked something in his abdominal area and had never felt it before. "I mean, concern is high, but I have no idea the extent of the injury," Kerr said after the game.
  • The reassurance. Guard Will Richard said Curry spoke to his teammates at halftime before leaving for the scan. "He made sure everybody knew he was OK," Richard said. That was Curry's message to the team, not a medical clearance.

The game without him

  • The result. Houston won 115-108, snapping Golden State's eight game winning streak, the team's longest since 2022. The Warriors fell to 8-4, and the Rockets improved to 9-3.
  • The Rockets' night. Kevin Durant scored 20 points, Alperen Sengun added 19 points, 13 rebounds and nine assists, and Amen Thompson scored 19. Reed Sheppard hit three fourth quarter three pointers as Houston pulled away late.
  • The fill ins. Rookie Yaxel Lendeborg led the Warriors with 25 points and 10 rebounds, and Brandin Podziemski added 22 points. De'Anthony Melton is in line for a bigger role if Curry misses time.
  • The flashpoint. Houston's Marcus Smart was ejected late in the third quarter for punching Golden State center Charles Bassey. The ejection added to a night that had already gone wrong for the visitors.
  • The road trip. The game opened a five game road trip for Golden State. The Warriors visit San Antonio on Friday night in an NBA Cup game, then play New Orleans, Dallas and Memphis.

Why it matters

  • The season he was having. Curry entered Wednesday ranked second in the NBA at 30.6 points a game, with 4.3 assists and 4.1 rebounds through nine games of his 18th season. He is 38, and the team has managed his minutes carefully all year.
  • The thin roster. Golden State is already short handed. Jimmy Butler and Moses Moody are out with knee injuries, Kristaps Porzingis and Gary Payton II are out, and Brandon Williams is expected to miss at least a week with a mild left hamstring strain. Will Richard is playing through a torn ligament in his thumb.
  • The missed games so far. Curry has already missed two games this season, held out of a loss at Phoenix on October 24 and a win over the Los Angeles Clippers on November 1, both times for right knee injury management on the second night of a back to back.
  • The unknown. The Warriors have announced no diagnosis and no timetable. Until the MRI results arrive, neither Curry's halftime reassurance nor Kerr's concern settles how long he will be out, and the Friday game at San Antonio is the first test.

What to watch next

  • The MRI results. The first update from the team will say whether the injury is a short absence or something that costs Curry weeks, and it will set the tone for the rest of the road trip.
  • The Friday status. Curry's availability for the San Antonio game will be the first public signal, with the team's injury report due the day before.
  • The backcourt plan. If Curry sits, Podziemski and Melton carry the offense, and the Warriors will have to find scoring from a roster that is already missing four rotation players.
  • The streak reset. Golden State's eight game run ended in Houston. The question now is whether the team can bank road wins without its best player or whether the trip turns into a slide.
