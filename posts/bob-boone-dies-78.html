Bob Boone, Catcher of Phillies' First World Series Title, Dies at 78

By Jayesh Marathe · November 10, 2026 · 7 min read

Bob Boone, the catcher who anchored the Philadelphia Phillies' first World Series championship team and stood at the center of baseball's first three generation family, has died at 78. His son Bret Boone announced the death on social media on Tuesday, and the Phillies said Boone died on Monday. No cause of death was announced.

The announcement

  • His son confirmed it. Bret Boone, a former major league second baseman, wrote that his father was "a man of great integrity" and "a great dad, husband, and patriarch," adding, "It was a privilege to call him my father. Baseball lost a great one today."
  • The Phillies mourned him. "Bob was an incredible player, teammate and family man. He embodied everything we believe exemplary athletes should represent, both on and off the field," the Phillies said in a statement. "We were proud to have him as part of the Phillies family during one of the most historic runs in team history."
  • The Yankees mourned him too. "The Yankees mourn the passing of Bob Boone, who grew up in our sport and spent a lifetime giving back to the game he loved as an exceptional player, respected manager and distinguished executive," the Yankees said in a statement. "His legacy is profound, and we can see the values he embodied carried forward by his son, Aaron, in his leadership of the Yankees."
  • The union confirmed it. The Major League Baseball Players Association also confirmed Boone's passing, and the Angels, one of his former clubs, joined the baseball community in mourning him.

The player

  • The 1980 title. Boone caught for the Phillies team that beat the Kansas City Royals in the 1980 World Series, the first championship in franchise history, and he batted .412 in the six game series with two doubles and four RBIs.
  • A four time All-Star. Boone made the National League All-Star team three times with the Phillies, in 1976, 1978, and 1979, and made the American League team in 1983 with the Angels.
  • Seven Gold Gloves. Boone won seven Gold Glove Awards as a catcher, the mark of a defender pitchers trusted to run a game.
  • Nineteen seasons. Boone played 2,264 games across 19 major league seasons, with the Phillies from 1972 to 1981, the California Angels from 1982 to 1988, and the Kansas City Royals in 1989 and 1990. He finished with 1,838 hits, 105 home runs, and 826 RBIs.
  • A catcher's record. Boone caught 2,225 games in his career, a record when he retired, and he was behind the plate for Mike Witt's perfect game against the Texas Rangers on September 30, 1984.
  • The Phillies honored him. Boone was inducted into the Philadelphia Baseball Wall of Fame in 2005, and he spent 10 of his 19 seasons in a Phillies uniform.

The manager and executive

  • Two clubs as manager. Boone managed the Kansas City Royals from 1995 to 1997 and the Cincinnati Reds from 2001 to 2003, compiling an overall record of 371 wins and 444 losses, and he managed his son Aaron for nearly three seasons in Cincinnati.
  • A second career in front offices. Boone joined the Washington Nationals' front office in 2005 and became the team's assistant general manager and vice president for player development in 2009, earning a championship ring for the Nationals' World Series title in 2019.
  • A teammate's tribute. "It was a sorrowful day yesterday as we learned of the passing of Bob Boone," Hall of Famer Mike Schmidt said in a statement. "My teammate for over a decade, and friend for 50 years. We played in the minors together where he gave up third base so I could play it, only to become one of the best catchers in baseball history."
  • A quiet leader. "Bob Boone was a quiet leader, both on and off the field," former Phillies shortstop Larry Bowa said. "Without question, he was the backbone of our team. He was a man of deep faith who loved his family dearly."

The family

  • Three generations. Boone was the middle link in baseball's first three generation major league family. His father, Ray Boone, was an All-Star infielder. His sons Aaron and Bret both played in the majors, and Aaron now manages the New York Yankees.
  • A Stanford start. Born in San Diego on November 19, 1947, Boone starred at Stanford and was drafted by the Phillies in the sixth round in 1969 as a third baseman before moving behind the plate.
  • The family he leaves. The Phillies extended condolences to Boone's wife Sue, his sons Bret, Aaron, and Matt, and his extended family, friends, and former teammates.

Why he mattered

  • The anchor of a first title. The Phillies had waited nearly a century for a championship before 1980, and Boone was the catcher who guided the pitching staff through it, a steady presence in the biggest games the franchise had ever played.
  • The standard for the position. Seven Gold Gloves and more than 2,200 games behind the plate made Boone the model of a defense first catcher, the kind of player whose value showed up in the standings more than in the box score.
  • A family that became baseball history. The Boones went from Ray to Bob to Aaron and Bret across three generations, and Bob Boone was the link that tied them together, a player, a manager, an executive, and the father of a manager still running a team today.
