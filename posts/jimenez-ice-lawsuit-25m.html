US Citizen Shot by ICE Sues for $25M After Charges Are Dropped

By Jayesh Marathe · November 9, 2026 · 7 min read

Carlos Jimenez, a US citizen from Ontario, California, filed a 54-page federal lawsuit on November 6 seeking more than $25 million from the Trump administration and several federal officers. The lawsuit alleges that an ICE officer shot him in the back and shoulder during an immigration operation in October 2025 and that prosecutors then pursued him on fabricated evidence, and the criminal charges against him were dismissed with prejudice on November 2.

The shooting

  • The morning. The complaint says Jimenez was driving to work at a food pantry on the morning of October 30, 2025, when he came upon ICE officers conducting an operation in his neighborhood.
  • The warning. The complaint says Jimenez approached the officers around 7 a.m. to warn them that children would soon arrive at a nearby bus stop.
  • The gun. The lawsuit alleges that ICE officer Eusebio Ortiz pointed a gun at Jimenez's face, abused him verbally, and threatened to shoot if he did not leave.
  • The shots. The complaint says Ortiz fired several rounds through a rear passenger-side window as Jimenez tried to turn his car around, hitting him in the back and right shoulder.
  • The injuries. The lawsuit says Jimenez suffered a displaced fracture of his right shoulder blade and that bullet fragments remain lodged near his right lung.
  • The hospital. The complaint says he was taken to Riverside Community Hospital as a trauma patient.

The prosecution and the dismissal

  • The charge. The Justice Department charged Jimenez with assaulting a federal officer, alleging that he rapidly accelerated in reverse toward an officer, and a grand jury returned an indictment on November 18, 2025.
  • The diversion. After a judge ordered the government to disclose internal communications and operational records, Jimenez entered a diversion agreement.
  • The dismissal. Judge Kenly Kiya Kato dismissed the criminal indictment with prejudice on November 2, which means the charges cannot be brought again.
  • The Justice Department's position. A Justice Department spokesperson did not comment on the lawsuit but said Jimenez could have taken the case to trial and instead requested that the office agree to resolve it through a diversion agreement.
  • The fabrication claim. The lawsuit alleges that an FBI agent falsified investigative reports by attributing statements to witnesses who later denied making them or witnessing the shooting.

The government's account

  • DHS version. The Department of Homeland Security has said Jimenez was not the target of the operation, pulled his vehicle in front of officers, was ordered to leave, and then reversed directly at them.
  • Self-defense. DHS has said the officer fired in self-defense.
  • The dispute. The two accounts disagree on whether any officer was in the vehicle's path when the shots were fired. The complaint says no officer was in the path, while DHS says Jimenez reversed at the officers.

The lawsuit

  • The defendants. The complaint names the Trump administration, ICE officer Eusebio Ortiz, two Customs and Border Protection officers identified as N. Jimenez and S. Cosey, FBI Special Agent Estevan R. Banuelos, San Bernardino County, and 20 unidentified defendants.
  • The claims. The 54-page complaint makes 13 claims, including excessive force, false arrest, malicious prosecution, and denial of medical care.
  • The money. It seeks $25 million in non-economic damages plus about $179,000 for medical bills, lost wages, property damage, and other costs.
  • The status. The allegations have not been tested in court, and the filing does not establish that the defendants are liable.
