Cristiano Ronaldo Retires From International Football After 23 Years With Portugal

By Editorial Team · 7 min read

Cristiano Ronaldo has retired from international football, closing a 23 year career with Portugal that produced a record 146 goals in 234 appearances. The 41 year old announced the decision on Thursday, November 5, 2026, in a video posted to his social media accounts, telling supporters the time had come to hand back the No. 7 shirt.

The announcement

  • The message. Ronaldo said the day had come to return the No. 7 shirt of the national team to the Portuguese people, adding that for 23 years he had the privilege and the responsibility to wear it, defend it, and carry it to stadiums around the world. He said the time had come to give it to the future.
  • The farewell line. He closed the video by saying he leaves with his head held high, proud of the path he took and aware that he gave everything he had for Portugal, and that although his playing journey with the national team is over, he will never stop being one of the Portuguese people. He captioned the post in Portuguese, "Portugal estará sempre em mim," meaning Portugal will always be in me.
  • The timing. Ronaldo said he would hand back the shirt after the November international break, and he announced the decision one day before Portugal were due to name their squad for two Nations League fixtures on November 14 and 17.
  • The club career continues. The retirement covers international football only. Ronaldo will keep playing for Al-Nassr in the Saudi Pro League, where he has played since 2023. He signed a two year extension with the club in June 2025 that runs to June 2027, and he told Vogue in an interview published in August that this is probably his last year of football.

A record that will stand for a generation

  • The appearances. Ronaldo made 234 appearances for Portugal, the most by any man in international football, after debuting as a substitute against Kazakhstan in August 2003 at 18 years old.
  • The goals. He scored 146 goals for Portugal, the most in men's international football, a record that sits far ahead of every other player in the history of the game.
  • The trophies. He captained Portugal to the country's first major title at Euro 2016 and added Nations League trophies in 2019 and 2025, the three major international honours of his career.
  • The World Cups. His career spanned six World Cups, and his last attempt ended at the 2026 tournament, where Portugal lost to Spain in the round of 16.
  • The final match. His last appearance in a Portugal shirt came on September 24, 2026, a 1-0 win over Wales in the Nations League, a match that now stands as the quiet end of the most prolific international career ever played.

The uneasy ending

  • The fallout. The retirement followed a dispute with Portugal head coach Jorge Jesus, who left Ronaldo out of the starting lineup during the September international window and did not use him against Norway.
  • The walkout. Angered by the decision, Ronaldo walked away from the national team on September 30, departing one day before a Nations League match in Denmark.
  • The punishment. The Portuguese Football Federation then fined Ronaldo 255 euros and handed him a 30 day suspension, a sanction that would not have seen him miss any matches.
  • The silence on the rift. Ronaldo did not mention Jesus or the dispute in his retirement announcement, and Jesus said on Thursday, November 5, that he had previously hoped to bring the forward back into the national team setup.
  • The contrast. The farewell arrived through a social media video rather than a stadium ovation, an ending that sits awkwardly against a career built on the biggest stages in world football.

The federation promises a tribute

  • The statement. The Portuguese Football Federation thanked Ronaldo in a statement on Thursday, calling him its eternal captain and describing him as an inspirational leader, a symbol of dedication, and an embodiment of the national team's spirit.
  • The highest praise. The federation said Ronaldo elevated Portugal's name and inspired generations of young people who dream of following in his footsteps, calling him, for many, the best player of all time.
  • The tribute plan. The federation said it refuses to let the farewell pass without a proper tribute and that it is its firm intention to organise, plan, participate in, and contribute to an event worthy of his career, giving fans and the country another chance to applaud the player who gave them so much. No date, venue, or format has been announced.
  • The president's words. Federation president Pedro Proenca wrote that Cristiano Ronaldo will be, for Portugal, more than a player, calling him a man, an athlete, a reference, a hero, and a leader, and adding, "Our captain."
  • The open door. The federation closed its message by telling Ronaldo that the national team will always be his home, and it signed off by looking ahead to his pursuit of 1,000 career goals, a milestone he can still chase at club level with Al-Nassr.
