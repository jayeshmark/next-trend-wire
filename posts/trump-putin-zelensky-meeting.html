Trump Says a Putin-Zelensky Meeting Is Coming 'Very Soon'.

By Editorial Team · 7 min read

President Donald Trump said a meeting between Vladimir Putin and Volodymyr Zelensky will be set up 'very soon,' claiming a lot of progress toward ending the Russia-Ukraine war. He gave no date, location, or format, and neither Moscow nor Kyiv has confirmed that the two leaders agreed to meet.

What happened

  • The claim, in his words. Trump told reporters at Joint Base Andrews on October 28 and said: "We're making a lot of progress with Russia-Ukraine. There'll be a meeting set up very soon." Asked who, he named Putin and Zelenskyy and said he thought a lot of progress would be made. He added it would happen "over the next not too long", and that progress could be made without a face-to-face meeting.
  • No details attached. Trump gave no date, location, or format, and neither Kyiv nor Moscow had publicly confirmed an arrangement for a leaders' summit after his remarks. What exists is a claim about a meeting, not a meeting itself. A face-to-face meeting would be unprecedented since Russia's invasion began in February 2022.
  • The setting. Trump had discussed both wars with Secretary of State Marco Rubio and envoy Steve Witkoff aboard Air Force One, returning from Texas ahead of the November 3 midterms.
  • The November track already exists. Zelensky said on Sunday that a trilateral meeting between Kyiv, Washington, and Moscow would take place in November to restart stalled talks. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said it was planned in the United Arab Emirates, and Zelensky said in remarks reported October 28 that he expected a meeting of the three teams in early November, cautioning that it depended on the US side.
  • The Moscow invitation. On October 27, Putin aide Yuri Ushakov said Putin had again invited Zelensky to Moscow during a call with Finnish President Alexander Stubb and Norwegian Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Støre, promising all necessary conditions including security. Zelensky has repeatedly rejected a Moscow trip, inviting Putin to Kyiv instead. Stubb called the call "frank and substantive", while Støre said Putin had largely repeated his established position and major differences remained.

Why it matters

  • A leaders' meeting is the missing piece. Everything so far has happened at team level or through intermediaries, while territorial issues, by Zelensky's own account, can only be settled at leadership level. It is the one format that could decide something rather than prepare to decide it.
  • Putin has said no to this format before. Putin has refused Kyiv's terms for a meeting, insisting on Moscow as the venue and documents prepared first, so the claim is either a breakthrough in his position or running ahead of it.
  • The timing is political. The claim came days before the November 3 midterms, from a president campaigning in Texas. A promise of peace progress plays well in an election week, and any meeting announced before then will read as campaign news.
  • The war is in its fifth year. Russia's invasion began in February 2022, tens of thousands have been killed, and a year of US-led talks since Trump returned in January 2025 produced no breakthrough. Against that record, "very soon" needs a date to mean anything.

Second order effects

  • Kyiv and Moscow now have to answer. The claim forces both capitals to say publicly whether they agreed to anything. Silence becomes its own signal, and a denial turns diplomacy into a credibility question.
  • The November track gets crowded. If a leaders' meeting is really coming "very soon", it collides with the November team-level meeting both sides already described. Which happens first: preparation for the leaders, or an excuse to delay them?
  • Europe's role sharpens. Stubb and Støre called Putin after consultations with Ukraine and the United States. If Washington drives toward a leaders' meeting, Europe will want a say in what is discussed, especially territory and security guarantees.
  • The credibility gap widens. Trump returned promising to end the war in 24 hours; more than a year later there is no settlement. Each "very soon" that passes without a meeting makes the next one harder to believe.

Third order effects

  • The meaning of "meeting" is at stake. Trump left open whether it would be face-to-face. If the event turns out to be a video call or trilateral session rather than Putin and Zelensky in a room, the headline will have promised more than the event delivers.
  • Territory is the wall. The core dispute has not moved: Putin insists Moscow intends to take the rest of eastern Ukraine, while Zelensky says territory must be settled at leadership level and Ukraine will not discuss enforced neutrality or limits on its armed forces. A meeting does not remove that wall.
  • A precedent for ending wars by summit. If it produces even a partial deal, it becomes the model for how this war ends: leaders first, details later. If it fails publicly, future leaders will refuse to meet until everything is pre-agreed.
  • Announcements substitute for progress. The longer the war runs, the more claims without a settlement train audiences to treat diplomacy as performance. The risk is not just that this meeting fails, but that people stop believing meetings matter.

What to watch next

  • A date, a place, or a denial. Watch for either capital to confirm a date and location, or to say no such meeting was agreed. That single fact decides whether this story is diplomacy or messaging.
  • The November trilateral meeting. Watch whether the team-level meeting happens in early November as Zelensky described, and whether it prepares a leaders' meeting. If it slips, the "very soon" claim slips with it.
  • The Moscow invitation. Watch whether Zelensky answers Putin's invitation to Moscow, or repeats his counter-invitation to Kyiv. The venue fight shows who sets the terms.
  • The Oleshky ceasefire. Watch whether the local ceasefire around Oleshky holds, or expands further, or collapses. A truce that holds is the only concrete progress on the table.
