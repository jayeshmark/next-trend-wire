Dallas Handed Oklahoma City Its First Loss of the Season, 120-96

By Editorial Team · 7 min read

The Dallas Mavericks beat the Oklahoma City Thunder 120-96 on October 28, 2026, at American Airlines Center, ending the Thunder's unbeaten start to the season. Cooper Flagg led Dallas with 26 points, eight assists and five rebounds without a turnover, while Oklahoma City fell to 4-1 and Dallas improved to 3-2.

What happened

  • The final score. According to the official NBA box score, Dallas won all four quarters, 33-30, 32-29, 29-22 and 26-15, on the way to a 120-96 final. Dallas led by as many as 27 points. The Mavericks led 65-59 at halftime, then pulled away in the second half as Oklahoma City managed only 37 points after the break.
  • Flagg's night. Flagg finished with 26 points, eight assists and five rebounds in 32 minutes and 56 seconds, shooting 10 of 23 from the field and committing zero turnovers. He scored 12 of his points in the first quarter, setting the tone before Dallas took control in the third.
  • The supporting cast. P.J. Washington added 18 points and nine rebounds, Kyrie Irving scored 15, and Zaccharie Risacher came off the bench for 13 points on 5 of 6 shooting. Dallas reserves combined for 43 points, with Caleb Martin scoring 10 in his first game of the season and Marcus Sasser and Morez Johnson Jr. adding 10 each.
  • The three point gap. Dallas made 16 of 45 three point attempts while Oklahoma City made only 5 of 32. The shooting gap decided a game that was close at halftime, because Dallas kept making shots while Oklahoma City went cold, scoring only 37 points after the break.
  • A short handed Thunder. Oklahoma City's roster listed Jalen Williams, Cason Wallace, Chet Holmgren and Alex Caruso as inactive with injury or illness, along with Nikola Topic and Thomas Sorber. That left Shai Gilgeous-Alexander and Ajay Mitchell to carry the offense, and Mitchell answered with a team high 29 points while Gilgeous-Alexander scored 21.
  • The records. Dallas moved to 3-2 and Oklahoma City fell to 4-1, ending the Thunder's four game winning streak to open the season.

Why it matters

  • Flagg is already the story. In his second season, Flagg did not just score against the defending champions, he ran the game, with eight assists against zero turnovers in nearly 33 minutes. A young star who can create for others as well as score changes what Dallas can be.
  • The loss is about depth, not the top. Oklahoma City still got 29 points from Mitchell and 21 from Gilgeous-Alexander, but nobody else reached 15, and the bench could not keep the game close once Dallas surged. That is the predictable cost of losing four rotation players at once, and it says little about the Thunder at full strength.
  • Dallas needed a statement. The Mavericks moved above .500 at 3-2, and they did it against the team that sets the standard in the West. Early season wins like this one change how a locker room sees itself, especially for a team still working out roles around a young star and a veteran guard.
  • The gap was effort as much as shooting. Dallas won the fast break battle 17-0, which points to energy and activity rather than luck. When a short handed team gets outworked in those categories, the scoreboard usually follows, and this one did.

Second order effects

  • Oklahoma City's rotation gets tested for real. With Williams and Wallace out for an extended stretch and Holmgren and Caruso also sidelined, the Thunder must now find reliable minutes from players who were supposed to be depth. That accelerates development for young players like Mitchell, but it also raises the risk of wearing down Gilgeous-Alexander early in a long season.
  • Mitchell's emergence changes the math. A 29 point night against Dallas suggests Oklahoma City has a third scorer it did not plan on needing this soon. If Mitchell keeps producing, his role may stay large even when the injured players return.
  • Dallas has a blueprint. The Mavericks showed they can beat an elite team by defending without fouling, sharing the ball, and letting Flagg make decisions. If that formula holds, Dallas has an identity, and identities travel better than hot shooting nights.

Third order effects

  • Rookie sensations reshape franchises faster than rebuilds used to. When a young player can already run an offense against the champions, the timeline for contention compresses. That pulls trade deadlines and contract decisions forward by years.
  • Injury luck becomes a championship variable. The Thunder built the deepest roster in the league, and even they look ordinary when four rotation players go down at once. That makes depth the actual margin between a title and an early exit.

What to watch next

  • The injury updates. Watch how long Williams, Wallace, Holmgren and Caruso stay out, because the length of those absences decides whether this loss was a blip or the start of a difficult stretch for Oklahoma City.
  • Flagg's consistency. Watch whether Flagg follows this game with similar all around performances, because one great night against a short handed team is a highlight, while a month of them is a leap.
  • Oklahoma City's shooting. Watch whether the Thunder's three point shooting recovers with healthier legs and better looks, because 5 of 32 is an outlier for a team built on spacing and ball movement.
  • The rematches. The teams meet again later in the season. Watch whether those games look different with a healthier Thunder roster, because that will say more about both teams than this one did.
