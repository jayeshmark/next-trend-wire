James Gunn and Peter Safran Step Down at DC Studios as Marvel Veteran Jeremy Latcham Takes Over

By Jayesh Marathe · November 9, 2026 · 7 min read

James Gunn and Peter Safran are stepping down as the heads of DC Studios after four years, and former Marvel Studios executive Jeremy Latcham will take over as president and chief creative officer on December 1. Skydance, which now owns the studio through its acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery, announced the leadership change on Monday.

The change

  • The announcement. Skydance announced on Monday that Gunn and Safran will step down as co-chief executives of DC Studios on December 1, four years after they took charge of the studio.
  • The successor. Jeremy Latcham, a former Marvel Studios executive, will become president and chief creative officer of DC Studios on December 1, reporting to Dana Goldberg and Josh Greenstein, the co-chairs of Skydance Motion Picture Group.
  • The new owner. The change comes weeks after Skydance completed its acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery on October 6, putting DC Studios under new ownership for the first time since Gunn and Safran were hired.
  • A return to filmmaking. Skydance said Gunn and Safran will return to creative work full time, starting with post-production on Man of Tomorrow, the sequel to the 2025 film Superman, which is set to reach theaters on July 9, 2027.

What Gunn said

  • His own statement. Gunn confirmed the move in a statement he posted on Threads, thanking fans who supported him and Safran and calling the job a dream he never expected to get.
  • A trying four years. "What only the closest people to me have known over the past four years is my time at DC Studios has been trying, to say the least," Gunn wrote, adding that he dislikes "the at-times corporate nature of the job" and thanking Safran for taking the brunt of it.
  • Relief, not regret. "I'm also incredibly relieved and excited to get back to pure filmmaking," Gunn wrote, saying the studio job stole the short breaks he used to take as a filmmaker from himself and his loved ones.
  • Not a goodbye to DC. "That's not to say Peter and I are immediately done with DC or superheroes," Gunn wrote. "We have Man of Tomorrow out this summer. And we've just been engaged on another DC project which I'm writing, and we'll see where it goes after that."
  • The joint statement. In a joint statement, Gunn and Safran said they set out four years ago "to unite DC under one creative vision" and "helped restore Superman to the heart of DC, while opening new doors and telling new stories in film and television with Peacemaker, Creature Commandos, Lanterns and Clayface."

What Skydance said

  • Praise for the pair. "James and Peter launched a new era for DC Studios and led it through a period of real transformation, resetting the foundation and building a slate we're incredibly proud of," Skydance chairman and chief executive David Ellison and co-chief executive Ynon Kreiz said in a statement.
  • A smooth handover. Ellison and Kreiz said they have "complete confidence that James and Peter will ensure a smooth, collaborative transition and continue this work with care."
  • Faith in Latcham. "Jeremy has been a longtime creative partner to both," Ellison and Kreiz said, adding, "We look forward to working with Jeremy to build the next generation of DC storytelling and to doubling down on our commitment to fans."
  • Latcham's own words. "DC's pantheon of characters is among the most iconic in American popular culture," Latcham said. "I've had the privilege to help bring some of the most celebrated superhero stories to life on the big screen, and I'm excited and honored for the opportunity to now lead DC Studios into its next era."

Who Jeremy Latcham is

  • A Marvel founding hand. Latcham was one of the earliest hires at Marvel Studios and rose to senior vice president of production and development, working under studio chief Kevin Feige.
  • The films on his record. He worked on Iron Man, The Avengers, the Guardians of the Galaxy films, which Gunn directed, and the Tom Holland Spider-Man films.
  • A shared history with Gunn. Gunn and Safran said they have "shared a creative history with Jeremy going back to Marvel" and are "eager to see the perspective he brings to DC's next chapter."
  • A different reporting line. Latcham will report to Goldberg and Greenstein at Skydance Motion Picture Group, while Gunn and Safran had reported directly to the Warner Bros. Discovery chief executive.

What happens to the DC slate

  • The films in motion. Man of Tomorrow, written and directed by Gunn and produced by Gunn and Safran, is in post-production for July 9, 2027, and The Batman Part II remains on the studio's slate.
  • The television slate. Creature Commandos is set for a second season, and the Superman spinoff series The People v. Gorilla Grodd is in development, alongside the Lanterns series.
  • No restart announced. Skydance has not announced plans to cancel the existing DC slate or restart the franchise, and Gunn and Safran will stay involved with the projects they started.
  • The open question. Skydance has not said whether Gunn and Safran chose to step down or were asked to leave, and the question matters because the pair were widely thought to be safe in their jobs only weeks ago.

Why this shakeup lands hard

  • A short era ends. Gunn and Safran were hired four years ago to unite DC under one creative vision, and their run produced a Superman film and television including Peacemaker, Creature Commandos, and Lanterns.
  • A Marvel mind running DC. Latcham built his career inside Marvel's system, and his appointment puts a producer shaped by the Marvel method in charge of its oldest rival.
  • The test ahead. The new chief inherits a slate built by his predecessors, and his first decisions will show whether DC keeps the universe Gunn and Safran built or starts over once again.
