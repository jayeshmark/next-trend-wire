Nominal Raises $240 Million at a $3 Billion Valuation to Build the Intelligence Platform for Hardware Engineering

By Editorial Team · 7 min read

Nominal announced on November 9 that it has raised a $240 million Series C at a $3 billion valuation, led by Lightspeed Venture Partners and co-led by Insight Partners. The hardware intelligence platform serves more than 80 commercial and government organizations, including five of the seven largest defense contractors.

The round

  • The headline numbers. Nominal announced a $240 million Series C at a $3 billion valuation, led by Lightspeed Venture Partners and co-led by Insight Partners.
  • A deep bench. Lux Capital, Sequoia, General Catalyst, Founders Fund, Avenir, Lockheed Martin Ventures, Salesforce Ventures, M13, DE Shaw Ventures, Deployment Capital, Avra, Red Glass Ventures, Carpenter Capital, Audacious, PSP Growth, Overmatch Ventures, XYZ, Haystack, and Brave Capital also took part.
  • The timing. The round lands as defense and industrial hardware teams race to bring AI into engineering work, and it follows the Air Force selecting Nominal for a $71.2 million funding increase to support Collaborative Combat Aircraft capabilities.
  • What the money is for. The funding will support deeper products for engineers and engineering enterprises, including agents and applications across a machine's lifecycle, from requirements verification to fleet health.

What Nominal does

  • The problem. Engineering data stays fragmented across legacy systems and disconnected tools, while critical knowledge often lives in individual engineers' experience, so teams struggle to build on previous work.
  • The platform. Nominal connects hardware data with the engineering context behind it, giving engineers tools to understand complex systems, investigate problems, and make better-informed decisions.
  • The AI layer. Nominal Analyst, the company's first AI agent, investigates engineering data and runs complex analyses, with answers traceable to the underlying evidence, while engineers keep control of decisions, reviews, and approvals.
  • Where it runs. The platform works in cloud deployments, on-premises infrastructure, air-gapped environments, and edge devices, and it connects to existing tools through SQL, APIs, and MCP.

The customers

  • The base. More than 80 commercial and government organizations use Nominal, including five of the seven largest defense contractors.
  • Anduril. More than 300 engineers at Anduril rely on Nominal across programs, and analysis that once took five to six hours now happens in near real time.
  • HII. A pilot at HII reduced certain analysis tasks from hours to minutes and cut some production test steps by approximately half.
  • Hermeus. Hermeus reduced the time between uploading data and reviewing it by 80 percent while designing and building its Quarterhorse Mk 1 aircraft in seven months.

What comes next

  • New industries and regions. The company will expand into new industries and regions, including through strategic acquisitions, after buying Fid Labs and Pull Systems this year.
  • The chief executive's case. "Hardware engineering is getting its AI moment, with real operational value that goes far beyond making existing workflows faster," co-founder and chief executive Cameron McCord said. "We're building the platform that puts that knowledge to work, so engineers can take on harder problems and build things that weren't possible before."
  • The lead investor's case. "Nominal has become an essential part of how some of the most ambitious engineering teams build and validate hardware," Lightspeed partners Guru Chahal and Sebastian Duesterhoeft said. "As AI creates new possibilities across the physical world, we believe Nominal is uniquely positioned to help these teams adopt it where the stakes are highest."
  • The five-year bet. "Over the next five years, companies that effectively bring AI into their engineering workflows will have a real competitive advantage," McCord said. "They'll be able to attempt more: more product lines, more configurations, and more performance from their assets in the field."
