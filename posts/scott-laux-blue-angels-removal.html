Navy Removes Blue Angels Pilot Scott Laux Before Season Finale in Pensacola

By Editorial Team · 7 min read

The U.S. Navy has removed Marine Corps Maj. Scott Laux, the Blue Angels' No. 6 opposing solo pilot, from the flight demonstration squadron, and the team flew with five jets instead of six at its homecoming air show in Pensacola on Friday. The Navy confirmed the removal on November 6 but has not said what Laux did to prompt it.

What the Navy said

  • The removal. The Chief of Naval Air Training confirmed that Blue Angel No. 6 was removed from the team for failing to uphold the standards expected of members of the Navy Flight Demonstration Squadron, and the statement said Laux will not participate in the team's remaining performances this season.
  • The exact words. The command's public affairs office said Laux was removed "for failing to uphold the standards expected of members of the Navy Flight Demonstration Squadron," a formulation that names a standard without identifying which one he failed to meet.
  • No reason given. The Navy has not identified a flight safety incident, a disciplinary offense, a misconduct allegation, or any other specific reason for the decision, and officials have not said whether the removal involved a disciplinary matter or an investigation.
  • No date given. Officials have not said when the decision to remove Laux was made, and a public affairs officer could not immediately confirm the timing when asked.
  • Profile taken down. Laux's photograph and biography have been removed from the Blue Angels' official website, where the team's current pilots are listed.

The five-jet show

  • A gap in the formation. The Blue Angels flew five jets instead of their usual six during the homecoming air show at Naval Air Station Pensacola on Friday, November 6, the first day of the two-day event.
  • The season finale. The homecoming show on November 6 and 7 is the last performance of the Blue Angels' 2026 season, and the team now heads into winter training after closing its 80th anniversary season.
  • A milestone weekend. The show also marks the 200th anniversary of Naval Air Station Pensacola, which began as a naval shipyard in 1826, and the Blue Angels call the base home.
  • A return after a gap year. Last year's homecoming show was canceled during the federal government shutdown, so this year's return drew a large crowd to the flight line for the team's final show of the season.
  • Free to attend. The air show runs Friday and Saturday with gates opening at 8 a.m., the flying starting at 9:30 a.m., and the Blue Angels scheduled to fly around 2 p.m. each day, with general admission and parking free.

Who Laux is

  • The No. 6 jet. Laux flew as the team's opposing solo pilot in his second season with the Blue Angels, one of the two solo positions in the six-jet demonstration.
  • A Marine in a Navy squadron. Laux is a major in the U.S. Marine Corps serving with the Navy's flight demonstration squadron, which draws its pilots from both services.
  • A short tenure. His removal comes as the team closes its 2026 season, so he will not fly with the squadron again this year, and the Navy has not said what assignment follows.

What remains unknown

  • The conduct. The Navy has said nothing about what Laux did or failed to do, so any explanation of the removal beyond the standards language would be speculation.
  • The timing. The date of the decision is unconfirmed, which leaves open whether it came days before the homecoming show or earlier in the season.
  • The next word. The Navy has not said when it will provide more detail, and the next confirmed information should come from the Chief of Naval Air Training or the squadron rather than from outside speculation.
