Physicl Raises $10 Million to Train Robots on the Edge Cases They Get Wrong

By Jayesh Marathe · November 7, 2026 · 7 min read

Physicl, a Paris startup building synthetic training data for physical AI, has raised $10 million in seed funding led by Inovia Capital. The company also launched Studio, a tool that generates the rare situations robots and world models get wrong.

What happened

  • Physicl announced the round on November 6. Physicl announced a $10 million seed round led by Inovia Capital, with participation from US Venture Partners.
  • The money funds the team and the platform. The round funds Physicl's team in Paris and the San Francisco Bay Area, and the platform behind Studio, its first app.
  • Studio launched the same day. Physicl launched Studio alongside the funding news, opening the tool in beta to research teams.
  • The beta is free. Studio is free during the beta, and Physicl is opening access to research teams in waves.
  • Inovia sees a critical gap. Inovia partner Steve Woods said scalable, high-quality physical data grounded in real-world physics is a critical piece of turning model capabilities into real-world performance.

What Studio does

  • It rebuilds the scene a model fails. A researcher describes the situation a model gets wrong, and Studio generates that situation thousands of ways with exact ground truth on every frame.
  • It starts from real 3D geometry. Studio draws on 10,000 environments and 100,000 assets built from real geometry with known materials, ready to vary on demand.
  • Its labels are exact by construction. Depth, normals, segmentation, boxes and camera pose come from the scene itself rather than being estimated from the picture afterwards, so they stay right even in dark corners and behind clutter.
  • It changes one condition at a time. Studio can change the light, the clutter, the camera or the objects, alone or together, so a model sees every version of a rare edge case before it meets one in the real world.
  • It targets the long tail. The tool aims at the rare cases that decide whether a robot works in a kitchen, a warehouse or a hospital: a cup half hidden by a bag, a shadow crossing a counter at sunset, a box stacked slightly off.

Why the edge case matters

  • Robots fail on rare situations. A robot that succeeds nine times out of ten makes a great demo, but no one can deploy a model that fails every tenth run alongside people.
  • Real edge cases cannot be replayed. Rare situations are almost impossible to capture in the real world, and when a team does catch one, it cannot replay it or vary it.
  • Physical AI has no ready dataset. Language models learned from the text of the internet and image models from billions of captioned photos, but the physical world does not come as a dataset.
  • Worlds have to be built, not scraped. Physicl argues that physical worlds cannot be scraped and have to be built from geometry and physics that are known, so every label is exact and every condition can be changed on purpose.
  • An early customer cut its error rate. SpAItial, which builds world models that turn real rooms into explorable 3D, more than halved its reconstruction error on real rooms after training with Physicl data, according to an evaluation by Matthias Niessner's team at TUM.

Who is behind Physicl

  • Alex de Vigan founded the company. Founder and chief executive Alex de Vigan spent a decade digitizing objects and rooms in 3D for large retailers before starting Physicl.
  • His last company sold 3D to retailers. De Vigan previously built nfinite, which made it easy for brands and retailers to create 3D models of their goods and display them online.
  • Advisers built the field. Physicl's advisers include Thomas Wolf of Hugging Face, Christoph Lassner of World Labs, Matthias Niessner of TUM, Yaser Sheikh and Sebastien Deguy.
  • The backers include Insight Partners. The company is backed by Inovia Capital, US Venture Partners and Insight Partners.
  • The team spans Paris and California. The round funds a team split between Paris and the San Francisco Bay Area.

What to watch next

  • Whether beta users convert. Studio is free in beta, so the test is whether research teams keep using it once Physicl starts charging.
  • How crowded the category gets. Physicl is entering a crowded field of startups selling data and tooling to foundation model builders and robot operators, and larger players are investing heavily in the same bottleneck.
  • Whether the error rate claim holds up. SpAItial's halved error rate is one early result, and Physicl will need more customer proof to show the approach works across many teams.
  • What comes after Studio. De Vigan says Studio is the first step and there is more to come, so the next products will show how far Physicl plans to go beyond edge case generation.
