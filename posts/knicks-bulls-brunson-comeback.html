Brunson Drops 38 as Knicks Erase 19-Point Deficit to Beat Bulls

By Editorial Team · 7 min read

Jalen Brunson scored 38 points as the defending champion New York Knicks erased a 19-point third-quarter deficit to beat the Chicago Bulls 133-127 on October 28, 2026, in Chicago. The win was New York's second in two nights after an 0-3 start, and it came with the bench carrying the comeback while starter Mikal Bridges sat.

What happened

  • The hole. Chicago stretched its lead to 87-68 with 4:57 left in the third quarter, behind an 18-3 run in which Norman Powell scored 10 points, and the Bulls led 62-56 at halftime after Tre Jones hit a half-court shot at the buzzer.
  • The answer. New York answered with 22 of the next 29 points to cut the deficit to four by the end of the third, then won the fourth quarter 43-31, with Landry Shamet's three-pointer with 6:26 left giving the Knicks a 112-109 lead they never gave up.
  • Brunson took over. Brunson finished with 38 points and seven assists, his highest scoring output of the young season, hitting 6 of 10 three-pointers after making just 4 of 20 across his previous four games, and he scored 14 points in the fourth quarter before splitting free throws with 6.2 seconds left and OG Anunoby sealing the result at the line.
  • The bench won it. Jordan Clarkson scored a season-high 18 points in 17 minutes, including 12 in the fourth, Shamet added 14 after twisting his right ankle in the first half, and Tyler Kolek had nine points and nine assists, with the three reserves combining for 41 points and 15 assists while Bridges watched.
  • Bridges sat. Bridges, who has struggled to start the season, finished with three points in 13 minutes and played only three minutes of the second half, his shortest appearance as a Knick outside of two brief cameos to maintain his consecutive games streak, as coach Mike Brown said the staff simply stayed with the combination that was working.
  • Chicago's side. Powell scored a season-high 30 points, Caleb Wilson added 20, Jalen Smith had 17 points and 11 rebounds, and Josh Giddey had 10 points, 11 assists, and eight rebounds, while the Bulls shot a season-best 18 of 42 from three-point range and still fell to 1-4.

Why it matters

  • The champs found a pulse. New York dropped its first three games of the season, then beat Detroit 111-97 on Tuesday and Chicago on Wednesday, so a team that looked flat in week one now has its first winning feeling of the season on the second night of a back-to-back.
  • Brunson broke his own slump. Brunson said after the game that he had been putting unnecessary commentary in his head during his shooting struggles, and his 6-of-10 night from deep, including a crossover three that sent a Bulls defender flying, looked like the version of him that carried the Knicks to the title.
  • Depth decided it. A title defense is rarely won by five players, and the Knicks got their win from the part of the roster that had barely played, since Clarkson had logged only 16 total minutes across his first three appearances this season and did not play at all against Detroit.
  • The Bridges question is real. Benching a starter of Bridges' standing in a comeback win is a statement about merit over salary and reputation, and how Brown handles his minutes from here is now one of the Knicks' early-season storylines.

Second order effects

  • A new backcourt pairing emerged. Brown said assistant coach Rick Brunson suggested pairing Kolek with Brunson to give the star a different look and get him off the ball, and the combination worked so well that Brown said it became harder to go back to what the Knicks started with.
  • Chicago's shooting arrived, its defense did not. The Bulls entered the night shooting an NBA-worst 23.8 percent from deep through four games, then drilled 42.9 percent of their threes against New York, yet still lost because they gave up 43 fourth-quarter points and missed nine free throws in a six-point defeat.
  • The glass told the story. New York won the rebounding battle 50-39, including 16 offensive rebounds to Chicago's eight, with Andre Drummond grabbing 11 boards in 15 minutes, which gave the Knicks the extra possessions a comeback needs.
  • The schedule gets crueler for Chicago. The Bulls now face Boston, Philadelphia twice, and a rematch with New York over the next nine days, so a 1-4 start could deepen quickly even though the offense finally showed signs of life.

Third order effects

  • Five games do not define a season. The Knicks are 2-3, not 5-0, and two wins in two nights change the temperature around the defending champions without changing the standings math, since the Eastern Conference race is decided across 82 games rather than one week.
  • One benching does not settle a rotation. If Bridges keeps sitting in crunch time, questions about his role and future will grow louder, but one comeback win is a small sample, and Brown's job is to weigh a hot night against a full season of evidence.
  • Chicago still has a bright spot. Wilson's 20-point night continued a start in which he became the fourth rookie in NBA history to record at least 15 points, a block, and a steal in each of his first five games, which gives the Bulls' rebuild something real even in a loss.
  • Comebacks become identity. Teams that rally from 19 down on the road, on the second night of a back-to-back, start to believe in their own resilience, and that belief tends to show up again in the next close game.

What to watch next

  • The rematch test. New York visits Philadelphia on Friday, October 30, against the 76ers team that beat the Knicks on opening day, which will show whether this two-game run is a turn or a blip.
  • Chicago's Cup opener. The Bulls visit Boston on Friday to open NBA Cup group play, a separate competition inside the season where an 0-1 start would add to an already rough week.
  • The closing lineup. Whether Bridges returns to the crunch-time five or the Clarkson, Shamet, and Kolek trio keeps its minutes is the clearest thing to track in the Knicks' next game.
  • Brunson's shot. The next few games will show whether the 6-of-10 night ended his early-season shooting slump or was simply one hot night inside it.
