Florida Flood Emergency Grows to 14 Counties as Rivers Keep Rising

By Jayesh Marathe · November 6, 2026 · 7 min read

Record rainfall across central Florida has killed two people in Palm Bay, forced hundreds of rescues and pushed the state of emergency to 14 counties, with rivers still rising days after the rain stopped. More than 24 inches fell near Malabar in about two days, a deluge the National Weather Service called a once in a thousand year event.

What happened

  • A slow storm parked over Florida. A broad area of low pressure moved across the Florida Peninsula from October 31 through November 2, pulling deep moisture off the Gulf and dumping days of heavy rain on the east central coast.
  • The rain totals broke records. More than 24 inches fell near Malabar in southern Brevard County from November 1 through late November 2, with about 20 inches near Palm Bay and Melbourne Beach, according to the National Weather Service.
  • A flash flood emergency went out Monday morning. The National Weather Service in Melbourne declared a flash flood emergency for West Melbourne and Palm Bay at 7:56 a.m. on November 2, warning of catastrophic flooding as the rain kept falling on ground that was already soaked.
  • Rescue crews pulled people from cars and homes. Brevard County sheriff's deputies and Florida Fish and Wildlife officers rescued more than 100 people caught in rising water on November 2, and crews were still finding people stranded in vehicles after 2 a.m. the next morning.
  • Election Day moved with the water. The flooding forced polling places to move in at least eight counties for the November 3 election, after the governor's emergency order let county supervisors propose substitute voting sites.

The two deaths in Palm Bay

  • Palm Bay police confirmed the first death on November 3. Joshua Bennett, 34, went missing during the record rain on Monday and was found in his truck in a flooded stretch of southeast Palm Bay.
  • The mayor named the second victim that afternoon. Palm Bay Mayor Rob Medina identified Archie Richards, 78, at a press conference on November 3. Richards was found in his vehicle near Richards Lane and Hall Road, a day after a Silver Alert went out for him.
  • The two men were not related. City officials said Bennett and Richards died on Monday, November 2, and the mayor said he could not promise the toll was finished while search and rescue work continued.
  • The deaths came from the water itself. Medina declined to go beyond water intrusion when asked about the manner of death, leaving the details for the official findings.

The emergency keeps growing

  • The governor declared an emergency on November 2. Governor Ron DeSantis signed Executive Order 26-227, declaring a state of emergency in Brevard, DeSoto, Hardee, Highlands, Indian River, Martin and St. Lucie counties and putting the Florida Division of Emergency Management in charge of the response.
  • A second order doubled the map on November 4. The governor signed Executive Order 26-228, adding Glades, Hendry, Lake, Okeechobee, Palm Beach, Seminole and Volusia counties, after the order cited record rain of more than 19 inches in 12 hours in Riviera Beach in Palm Beach County.
  • The order unlocks state help. The emergency declaration lets the state coordinate the response, waive tolls for emergency travel and switch on price gouging protections for essential goods in the affected counties.
  • Schools stayed shut for days. Brevard County public schools closed on November 2 and reopened on Thursday, November 5. Indian River County schools stayed closed through Friday, November 6, with shelters open at two schools and more than 30 roads closed across the county.

The rivers are still rising

  • The St. Johns River is the slow threat now. Flood warnings stayed in effect on November 5 and 6 for the St. Johns near Cocoa, Geneva, Sanford, DeLand and Astor, with the river forecast to keep rising into next week as the Brevard rain drains north through the basin.
  • Major flooding is forecast near Cocoa. The river near Cocoa stood at 16.9 feet on Thursday morning, with moderate flooding under way and major flooding forecast later in the week, when water could reach homes around Lake Poinsett and parts of Rockledge.
  • Sanford faces water in low lying homes. The St. Johns near Sanford stood at about 5.1 feet on Thursday and is forecast to reach about 6.4 feet on Monday, approaching the level at which some low lying homes in the Stone Island and Stillbrook subdivisions could take on water.
  • The Peace River hit major flood stage. The Peace River at Arcadia reached major flood stage on November 5, flooding nearby homes, with a crest near 16.4 feet forecast for Thursday evening.
  • Boating is restricted on the river. The Florida Fish and Wildlife Conservation Commission restricted boating in parts of the St. Johns near Astor, Lake Monroe and Mullet Lake because of the high water.

What comes next

  • The water will take weeks to drain. The National Weather Service forecasts minor to moderate St. Johns River flooding for several weeks as the record rainfall works its way north toward Jacksonville.
  • Damage reports are now being collected. Brevard County Emergency Management is taking damage reports from residents and businesses through its CrisisTrack self report form, with 211 Brevard serving as the disaster information hotline.
  • Cleanup help is mobilizing. Volunteer groups are organizing cleanup help for flooded homes in Palm Bay and Melbourne, where floodwater entered homes across the county.
  • The emergency runs for 60 days. The governor's order stays in effect for 60 days unless it is extended, giving the state room to keep coordinating aid while the rivers fall.
  • The next test is the river crest. The focus now shifts from the rain that fell to the river levels that follow, with forecasters watching the St. Johns at Cocoa, Geneva and Astor through the weekend and into next week.
