Check the Bottle: 3,744 Bottles of a Generic Blood Pressure Pill Were Recalled

By Jayesh Marathe · November 3, 2026 · 7 min read

Searches for olmesartan surged in the United States this week as word spread about a nationwide recall of a generic three in one blood pressure pill. Macleods Pharma USA recalled 3,744 bottles in two lots after the tablets failed a dissolution test, and the Food and Drug Administration classified the recall as Class II on October 26, 2026.

Which bottles are affected

  • The product. The recall covers olmesartan medoxomil, amlodipine, and hydrochlorothiazide tablets in the 40 mg, 10 mg, and 12.5 mg strength, a generic version of the brand name drug Tribenzor, and the brand name Tribenzor itself is not part of the recall.
  • The 30 count lot. The 30 count bottles carry NDC 33342-246-07 and lot number 12251163A, with an expiration date of May 31, 2027, and 3,528 bottles of this lot were recalled.
  • The 90 count lot. The 90 count bottles carry NDC 33342-246-10 and lot number 12251163B, with the same expiration date of May 31, 2027, and 216 bottles of this lot were recalled.
  • The scope. Only these two lots of this strength are listed in the recall, and other strengths and other lots of this strength are not included.

Why the tablets were recalled

  • The failed test. The Food and Drug Administration lists the reason as failed dissolution specifications, meaning the tablets did not release their active ingredients at the required rate in laboratory testing.
  • The detail behind the failure. In 9 month stability testing of batch 12251163B, the olmesartan component dissolved at a mean of 79 percent against a requirement of not less than 80 percent, while every individual unit met the unit level criteria and the amlodipine and hydrochlorothiazide components complied with their specifications.
  • The possible effect. The company notice says use of the affected product may result in reduced or delayed absorption of olmesartan, potentially leading to a temporary and medically reversible reduction in blood pressure control, and it puts the probability of serious adverse health consequences as remote.
  • No adverse event reports for one batch. The company's health hazard assessment identified no adverse event reports or spontaneous safety reports for batch 12251163B.
  • The timeline. Macleods initiated the voluntary recall on October 7, 2026, the Food and Drug Administration classified it as Class II on October 26, 2026, in its Enforcement Report under recall number D-0077-2027, and the batch was distributed between November 21, 2025, and April 27, 2026.

What Class II means

  • The middle tier. Class II is the Food and Drug Administration's middle severity tier, covering products that may cause temporary or medically reversible health consequences where the probability of serious harm is remote.
  • A quality failure, not contamination. The recall rests on a dissolution test result, not on a finding that the tablets were contaminated or contained the wrong drug.
  • Keep taking it unless told otherwise. Under the Food and Drug Administration's general guidance for Class II drug recalls, patients can generally keep taking the medicine unless the recalling company or the agency directs otherwise.

What to do now

  • Check the label. Look on the bottle for the maker name Macleods and the lot number, and compare it with lots 12251163A and 12251163B.
  • Ask your pharmacist if the lot is missing. If your pharmacy put the pills in its own bottle, the lot number may not be printed on it, so ask the pharmacy whether your supply came from one of the recalled lots.
  • Call before changing anything. If your bottle matches, call your pharmacist or doctor for guidance on a replacement lot or maker, and do not stop taking blood pressure medicine on your own without medical advice.
  • How patients were notified. The recall is being conducted at the retail and pharmacy level, and the company notified customers by letter, while the Food and Drug Administration issued no separate press release for this recall.
