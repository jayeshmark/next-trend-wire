Synctera Raises $24.5 Million as Banks Return to Banking as a Service

By Jayesh Marathe · November 7, 2026 · 7 min read

Synctera has raised $24.5 million in new funding, the embedded finance company announced November 6, bringing its total funding to $126.7 million. The round lands as banks return to banking as a service, with Main Street Bank joining as its newest sponsor bank.

What happened

  • The round. Synctera announced $24.5 million in new funding, bringing its total funding to $126.7 million. Participants include Multiplier Capital, Fin Capital, 1st and Main Growth Partners, Diagram, and NAventures, and the funding backs Synctera's work with sponsor banks and fintechs as more banks get back into banking as a service.
  • The runway to break even. The round strengthens Synctera's balance sheet and provides the runway required to break even. The company frames the raise as a durability move, not a growth sprint, and its chief executive says the company raises with the long run in mind because the banks and fintechs that build on it need to know it will still be there.
  • The new bank partner. Main Street Bank, out of Massachusetts, has joined Synctera's network of sponsor banks. Synctera now works with eight active sponsor banks, with two more in the final stages of implementation and going live with programs in the fourth quarter.
  • The customer momentum. Twenty clients have signed with Synctera in 2026 across financial services, property management, and small business lending, ranging from smaller fintechs to publicly traded companies. The company has also doubled its gross merchandise volume and interchange year over year.

Why banks are coming back

  • Two years ago, banks were asking whether to be in the business at all. Now they ask how to do it well, according to Synctera co-founder and chief executive Peter Hazlehurst. Banks answer for what happens in a fintech program, so the shift from whether to how is the clearest sign that the industry has moved past its crisis of confidence.
  • Building it alone is too hard. Banks are arriving with a plan, often built with major financial services advisors, and concluding that building banking as a service on their own is far too hard. The work needs a ledger, reconciliation, compliance testing, and program management, and most banks would rather buy that stack than build it.
  • The model spreads the risk. Synctera is a software company, not a bank, and its multi-bank model spreads risk across banks that each maintain responsibility for their own programs instead of resting every program on a single charter. One shift in a bank's business model or risk appetite does not put everything in question, and the consumers behind every program stay protected.

The compliance pitch

  • Compliance sits at the center. Banks have long checked compliance by sampling, but Synctera acquired Cable in April 2026, and Cable tests every control continuously across banks, fintechs, and service providers. Continuous testing shows whether controls work in practice and not just on paper, which is what regulators increasingly expect.
  • The money services license matters. Synctera has also built out a full program management team and set of processes, and registered as a money services business regulated by the Financial Crimes Enforcement Network. Together, Cable and the money services status give sponsor banks the continuous oversight that regulators now demand from banking as a service.
  • Trust is the product. "Banking is hard. It runs on trust, and trust is built over years," Hazlehurst said in the announcement. "You can't mess with people's money." The quote is the company's whole pitch in one line: banks lend their name and reputation to every program, so they need a platform they can treat like infrastructure, durable and always available.
  • The investors agree. "Embedded finance only works when banks can trust what's happening on their charter, and Synctera has built its platform around exactly that," said Ash Vaidya, managing director at Multiplier Capital. "As banks return to banking as a service, Synctera is the partner they need."

What comes next

  • Synctera Labs launches in February. Synctera Labs, introduced in October, is a launch platform for fintechs with a new embedded finance idea, giving teams real accounts, cards, and customers with oversight and compliance built in. The first programs go live in February 2027, and the pitch is a lower upfront cost than a custom program.
  • Two more banks are nearly live. The two sponsor banks in the final stages of implementation will take Synctera to ten active partners, and their programs go live in the fourth quarter. Each new bank widens the network that fintechs can build on, which is the flywheel the company is selling.
  • Cable keeps expanding. Since the acquisition, Cable has added one new bank client and four new fintechs, and it now offers free Cable services to sponsor banks in Synctera's network. Free compliance tooling for banks is a land grab move, and it shows where Synctera thinks the next fight is: not on price, but on who banks trust with their charter.
