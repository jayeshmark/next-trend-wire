Project Omega Raises $80 Million to Recycle Nuclear Fuel and Build Long Duration Power Systems

By Jayesh Marathe · November 9, 2026 · 7 min read

Project Omega raised $80 million in Series A financing on November 9 to build nuclear refineries that turn America's used nuclear fuel into reactor fuel, strategic isotopes and power systems that run for years without refueling, in a bet on a domestic nuclear fuel cycle.

The round

  • The headline. Project Omega announced an $80 million Series A led by B Capital, with participation from noa VC, 645 Ventures and other investors in nuclear technology and national security.
  • The backers. Existing investors joined the round, including Starship Ventures, Mantis Ventures, Decisive Point, Virta Ventures, Pace Ventures, Essential Capital and Washington Harbour Partners.
  • The board. Jeff Johnson, General Partner and Global Head of Energy at B Capital, will join Project Omega's Board of Directors as part of the investment.
  • The investor. B Capital is a multi stage global investment firm with more than $12 billion in assets under management, and its past investments include Perplexity, Precision Neuroscience, Axiom and Fervo Energy.
  • The step up. The Series A follows a $12 million seed round disclosed when the company emerged from stealth in February, and ranks among the largest Series A deals ever in US energy.

What Omega does

  • The problem. The United States relies heavily on foreign sources for uranium while spending billions to manage and store used nuclear fuel, and meeting America's growing energy demand, from AI to advanced manufacturing, will require far more nuclear power and a more secure domestic fuel supply chain.
  • The resource. The country holds nearly 100,000 metric tons of used nuclear fuel, which founder and chief executive Dr. Stafford Sheehan calls one of America's largest untapped energy resources.
  • The process. Omega recycles that fuel through an advanced, non-aqueous pyroprocessing platform, recovering valuable isotopes such as strontium-90 without separating pure plutonium, and cutting the volume of nuclear waste by up to 90 percent.
  • The products. The recovered materials become long duration power sources, reactor feedstock and isotopes for commercial, scientific and medical use, turning the back end of the fuel cycle from a waste problem into a source of value.
  • The pitch. "The country's nuclear renaissance will not happen without the fuel," Sheehan said. "We are building a vertically integrated strategy around an abundant domestic feedstock, with a non-aqueous recycling process that recovers useful isotopes, and products with clear strategic demand."

The technology

  • Radiovoltaic power. Omega's first products are long duration power sources built on radiovoltaic power, solid state systems that convert the natural decay of radioisotopes directly into electricity.
  • Where it runs. The systems are designed for missions where recharging, battery replacement or human access is difficult or impossible, providing persistent power for sensors, autonomous systems, communications nodes, spacecraft, undersea platforms and other mission critical equipment.
  • The X Series. Omega has launched the X Series, a family of modular, intelligent expeditionary power systems that integrate battery storage and generators with an intelligent control system, starting with solar and wind and adding isotope power in the future.
  • The track record. Since emerging from stealth eight months ago, Omega has advanced its recycling technology with Idaho National Laboratory through the Department of Energy's ARPA-E CURIE program, demonstrated stable electrical current from strontium-90 at the Pacific Northwest National Laboratory, and developed next generation radioisotope power systems through DARPA's Rads to Watts program.

What comes next

  • The pilot plant. The Series A will fund a pilot facility for domestic isotope recovery and feedstock production, and let Omega begin construction of its nuclear fuel recycling facility.
  • The manufacturing push. The funding will also advance the technology and manufacturing of the X Series products, moving the company from prototype toward commercial production.
  • The bigger bet. "Project Omega has progressed well beyond a compelling scientific idea," said Howard Morgan, Co-Founder, Chair and General Partner at B Capital. "The company is building the fuel-cycle infrastructure needed to support America's growing nuclear industrial base and help power the country's next era of innovation."
  • The footprint. Omega has offices in Newport, Rhode Island and Hailey, Idaho, and a cold lab in Fall River, Massachusetts, and says further details about its new facilities will be announced in the coming months.
