Clocks Fall Back on November 1 as Daylight Saving Time Ends for 2026

By Jayesh Marathe · November 1, 2026 · 7 min read

Most of the United States returns to standard time at 2 a.m. on Sunday, November 1, 2026, when clocks move back one hour to 1 a.m. and the hour between 1 and 2 a.m. happens twice. The change brings an extra hour of sleep and earlier sunsets, and it arrives even though the House voted to end the clock changes.

What changes on November 1

  • The moment itself. At 2 a.m. local daylight time on Sunday, November 1, clocks in observing areas move back to 1 a.m. local standard time, so the day runs 25 hours long and the hour from 1 a.m. to 2 a.m. occurs twice.
  • The immediate effect. People gain an extra hour overnight, and sunrise and sunset each shift about one hour earlier by the clock, bringing brighter mornings and darker evenings through the fall and winter.
  • The rule behind it. Under the current federal schedule, daylight saving time begins at 2 a.m. on the second Sunday in March and ends at 2 a.m. on the first Sunday in November, a pattern in place since 2007 under the Energy Policy Act of 2005, and the National Institute of Standards and Technology lists the 2026 period as March 8 through November 1.
  • The timing quirk. The change falls the night after Halloween this year, because Halloween landed on a Saturday, so trick-or-treaters kept their later evening daylight on Saturday and the clocks changed the very next night.

Who does not change

  • The exceptions. Hawaii and most of Arizona do not observe daylight saving time, so clocks there do not move on November 1, and the same holds for American Samoa, Guam, Puerto Rico and the U.S. Virgin Islands.
  • The exception inside the exception. The Navajo Nation, which extends into Arizona, does observe daylight saving time, so clocks there do fall back even though most of the state stays put.
  • The practical point. Phones, computers and other connected devices adjust on their own, but analog clocks, car clocks and some appliance clocks need to be set back by hand, ideally before bed on Saturday night.

Why the clocks still change

  • The House acted. The House of Representatives passed the Sunshine Protection Act, H.R. 139, on July 14, 2026, by a vote of 308 to 117, a bill that would make daylight saving time permanent for states that observe it and end the twice yearly switch.
  • The Senate has not. The Senate received the bill on July 15 and referred it to the Committee on Commerce, Science, and Transportation, where it has sat without a floor vote, so no new law has taken effect and the existing schedule stands.
  • The state level push. Nineteen states have passed laws for permanent daylight saving time, but those laws cannot take effect without Congress, because federal law lets a state opt out of daylight saving time but does not let a state adopt permanent daylight saving time on its own.
  • The bottom line for now. Until both chambers agree and a bill is signed, the twice yearly changes continue, and the next one is already scheduled: clocks spring forward again on Sunday, March 14, 2027.

What to do about it

  • Set the manual clocks. Turn back wall clocks, wristwatches, car clocks and microwave and oven clocks before bed on Saturday, October 31, so Sunday morning starts on the right time.
  • Check the alarms. Confirm that alarms set on manual clocks and older alarm clocks still go off when intended, since the repeated 1 a.m. hour can confuse devices that do not update automatically.
  • Use the extra hour. The fall change is the easy one, an extra hour of sleep or a slower Sunday morning, before the darker evenings settle in and the countdown to the March 14, 2027 spring forward begins.
