Christian Watson Went to the Hospital Mid-Game. Then He Came Back and Finished It.

By Editorial Team · 7 min read

Green Bay Packers wide receiver Christian Watson was taken by ambulance to a hospital during Thursday night's game against the Carolina Panthers. Then he came back, ran out of the locker room midway through the third quarter, and helped the Packers close out a 27-19 win at Lambeau Field.

What happened

  • The catch. Midway through the second quarter, Watson hauled in a 53-yard deep pass from Jordan Love between two Panthers defenders, then landed hard on top of the ball as he hit the ground and stayed down, rolling onto his back while trainers attended to him.
  • The hospital trip. Watson walked off the field under his own power and was checked in the blue medical tent, then walked to the locker room and boarded an ambulance, still under his own power, to a local hospital for precautionary testing on a chest injury. The Packers first listed him as questionable to return.
  • The other man down. Panthers cornerback Will Lee III, who was in coverage on the play, also went down clutching his hamstring.
  • The drive went on. Green Bay scored on the drive Watson was hurt on, retaking the lead at 13-10 midway through the second quarter, after trailing 10-6 when he went down.

The return

  • Cleared to come back. During the second half, Amazon sideline reporter Kaylee Hartung reported that all of Watson's hospital tests were clear, and video showed him climbing out of the ambulance back inside Lambeau Field and jogging to the locker room with a bandage on his right arm from an IV.
  • Back on the field. Watson ran out of the locker room midway through the third quarter and returned to the game on the next offensive possession. Jordan Love targeted him on his first snap back, but the pass fell incomplete against double coverage.
  • The finish. Watson caught a 6-yard pass on the opening play of the fourth quarter and finished with eight catches for 113 yards, after six catches for 100 yards before he left. The Packers won 27-19.
  • His own words. After the game Watson told Hartung, "It was tough. Honestly, I never wanted to go," and said he had been hoping the staff would let him stay. He said the tests were to rule out anything serious: "No internal bleeding. No anything that was raising suspicion," adding that he had been spitting a little blood at first, so doctors wanted to make sure there was nothing crazy.
  • A broken rib. Watson told reporters after the game that he had a broken rib, and said he will wear padding on his ribs over the team's mini-bye and expects to play in Week 9 against the New England Patriots. "I've got a little pads on the ribs to make sure to take care of that over the extra little break we've got before our next game, and we'll be good to go," he said.

Why it matters

  • The team's top target. Watson entered Thursday night leading the Packers with 34 catches for 595 yards and six touchdowns, and the team also welcomed back receiver Matthew Golden from shoulder and foot injuries the same night.
  • A fifth-year receiver. Watson, 27, is in his fifth season with Green Bay, and the sight of a team's top receiver leaving in an ambulance is the kind of moment that usually ends a night. His return changed the story of the game.
  • A short week, a long season. The mini-bye gives Watson extra days before Week 9, and his own verdict was upbeat: he said he was feeling good.

What to watch next

  • Week 9. The Packers face the New England Patriots in Week 9, and Watson's rib will be the injury to watch through the week.
  • The official report. The team's official injury report will say whether the broken rib limits his practice, even if Watson himself expects to play.
