Two High-Speed Trains Collided in Spain. What We Know.

By Editorial Team · 7 min read

Two high-speed trains collided in central Spain on October 28, 2026, killing 68 people, according to the Castilla-La Mancha regional government. A Renfe AVE train struck the rear of a stationary Iryo train near Urda, derailing several carriages, two of which fell down an embankment. The government declared three days of official mourning while investigators establish what happened.

What happened

  • A rear-end collision. Around 8:54, at kilometre 118 between Mora, Orgaz and Urda, a Renfe AVE train struck the rear of a stationary Iryo train. The AVE had left Madrid at 8:00 for Seville with 378 passengers aboard, while the Iryo was travelling from Madrid to Seville with 147 people aboard, including a baby. Several carriages derailed, and two fell down an embankment.
  • The toll. The regional government's confirmed figure moved from 10 to more than 30 by late morning, then to 64, and then to 68, by Castilla-La Mancha president Emiliano Garcia-Page: 24 of the dead were travelling on the AVE and 44 on the Iryo. It could still rise, because rescue teams had not reached every carriage and wreckage inspection awaited judicial authorization.
  • The injured. Garcia-Page reported 53 injured people with minor injuries and 15 in serious condition. Health Minister Monica Garcia said about 80 patients had been taken to hospitals. Among the dead was former Labour minister Fatima Banez, on the Iryo to Seville.
  • The line stopped. Adif suspended high-speed traffic on the Madrid-Andalusia line. Iryo cancelled all its services from Madrid to the southern corridor. Renfe offered free changes and activated its victim assistance plan, while firefighters, Civil Protection, the Civil Guard and the Military Emergency Unit, expanded to 200 personnel, worked at the site with medical helicopters and intensive care units.
  • Three days of mourning. Prime Minister Pedro Sanchez announced three days of official mourning, from midnight on October 28 through the end of October 31, the days 29, 30 and 31, with flags at half-mast. Sanchez said the government's priority was the victims and their families.
  • The second crash this year. On January 18, 2026, one near Adamuz killed 46 people and injured over 120, after an Iryo train derailed and collided with a Renfe train.

Why it matters

  • The scale. With 68 dead, this is Spain's deadliest rail accident since 2013, and the second high-speed crash this year.
  • The system, not the trains. Transport Minister Oscar Puente said an LZB safety system failure left a 63 kilometre stretch without automation, stopping at least six trains. Adif's control centre then authorized the AVE to resume at 8:36, despite the Iryo stopped on that track, and it travelled 25 kilometres before striking it. Puente called that decision inexplicable and confirmed a human error by an Adif operator amid a signalling breakdown. The cause remains under investigation, with the Civil Guard and the rail accident investigation commission questioning the operator.
  • The promise. Sanchez promised to get to the bottom of the crash: if there was negligence it would be revealed, and if there were system failures there would be a strong response. He called two high-speed accidents in one year unacceptable, and vowed to reinforce safety.
  • Trust. Spain runs the second largest high-speed network, with 4,000 kilometres of track. A crash caused by a control decision strikes at the part of the system passengers never see and must trust: the signalling and its operators.

Second order effects

  • The corridor stays shut. High-speed traffic between Madrid and Andalusia remained suspended, with ticket sales cancelled. Unaffected sections began moving again, bus alternatives were announced, and Iberia added 520 seats on flights to Andalusia, capped at 99 euros.
  • Judicial, technical, and accountable. The judge in Orgaz oversees the site, and judicial authorization gates wreckage inspection. The rail accident investigation commission, the Civil Guard, and internal Renfe and Adif investigations run alongside. What they establish about the 8:36 order will decide whether this ends in a disciplinary matter, a criminal matter, a system redesign, or all. Accountability is sharpened by two different operators running on a state-managed network: Adif runs the infrastructure and control centre, while Renfe and Iryo run the trains.
  • The political pressure builds. The government and the opposition cancelled their agendas, and political parties suspended campaign activity. After the Adamuz maintenance debate, the government now faces the same questions a second time, with a minister describing the control decision as inexplicable before the investigation has reported.

Third order effects

  • Manual fallback. The crash exposes what happens when automation fails: the safety system stopped six trains, and then a human decision restarted one into the path of a train that had not moved. Expect scrutiny of fallback procedures and whether manual operation should carry speed limits without automated protection.
  • Maintenance and signalling. Adamuz had pushed maintenance investment into debate. A second crash on the same corridor, tied in preliminary accounts to a signalling failure and a control decision, will push signalling upgrades, inspection regimes and maintenance budgets to the fore.
  • Passengers and other networks. Two crashes in a year on the same corridor could push travellers toward flights and cars for Madrid-Andalusia trips, undercutting the climate and congestion case for high-speed rail if confidence is not restored. Spain's system is a reference, so how its investigation reports and what it changes about control centre procedure will be studied by operators worldwide running mixed state and private services on infrastructure.

What to watch next

  • The toll. Watch whether the figure of 68 rises once wreckage inspection is fully authorized.
  • The first findings. Watch what investigators establish about the 8:36 order and whether the operator followed protocol.
  • The reopening. Watch when high-speed service between Madrid and Andalusia resumes, and whether safety measures are imposed.
  • The government's response. Watch what Sanchez does if negligence or system failures are confirmed.
