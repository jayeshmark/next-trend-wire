Jokic Records 200th Triple-Double as Nuggets Beat Pelicans

By Editorial Team · 5 min read

Nikola Jokic recorded his 200th career regular-season triple-double on October 28, 2026, finishing with 21 points, 13 rebounds and 10 assists as the Denver Nuggets beat the New Orleans Pelicans 104-91 in New Orleans. Jokic became the second player in NBA history to reach 200, behind only Russell Westbrook.

What happened

  • The milestone. Nikola Jokic finished with 21 points, 13 rebounds and 10 assists on 8-of-13 shooting for his 200th career regular-season triple-double, becoming the second player in NBA history to reach 200, behind only Russell Westbrook and his 209. It was Jokic's second triple-double of the season, and both have come against New Orleans.
  • The fast start. Denver never trailed. The Nuggets shot 62 percent in the first quarter while building a 37-18 lead, with Jamal Murray making his first three shots, all from deep, and a Marvin Bagley III runner stretched the lead to 44-22, the largest of the night, before Denver took a 57-45 lead into halftime.
  • The Pelicans push. New Orleans refused to fold, and Zion Williamson scored 25 points, including a fourth-quarter three-pointer that pulled the Pelicans as close as 88-83. Murray answered with a three-pointer and a driving finger roll, and Christian Braun knocked down back-to-back three-pointers during a critical late stretch to put the game away.
  • The supporting cast. Murray led Denver with 23 points, hitting 5 of 11 three-pointers, Aaron Gordon added 15 points and 11 rebounds, Braun scored 15, and rookie Trevon Brazile had 11 points and eight rebounds in his first career start, so all five Denver starters finished in double figures.
  • The short bench. Denver played without Cam Johnson, who has an ankle injury, and Alpha Diallo, who has a sprained wrist, and remained without Julian Strawther and Spencer Jones, which left the Nuggets with a nine-man rotation in the second game of a home-and-home set against the same opponent.
  • New Orleans side. Derik Queen came off the bench for 11 points and 12 rebounds, Jeremiah Fears scored 11, and the Pelicans shot just 7 of 30 from three-point range, falling to 0-4 under first-year head coach Jamahl Mosley.
  • The previous meeting. Four days earlier, Jokic recorded 35 points, 15 rebounds and 10 assists for his 199th career triple-double, Murray scored 42, and Denver rallied from 23 points down to beat New Orleans 141-135 in overtime, which means both of Denver's wins this season have come against the Pelicans.

Why it matters

  • Only two ever. Westbrook retired after last season with 209 triple-doubles in 18 seasons, and Jokic needs 10 more to pass him, so the chase for the all-time record is now a nightly storyline rather than a distant milestone.
  • Denver needed a clean win. The Nuggets moved to 2-2 with their most complete performance of the young season, but both wins have come against the same winless opponent, so the record says less about Denver than the milestone does.
  • The zone problem. Midway through the game, New Orleans switched to a zone defense and Denver went nearly six minutes without a field goal while the Pelicans cut into the lead with a 14-2 run, a reminder that Denver's half-court offense still stalls when the three-pointers stop falling.
  • The Pelicans' hole. New Orleans is winless through four games, its perimeter shooting sits at 23.3 percent for the night, and scoring beyond Williamson was thin, which leaves a first-year head coach chasing a first win before the season has found its shape.

Second order effects

  • The record chase. Every Jokic triple-double from here moves him closer to Westbrook, and at his current pace the record is in play this season, which brings national attention to Denver's ordinary regular-season games.
  • Murray's form. Murray's 23 points, including 5 three-pointers and three straight makes to open the game, matter because his shooting sets the ceiling for Denver's offense on nights when Jokic is doing everything else.
  • Rotation depth. A nine-man rotation with four players out is workable for one night, but if the injuries linger, the bench gets tested early, and a rookie starter in Brazile becomes a structural piece rather than a stopgap.
  • DeRozan's quiet value. DeMar DeRozan scored just 6 points on 1-of-6 shooting, but he added five assists and two steals, including the passes that set up Braun's late three-pointers, which is the kind of bench contribution that does not show up in a scoring line.

Third order effects

  • Legacy framing. Passing Westbrook would give Jokic a record many considered untouchable when Westbrook set it, and it would arrive while Jokic is still in his prime, which changes how the rest of his career gets framed.
  • The measuring-stick problem. Denver's two wins are both against New Orleans, so Friday's NBA Cup opener at Phoenix is a truer test of where the Nuggets actually stand.
  • The Pelicans' margin. An 0-4 start leaves little room in the Western Conference, and a winless opening stretch can harden into a lost season faster than a roster expects.
  • The attention effect. Teams now game-plan for a player chasing history every single night, which brings extra scrutiny to Denver's early-season games and extra pressure on the supporting cast to hold up its end.

What to watch next

  • The Phoenix test. Denver visits Phoenix on Friday night in an NBA Cup group stage opener, the first real measuring stick after two wins over New Orleans.
  • The count. Jokic sits at 200, so his next triple-double makes it 201, and the gap to Westbrook's 209 is 10.
  • The rotation. Whether Brazile keeps his starting spot while Johnson remains out, and whether the nine-man group holds up across a longer stretch.
  • The Pelicans' response. New Orleans hosts Sacramento on Thursday night, still looking for its first win of the season.
