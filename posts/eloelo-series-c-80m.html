EloElo Group Raises $80 Million to Take Its Microdrama Platform Beyond India

By Jayesh Marathe · November 5, 2026 · 7 min read

EloElo Group, the Bengaluru company behind Story TV, has raised $80 million in a Series C round led by Hornbill Capital and NBCUniversal, the company announced on November 4, 2026. The round takes EloElo's total capital raised to $130 million and will fund more original content, an international launch, and further investment in its AI creative suite.

The round

  • The raise. EloElo announced an $80 million Series C led by Hornbill Capital and NBCUniversal, with MIT Investment Co. and existing investors Play Ventures, Kalaari Capital, Waterbridge Ventures, Griffin Gaming Partners and others also taking part.
  • The total so far. The new round brings EloElo Group's total capital raised to $130 million to date, and the company says it has grown rapidly on profitable unit economics, with revenue growing several times over in the last year.
  • The advisor. Avendus Capital acted as exclusive financial advisor on the funding round.
  • The founder. EloElo Group was founded in Bengaluru in 2020 by Saurabh Pandey, a former Flipkart executive, who remains founder and chief executive.

What Story TV is

  • The flagship app. Story TV is EloElo's microdrama platform, which the company calls India's leading platform in the format, with a slate of more than 5,000 microdramas in six languages.
  • The viewing habit. EloElo says a new drama launches every three hours on Story TV and that users spend 95 minutes per user every day on the platform, a pattern built around short episodes that fit around a day rather than a long evening viewing session.
  • The wider group. Beyond Story TV, EloElo runs Master, a microlearning platform, Dolphin AI, its agentic AI creative suite, and Voxy AI, an AI companion agent, and says its apps together have more than 40 million monthly active users and more than 300 million installs.
  • The pitch in one line. Pandey frames the business around phone first viewing, saying a billion people now reach for the phone before any other screen and that the entertainment built for them will be video first, in their own language, in episodes that fit their day.

Where the money goes

  • More originals. EloElo will use the Series C capital to fund new original content across local languages, with a sharper focus on premium originals.
  • The international launch. The company will launch its microdramas internationally, taking a format that has grown in India to new audiences and new markets.
  • The AI bet. EloElo will double down on Dolphin AI, the agentic AI creative suite it recently launched, which it presents as the production engine behind its content economics.
  • The ownership argument. The company says what sets it apart is that it owns all three layers of the business: the content, the distribution, and the AI powered production engine.

Why NBCUniversal matters

  • The strategic backer. NBCUniversal joins as a co lead investor, and EloElo says the partnership brings NBCUniversal's global capabilities to its expansion beyond India, including into the United States market.
  • The NBCUniversal view. Deepak Arya, executive vice president for corporate strategy and development at NBCUniversal, said the company sees significant opportunity in mobile first storytelling and emerging formats like microdramas to engage audiences and meet evolving viewing habits.
  • The Hornbill view. Manoj Thakur, founder and managing partner of Hornbill Capital, said EloElo combines strong creative capabilities with the ability to scale content through technology, and pointed to the team's execution, financial discipline, and AI capabilities as reasons to back its next phase.
  • The US angle. For American viewers, the practical point is distribution: a microdrama library built for phones in India is now being positioned for an international audience, with a major US media company as a backer rather than only as a future buyer of shows.

The market EloElo cites

  • The global figure. EloElo cites research firm Omdia, which estimates microdramas will generate $14 billion globally by the end of 2026, placing the format among the fastest growing in global entertainment.
  • The India figure. In India, EloElo cites Redseer estimates that 40 million people paid for a microdrama at least once in fiscal year 2026, a sign that short paid episodes already have a paying audience in its home market.
  • The longer horizon. EloElo also cites Lumikai's State of India Interactive Media Report, which projects the Indian market will reach $4.5 billion by 2030, giving the company a longer runway to build toward if the format keeps growing.
  • What to watch. The test of this round is not the fundraising itself but the international launch: whether short dramas made for Indian language audiences travel to new markets, and whether Dolphin AI can keep production costs low enough to make that expansion pay.
