Salesforce Signs Deal to Acquire Voice AI Company Cresta

By Jayesh Marathe · November 4, 2026 · 7 min read

Salesforce has signed a definitive agreement to acquire Cresta, the voice-first operating system for customer experience, bringing AI voice agents for complex customer calls into Service Cloud. Salesforce announced the agreement on November 2, 2026, and expects it to close in the first quarter of fiscal year 2028, subject to regulatory approvals. No purchase price is disclosed in the announcement.

The deal

  • The agreement. Salesforce signed a definitive agreement to acquire Cresta, the voice-first operating system for customer experience.
  • The closing timeline. Salesforce expects the transaction to close in the first quarter of fiscal year 2028, subject to customary closing conditions, including receipt of required regulatory approvals.
  • The price. The announcement discloses no purchase price or other financial terms for the acquisition.
  • The pairing. Salesforce plans to bring Cresta together with Service Cloud, so businesses can connect every conversation to the customer and to the outcome it drives, which Salesforce lists as resolution, retention and revenue.
  • The continuity promise. After the transaction closes, Cresta will continue to support the leading contact center and telephony platforms its customers rely on today.

What Cresta adds

  • The voice agents. Cresta's voice agents resolve complex customer calls end to end, holding natural, real-time conversations, handling interruptions and operating at enterprise scale.
  • The human coaching. The same platform guides human representatives in real time, tests and improves voice agents, and holds both people and agents to the same standard of quality, so every interaction makes the next one better.
  • The analysis layer. Cresta's platform analyzes every interaction, turning conversations that Salesforce says are too often trapped in silos into insight connected to customer context and the tools teams use to act.
  • The named customers. Salesforce names Marriott, Comcast and Alaska Airlines among the leading brands running Cresta's voice agents at enterprise scale.
  • The service logic. Salesforce frames voice as the channel customers turn to for their highest-stakes issues, and argues that as voice agents tackle more complex interactions, listening alone is not enough and businesses must connect each insight to the customer to drive real outcomes.

Why Salesforce wants the phone call

  • The hardest channel. "Voice is where service is hardest to get right, and where it matters most to customers," said Kishan Chetan, executive vice president and general manager of Agentforce and Service Cloud at Salesforce.
  • The scale argument. Chetan said Cresta has built voice agents that handle complex, high-volume calls for some of the world's largest brands, and that as more service is resolved inside the conversation itself, the combination will bring the customer, the conversation and the outcome into one place.
  • The customer history edge. Salesforce brings the customer's history and the systems to get work done, while Cresta brings a deep understanding of every conversation and the ability to drive action with voice agents, according to the announcement.
  • The agentic bet. The deal extends Salesforce's push to resolve service inside the conversation itself, rather than treating the call as a handoff to a separate system or a record created after the fact.

The company behind the platform

  • The company. Cresta Intelligence, Inc. is headquartered in Sunnyvale, California, and describes itself as an enterprise customer experience AI platform that unifies human and AI agents on a single system of intelligence.
  • The origins. Cresta was founded in 2017 as a spinout of the Stanford AI Lab, co-founded by Sebastian Thrun, Zayd Enam and Tim Shi, and is led today by Ping Wu, the former co-founder of Google's Contact Center AI.
  • The product set. Cresta's platform combines autonomous AI agents, real-time Agentic Assist for human agents, Deputy Agent for end-to-end workflow automation, and Conversation Intelligence, all built on Cresta Opera, a no-code AI orchestration layer.
  • The scale claimed. Cresta says it has processed more than 100 million conversations for enterprise customers, and its publicly referenced customers include Verizon, United Airlines, Cox Communications, Marriott, Hilton, CVS and Alaska Airlines.
  • The chief executive's case. "Cresta's mission is to turn every customer interaction into a competitive advantage for our customers through a single agentic operating system for customer experience," said Ping Wu, CEO of Cresta, adding that uniting with Salesforce will let the company realize that vision at scale.

What happens next

  • The approvals. The deal still needs to clear its customary closing conditions, including required regulatory approvals, before it can close in the first quarter of fiscal year 2028.
  • The integration. Salesforce plans to embed Cresta's real-time conversation intelligence and agentic experiences directly into its customer service platform, alongside Service Cloud.
  • The open question. With no price disclosed in the announcement, the financial size of the deal remains unstated by either company, leaving the strategic shape of the acquisition, a voice layer inside Salesforce's customer service platform, as the confirmed story for now.
