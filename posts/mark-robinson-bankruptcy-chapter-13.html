Mark Robinson Files for Bankruptcy With $767 in the Bank

By Jayesh Marathe · November 11, 2026 · 7 min read

Mark Robinson, the former lieutenant governor of North Carolina, filed for Chapter 13 bankruptcy protection on November 3 with his wife Yolanda Hill, reporting $766.98 in liquid assets against nearly $800,000 in debt. The filing lands two years after his campaign for governor collapsed, and it puts the couple under court supervision as they seek to repay what they owe.

The filing

  • The numbers. In the November 3 filing, Robinson and Hill reported $766.98 in the bank while owing creditors nearly $800,000. The petition estimates both their assets and their liabilities at between $500,000 and $1 million.
  • What they owe. The debts include about $120,000 in credit card debt, another $94,000 in other debt and about $100,000 in accounts held by collection agencies. More than $475,000 is secured debt, mostly tied to the couple's home and vehicles, and more than $300,000 is non-priority unsecured debt.
  • What Chapter 13 means. Chapter 13 lets wage earners pay down debts on a court approved repayment plan over several years while keeping personal property such as a home. Filing for protection is not the same as having debts erased, and the court must approve the plan.
  • What they hold. The couple still holds more than $500,000 in property, including a home, vehicles and firearms, according to the filing.

The disputed debt

  • A lawsuit that followed the campaign. Among the listed liabilities is a potential debt tied to a lawsuit from Greensboro musician Louis Love Money. Robinson sued Money for defamation during his 2024 run for governor, then dropped the suit after losing the race. Money sued in May to recover the legal costs of defending himself.
  • Listed as disputed. The filing marks the Money claim as disputed, which means Robinson's responsibility for the amount has not been established as an uncontested debt, and the litigation remains pending.
  • The nonprofit's separate debt. The filing also names Hill's former nonprofit, Balanced Nutrition Inc., as a co-debtor. The state ordered the nonprofit to repay more than $100,000 after a review found it overstated billing, and that money was uncollected as of March. The order was issued to the nonprofit, not to Hill as an individual.

The fall from office

  • Four years as lieutenant governor. Robinson served as North Carolina's lieutenant governor from 2021 to 2025, a post that now pays more than $173,000 a year. He rose to prominence as a conservative voice before winning the office.
  • The 2024 campaign. Robinson's campaign for governor faltered after CNN reported posts linked to him on a pornography site's message board, which he denied writing. Democrat Josh Stein beat him in a landslide.
  • What he does now. The filing identifies Robinson's current occupation as a podcast host for Real Truth Media Inc., where he presents the political commentary program Mark Robinson Unscripted.
  • Not his first filing. Robinson has sought bankruptcy protection before, with filings in 1998, 1999 and 2003. The 2003 case ended with the couple losing Chapter 13 protection after failing to comply with the requirements of the plan.

What happens next

  • The plan. The court will now supervise the couple's finances as they propose a repayment plan, and creditors will have a chance to weigh in before a judge approves it.
  • The open question. The filing shows how far Robinson's finances have fallen since he held statewide office, and the repayment plan will show how much of the nearly $800,000 creditors can expect to recover.
