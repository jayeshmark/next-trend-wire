Apple Reports a Record $114.5 Billion September Quarter on Strong iPhone Sales

By Editorial Team · 7 min read

Apple reported November 2 that revenue reached $114.5 billion in its fiscal 2026 fourth quarter, up 12 percent from a year earlier and a record for a September quarter. Diluted earnings per share rose 14 percent to $2.10, also a September quarter record, in the first earnings report since John Ternus became chief executive.

What happened

  • The headline numbers. Apple posted quarterly revenue of $114.5 billion for the three months ended September 26, 2026, up 12 percent year over year, with net income of $30.71 billion. Diluted earnings per share came in at $2.10, up 14 percent from a year earlier, and the company called total revenue, iPhone revenue, net income, and earnings per share all September quarter records.
  • The tariff refund effect. Company gross margin was 47.9 percent, including a favorable impact of approximately 1 percentage point from tariff refunds, while earnings per share included a favorable impact of $0.05 from the same refunds. The refunds explain part of both the margin and the earnings growth, so the underlying result is slightly less strong than the headline suggests.
  • The chief executive's framing. John Ternus, Apple's chief executive, pointed to iPhone reaching a September quarter record and Services reaching an all-time high, and described the quarter as "capping off our best fiscal year ever". He also set up the holiday season around what he called Apple's most ambitious lineup yet, including the iPhone Duo, the iPhone 18 Pro and iPhone 18 Pro Max, Touch on Mac for the first time, and a new Siri AI.

The quarter by category

  • The iPhone carried the quarter. iPhone revenue rose 17.5 percent to $57.583 billion, a September quarter record that supplied more than half of Apple's total sales. The quarter ended September 26, so it captures only the opening days of iPhone 18 Pro sales, while sales of the iPhone Duo, which went on sale in October, fall entirely into the current quarter.
  • The Mac grew fastest. Mac revenue rose 23.7 percent to $10.792 billion, the fastest growth rate of any category Apple reports. The result gives the Mac a strong base heading into a holiday quarter in which Apple is pushing Touch on Mac as a first, a feature Ternus singled out in his comments on the new lineup.
  • Services set another record. Services revenue rose 9.8 percent to $31.573 billion, a new all-time high for the business that covers the App Store, subscriptions, and payments. Services is now Apple's second largest business by a wide margin, and its steady growth matters because it recurs quarter after quarter in a way hardware sales do not.
  • Two categories slipped. iPad revenue fell 10.4 percent to $6.229 billion, while Wearables, Home and Accessories declined 7.9 percent to $8.300 billion. The declines show the quarter was not uniformly strong, and they leave Apple with two hardware lines heading into the holidays needing a better showing than they managed in September.
  • Greater China grew. Greater China revenue rose to about $17.5 billion from about $14.5 billion a year earlier, growth of roughly 21 percent. The region keeps pace with the company overall, which matters because China has been one of the most closely watched markets for iPhone demand.

The full year and the cash

  • A record fiscal year. For fiscal 2026 as a whole, Apple reported all-time record revenue of $479 billion, up from $416.2 billion in fiscal 2025, with double digit earnings per share growth. Full year net income reached $132.2 billion, up from $112.0 billion a year earlier, closing out a year in which the iPhone alone generated $254.1 billion in sales.
  • The installed base kept growing. Parekh said the installed base of active devices reached a new all-time high across all major product categories and geographic segments, crediting very high levels of customer satisfaction and loyalty. That base is the foundation of the Services business, since every active device is a potential subscriber.

What the quarter does not show

  • The Duo is still untested in the numbers. Because the quarter ended September 26, none of the iPhone Duo's October sales appear in these results, and Ternus described the Duo as the most transformational iPhone since the original. Whether that description translates into sales is a question for the December quarter, not one these numbers answer.
  • The tariff benefit may not repeat. Both the gross margin and earnings per share figures carry a tariff refund benefit, about 1 percentage point of margin and $0.05 of earnings per share, and the announcement does not say whether similar refunds will recur. Investors comparing future quarters with this one will need to separate the refund effect from the underlying trend.
  • Two hardware lines need watching. The iPad and Wearables, Home and Accessories declines sit alongside the iPhone and Mac gains, and the announcement offers no explanation for either slip. If those lines keep shrinking, the company's growth story narrows to fewer products.

What comes next

  • The holiday quarter is the real test. Apple heads into the December quarter with the iPhone Duo on sale, the iPhone 18 Pro line in full swing, Touch on Mac, and a new Siri AI, all of which Ternus framed as the lineup that will carry the company into the holidays. The September quarter set records, but the December quarter is where the new products either prove themselves or do not.
  • The test to watch. The quarter answers the question of whether Apple could still set records under a new chief executive, and the answer is yes, but the harder question is whether the iPhone Duo and the rest of the new lineup can repeat the feat in a quarter that counts them in full.
