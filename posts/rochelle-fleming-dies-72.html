Rochelle Fleming, Lead Singer of Disco Group First Choice, Dies at 72

By Editorial Team · 7 min read

Rochelle Fleming, the founding lead singer of the Philadelphia soul and disco group First Choice, died on November 5 in Philadelphia at 72. Her manager announced the death on Saturday in a post on her official Instagram page, and no cause of death was disclosed.

The announcement

  • The death. Fleming died on November 5 in Philadelphia at 72, her manager George Iz Correia announced in a post on her official Instagram page on Saturday, November 7.
  • The manager's words. "It is with a heavy heart to share the passing of Rochelle Fleming-Finney," Correia wrote. "She was the founder of the R&B group, First Choice. She passed away on November 5 at the age of 72 in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania. She brought light, laughter and love to the world."
  • What is not known. No cause of death has been disclosed, and details of a service to celebrate her life will be announced at a later date.
  • The label's tribute. BMG, which represents part of First Choice's catalog through Salsoul, said it was "deeply saddened by the passing of Rochelle Fleming" in a tribute published on November 9.

The voice of First Choice

  • The beginning. Fleming began singing with school friends in Philadelphia in a group called the Debonettes, with fellow Overbrook High School students Annette Guest and Wardell Piper, and the group became First Choice.
  • The breakthrough. First Choice broke through in 1973 with "Armed and Extremely Dangerous", followed by "Smarty Pants" and "The Player", with Fleming's powerful lead vocal giving the group's recordings their character.
  • The Salsoul years. Working with producer Norman Harris, the group recorded for Gold Mind, a label associated with Salsoul, and their 1977 album Delusions featured "Doctor Love" and "Let No Man Put Asunder".
  • The solo years. After First Choice disbanded in 1980, Fleming continued to perform and record as a solo artist, carrying the group's songs to audiences around the world.

The legacy

  • The song that lived on. "Let No Man Put Asunder" became one of the most sampled and remixed records in dance music, embraced by generations of DJs and producers through remixes, samples, and reinterpretations.
  • The label's verdict. "Rochelle was a remarkable singer, and the records she made with First Choice have had a lasting influence on dance music," Dan Baxter, senior vice president of global catalog recordings at BMG, said. "'Doctor Love', 'Love Thang' and 'Let No Man Put Asunder' still connect with dancers and DJs around the world."
  • The voice that carried. "Her voice and the joy it brought to so many will not be forgotten," Baxter said, offering condolences to Fleming's family, friends, and fellow members of First Choice.
