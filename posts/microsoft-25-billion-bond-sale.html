Microsoft Priced a $25 Billion Bond Sale. Closing Is Expected Today.

By Jayesh Marathe · November 4, 2026 · 7 min read

Microsoft priced a $25 billion offering of senior unsecured notes on October 29, and the offering is expected to close on November 4, 2026. The sale spans ten tranches with maturities from 2028 to 2066, and Microsoft says the proceeds will fund general corporate purposes, including capital expenditures.

The deal Microsoft priced

  • The size. Microsoft announced the pricing of $25 billion in aggregate principal amount of senior unsecured notes on October 29, 2026, splitting the offering into ten separate tranches so that repayment dates spread across nearly four decades.
  • The floating rate notes. The offering includes $1.00 billion of floating rate notes due November 3, 2028, and $1.00 billion of floating rate notes due November 3, 2029, giving Microsoft two short dated pieces whose interest payments move with market rates rather than locking in a fixed coupon.
  • The fixed rate notes. The eight fixed rate tranches run from $2.00 billion of 4.85 percent notes due November 3, 2028, to $2.00 billion of 6.35 percent notes due November 3, 2066, with the middle of the ladder carrying $3.25 billion of 5.00 percent notes due in 2029, $3.25 billion of 5.20 percent notes due in 2031, $2.50 billion of 5.35 percent notes due in 2033, $4.00 billion of 5.55 percent notes due in 2036, $2.00 billion of 6.15 percent notes due in 2046, and $4.00 billion of 6.25 percent notes due in 2056.
  • The closing date. The offerings are expected to close on November 4, 2026, which makes the pricing announcement from October 29 the setup and the closing the event that turns the sale into funded debt on Microsoft's balance sheet.
  • The banks running the sale. J.P. Morgan Securities, UBS Securities, and Wells Fargo Securities lead a group of eleven joint book-running managers, with Barclays, BNP Paribas, BofA Securities, Citigroup, Goldman Sachs, HSBC, Morgan Stanley, and RBC Capital Markets also on the book-running list, plus twelve co-managers.
  • The use of proceeds. Microsoft intends to use the net proceeds for general corporate purposes, which may include funding for working capital, capital expenditures, repurchases of capital stock, acquisitions, and repayment of existing debt, a broad formulation that leaves the company free to direct the money wherever its plans require it.

What the release does not say

  • No single project gets the money. The release ties the proceeds to general corporate purposes rather than to any one program, product, or building plan, so readers should not treat the full $25 billion as earmarked for artificial intelligence, data centers, or any other specific investment.
  • No AI pledge appears in the terms. Artificial intelligence appears in the release only in its closing company description, which says Microsoft creates platforms and tools powered by AI, while the sections that set out the notes, the tranches, and the use of proceeds say nothing about AI spending.
  • The distinction matters. Borrowing for general purposes and borrowing to build data centers are different claims, and only the first is what Microsoft actually announced, so the honest reading is that capital expenditures sit among several possible uses, not that the whole sale funds one of them.

Why Microsoft is borrowing now

  • The earnings backdrop. Microsoft reported revenue of $92.3 billion for the quarter ended September 30, 2026, up 19 percent from the same period a year earlier, one day before it priced the bond sale, which puts the borrowing in the context of a growing business rather than a company raising emergency cash.
  • The cloud engine. Microsoft Cloud revenue reached $63.9 billion in that quarter, up 30 percent, and Azure revenue increased 48 percent, showing the part of the business that demands the most infrastructure investment growing faster than the company as a whole.
  • The timing. Pricing the notes on October 29, immediately after reporting those results, let Microsoft borrow while its latest numbers were fresh in front of investors, and the November 4 closing date keeps the whole transaction inside a single week of market attention.
  • The maturity ladder. Spreading repayment from 2028 to 2066 means Microsoft faces no single refinancing wall from this sale, since the largest individual tranches, the $4.00 billion pieces due in 2036 and 2056, come due twenty years apart.

What to watch next

  • The closing. The first marker is whether the offering closes on November 4, 2026, as the release expects, since closing converts priced notes into money Microsoft can actually spend.
  • The spending trail. Later filings and earnings reports will show where the proceeds actually go, whether toward capital expenditures, share repurchases, acquisitions, or debt repayment, and that allocation will say more about Microsoft's priorities than the broad language of the release does.
  • The next quarter. Microsoft's next earnings report will show whether cloud growth keeps running near the 48 percent Azure pace reported for the September quarter, which is the demand signal that decides whether heavy infrastructure spending stays justified.
