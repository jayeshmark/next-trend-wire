Gunman Kills Six in Krakow Main Square as Three More Bodies Are Found at His Home

By Jayesh Marathe · November 7, 2026 · 7 min read

A gunman drove into Krakow's Main Market Square on Saturday afternoon and opened fire on crowds, killing six people and wounding 15 before police shot and arrested him, and hours later three more bodies were found at his home as Poland's prime minister called the attack terrorism.

What happened

  • The attack. Around 3 p.m. local time on Saturday, November 7, a man drove a car into the Main Market Square in the heart of Krakow's Old Town and began firing at random people from the vehicle, according to Malopolska Police.
  • The toll in the square. Six people were killed, four women and two men, and 15 people were wounded, police said, and two of the wounded were police officers, one of them in critical condition after being shot three times, according to Malopolska Police.
  • The gunman was stopped. Officers returned fire, shot the gunman, and arrested him, and he was taken to a hospital, where he underwent surgery and was in stable but critical condition, according to Malopolska Police.
  • He acted alone. Police said the gunman was a Polish citizen and the only known perpetrator, and they denied reports of a second attacker and denied reports that a police officer had been killed, according to Malopolska Police.
  • The weapon and the response. A police spokesperson told the broadcaster TVN24 that the gunman used an AR-15 rifle and held a firearms licence, and police said many other weapons and ammunition were found in his car, while police said the first patrol reached the square 23 seconds after the first report and a medical helicopter landed on the square.

The second scene

  • Three more bodies. Late on Saturday, police searching the gunman's home in Ostrowiec Świętokrzyski, about 200 kilometres north of Krakow, found the bodies of two women and a man, and Prime Minister Donald Tusk said there were strong indications they were members of the gunman's immediate family.
  • The investigation widens. Seven prosecutors from three offices are working the case, and police and prosecutors were still working at the square and at the home on Sunday as they tried to establish whether anyone helped the gunman.
  • The victims. One of the six killed in the square was a British citizen, police said, and five of the wounded were foreign nationals from Austria, Germany, Portugal, Russia, and Britain.

The official response

  • Terrorism, the prime minister said. Prime Minister Donald Tusk called the shooting an act of terrorism, and Defence Minister Władysław Kosiniak-Kamysz described it the same way, while Tusk travelled to Krakow on Saturday night and chaired a crisis meeting with ministers, security officials, and the mayor.
  • A possible motive, not a confirmed one. Interior Minister Marcin Kierwiński said early indications pointed to the gunman being fascinated by far-right ideology and holding ultra-nationalist views, but he said that would be part of the investigation and police have not established a motive.
  • Mourning. Krakow Mayor Łukasz Gibała signed an order declaring a period of mourning in the city until November 11, according to Łukasz Gibała, and Poland declared two days of national mourning for November 8 and 9, while Gibała said the city would stay open to visitors and the square would belong to Krakow again.

The place

  • The square. The Main Market Square sits at the heart of Krakow's Old Town in Poland's second-largest city, and it ranks among the country's busiest tourist draws, which is why the square was crowded on a Saturday afternoon.
  • The timing. The attack came four days before Poland's Independence Day on November 11, and the city's mourning period runs until that day.

Why it matters

  • A rare scale of violence. Mass shootings are rare in Poland, and the attack struck one of the country's busiest tourist squares, the Main Market Square in Krakow's Old Town, days before Poland's Independence Day on November 11.
  • The home deaths change the case. The three bodies found at the gunman's home mean the investigation now covers two scenes and a possible family killing before the square attack, and it is not yet known when the three people at the home died.

What to watch next

  • The gunman's condition. The gunman is in hospital in critical condition, and whether he recovers enough to be questioned will shape how fast prosecutors can establish a motive.
  • The investigation's findings. Police and prosecutors are still establishing the full sequence, including whether anyone helped the gunman, and the interior minister said the far-right ideology angle would be investigated.
