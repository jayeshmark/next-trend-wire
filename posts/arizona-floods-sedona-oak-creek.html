Flash Flooding Forces Sedona Evacuations as Oak Creek Surges Past Flood Stage

By Editorial Team · 7 min read

Flash flooding on Oak Creek forced immediate evacuations in Sedona, Arizona, on Sunday, November 1, closed highways across northern Arizona, and left a flood warning in place into Monday morning as water levels fell in Sedona but stayed above flood stage downstream at Cornville.

What happened

  • The creek surged overnight. Heavy rain fell Saturday night onto ground already soaked by earlier storms, and Oak Creek rose fast through the early hours of Sunday, November 1, pushing Sedona into emergency evacuations on Sunday morning.
  • The gauge passed flood stage. The provisional gauge reading for Oak Creek at Sedona reached 15.06 feet at 7:45 a.m., above its 14 foot flood stage, according to the National Weather Service flash flood warning issued at 8:15 a.m., while downstream at Cornville the creek stood at 8.88 feet at 8:00 a.m., above its 8 foot flood stage.
  • The warning turned urgent. The National Weather Service in Flagstaff warned of life threatening flash flooding of creeks and streams, normally dry washes, urban areas, highways, streets and underpasses, after between 1.25 and 2.5 inches of rain had fallen across the warned area.

The evacuations

  • Three zones ordered out. The City of Sedona placed evacuation zones 8, 9 and 10 on GO status at 7:45 a.m. on Sunday, meaning danger was imminent and residents had to leave at once, with evacuees directed to Sedona Red Rock High School, according to the city's evacuation map.
  • The creekside neighborhoods. The zones covered properties along Oak Creek, including Trails End Road, Sycamore Road, the Villas at Poco Diablo Resort, Rancho Sedona RV Park, and homes next to the creek, where the city warned residents to stay away from creek beds and washes and never to cross running water on a road.
  • Rescues from flooded homes. The Sedona Fire District called in extra personnel for what it described as an excessive amount of rescue operations, as crews responded to reports of residents trapped in their homes by rising water.
  • Camp Verde ordered out too. Around noon on Sunday, the Camp Verde Marshals Office ordered residents near the Verde River, West Clear Creek and Wet Beaver Creek to evacuate immediately, extending the emergency beyond Sedona into the Verde Valley.
  • A sewage spill followed. The City of Sedona reported that about 2,700 gallons of sewage entered Oak Creek on Sunday morning after flooding overwhelmed a pump station near Bear Wallow Lane, when heavy rainfall pushed inflow past what the station's pumps could handle.

The roads closed

  • Interstate 17 shut, then reopened. The Arizona Department of Transportation closed Interstate 17 northbound at State Route 179 and southbound at milepost 337 near the Flagstaff airport on Sunday morning, then reopened the freeway in both directions by Sunday afternoon, according to real time highway conditions from ADOT.
  • State routes stayed shut. The Arizona Department of Transportation reported State Route 89A between Sedona and Kachina Village, State Route 73 between Indian Pine and White River, State Route 238 between Maricopa and Gila Bend, and State Route 260 near Pinetop-Lakeside still closed with no estimated time to reopen, as crews responded to flooding, debris and rockfall across northern Arizona.
  • Rockfall added to the risk. ADOT crews worked across northern Arizona clearing rockfall and debris from routes, and officials warned drivers to check conditions before travelling, since standing water, debris and rockfall remained a risk even where a road had reopened.

The state response

  • The governor monitored the response. Governor Katie Hobbs said she was closely monitoring the Oak Creek flooding and the evacuations across Arizona and had directed state resources to help, with state air rescue crews assisting with rooftop evacuations and transportation crews clearing debris from roads.
  • Mutual aid poured in. Phoenix Fire sent a battalion chief, a heavy rescue unit and a rescue company to help the Verde Valley Fire District under a statewide mutual aid request, the department said, with Surprise sending a brush tender company and Glendale sending a heavy rescue unit.
  • Nearly 20 homes needed help. Nearly 20 homes near Oak Creek had residents who needed emergency assistance to relocate, Yavapai County spokesperson David McAtee said, and no injuries or deaths had been reported by Sunday afternoon.

What remains in place Monday

  • The warning runs to noon. The National Weather Service in Flagstaff issued a flood warning on Monday, November 2, at 5:39 a.m. MST, running until noon MST, warning that roads were closed and impassable and that flooding over low water crossings could prove deadly.
  • Cornville stayed above flood stage. Oak Creek at Cornville stood at 10.57 feet at 5:00 a.m. on Monday, above its 8 foot flood stage, even as water levels continued to fall through the morning, the warning said.
  • Sedona fell back below flood stage. Oak Creek at Sedona stood at 7.06 feet at 4:45 a.m. on Monday, below its 14 foot flood stage but still at action stage, while water levels along the creek continued to fall through the morning, the warning said.
  • Two highways stayed in the warning. The Monday warning still listed State Route 179 between mileposts 310 and 313 and State Route 89A between mileposts 373 and 393, a sign that creekside travel around Sedona remained unsafe even after the worst of the rain had passed.

What to watch next

  • The evacuation orders. Watch for the next update on the evacuation orders for zones 8, 9 and 10, and on when crews clear homes and roads along Oak Creek for residents to return.
  • The Cornville gauge. Watch whether Oak Creek at Cornville keeps falling through Monday, since it remained above flood stage on Monday morning even as Sedona's gauge fell.
  • The reopening of state routes. Watch when State Route 89A, State Route 73, State Route 238 and State Route 260 reopen, since ADOT gave no estimated time for those closures on Sunday.
  • The next round of rain. Watch whether further rainfall upstream pushes Oak Creek back up, since the creek remained above flood stage at Cornville on Monday morning even as levels fell.
