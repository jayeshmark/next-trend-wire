Yella Beezy Gets Life Without Parole for Mo3 Murder

By Jayesh Marathe · November 12, 2026 · 7 min read

A Dallas jury found rapper Yella Beezy guilty of capital murder on Wednesday for hiring the gunman who killed fellow rapper Mo3 in 2020. The judge sentenced him to life in prison without the possibility of parole, the mandatory punishment in Texas when prosecutors do not seek the death penalty.

The verdict

  • The conviction. Yella Beezy, 35, whose real name is Markies Conway, was unanimously convicted of capital murder by a Dallas County jury on Wednesday, November 11, after two weeks of testimony.
  • The sentence. The judge sentenced Conway to life in prison without the possibility of parole immediately after the verdict was read. The sentence is mandatory under Texas law for an adult convicted of capital murder when prosecutors are not seeking the death penalty.
  • The charge. Prosecutors said Conway paid a man named Kewon White $40,000 to kill Mo3, whose real name was Melvin Noble, after a feud between the two rappers that had lasted years.
  • The plea. Conway pleaded not guilty and denied taking part in the killing.

The killing

  • The day. Mo3, 28, was shot and killed on Interstate 35 in south Dallas around noon on November 11, 2020, exactly six years to the day before the verdict. White pulled alongside Noble's car and fired more than 40 rounds from an AK-47 style pistol while chasing him, prosecutors said.
  • The gunman. White was separately convicted of capital murder in September 2026 and sentenced to life in prison without parole. A third defendant, Devin Brown, is also charged in connection with the killing and is awaiting trial.
  • The courtroom. People in the courtroom cried as the verdict was read, including the rapper's young son, according to local television station WFAA. The prosecutor announced there will be no victim impact statements.

The case

  • The feud. Prosecutors said the killing was the result of an escalating feud between Conway and Noble that had lasted years. Conway had been shot in a roadway shooting in 2018, an incident prosecutors drew a comparison with during the trial.
  • The trial. The trial ran for two weeks, with closing arguments on Tuesday, November 10. The judge told the jury that statements made before the grand jury were inadmissible as proof of the claims against Conway.
  • The closing. The conviction closes the most closely watched chapter of a case that has run for six years, with one defendant still to face trial.
