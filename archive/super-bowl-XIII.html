The game story

Super Bowl XIII was the most anticipated Super Bowl to that point, and it delivered. It was the first Super Bowl rematch, Pittsburgh against Dallas again after Pittsburgh's 21 to 17 win in Super Bowl X, and both teams were trying to become the first club to win three Super Bowls. Dallas was the defending champion, having beaten Denver the year before. Pittsburgh was 14 and 2 in the regular season, Dallas was 12 and 4, and both teams had survived tough playoff runs. The game, played at the Orange Bowl for the fifth and last time, was close, high scoring, and decided in the final minute, and it is still ranked among the best Super Bowls ever played.

The first half was a shootout in bursts. Pittsburgh scored first when Bradshaw threw a 28 yard touchdown pass to John Stallworth. Dallas answered when Staubach threw a 39 yard touchdown pass to Tony Hill as the first quarter ended. In the second quarter, Dallas took the lead when Hegman stripped the ball from Bradshaw and ran 37 yards for a touchdown, making it 14 to 7. Bradshaw answered immediately with a 75 yard touchdown pass to Stallworth, his second touchdown catch of the game, tying it at 14. Then, just before halftime, Bradshaw threw a 7 yard touchdown pass to Rocky Bleier, who leaped high in the corner of the end zone to catch it, and Pittsburgh led 21 to 14 at the break. Bradshaw already had 3 touchdown passes, and the game had already produced more drama than most Super Bowls manage in four quarters.

The third quarter produced only one score, and it produced the play that Dallas fans still talk about. Trailing 21 to 14, Dallas drove from its own 42 to the Pittsburgh 10. On third down, Staubach saw tight end Jackie Smith alone in the end zone and threw to him. Smith slipped as he reached for the pass, the ball fell incomplete, and Dallas settled for a 27 yard field goal by Rafael Septien, cutting the lead to 21 to 17. It was the kind of play that changes a game without appearing in the scoring summary as anything more than a field goal, because a touchdown there would have given Dallas the lead. Instead, Dallas trailed by 4 going into the fourth quarter, and Pittsburgh took control.

Early in the fourth quarter, Pittsburgh scored twice in quick succession. First, Franco Harris ran 22 yards for a touchdown, a drive helped by a pass interference penalty against Dallas cornerback Benny Barnes that cost Dallas 33 yards. Then, after Pittsburgh recovered the ball again, Bradshaw threw an 18 yard touchdown pass to Lynn Swann, his fourth touchdown pass of the game, and Pittsburgh led 35 to 17. The game looked over. It was not. Dallas scored with 2:27 left when Staubach threw a 7 yard touchdown pass to Billy Joe DuPree, recovered an onside kick, and scored again with 22 seconds left when Staubach threw a 4 yard touchdown pass to Butch Johnson. The score was 35 to 31, and Dallas had one more onside kick to try. Bleier recovered it for Pittsburgh with 17 seconds left, and the Steelers ran out the clock.

Bradshaw finished 17 of 30 for 318 yards and 4 touchdowns, a Super Bowl record for touchdown passes at the time, and was named MVP. Swann caught 7 passes for 124 yards and a touchdown, and Stallworth caught 3 for 115 yards and 2 touchdowns, making them the first pair of teammates to each gain 100 receiving yards in a Super Bowl. Staubach was also 17 of 30, for 228 yards and 3 touchdowns, and Dorsett ran for 96 yards, but the dropped pass by Smith and the two quick Pittsburgh touchdowns in the fourth quarter were the difference. Pittsburgh became the first team to win three Super Bowls, and the 66 combined points were a Super Bowl record at the time. Dallas had come back from 18 points down in the final minutes to within 4, and still lost, which is why this game is remembered as both a classic and a heartbreak, depending on which sideline you stood on.

Why this game mattered

Super Bowl XIII confirmed the Steelers and Cowboys as the two defining franchises of the 1970s, and it gave their rivalry its signature game. It was the first Super Bowl rematch, the first game in which a team won a third Super Bowl, and at the time the highest scoring Super Bowl ever played, with 66 points. The Jackie Smith drop became one of the most famous missed chances in Super Bowl history, a shorthand for how a single play can swing a championship. The game also marked the end of an era at the Orange Bowl, which had hosted five Super Bowls and would never host another. For Pittsburgh, it was the third of four titles in six years. For Dallas, it was the last Super Bowl appearance for Staubach, who retired after the following season, and the start of a long wait for the Cowboys' next championship.
