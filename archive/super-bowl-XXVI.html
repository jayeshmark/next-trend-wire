Super Bowl XXVI: Washington vs Buffalo Bills

By Editorial Team

Result in brief: Washington won its third Super Bowl title in ten seasons. Buffalo suffered a second straight Super Bowl defeat, and the 37 to 24 loss was the most one sided of the Bills' four Super Bowl defeats.

The game story

Washington arrived in Minneapolis as the most complete team of the 1991 season. The team had finished 14 and 2, had scored a league leading 485 points, and had allowed the second fewest points in the league. Head coach Joe Gibbs was chasing a third Super Bowl title, this time with Mark Rypien, his third different starting quarterback in a Super Bowl. Rypien had led the NFC in passing yards and touchdown passes during the regular season, and he threw to Gary Clark and Art Monk, two receivers who could win on the outside and over the middle. The offensive line, known as the Hogs, had allowed only 9 sacks all season. Washington was favored by 7 points, and it played like the favorite from the second quarter on.

Buffalo arrived with a different problem. The Bills had finished 13 and 3 and had reached a second straight Super Bowl behind the no huddle K-Gun offense, Jim Kelly, and Thurman Thomas. But the defense had ranked near the bottom of the league in yards allowed, and the team had lost key defensive players to injury during the season. Buffalo needed its offense to set the pace. Instead, Washington set it, and Buffalo never caught up.

The first quarter ended scoreless, which suited Washington. Early in the second quarter, Chip Lohmiller kicked a 34 yard field goal for a 3 to 0 lead. Rypien then threw a 10 yard touchdown pass to Earnest Byner, and Gerald Riggs ran 1 yard for a touchdown. In less than half a quarter, a tight game had become a 17 to 0 Washington lead. Buffalo had no answer. Kelly was under pressure, Thomas had no room, and the Bills punted and turned the ball over while Washington kept the ball and kept scoring.

The decisive sequence came at the start of the second half. Sixteen seconds into the third quarter, Kurt Gouveia intercepted Kelly and returned the ball to the Buffalo 2 yard line. Riggs scored on the next play, and the lead was 24 to 0. Buffalo did score after that, with a field goal and a 1 yard run by Thomas, but Washington answered with a 30 yard touchdown pass from Rypien to Clark, and the lead was back to 21 points at 31 to 10. Lohmiller added two more field goals in the fourth quarter, and the score reached 37 to 10 before Buffalo scored two late touchdowns against a Washington team that was already celebrating.

Rypien was named most valuable player after passing for 292 yards and 2 touchdowns. Clark and Monk each passed 100 receiving yards. For Washington, the win was a third Super Bowl title in ten seasons, and it confirmed Gibbs as one of the great coaches of his era, able to win with three different quarterbacks. For Buffalo, the loss was a second straight Super Bowl defeat, and it exposed the gap between a great offense and a complete team. Buffalo would reach two more Super Bowls after this one and lose both, but this game was the clearest of the four defeats. Washington did not win on a late kick or a single break. It won by controlling the line, by pressuring Kelly, and by turning a close game into a rout in one quarter.

Scoring timeline

  1. Washington breaks the deadlock. Lohmiller's field goal opens the scoring early in the second quarter.
  2. Washington doubles the lead fast. Rypien finds Byner for a touchdown, and the Bills are chasing the game.
  3. Washington stretches it to 17 to 0. Riggs scores on the ground, and Buffalo has no offensive rhythm.
  4. Washington lands the knockout blow. Gouveia intercepts Kelly seconds into the second half, and Riggs scores one play later for 24 to 0.
  5. Buffalo shows life. A field goal and a Thomas touchdown cut the lead to 24 to 10.
  6. Washington answers at once. Rypien finds Clark for 30 yards and a touchdown, restoring a 21 point lead.
  7. Washington closes the door. Two Lohmiller field goals in the fourth quarter make it 37 to 10.
  8. Buffalo scores twice late. Metzelaars and Beebe catch touchdown passes from Kelly, but the result is no longer in doubt.

Cultural context

This was the first Super Bowl played in Minneapolis, and it was played indoors in January while the temperature outside stayed well below freezing. The Metrodome was the smallest stadium ever to host a Super Bowl, and the attendance of 63,130 was among the lowest in Super Bowl history. The game also marked a turning point for halftime television. The audience loss to In Living Color convinced the league that halftime could no longer be a marching band interlude. The next year, Michael Jackson headlined the show, and the modern era of the halftime show began.

On the field, the game closed the book on Washington as a dominant team of the 1980s and early 1990s. Gibbs would leave after the next season, and Washington has not returned to a Super Bowl since. For Buffalo, the game confirmed a painful pattern. The Bills could reach the Super Bowl, but they could not match a complete NFC champion in the early 1990s. Thomas, Kelly, Andre Reed, and Bruce Smith are all in the Pro Football Hall of Fame, yet none of them won a championship, and this 37 to 24 defeat was the most one sided of their four Super Bowl losses.
