The game story

Super Bowl XII was a matchup with a built in storyline. Denver's quarterback was Craig Morton, who had started for Dallas in Super Bowl V and lost it, and who had then been traded and rebuilt his career in Denver, leading the Broncos to their first Super Bowl behind the Orange Crush defense and a wave of fan enthusiasm. Dallas, meanwhile, was the defending NFC power, back in the Super Bowl for the fourth time under Tom Landry, with Roger Staubach at quarterback and the Doomsday Defense on the other side. It was also a historic occasion for the event itself: the first Super Bowl played indoors, in the Louisiana Superdome, and the first played in prime time.

The game did not live up to the storyline, at least not for Denver. Dallas's defense hounded Morton from the first series. Morton was intercepted on Denver's early possession, and Tony Dorsett ran 3 yards for a touchdown to give Dallas a 7 to 0 lead. Morton was intercepted again, and Efren Herrera kicked a 35 yard field goal to make it 10 to 0. Herrera added a 43 yard field goal just before halftime, and Dallas led 13 to 0 at the break, having forced Morton into one of the worst performances by a starting quarterback in Super Bowl history. Morton finished 4 of 15 for 39 yards, with 4 interceptions, and was benched in the third quarter for Norris Weese.

Denver did fight back in the third quarter, briefly. The Broncos took the opening possession of the second half and got on the board with a 47 yard field goal by Jim Turner, making it 13 to 3. Dallas answered when Staubach threw a 45 yard touchdown pass to Butch Johnson, a diving catch in the end zone that was controversial at the time because Johnson appeared to lose control of the ball as he rolled over the goal line, but which stood as a touchdown and made it 20 to 3. Denver answered again, when Rick Upchurch returned the kickoff to the Dallas 26 and Rob Lytle ran 1 yard for a touchdown, cutting the lead to 20 to 10 at the end of the third quarter. For a moment, Denver had life.

It did not last. In the fourth quarter, Harvey Martin stripped Weese of the ball, setting Dallas up at the Denver 29, and on the very next play, fullback Robert Newhouse threw a 29 yard touchdown pass to Golden Richards on a fullback option, making it 27 to 10. That was the final score. Dallas had committed its own mistakes, fumbling 6 times, though only 2 were lost, but Denver's 8 turnovers were too many to overcome, and Dallas's defense never let Denver's offense find any rhythm. Denver's quarterbacks completed only 8 of 24 passes all game, and the Broncos gained only 156 yards.

The MVP vote reflected how the game had been won. With no offensive player standing out, the voters split the award between White and Martin, the leaders of the Dallas defensive line. It was the first time two players had shared the award, and it remains the only time it has happened. For Landry, it was a second Super Bowl title, and his last. For Morton, it was a painful bookend: he had now lost Super Bowls as the starting quarterback for two different teams, and his performance in this one, 4 interceptions and 3 lost fumbles before being benched, is still cited as one of the worst by a quarterback on this stage. For Denver, the loss was the first Super Bowl appearance in franchise history, and the Broncos would have to wait nearly a decade to get back. For Dallas, it was confirmation that the Doomsday Defense, more than any single offensive star, was the identity of the team.

Why this game mattered

Super Bowl XII mattered because it changed where and when the Super Bowl could be played. Moving indoors to the Superdome and into prime time turned the game into an evening television event, which is how it has stayed ever since. The co-MVP award remains unique, and it says something about how this game was won: not by a quarterback or a running back, but by a defensive line that forced 8 turnovers and held an opponent to 156 yards. Denver's Orange Crush era is remembered fondly in Colorado despite this loss, because reaching the Super Bowl at all was a breakthrough for a franchise that had never won a playoff game before that season. Dallas's win, meanwhile, gave Landry his second title and set up the most famous Cowboys and Steelers rematch of all, Super Bowl XIII, the following January.
