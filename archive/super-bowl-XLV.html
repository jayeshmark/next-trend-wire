Super Bowl XLV: Green Bay Packers vs Pittsburgh Steelers

By Editorial Team

Green Bay won its fourth Super Bowl title, beating Pittsburgh 31-25 after leading 21-3 in the second quarter. Green Bay entered the playoffs as the sixth seed in the NFC and won three road playoff games to reach the game.

The game story

The stakes were about pedigree on both sides, but the paths to Arlington could not have been more different. Pittsburgh was playing in its eighth Super Bowl, a franchise that expected to be there, with Ben Roethlisberger chasing a third title before his twenty ninth birthday. Green Bay was the sixth seed in the NFC, a 10-6 team that had won three road playoff games just to reach the game, and Aaron Rodgers was playing in his first Super Bowl, still measured against Brett Favre, the quarterback he had replaced. The Packers were the underdog in seeding only. On the field, they had the better quarterback that season, and it showed early.

The first quarter was a Green Bay statement. Rodgers found Jordy Nelson for a 29 yard touchdown, and on Pittsburgh's next possession, Roethlisberger threw deep toward Mike Wallace and Nick Collins intercepted the pass and returned it 37 yards for a touchdown. Green Bay led 14-0 before Pittsburgh had scored, and the Steelers looked rattled. Shaun Suisham kicked a 33 yard field goal early in the second quarter, but Rodgers answered with a 21 yard touchdown pass to Greg Jennings, making it 21-3. Roethlisberger found Hines Ward for an 8 yard touchdown just before halftime, and the score at the break was Green Bay 21, Pittsburgh 10. The Steelers were down but not out, and the second half proved it.

Pittsburgh opened the third quarter with its best drive of the night. Rashard Mendenhall ran eight yards for a touchdown, and the lead was down to 21-17. The Steelers had another chance to close the gap, but Suisham missed a 52 yard field goal, and the third quarter ended with Green Bay still ahead by four. The game turned for good on the first play of the fourth quarter. Mendenhall took a handoff up the middle, Clay Matthews forced a fumble, and Desmond Bishop recovered at the Green Bay 38. Rodgers needed only a few plays to make Pittsburgh pay: he found Jennings again, this time from eight yards out, and Green Bay led 28-17 with 12:03 left.

Pittsburgh answered the way it had all night, with Roethlisberger throwing. He found Mike Wallace for a 25 yard touchdown with 7:40 left, and Antwaan Randle El ran in the two point conversion, cutting the lead to 28-25. Green Bay took over and did what championship teams do with a three point lead: it held the ball, moved it in small pieces, and let Mason Crosby kick a 23 yard field goal with 2:10 left, making it 31-25. Pittsburgh had one last drive, but it stalled near midfield, and Green Bay ran out the clock.

The legacy of the game is Rodgers. His line, 24 of 39 for 304 yards and three touchdowns with no interceptions, won him the MVP award and ended, in one night, the argument that he was living in Favre's shadow. Nelson caught nine passes for 140 yards, Jennings caught two touchdowns, and the Packers won a Super Bowl as an NFC wild card team. For Pittsburgh, the loss was the franchise's second in three Super Bowl appearances, and it marked the end of the Roethlisberger era's peak: the Steelers would reach the playoffs again, but they would not return to the Super Bowl. The attendance of 103,219 was among the largest crowds in Super Bowl history, fitting for a game played in the league's newest palace, and the night belonged to the road team that had refused to lose since December.

Why this game mattered

Super Bowl XLV was the Auto Bowl, and the commercials told the story of an industry trying to come back. Nine automakers advertised, up from eight the year before, and the two most remembered spots were both car ads with opposite strategies: Volkswagen's charming, dialogue free "The Force", released online a week before the game, and Chrysler's two minute "Imported from Detroit", a somber civic statement that aired only on television. The Volkswagen spot changed Super Bowl advertising itself, because its online first release became the model nearly every brand has followed since. The Chrysler spot changed Detroit's image, at least for a night, by treating the city as a source of pride rather than a punchline.

The halftime show marked a different changing of the guard. After six years of legacy rock acts booked in the wake of the 2004 halftime controversy, the league gave the stage to the Black Eyed Peas, with Usher and Slash as guests, in an explicit attempt to bring a younger audience back to the show. The performance itself was widely criticized for its vocals and staging, but the booking decision mattered: the era of safe legacy acts was over, and pop headliners returned to the slot for good.

On the field, the game confirmed that the playoffs, not the regular season, decide legacies. Green Bay's 10-6 record was the worst of any team in the game, and the Packers had needed help just to make the postseason. Three road wins later, they were champions, and Rodgers had the MVP award that separated him from Favre in the record books. Pittsburgh, the steadier team all season, lost because it turned the ball over three times and could not survive the fourth quarter fumble. The margin between the two teams was six points, and the difference was one play at the start of the fourth quarter.
