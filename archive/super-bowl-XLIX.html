Super Bowl XLIX: New England Patriots vs Seattle Seahawks

By Editorial Team

Both teams entered the game with 12-4 regular season records. Seattle was the defending champion, having won Super Bowl XLVIII the previous season. New England was seeking its first Super Bowl win since Super Bowl XXXIX, ten years earlier.

Quarterbacks

  • Tom Brady, New England: 37 of 50 passing for 328 yards, 4 touchdowns, 2 interceptions. His 37 completions were a Super Bowl record at the time.
  • Russell Wilson, Seattle: 12 of 21 passing for 247 yards, 2 touchdowns, 1 interception. He also rushed for 39 yards.

Rushing leaders

  • Marshawn Lynch, Seattle: 24 carries for 102 yards and 1 touchdown. He was the game's leading rusher.
  • LeGarrette Blount, New England: 14 carries for 40 yards. He was New England's leading rusher.

Receiving leaders

  • Julian Edelman, New England: 9 receptions for 109 yards and 1 touchdown.
  • Chris Matthews, Seattle: 4 receptions for 109 yards and 1 touchdown. Matthews had not caught a pass in the regular season or postseason before this game.

  • Jermaine Kearse, Seattle: 3 receptions for 45 yards.
  • Doug Baldwin, Seattle: 1 reception for 3 yards and 1 touchdown.

Decisive defensive plays

  • Jeremy Lane, Seattle, intercepted Tom Brady at the goal line in the first quarter, stopping a long New England drive. Lane was injured on the return and did not return to the game.
  • Bobby Wagner, Seattle, intercepted Tom Brady in the third quarter. Seattle turned the short field into a touchdown.
  • Malcolm Butler, New England, intercepted Russell Wilson at the goal line with 20 seconds left in the fourth quarter, ending Seattle's final drive and sealing the win. It was Butler's first career interception.

The game story

The game arrived with two very different kinds of pressure attached to it. Seattle came to Glendale as the defending champion, a team built around a loud, physical defense and a running game that could wear an opponent down. New England came as the team trying to prove that its earlier run of titles was not finished, and trying to do it while a controversy about underinflated footballs in the AFC Championship Game dominated the week before the game.

For a long stretch, the game refused to give either side a comfortable lead. The first quarter ended without a point, which was less a sign of caution than a sign of how well both defenses were playing in the early exchanges. New England moved the ball on its second drive and reached the Seattle 10 yard line, only for Jeremy Lane to intercept Brady at the goal line. Lane paid for the play with an injury that ended his night, and his replacement, Tharold Simon, became a target for the rest of the game.

The second quarter turned the game into a sprint. Brady found Brandon LaFell for an 11 yard touchdown to open the scoring, and Seattle answered with a 3 yard touchdown run by Marshawn Lynch after Chris Matthews, a player who had been on and off the practice squad during the season, made a 44 yard catch to set the drive in motion. Brady then found Rob Gronkowski for a 22 yard touchdown, and it looked as if New England would take a lead into halftime. Seattle had other ideas. In the final 29 seconds of the half, Wilson drove Seattle 80 yards in five plays and found Matthews for an 11 yard touchdown with two seconds left. The teams went to halftime tied at 14, after a half in which New England had controlled much of the play but Seattle had refused to fall behind.

Seattle took control after halftime. Steven Hauschka kicked a 27 yard field goal, and after Bobby Wagner intercepted Brady, Wilson found Doug Baldwin for a 3 yard touchdown. At 24-14, with the third quarter nearly over, Seattle looked like the team that would close the game out. New England, however, had Brady, and Brady had the short passing game that had kept the Patriots moving all night.

The fourth quarter belonged to New England's passing attack. Brady led a 68 yard drive that ended with a 4 yard touchdown pass to Danny Amendola, cutting the lead to 24-21 with 7:55 left. Seattle's next drive stalled, and Brady went back to work. On the drive that followed, he completed all eight of his passes for 65 yards, spreading the ball to four different receivers, and finished it with a 3 yard touchdown pass to Julian Edelman with 2:02 remaining. New England led 28-24, and the game that had seemed to be slipping away was now New England's to lose.

Seattle almost took it back. Wilson opened the final drive with a 31 yard completion to Lynch, then found Ricardo Lockette for a first down. The play that followed should have been remembered as the catch that won a Super Bowl. Wilson threw deep down the right sideline to Jermaine Kearse, who was covered by Malcolm Butler. Butler got a hand to the ball, Kearse tipped it to himself while falling, and he held on as he landed on his back. Butler recovered in time to push Kearse out of bounds at the New England 5 yard line, but Seattle had first down with just over a minute left. On the next play, Lynch ran four yards to the 1 yard line.

What happened next is still argued about, and it will probably be argued about for as long as this game is remembered. With 26 seconds left and second down at the 1 yard line, Seattle did not hand the ball to Lynch. Wilson threw a slant pass intended for Ricardo Lockette. Butler read the play, stepped in front of Lockette at the goal line, and intercepted the pass with 20 seconds left. New England ran out the clock, and the game was over.

The legacy of the game is split in two directions, and both directions are true. For New England, it was a return to the top after a decade without a Super Bowl win, a fourth title for Brady and head coach Bill Belichick together, and a third Super Bowl MVP award for Brady. His 37 completions and four touchdown passes in the game, and his calm fourth quarter comeback against the league's top ranked defense, strengthened the case that he belonged in any conversation about the greatest quarterbacks of all time. For Seattle, it was the game that stopped a repeat. A team that had won so convincingly the year before, and that had fought back to the 1 yard line in the final minute, left Glendale without a second straight title, and the decision to throw rather than run became the defining memory of that team. The game is remembered, above all, for its finish: one yard, one pass, and one rookie cornerback who was ready for it.

Scoring timeline

  1. First quarter, Jeremy Lane interception. Brady drives New England to the Seattle 10 yard line, but Lane intercepts the pass at the goal line. Lane is injured on the return and leaves the game, a loss that shapes Seattle's secondary for the rest of the night.
  2. Second quarter, LaFell touchdown. Brady finishes a 65 yard drive with an 11 yard touchdown pass to Brandon LaFell. New England leads 7-0.
  3. Second quarter, Lynch touchdown. Chris Matthews makes a 44 yard catch, then Marshawn Lynch runs 3 yards for the touchdown. The game is tied 7-7.
  4. End of the second quarter, two touchdowns in 31 seconds. Brady finds Rob Gronkowski for a 22 yard touchdown, then Wilson answers with an 80 yard drive in 29 seconds, finished by an 11 yard touchdown pass to Chris Matthews with two seconds left. Halftime score: 14-14.
  5. Third quarter, Hauschka field goal. Seattle opens the second half with a drive that ends in a 27 yard field goal. Seattle leads 17-14.
  6. Third quarter, Wagner interception and Baldwin touchdown. Bobby Wagner intercepts Brady, giving Seattle a short field, and Wilson finds Doug Baldwin for a 3 yard touchdown. Seattle leads 24-14.
  7. Fourth quarter, Amendola touchdown. Brady leads a 68 yard drive and finds Danny Amendola for a 4 yard touchdown with 7:55 left. Seattle leads 24-21.
  8. Fourth quarter, Edelman touchdown. Brady completes all eight passes on a 64 yard drive and finds Julian Edelman for a 3 yard touchdown with 2:02 left. New England leads 28-24.
  9. Final minute, Kearse catch. Wilson throws deep to Jermaine Kearse, who tips a deflected ball to himself and catches it while falling on his back. The 33 yard gain gives Seattle first down at the New England 5 yard line, and Lynch then runs to the 1 yard line.
  10. Final seconds, Butler interception. On second down from the 1 yard line with 26 seconds left, Seattle passes instead of running. Malcolm Butler steps in front of Ricardo Lockette at the goal line and intercepts Russell Wilson with 20 seconds left, sealing the 28-24 win.

Why this game mattered

This game mattered because it settled, at least for a night, two arguments that had run all season. The first was whether New England's championship window had closed. Ten years had passed since its previous Super Bowl win, and the team had lost two Super Bowls in the meantime. A win here put Brady and Belichick back on top with a fourth title together and gave Brady a third Super Bowl MVP award. The second was whether Seattle was becoming a dynasty. Seattle had won the previous Super Bowl convincingly and returned with the same core. A repeat would have put it in rare company. Instead, the loss became the moment people point to when they talk about that team's missed chance.

The game also became a shared cultural reference in ways that had little to do with football tactics. The final play call, pass instead of run from the 1 yard line, is still used as shorthand for a decision that looks obvious in hindsight and is debated endlessly in the moment. The halftime show produced Left Shark, one of the most shared Super Bowl images of the decade. The broadcast itself drew an average audience of 114.4 million viewers in the United States, a record at the time, with 118.5 million viewers for the halftime show. A game that was scoreless after one quarter ended as one of the most watched and most argued about Super Bowls ever played, and its finish, one yard from a Seattle win, is why it is still replayed.
