Decisive defensive plays

  • Duron Harmon interception: Harmon intercepted a Nick Foles pass in the second quarter, giving New England the ball at its own 10 yard line. New England turned the takeaway into a touchdown drive that cut Philadelphia's lead to 15-12.
  • Brandon Graham strip sack: Graham stripped the ball from Tom Brady late in the fourth quarter, with Philadelphia leading 38-33. Rookie Derek Barnett recovered the fumble. It was Philadelphia's only sack of the game, and it was New England's only turnover.
  • Final defensive stand: on the last play of the game, Brady's Hail Mary pass fell incomplete as time expired, sealing Philadelphia's 41-33 win.

The game story

Philadelphia arrived in Minneapolis as a team that had already been written off once, and New England arrived as the team that almost never lost this game. The Patriots were the defending champions, back in the Super Bowl for the third time in four seasons, with Tom Brady and Bill Belichick chasing a sixth title together. The Eagles were in their third Super Bowl, still looking for the first win in franchise history, and they were doing it with a backup quarterback. Carson Wentz had led Philadelphia to a 10-2 start before suffering a season ending knee injury on December 10, 2017. Nick Foles took over, and the Eagles still finished 13-3 and earned the top seed in the NFC, yet they entered every playoff game as underdogs. Players and fans embraced that status, wearing dog masks as a symbol of how little outsiders believed in them.

The matchup also carried history. New England had beaten Philadelphia 24-21 in Super Bowl XXXIX, thirteen seasons earlier, and Philadelphia had not won an NFL championship since 1960. A city famous for its sports heartbreak was now one win from its first Lombardi Trophy, against the dynasty that had defined the era. The Patriots were favored by 5.5 points. The Eagles, under head coach Doug Pederson, decided early that they would not play this game hoping to keep it close. They would play it to win, on their terms, even when conventional coaching said to take the safe points.

That intent showed up in the first half. Philadelphia moved the ball with confidence, mixing Foles passes with runs from LeGarrette Blount and Jay Ajayi. The Eagles led 9-3 after the first quarter, then stretched the lead to 15-3 on a 21 yard Blount touchdown run. New England answered, as great teams do, and a missed extra point kept the margin within reach. Then came the moment that now defines the game. Facing fourth down and goal from the 1 yard line in the final minute of the first half, Pederson kept his offense on the field instead of kicking a field goal. Foles lined up behind the offensive line, the ball was snapped directly to running back Corey Clement, Clement pitched it to tight end Trey Burton, and Burton threw a touchdown pass to a wide open Foles in the end zone. The play, known as the Philly Special, gave Philadelphia a 22-12 halftime lead. It also told the Patriots, and everyone watching, that Philadelphia was not waiting for permission to win. New England had tried a similar trick play earlier in the game, with a pass intended for Brady falling incomplete. Philadelphia executed its version perfectly, and Foles became the first player to throw and catch a touchdown in the same Super Bowl.

The second half refused to slow down. Gronkowski caught a touchdown to open the third quarter, Clement answered with a touchdown catch of his own, and Chris Hogan pulled New England within three points. The offenses were producing at a pace that made defense look almost optional. Brady finished with 505 passing yards, a Super Bowl record, and the teams combined for 1,151 total yards, the most in any NFL game, regular season or postseason, up to that point. There was only one punt in the entire game. Both quarterbacks kept answering each other, drive after drive, and the game turned into a test of nerve as much as talent. Philadelphia kept scoring just enough to stay ahead, until New England finally took its first lead of the game in the fourth quarter, 33-32, on Gronkowski's second touchdown catch, with 9:22 remaining.

That is usually the point in the story where the Patriots close the door. Brady had built a career on those moments, and the year before he had led the largest comeback in Super Bowl history against Atlanta. This time, Foles answered with a long, composed drive, capped by an 11 yard touchdown pass to Zach Ertz with 2:21 remaining. The touchdown survived a replay review, and Philadelphia led 38-33 after a failed two point conversion attempt. Brady still had time, and he still had the ball, which meant Philadelphia still had a problem. The Eagles defense had not sacked Brady all game. Then, with just over two minutes left, defensive end Brandon Graham got home, knocked the ball loose, and Barnett recovered it. Jake Elliott kicked a 46 yard field goal with 1:05 remaining to make it 41-33, and Brady's final Hail Mary fell incomplete as time expired.

The legacy of the game reaches beyond one trophy. For Philadelphia, it ended a 57 year wait for an NFL championship and gave a city its first Super Bowl title. For Foles, it completed one of the great backup quarterback runs in league history, from a player who had nearly left football to Super Bowl MVP. For Pederson, the Philly Special and the repeated fourth down aggression became a reference point for coaches who argued that playing boldly against a superior opponent was not reckless, but rational. And for the league, the game remains a reminder that a Super Bowl can be decided not by one defense dominating, but by one defense making a single play at the exact moment it matters most. In a game with 74 combined points and almost no punts, the loudest moment was still a strip sack.

Scoring timeline

  1. First quarter, Jeffery touchdown: Foles finds Jeffery for 34 yards, and Philadelphia takes a 9-3 lead after a missed extra point. The Eagles show early they can score on New England.
  2. Second quarter, Blount touchdown: Blount runs 21 yards for a score, pushing the lead to 15-3. Philadelphia's running game is working against a defense expecting pass.
  3. Second quarter, Harmon interception: Harmon intercepts Foles, and New England converts the turnover into a White touchdown, cutting the lead to 15-12. The game tightens just before halftime.
  4. Second quarter, Philly Special: on fourth down and goal from the 1 yard line in the final minute of the half, Burton throws a touchdown pass to Foles. Philadelphia leads 22-12 at halftime and owns the boldest moment of the night.
  5. Third quarter, Gronkowski and Clement trade touchdowns: Gronkowski scores to open the half, Clement answers, and Philadelphia holds a 29-19 lead. Neither offense is slowing down.
  6. Third quarter, Hogan touchdown: Hogan catches a 26 yard touchdown from Brady, cutting the lead to 29-26. New England is within one score entering the fourth quarter.
  7. Fourth quarter, Gronkowski's second touchdown: Gronkowski scores from 4 yards out, giving New England its first lead of the game, 33-32, with 9:22 remaining. The defending champions appear ready to complete another comeback.
  8. Fourth quarter, Ertz touchdown: Foles finds Ertz for 11 yards with 2:21 remaining. The score survives review, and Philadelphia retakes the lead, 38-33.
  9. Fourth quarter, Graham strip sack: Graham strips Brady and Barnett recovers, with just over two minutes left. It is Philadelphia's only sack of the game and New England's only turnover.
  10. Fourth quarter, Elliott field goal and final play: Elliott kicks a 46 yard field goal with 1:05 remaining for a 41-33 lead, then Brady's Hail Mary falls incomplete as time expires.

Why this game mattered

Super Bowl LII mattered because it broke a pattern. For years, the story of the Patriots in the Super Bowl was that no lead against them was safe and no deficit was too large. Philadelphia did not try to protect a lead against that history. It attacked it, with fourth down calls, trick plays, and a backup quarterback playing with nothing to lose. The Philly Special became shorthand for that approach, copied, debated, and replayed for years, and it helped push the wider league toward more aggressive fourth down decision making.

The game also mattered to Philadelphia in a way that box scores cannot capture. The Eagles had lost two previous Super Bowls, and the city had waited since the 1960 NFL Championship for a title. The underdog masks, worn through the playoffs after Philadelphia was doubted despite being the NFC's top seed, turned a slight into an identity. When the final Hail Mary fell incomplete, that identity flipped. The underdogs were champions, and the win gave Philadelphia its first Lombardi Trophy.

Finally, the game set a statistical marker that still frames how people remember it. With 1,151 combined yards, 74 combined points, only one punt, and a 505 yard passing game in a losing effort, it was the highest scoring, most wide open Super Bowl of its era. It proved that a Super Bowl could be great without a defensive struggle, and that a single defensive play, Graham's strip sack, could still decide a shootout.
