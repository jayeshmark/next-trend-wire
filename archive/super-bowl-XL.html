Super Bowl XL: Pittsburgh Steelers vs Seattle Seahawks

By Editorial Team

Pittsburgh won its fifth Super Bowl title, becoming the first sixth seeded team to win a Super Bowl. Seattle was in its first Super Bowl. The game is still debated for its officiating, with several calls going against Seattle at key moments.

  • Roethlisberger's one yard touchdown run in the second quarter was upheld on review.

The game story

The stakes carried a sentimental edge that few Super Bowls can match. Pittsburgh running back Jerome Bettis, one of the great players of his generation, had announced that the season would be his last, and the Steelers were playing to send him home to Detroit, his hometown, as a champion. Seattle, meanwhile, was in its first Super Bowl after the best regular season in franchise history at 13-3, with league MVP Shaun Alexander leading the ground game. Pittsburgh had reached the game as a sixth seed, winning three road playoff games to get there, a path no team had ever ridden all the way to a title.

The first half was a story of missed chances for Seattle. Josh Brown kicked a 47 yard field goal with 27 seconds left in the first quarter to give the Seahawks a 3-0 lead. Seattle moved the ball for most of the half but could not finish drives, and Brown missed a 54 yard field goal attempt at the end of the second quarter. Pittsburgh's only first half points came on a one yard Ben Roethlisberger touchdown run with 2:00 left in the half, a play that was reviewed and upheld, giving Pittsburgh a 7-3 lead at the break. Seattle had outgained Pittsburgh in the half but trailed, and the feeling in the stadium was that the Seahawks had let their best chance slip.

The second half opened with the play that changed the game. On the second play of the third quarter, Willie Parker ran 75 yards for a touchdown, the longest run from scrimmage in Super Bowl history at the time, and Pittsburgh led 14-3. Seattle answered with a 16 yard touchdown pass from Matt Hasselbeck to Jerramy Stevens, cutting the lead to 14-10, but Brown missed a 50 yard field goal attempt on the next Seattle drive, and the Seahawks never got closer. The fourth quarter belonged to Pittsburgh's trickery: on a reverse, Parker pitched to Antwaan Randle El, who threw a 43 yard touchdown pass to Hines Ward with 9:04 left, making it 21-10. Seattle's final drive stalled in Pittsburgh territory, and the Steelers ran out the clock.

The legacy of the game is contested, and that is why it is still talked about. Pittsburgh's win gave the franchise its fifth Super Bowl title, joining San Francisco and Dallas as the only teams with five at the time, and it gave Bettis his storybook ending in his hometown. Ward was named MVP after catching five passes for 123 yards and a touchdown, plus 18 rushing yards. But the officiating became the dominant memory for many viewers: several calls went against Seattle at important moments, including an offensive pass interference call that wiped out a Seattle touchdown, a holding call that pushed Seattle back during a drive, and a low block call on Hasselbeck after an interception. The game is therefore remembered two ways at once: as Bettis's farewell triumph, and as the Super Bowl that made officiating a central part of the story.

Why this game mattered

Super Bowl XL is remembered as the game that made people argue about referees as much as about football. The officiating debate overshadowed, for many fans, what was otherwise a landmark result: the first sixth seed to win a Super Bowl, a fifth title for Pittsburgh, and a farewell championship for Jerome Bettis in his hometown. The halftime show added its own footnote: the Rolling Stones played a three song set on a giant tongue shaped stage, with some lyrics censored on the broadcast, a reminder of how cautious live television had become after the 2004 halftime controversy. The commercials, meanwhile, were dominated by humor, with FedEx's caveman and Bud Light's secret fridge leading the polls, and the night's ad debate, over which spot truly won, mirrored the game's own argument about what had really decided it.
