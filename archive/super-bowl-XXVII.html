Super Bowl XXVII: Dallas Cowboys vs Buffalo Bills

By Editorial Team

Result in brief: Dallas won its third Super Bowl title and its first since the 1970s. Buffalo suffered a third straight Super Bowl defeat, a record no team had set before, and the 52 to 17 loss remains the largest margin of the Bills' four defeats.

  • Dallas defensive tackle Leon Lett recovered a fumble late in the game and ran toward the end zone, but Buffalo receiver Don Beebe chased him down from behind and knocked the ball out of his hands through the end zone for a touchback. The play kept Dallas from reaching a then record point total, and it became the most replayed moment of a one sided game.

The game story

Buffalo arrived in Pasadena trying to make history in the wrong direction, or to avoid it. The Bills had lost the previous two Super Bowls, to the New York Giants and to Washington, and no team had ever lost three Super Bowls in a row. The team had also produced one of the great comebacks in playoff history just weeks earlier, rallying from 32 points down to beat Houston in the wild card round. That win, led by backup quarterback Frank Reich after Jim Kelly was hurt, gave Buffalo a sense of destiny. Dallas arrived with a younger, faster team that had gone 13 and 3 in the regular season and had beaten San Francisco in the NFC Championship Game. Head coach Jimmy Johnson had rebuilt Dallas around Troy Aikman, Emmitt Smith, and Michael Irvin, and the Cowboys were favored.

The start favored Buffalo. Steve Tasker blocked a Dallas punt, Buffalo took over at the Dallas 16 yard line, and Thurman Thomas ran 2 yards for a touchdown and a 7 to 0 lead. For a few minutes, the game looked like it might follow the script Buffalo wanted. Then the turnovers began, and they never stopped.

Late in the first quarter, James Washington intercepted Kelly. Aikman threw a 23 yard touchdown pass to Jay Novacek to tie the game at 7 to 7. On the very next play, Kelly fumbled, Jimmie Jones scored, and Dallas led 14 to 7. Buffalo kicked a field goal in the second quarter to cut the lead to 14 to 10, but Aikman threw two touchdown passes to Irvin in the final two minutes of the half, the second coming only 18 seconds after the first, and Dallas led 28 to 10 at halftime. Kelly had been knocked out of the game by then, and Reich, the hero of the Houston comeback, could not stop the slide.

The second half only widened the gap. Dallas kicked a field goal, Buffalo scored on a 40 yard pass from Reich to Beebe at the end of the third quarter, and Dallas then scored 21 points in the fourth quarter: a 45 yard pass from Aikman to Alvin Harper, a 10 yard run by Smith, and a 9 yard fumble return by Ken Norton Jr. The final score was 52 to 17, and the combined total of 69 points was a Super Bowl record at the time.

Aikman was named most valuable player after throwing 4 touchdown passes without an interception. Smith ran for 108 yards. Irvin caught 2 touchdown passes. For Dallas, the win was a third Super Bowl title and the first since the 1970s, and it announced a new dynasty. Johnson became the first coach to win a college national title and a Super Bowl. For Buffalo, the loss was a third straight Super Bowl defeat, a record no team had set before, and the manner of it hurt as much as the result. Buffalo did not lose on a missed kick or a late drive. It lost the ball 9 times, and Dallas scored again and again off those mistakes. The Bills would lose a fourth straight Super Bowl the next year, but this 52 to 17 defeat remains the largest margin of the four.

Scoring timeline

  1. Buffalo strikes first. Thomas scores after a blocked punt, and Buffalo leads 7 to 0.
  2. Dallas ties it. Washington intercepts Kelly, and Aikman finds Novacek for a touchdown.
  3. Dallas takes the lead for good. Kelly fumbles on the next play, and Jones scores for 14 to 7.
  4. Dallas breaks the game open. Irvin catches two touchdown passes in the final two minutes of the first half, and the lead is 28 to 10.
  5. Buffalo scores late in the third quarter. Reich finds Beebe for 40 yards, cutting the lead to 31 to 17.
  6. Dallas answers with 21 fourth quarter points. Harper, Smith, and Norton score, and the final margin reaches 35 points.
  7. The lasting image is not a score. Beebe chases down Lett and strips the ball through the end zone, denying Dallas a record point total and giving Buffalo one moment of pride in a rout.

Cultural context

The game mattered most for what happened at halftime. After CBS lost viewers to In Living Color during the previous year's halftime show, the league booked the biggest pop star in the world, and Jackson delivered a performance that changed the halftime show from an interlude into a headline event. Every modern halftime show, built around a single major star, follows the model set at the Rose Bowl in 1993. The game itself also confirmed a changing of the guard in the NFL. Dallas, built through the draft and through Johnson's rebuilding plan, replaced San Francisco as the NFC power, and the Cowboys would win again the next year. Buffalo, meanwhile, became a cautionary tale about how hard it is to win a championship even after reaching the final game four times in a row. The Bills have not returned to a Super Bowl since.
