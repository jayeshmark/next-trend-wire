Decisive defensive plays

  • The Giants sacked Brady 5 times. Justin Tuck had 2 sacks and forced a fumble.
  • Jay Alford sacked Brady for a 10 yard loss on New England's final possession, after New England had started at its own 26 with 29 seconds left.
  • Corey Webster broke up a deep pass to Moss, and Gibril Wilson broke up the final fourth down pass to Moss, ending New England's last chance.
  • Asante Samuel could not hold a pass from Manning on the Giants' final drive, a missed interception that would have ended the drive with New England ahead.
  • Ellis Hobbs intercepted a Manning pass earlier in the game, for 23 return yards. It was the game's only interception by New England.

The game story

Perfection was the story before a ball was snapped, and it stayed the story until the final second ran out. New England arrived in Glendale at 18 and 0, after a 16 and 0 regular season, and a win would have completed a 19 and 0 season, the first perfect season since the 1972 Miami Dolphins. New York arrived at 10 and 6 in the regular season, as a wild card team that had already won three road playoff games, at Tampa Bay, at Dallas, and at Green Bay. The regular season meeting between the teams had ended 38 to 35 for New England on December 29, 2007, a close game that gave the Giants a reason to believe, and gave New England a reason to expect another fight. New England was favored by 12.5 points at kickoff, a wide margin that said most observers expected the favorite to finish the job.

The game itself refused to follow that script. New York took the opening kickoff and held the ball for 9 minutes and 59 seconds, running 16 plays for 63 yards before Lawrence Tynes kicked a 32 yard field goal. New England's offense was kept off the field for almost the entire first quarter. New England answered early in the second quarter, when Laurence Maroney scored on a 1 yard run to put the Patriots ahead 7 to 3. The Giants' defensive line was the main reason the score stayed low. It sacked Brady 5 times in the game, hit him often, and kept the deep passing game from taking over. Randy Moss was held to 5 catches, and most of New England's passing yards came on shorter throws to Wes Welker and Kevin Faulk.

The third quarter produced no points at all, and that scoreless stretch kept the game within one score. New York's offense, quiet for long stretches, found its rhythm in the fourth quarter. Kevin Boss caught a 45 yard pass that helped set up the Giants' first touchdown, a 5 yard pass from Manning to David Tyree with 11 minutes and 5 seconds left, giving New York a 10 to 7 lead.

New England responded the way great teams do. Brady led a 12 play, 80 yard drive that ended with a 6 yard touchdown pass to Moss with 2 minutes and 42 seconds left, putting New England ahead 14 to 10. New York now needed a touchdown, not a field goal. New York started its final drive at its own 17 with 2 minutes and 39 seconds left and three timeouts. Manning completed passes to Amani Toomer, and Brandon Jacobs converted a fourth and 1 with a 2 yard run. Then came the play that could have ended it. Manning threw toward Tyree, and Asante Samuel got his hands on the ball but could not hold it. A catch there would almost certainly have sealed a New England win and a perfect season.

One play later, on third and 5 from the Giants' own 44 with 1 minute and 15 seconds left, Manning escaped pressure, broke free from defenders who had hold of him, and threw deep downfield. Tyree leapt with safety Rodney Harrison draped over him and pinned the ball against his helmet with one hand as he fell to the ground, a 32 yard gain that kept the drive alive. It is the play now known simply as the Helmet Catch, and it remains one of the most replayed plays in Super Bowl history because it combined escape, risk, and an improbable catch at the exact moment New York's season was about to end. Two plays later, Steve Smith caught a 12 yard pass on third and 11 to move the ball to the New England 13. On the next play, Manning threw to Plaxico Burress in the end zone, and Burress caught the 13 yard touchdown with 35 seconds left for a 17 to 14 lead.

New England had one last chance from its own 26 with 29 seconds left, but Alford's sack, followed by two incomplete deep passes to Moss, ended it. After one second was restored to the clock, Manning took a knee to finish the game. Manning was named Most Valuable Player after completing 19 of 34 passes for 255 yards and 2 touchdowns, both in the fourth quarter. The Giants had stopped a perfect season one game short, as a wild card team that had won every playoff game on the road before a neutral site final. The loss left New England at 18 and 1. For New York, it was a title built on a defensive line, a quarterback who played his best football late, and a final drive that needed a dropped interception, an escape, and a catch against a helmet to survive.

Scoring timeline

  1. Opening drive, 1st quarter: New York runs 16 plays for 63 yards over 9:59 and takes a 3 to 0 lead on a 32 yard field goal by Lawrence Tynes. New England's offense waits almost the whole quarter.
  2. First touchdown, 2nd quarter: Laurence Maroney scores on a 1 yard run, and Stephen Gostkowski's kick puts New England ahead 7 to 3, the halftime score.
  3. Scoreless third quarter: neither team scores, so New England's 7 to 3 lead stays fragile and New York stays one touchdown from the lead.
  4. Giants take the lead, 4th quarter, 11:05 left: Kevin Boss's 45 yard catch helps set up David Tyree's 5 yard touchdown catch from Eli Manning. Giants 10, Patriots 7.
  5. Patriots retake the lead, 4th quarter, 2:42 left: Tom Brady completes a 12 play, 80 yard drive with a 6 yard touchdown pass to Randy Moss. Patriots 14, Giants 10.
  6. The missed interception, final drive: on the Giants' last possession, Asante Samuel gets his hands on a Manning pass intended for Tyree but cannot hold it, keeping New York alive while trailing 14 to 10.
  7. The Helmet Catch, 1:15 left: on third and 5 from the Giants' own 44, Manning escapes pressure and throws 32 yards to Tyree, who pins the ball against his helmet while falling with Rodney Harrison over him. The gain moves New York to the New England 24.
  8. Third down conversion, final drive: Steve Smith catches a 12 yard pass on third and 11, moving the ball to the New England 13.
  9. The winning touchdown, 0:35 left: Manning throws a 13 yard touchdown pass to Plaxico Burress, completing a 12 play, 83 yard drive. Giants 17, Patriots 14.
  10. The final stand: New England starts at its own 26 with 29 seconds left, Jay Alford sacks Brady for a 10 yard loss, and two deep passes to Moss fall incomplete. After one second is restored to the clock, Manning takes a knee to end the game.

Why this game mattered

This game mattered because it stopped history, not just because it decided a title. New England entered at 18 and 0, and a win would have made it the first team to finish a season undefeated since the 1972 Miami Dolphins, and the first to do it in a 16 game regular season and a four round playoff run. The loss turned a season that could have been described as perfect into a season described by one number, 18 and 1, and that number has stayed attached to the Patriots ever since. For the Giants, the win gave a wild card team a title after three road playoff wins, and it gave Eli Manning a championship and a Most Valuable Player award in his own right, one year after his brother Peyton won the award. The Helmet Catch became a lasting image because it was not a routine great catch. It was a quarterback escaping a sack and a receiver holding a ball against a helmet while falling, on a drive that had already survived a dropped interception. The game also reinforced the Super Bowl as a shared television event: about 97.5 million viewers watched on average in the United States, a record for a Super Bowl at the time, and the advertising around it, from the Budweiser Clydesdale to the E-Trade baby, became part of how people remember the night. Tom Petty's halftime set, built entirely from familiar songs, fit the same mood. The night was not about a new star taking over. It was about a familiar team chasing a historic finish, and an underdog refusing to let that finish happen.
