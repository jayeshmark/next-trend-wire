Super Bowl XXX: Dallas Cowboys vs Pittsburgh Steelers

By Editorial Team

Result in brief: Dallas won its fifth Super Bowl title and its third title in four years, completing the 1990s dynasty. Pittsburgh suffered its first Super Bowl defeat in franchise history, after four wins in the 1970s.

Decisive defensive plays

  • Dallas cornerback Larry Brown intercepted O'Donnell twice in the second half. Both interceptions led directly to Dallas touchdowns.
  • Brown's first interception came midway through the third quarter. He returned it 44 yards to the Pittsburgh 18 yard line, and Smith scored from the 1 yard line on the next drive.
  • Brown's second interception came with Pittsburgh trailing only 20 to 17 and driving. He returned it 33 yards to the Pittsburgh 6 yard line, and Smith scored from 4 yards out two plays later.
  • O'Donnell threw a third interception on the final play of the game, to Dallas safety Brock Marion.
  • Pittsburgh outgained Dallas 310 yards to 254 yards, and held Dallas to only 56 rushing yards, but could not overcome the three interceptions.

The game story

Dallas and Pittsburgh had met twice before in the Super Bowl, and Pittsburgh had won both times, in 1976 and 1979. This third meeting was a chance for Dallas to even the score and to tie San Francisco's record of five Super Bowl titles. Pittsburgh, meanwhile, was back in the Super Bowl for the first time since 1980, under head coach Bill Cowher, and the Steelers were trying to win a fifth title of their own. Dallas was favored by a wide margin, but Pittsburgh made the favorite work for every point.

Dallas scored on its first three possessions. Chris Boniol kicked field goals of 42 and 35 yards, and Aikman threw a 3 yard touchdown pass to Novacek. The score was 13 to 0, and the game looked like it might follow the pattern of Dallas routs from earlier in the decade. Pittsburgh answered just before halftime, when O'Donnell threw a 6 yard touchdown pass to Thigpen with 13 seconds left in the half. The score was 13 to 7, and Pittsburgh had life.

The second half belonged to Larry Brown. Pittsburgh had the ball near midfield midway through the third quarter when O'Donnell threw a third down pass that Brown intercepted at the Dallas 38 yard line. Brown returned it 44 yards to the Pittsburgh 18. Aikman completed a 17 yard pass to Irvin, and Smith ran 1 yard for a touchdown. Dallas led 20 to 7. Pittsburgh did not quit. Norm Johnson kicked a 46 yard field goal, Cowher called a surprise onside kick that Pittsburgh recovered, and Morris ran 1 yard for a touchdown with 6 minutes and 36 seconds left. The score was 20 to 17, and Dallas was in trouble.

Pittsburgh forced a punt and took over at its own 32 yard line with 4 minutes and 15 seconds left, trailing by only 3 points. On second down, Brown intercepted O'Donnell again, this time at the Pittsburgh 39, and returned it 33 yards to the 6 yard line. Two plays later, Smith scored from 4 yards out, and Dallas led 27 to 17 with 3 minutes and 43 seconds left. Pittsburgh drove to the Dallas 40 on its final possession but turned the ball over on downs, and O'Donnell's final pass was intercepted by Marion as time expired.

Brown was named most valuable player, the first cornerback ever to win the award. Smith scored both Dallas touchdowns in the second half despite gaining only 49 rushing yards, the lowest total for a winning team's leading rusher in a Super Bowl at the time. For Dallas, the win was a fifth Super Bowl title and a third title in four years, completing the 1990s dynasty. For Pittsburgh, the loss was a first Super Bowl defeat in franchise history, after four wins in the 1970s. The irony was sharp: O'Donnell had entered the game as the NFL career leader in fewest interceptions per pass attempt, and he threw three of them on the biggest stage, two of them to the same cornerback, both of them turned into touchdowns.

Cultural context

This was the last Super Bowl played in a college stadium, and the last hosted in a stadium with bleacher seats. It was also the first Super Bowl in which the Lombardi Trophy was presented on the field rather than in the winning locker room, a tradition that has continued ever since. On the field, the game closed the Dallas dynasty. The Cowboys have not returned to a Super Bowl since, and the 1990s team of Aikman, Smith, and Irvin is now remembered as a closed chapter rather than an ongoing run. For Pittsburgh, the loss began a new era under Cowher, who would return to the Super Bowl a decade later and win it. The game is also remembered for Diana Ross, whose helicopter exit became one of the most famous halftime endings ever, and for Larry Brown, whose two interceptions turned an unlikely most valuable player into a permanent answer to a trivia question: who stopped Pittsburgh's comeback in Super Bowl XXX?
