The game story

The stakes were clear before kickoff, and they were personal as well as historic. Kansas City arrived as the defending champion, trying to become the first team in nearly two decades to win consecutive Super Bowls. San Francisco arrived looking for a sixth championship, which would have tied the record shared by New England and Pittsburgh, and looking for revenge for its loss to Kansas City in Super Bowl LIV four years earlier. This was also the first Super Bowl played in Las Vegas. The game then took its time living up to that buildup, and when it finally opened up, it did so in a rush that carried all the way into overtime.

For much of the first half, the story was San Francisco's defense and Kansas City's frustration. The 49ers controlled the line of scrimmage, forced punts, and made Mahomes look hurried. The first real chance for San Francisco ended badly, when McCaffrey fumbled on the opening drive, but the 49ers recovered and struck first through kicking and creativity. Jake Moody opened the scoring with a 55 yard field goal, at the time the longest field goal in Super Bowl history. Then Kyle Shanahan reached into his playbook. On a trick play, wide receiver Jauan Jennings took a lateral and threw a 21 yard touchdown pass to McCaffrey, and San Francisco led 10 to 0. Kansas City finally answered just before halftime, when Harrison Butker kicked a 28 yard field goal with 20 seconds left in the second quarter. The 49ers had been the better team, but the Chiefs were still close enough for Mahomes to matter.

The second half turned on an injury, a turnover, and a kicking record that lasted about one quarter. Early in the third quarter, Mahomes was intercepted by Brown, but San Francisco could not turn that chance into points. Butker then kicked a 57 yard field goal, breaking Moody's record from the second quarter and cutting the lead to 10 to 6. The decisive swing came later in the third quarter, when a San Francisco punt hit Luter's foot and Kansas City recovered. One play later, Mahomes threw a 16 yard touchdown pass to Marquez Valdes-Scantling, and Kansas City led for the first time, 13 to 10.

The fourth quarter was a series of answers, each one more urgent than the last. San Francisco went back in front when Purdy threw a 10 yard touchdown pass to Jennings, making Jennings the second player in Super Bowl history to throw for a touchdown and catch a touchdown in the same Super Bowl. The extra point was blocked, however, so the lead was only 16 to 13 instead of 17 to 13, a difference that would matter enormously. Butker tied the game at 16 to 16 with a 24 yard field goal. Moody then put San Francisco back in front, 19 to 16, with a 53 yard field goal. Kansas City had one last drive in regulation, and Mahomes moved the Chiefs into range for Butker to kick a 29 yard field goal with 3 seconds left. The game was tied at 19 to 19, and for only the second time in Super Bowl history, the championship would be decided in overtime.

Overtime brought a second layer of drama, because the playoff overtime rules had changed since the last Super Bowl overtime game. Under the revised rules, both teams were guaranteed a possession even if the first team scored a touchdown. San Francisco won the coin toss and chose to receive, a decision Shanahan later explained as a plan to have the ball third if scores were matched. The 49ers drove to the Kansas City 9 yard line but settled for a 27 yard field goal from Moody, leading 22 to 19. That left Kansas City a clear task: a touchdown would win, a field goal would extend it. Mahomes answered with a 75 yard drive in 13 plays. He converted fourth and 1 with an 8 yard run, and later converted third and 1 with a 19 yard scramble, then found Mecole Hardman for a 3 yard touchdown with 3 seconds left in overtime. The final score was 25 to 22. It was the first Super Bowl in which the lead changed on the final play, and at 74 minutes and 57 seconds, it was the longest Super Bowl ever played.

The legacy of the game is mostly about Kansas City. The win gave the Chiefs their third Super Bowl title in five seasons and their second in a row, the first repeat champions since New England in the 2003 and 2004 seasons. It gave Mahomes his third Super Bowl MVP award, making him only the third player to win the award at least three times, and it gave Andy Reid his third championship as a head coach. For San Francisco, the legacy is harder. The 49ers led in the fourth quarter of both of Shanahan's Super Bowl appearances and lost both, each time to Kansas City. This loss was shaped by small margins: a fumble on the first drive, a blocked extra point, a muffed punt, and a field goal in overtime where a touchdown would have forced Kansas City to match it. Together they explain why this game is remembered as a classic, and why it confirmed Kansas City as the team of its era.

Scoring timeline

  1. Opening drive fumble: Christian McCaffrey fumbled on San Francisco's first drive, costing the 49ers an early scoring chance and setting a tone of missed opportunities.
  2. Record field goal, then a better one: Jake Moody's 55 yard field goal set a Super Bowl record in the second quarter. Harrison Butker broke it with a 57 yard field goal in the third quarter.
  3. The trick play touchdown: Jauan Jennings threw a 21 yard touchdown pass to Christian McCaffrey, giving San Francisco a 10 to 0 lead in the second quarter.
  4. Greenlaw lost: Dre Greenlaw suffered a torn Achilles tendon while running onto the field in the second quarter and missed the rest of the game, weakening San Francisco's defense.
  5. The third quarter interception: Ji'Ayir Brown intercepted Patrick Mahomes on Kansas City's first drive of the second half, but San Francisco did not score from the turnover.
  6. The muffed punt: A San Francisco punt hit Darrell Luter Jr.'s foot in the third quarter and Kansas City recovered. One play later, Mahomes threw a touchdown pass to Marquez Valdes-Scantling for a 13 to 10 Kansas City lead.
  7. The blocked extra point: After Jauan Jennings caught a touchdown pass in the fourth quarter, the extra point was blocked, leaving San Francisco ahead by only 3 points instead of 4.
  8. The tying kick in regulation: Harrison Butker kicked a 29 yard field goal with 3 seconds left in the fourth quarter, tying the game at 19 to 19 and forcing overtime.
  9. The overtime choice: San Francisco won the overtime coin toss, chose to receive, and settled for a field goal at the Kansas City 9 yard line, leading 22 to 19.
  10. The walk off touchdown: Patrick Mahomes led a 13 play, 75 yard drive in overtime, including a fourth and 1 8 yard run and a third and 1 19 yard scramble, and threw a 3 yard touchdown pass to Mecole Hardman with 3 seconds left in overtime.

Why this game mattered

Super Bowl LVIII was the first Super Bowl played in Las Vegas, a city that had long been kept at arm's length by the NFL and that by 2024 had become a full sports city, with the Raiders at Allegiant Stadium. It was also the game where the NFL's biggest off field story of the season, the relationship between Travis Kelce and Taylor Swift, reached its largest audience, with Swift shown in a suite throughout the broadcast. The audience followed: the game averaged more than 123 million viewers across platforms, a record for a Super Bowl at the time, and more than 200 million people watched at least part of it. The game also settled a football argument. Kansas City had entered the season looking flawed by its own standards, with a less explosive offense than in past years, yet won the championship through defense, kicking, and Mahomes in the biggest moments. That combination, plus the second overtime Super Bowl ever and the first under the revised playoff overtime rules, made this the game that turned a very good Chiefs run into a dynasty claim that few disputed afterward.
