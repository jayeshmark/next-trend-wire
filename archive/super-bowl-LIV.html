Super Bowl LIV: San Francisco 49ers vs Kansas City Chiefs

By Editorial Team

Kansas City won its first Super Bowl title in 50 years, beating San Francisco 31-20 after trailing 20-10 in the fourth quarter. The Chiefs scored 21 unanswered points in the final 6:17 of the game.

The game story

The stakes were about droughts. Kansas City arrived at 12-4, seeking its first Super Bowl title since Super Bowl IV, fifty years earlier, behind Patrick Mahomes, the young quarterback who had already won a league MVP award. San Francisco arrived at 13-3, two seasons removed from a 4-12 finish, behind a punishing running game and one of the league's best defenses, seeking its sixth title. The 49ers were the slight favorite in some markets and the Chiefs in others, but everyone agreed on the shape of the matchup: San Francisco wanted to run the ball, control the clock, and let its defensive line chase Mahomes, while Kansas City wanted to turn the game into a shootout its quarterback could win.

The first half was even. San Francisco scored first, on a 38 yard Robbie Gould field goal, and Kansas City answered with a 1 yard Mahomes touchdown run to lead 7-3 after one quarter. Harrison Butker kicked a 31 yard field goal for Kansas City early in the second quarter, and San Francisco tied the game at 10-10 on a 15 yard touchdown pass from Jimmy Garoppolo to fullback Kyle Juszczyk. The halftime score was 10-10, and San Francisco's plan was working: the 49ers had run the ball effectively, Garoppolo had been efficient, and Mahomes had thrown an interception to Fred Warner.

The third quarter belonged to San Francisco. Gould kicked a 42 yard field goal to make it 13-10, and then Raheem Mostert ran 1 yard for a touchdown, giving the 49ers a 20-10 lead with 2:40 left in the quarter. Kansas City's offense had gone quiet, Mahomes had thrown a second interception, this time to Tarvarius Moore, and San Francisco seemed to be strangling the game the way it had strangled opponents all season. Entering the fourth quarter, the 49ers led by 10 points, and their running game was positioned to close the game out.

Then the game flipped, the way Kansas City games had flipped all postseason. The turning point came with 7:13 left, on third and 15 from the Kansas City 35 yard line. Mahomes, under pressure, threw deep to Tyreek Hill for 44 yards, moving the Chiefs to the San Francisco 21 yard line. Three plays later, Mahomes threw a 1 yard touchdown pass to Travis Kelce, and the lead was down to 20-17 with 6:17 left. San Francisco's next drive stalled, Kansas City took over, and Mahomes led the Chiefs downfield, finishing with a 5 yard touchdown pass to Damien Williams with 2:50 left. Kansas City led 24-20. San Francisco's final chance ended when Kendall Fuller intercepted Garoppolo with 1:08 left, and Williams added a 38 yard touchdown run with 1:20 left to finish a 31-20 win.

The legacy of the game is the start of the Mahomes era. The win gave Kansas City its first championship in half a century, gave Andy Reid his first Super Bowl title as a head coach after more than two decades in the job, and gave Mahomes the MVP award at 24 years old. For San Francisco, the loss was a collapse in the fourth quarter, with a 10 point lead gone in just over six minutes of game time. The 49ers would not return to the Super Bowl for four years. The Chiefs, meanwhile, would become the league's defining team of the decade, and this comeback, built on one third and 15 throw, was where that run began.

Scoring timeline

  1. Gould opens the scoring, first quarter: the 38 yard field goal gives San Francisco a 3-0 lead and an early reward for its ball control plan.
  2. Mahomes answers on the ground, first quarter: the 1 yard run makes it 7-3 Kansas City and shows the Chiefs can score without a big passing play.
  3. Juszczyk ties it, second quarter: Garoppolo's 15 yard touchdown pass to the fullback makes it 10-10 at a point when San Francisco is moving the ball well.
  4. Mostert extends the lead, third quarter: the 1 yard run caps San Francisco's best stretch and makes it 20-10.
  5. Third and 15, fourth quarter: Mahomes finds Hill for 44 yards with 7:13 left, the play that turns the game. Without it, Kansas City faces fourth down deep in its own territory.
  6. Kelce cuts the lead, fourth quarter: the 1 yard touchdown pass makes it 20-17 with 6:17 left.
  7. Williams gives Kansas City the lead, fourth quarter: the 5 yard touchdown pass makes it 24-20 with 2:50 left.
  8. Fuller seals it, fourth quarter: the interception of Garoppolo with 1:08 left ends San Francisco's last drive, and Williams' 38 yard run finishes the 31-20 win.

Why this game mattered

Super Bowl LIV is remembered as the last Super Bowl played before the world changed. It took place on February 2, 2020, weeks before the pandemic reshaped American life, and it was staged in a full, unmasked stadium. That timing gives the game a strange glow in retrospect: a packed Hard Rock Stadium, a halftime show built for a crowd, and a comeback that felt like pure entertainment, all just before entertainment itself went quiet.

The halftime show added its own history. Shakira and Jennifer Lopez, two Latina artists, co-headlined the Super Bowl halftime show, performing in English and Spanish, with guests Bad Bunny and J Balvin, and Lopez's daughter Emme joining her on stage. The performance drew praise for its choreography and energy, and debate about its staging, and it became one of the most watched halftime shows of the decade. On the field, the game launched the Chiefs' run as the league's signature franchise: Mahomes would return to the Super Bowl twice more in the next three seasons, winning again, and the third and 15 throw to Hill became the signature play of his early career.
