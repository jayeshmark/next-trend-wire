Super Bowl LVII: Kansas City Chiefs vs Philadelphia Eagles

By Editorial Team

Kansas City won its third Super Bowl title, coming back from a 10 point halftime deficit. Mahomes, playing on a sprained ankle he reaggravated during the game, was named MVP for the second time. This was the first Super Bowl in which two Black quarterbacks started, Mahomes and Jalen Hurts, and the first in which two brothers, Travis Kelce of Kansas City and Jason Kelce of Philadelphia, played against each other.

The game story

The stakes were about two quarterbacks at the top of their powers and two teams that had dominated their conferences. Both teams finished 14-3. Philadelphia had the league's best rushing attack and a defense built to pressure the passer. Kansas City had Mahomes, the league MVP, and an offense that had reinvented itself around quick throws after trading Tyreek Hill. The game also carried two firsts that mattered beyond football: Mahomes and Hurts were the first two Black quarterbacks to start a Super Bowl against each other, and Travis and Jason Kelce were the first brothers to play against each other in a Super Bowl.

Philadelphia controlled the first half. Hurts opened the scoring with a 1 yard run with 10:13 left in the first quarter, and after Mahomes answered with an 18 yard touchdown pass to Travis Kelce, Hurts threw a 45 yard touchdown pass to A.J. Brown on the first play of the second quarter to make it 14-7. Kansas City's only answer before halftime came from its defense: Bolton scooped up a Hurts fumble and returned it 36 yards for a touchdown to tie the game at 14-14. Hurts responded with a 4 yard touchdown run, and after Mahomes was forced to limp off near the end of the half, having reaggravated the ankle sprain he had suffered in the divisional round, Jake Elliott kicked a 35 yard field goal with 4 seconds left to give Philadelphia a 24-14 halftime lead.

The second half belonged to Kansas City, and it started with the most important drive of the game. Mahomes, moving better than anyone expected after halftime, led a drive capped by Isiah Pacheco's 1 yard touchdown run, cutting the lead to 24-21. After Elliott's 33 yard field goal made it 27-21, the fourth quarter turned into a blur of big plays. Mahomes found Kadarius Toney wide open for a 5 yard touchdown with 12:08 left, giving Kansas City its first lead of the game at 28-27. Minutes later, Toney returned a punt 65 yards to the Philadelphia 5 yard line, and Mahomes hit Skyy Moore for a 4 yard touchdown to make it 35-27. Philadelphia was not done: Hurts ran in his third touchdown from 2 yards out with 5:20 left and ran in the two point conversion himself, tying the game at 35-35.

The final drive is now part of Super Bowl lore. Starting at its own 25 yard line with 5:15 left, Kansas City moved methodically, the signature play a 26 yard Mahomes scramble to the Philadelphia 17 yard line on a bad ankle. With 1:54 left, on third down, Bradberry was called for defensive holding on JuJu Smith-Schuster, giving Kansas City a first down at the Philadelphia 15. The Chiefs then did the smart, cold blooded thing: Jerick McKinnon ran to the 2 yard line and slid down rather than scoring, Mahomes took two knees, and Harrison Butker kicked a 27 yard field goal with 8 seconds left. Philadelphia's final play fell incomplete, and Kansas City won 38-35.

The legacy of the game is mostly about Mahomes. The win gave him a second Super Bowl title and a second Super Bowl MVP award, and it did so on a night when he was visibly hurt, which became the defining image of his career to that point: a quarterback who would not leave the field and who got better as the game got later. For Philadelphia, the loss became a study in how a game can slip away in small pieces: a fumble returned for a touchdown, a punt return that flipped field position, and a holding call that let the clock bleed out. Hurts, with 304 passing yards, 70 rushing yards, and 4 total touchdowns, played well enough to win most Super Bowls. This one, decided by a field goal with 8 seconds left, was not most Super Bowls.

Why this game mattered

Super Bowl LVII mattered because of who was on the field before a ball was snapped. Mahomes and Hurts were the first two Black quarterbacks to start a Super Bowl against each other, a milestone that arrived 35 years after Doug Williams became the first Black quarterback to start one. The Kelce brothers, Travis and Jason, were the first brothers to play against each other in a Super Bowl, and their mother Donna, wearing a half Chiefs half Eagles jacket, became a celebrity of the week in her own right.

The halftime show added a different kind of first. Rihanna had not performed live since 2018, and her return, performed on floating platforms above the field, became instantly famous for a reveal she never announced: she was visibly pregnant, and the news broke through the performance itself rather than a statement. Her catalog surged back onto the charts in the week after the game.

The ending also changed how fans talk about late game officiating. The Bradberry holding call, on a play where the pass was arguably uncatchable, was debated for weeks, but Bradberry himself admitted after the game that he had held Smith-Schuster. That admission mostly ended the controversy and left the cleaner story: Kansas City managed the clock perfectly, Philadelphia ran out of time, and a field goal with 8 seconds left decided one of the best Super Bowls ever played.
