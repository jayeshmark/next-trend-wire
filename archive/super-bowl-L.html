Super Bowl L: Denver Broncos vs Carolina Panthers

By Editorial Team

Denver won its third Super Bowl title, beating Carolina 24-10 in a defensive game. Denver led from the first quarter onward and never trailed. The game was played under the name Super Bowl 50, using an Arabic numeral instead of a Roman numeral for the golden anniversary edition.

The game story

The stakes were a study in contrasts. Carolina arrived at 15-1, with the league's highest scoring offense and Cam Newton, the league's most valuable player that season, at quarterback. Denver arrived at 12-4 behind the league's best defense and Peyton Manning, 39 years old, playing in what proved to be the final game of his career. Carolina was favored. The popular expectation was a shootout, or at least a game in which Carolina's offense set the terms. Denver's defense had other ideas, and from the first quarter on, it never let Carolina play its game.

Denver scored first, on a 34 yard Brandon McManus field goal, and then the game's defining play arrived. Von Miller beat the Carolina blocking, knocked the ball from Newton's hands, and Malik Jackson fell on it in the end zone for a touchdown. Denver led 10-0, and the tone was set: Carolina would have to earn everything against a defense that attacked its quarterback on nearly every dropback. Carolina answered in the second quarter with a 1 yard touchdown run by Jonathan Stewart, cutting the lead to 10-7, but McManus kicked a 33 yard field goal before halftime, and Denver led 13-7 at the break.

The third quarter produced only one score, a 30 yard McManus field goal that made it 16-7. Carolina's offense kept stalling, undone by pressure, dropped passes, and its own mistakes. The Panthers crossed midfield but could not finish drives, and Newton's receivers found little separation against Denver's secondary. The fourth quarter followed the same pattern. Graham Gano kicked a 39 yard field goal to cut the lead to 16-10 with 10:26 left, and Carolina had a pulse. But Denver's defense answered the only way it knew how. Miller strip sacked Newton again, this time at the Carolina 4 yard line, and Denver took over with a short field. Three plays later, C.J. Anderson ran 2 yards for a touchdown, and Manning threw to Bennie Fowler for the two point conversion. The lead was 24-10 with 3:13 left, and the game was over in everything but the clock.

The legacy of the game belongs to a defense. Denver won a championship with a quarterback who threw for 141 yards and no touchdowns, because its defense scored a touchdown, set up another with a strip sack, and never allowed Carolina's offense to reach the end zone through the air. Miller's MVP award recognized the most disruptive defensive performance in a Super Bowl in years. For Manning, the win was a second Super Bowl title and a farewell, the final game of a career that had already secured his place among the great quarterbacks. Denver's win also gave the franchise its first championship since its back to back titles of the late 1990s. For Newton and Carolina, the loss was a bitter end to a 15-1 season, and the image that lingered was not a play but a moment: Newton not diving for a loose ball late in the game, a snapshot that followed him into the offseason debate about the Panthers and their quarterback.

Scoring timeline

  1. McManus opens the scoring, first quarter: the 34 yard field goal gives Denver a 3-0 lead it never gives up.
  2. Miller's first strip sack, first quarter: Jackson recovers the fumble in the end zone for a touchdown, and Denver leads 10-0.
  3. Stewart answers, second quarter: the 1 yard run cuts the lead to 10-7 and gives Carolina its only touchdown.
  4. McManus before halftime, second quarter: the 33 yard field goal restores a two score cushion at 13-7.
  5. The scoreless third quarter for Carolina: McManus adds a 30 yard field goal, and Carolina's offense still cannot reach the end zone.
  6. Gano's field goal, fourth quarter: Carolina cuts the lead to 16-10 with 10:26 left and has a chance.
  7. Miller's second strip sack, fourth quarter: the fumble at the Carolina 4 yard line sets up Anderson's closing touchdown and the two point conversion that makes it 24-10.

Why this game mattered

Super Bowl 50 was a milestone game in presentation as much as in football. The league dropped the Roman numeral for the golden anniversary, branding it Super Bowl 50, and built the week around the history of the game, including a pregame ceremony honoring past Super Bowl MVPs. The halftime show leaned into that history too, with Coldplay joined by Beyonce and Bruno Mars, two previous halftime headliners, and a video montage of past halftime performances during "Fix You".

The game also marked the end of an era at quarterback. Manning's win gave him Super Bowl titles with two different franchises, Indianapolis and Denver, and he announced his retirement weeks later. His final season had been defined by declining arm strength and a defense that carried the team, and the Super Bowl was the purest expression of that formula. For Carolina, the loss closed a season that had felt charmed, from Newton's celebratory "dabbing" becoming a national talking point, and it left the franchise still searching for its first championship.
