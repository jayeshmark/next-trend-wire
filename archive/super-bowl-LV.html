Super Bowl LV: Tampa Bay Buccaneers vs Kansas City Chiefs

By Editorial Team

Tampa Bay won its second Super Bowl title. Kansas City, the defending champion, was held without a touchdown. Tampa Bay became the first team to play a Super Bowl in its own home stadium. Because of COVID-19 protocols the crowd was capped, making this the least attended Super Bowl.

The game story

The stakes were simple to state and hard to overstate. Kansas City arrived as the defending champion, a 14-2 team with the league's most explosive offense, trying to become the first repeat champion since New England in the 2003 and 2004 seasons. Tampa Bay arrived as the challenger that had rebuilt itself in one offseason, a wild card team that signed 43 year old Tom Brady away from New England and then added Rob Gronkowski, Leonard Fournette, and Antonio Brown around him. The game would be played in Tampa Bay's own stadium, something no Super Bowl team had ever done before, but the building would be far from full: COVID-19 protocols capped attendance at 24,835, and 7,500 vaccinated health care workers were invited in free of charge.

Kansas City scored first, and for a few minutes the expected game seemed to be on. Harrison Butker kicked a 49 yard field goal with 5:14 left in the first quarter. Tampa Bay answered before the quarter ended. Brady found Gronkowski for an 8 yard touchdown with 41 seconds left, and the Buccaneers led 7-3. That score set the pattern for the night: Tampa Bay's offense finished drives, and Kansas City's did not.

The second quarter broke the game open. Brady hit Gronkowski again, this time for 17 yards, with 6:11 left in the half, making it 14-3. Butker answered with a 34 yard field goal, but Tampa Bay had the last word of the half, and it was a backbreaker. With 10 seconds left, Brady threw a 1 yard touchdown pass to Antonio Brown, and Tampa Bay took a 21-6 lead into halftime. Kansas City's offense had moved the ball but could not finish, and its defense could not get off the field when it mattered.

The second half confirmed what the first half had suggested. Butker kicked a 52 yard field goal early in the third quarter to make it 21-9, and that was the last time Kansas City scored. Tampa Bay answered with its best run of the night, a 27 yard Leonard Fournette touchdown around the right end with 7:51 left in the third quarter, and Ryan Succop added a 52 yard field goal to make it 31-9. The fourth quarter was a formality. Mahomes spent it running from Tampa Bay's pass rush, throwing twice into interceptions, and never getting his team into the end zone.

The legacy of the game is mostly about Brady, and it is hard to argue otherwise. The win gave him a seventh Super Bowl title as a starting quarterback, in his first season with a new team, at age 43, and it gave him a fifth Super Bowl MVP award. It also settled, for most observers, the argument about whether his New England success belonged to him or to his old coach and system: he left both behind and won anyway. For Kansas City, the loss became a lesson in how quickly a great offense can be neutralized when its line cannot hold up. Mahomes was under pressure all night, was sacked 3 times, and finished without a touchdown pass. The Chiefs would be back, but this night belonged to the team playing at home, in front of a small crowd, in a season like no other.

Scoring timeline

  1. Butker opens the scoring, first quarter: a 49 yard field goal gives Kansas City a 3-0 lead, but it is the only lead the Chiefs hold all night.
  2. Gronkowski's first touchdown, first quarter: Brady's 8 yard pass to Gronkowski puts Tampa Bay ahead 7-3, and the Buccaneers never trail again.
  3. Gronkowski again, second quarter: a 17 yard touchdown makes it 14-3 and shows Kansas City cannot stop the Brady to Gronkowski connection.
  4. Brown's touchdown before halftime, second quarter: a 1 yard pass with 10 seconds left makes it 21-6 and turns a competitive game into a steep climb.
  5. Fournette's run, third quarter: a 27 yard touchdown makes it 28-9 and ends any realistic Kansas City comeback.
  6. Succop's field goal, third quarter: a 52 yard kick makes it 31-9, the final score, with a full quarter still to play.

Why this game mattered

Super Bowl LV was the COVID-19 Super Bowl, and everything about it looked different. The crowd of 24,835 was the smallest in Super Bowl history, the usual week of parties and events was scaled back, and the game itself was played in a season that nearly did not finish. That context made Tampa Bay's win stranger and more impressive at once: a team built through free agency in a single offseason won a title in its own stadium, in front of a crowd that included thousands of health care workers invited free as a thank you.

The game also became a hinge point in the Brady debate. Before it, a reasonable case existed that Brady's six New England titles were a product of the Patriots system. After it, with a seventh title won in Tampa Bay in year one, that case mostly collapsed. At 43, Brady became the oldest player to win a Super Bowl as a starting quarterback, and the win reframed his career as something that traveled with him rather than something that stayed in one place.

The halftime show fit the strange night. The Weeknd performed solo, with no guest, in a mirrored maze set built into the stands, and he reportedly spent 7 million dollars of his own money on top of the league's production budget to realize it. The performance, and the memes it generated, became one of the night's most shared moments even as the game itself turned one sided.
