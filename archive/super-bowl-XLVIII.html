Super Bowl XLVIII: Seattle Seahawks vs Denver Broncos

By Editorial Team

Seattle won its first Super Bowl title in franchise history, beating Denver 43-8 in one of the most one sided Super Bowls ever played. Denver, which had entered with the league's most celebrated offense, never led and did not score until the final seconds of the third quarter.

The game story

The stakes looked like a classic matchup of strength against strength. Denver arrived at 13-3 behind Peyton Manning, whose offense had been the talk of the season, and Seattle arrived at 13-3 behind the league's best defense, a young and physical secondary that called itself the Legion of Boom. It was the first Super Bowl played outdoors in a cold weather city, at MetLife Stadium in New Jersey, and the weather debate that filled the two weeks before the game turned out not to matter at all. The game was decided almost immediately, and it was never close again.

The tone was set on the first play from scrimmage. Denver's opening snap sailed over Manning's head into the end zone, and Moreno was tackled there for a safety. Seattle led 2-0 before most viewers had settled in. Steven Hauschka added field goals of 31 and 33 yards, and Seattle led 8-0 after one quarter without scoring a touchdown. In the second quarter, Marshawn Lynch ran 1 yard for a touchdown, and then the play that broke the game open: Manning's pass, tipped by Avril, was intercepted by Malcolm Smith, who returned it 69 yards for a touchdown. Seattle led 22-0 at halftime, and Denver had managed no points and little rhythm against a defense that seemed to be everywhere at once.

The second half opened the way the first half had ended, with a Seattle touchdown. Percy Harvin returned the second half kickoff 87 yards for a touchdown, making it 29-0. Russell Wilson then threw a 23 yard touchdown pass to Jermaine Kearse, who broke tackles along the sideline, and Seattle led 36-0. Denver finally scored with three seconds left in the third quarter, when Manning threw a 14 yard touchdown pass to Demaryius Thomas, followed by a two point conversion pass to Wes Welker. The score was 36-8, and the only remaining question was the final margin. Wilson threw a 10 yard touchdown pass to Doug Baldwin early in the fourth quarter, and Seattle closed out a 43-8 win.

The legacy of the game is about a defense answering a question that had followed it all season. Seattle's secondary, led by Richard Sherman, Earl Thomas, and Chancellor, had been brilliant all year, but skeptics wondered whether it could stop Manning, a quarterback who seemed to solve every defense he faced. The answer was emphatic: two interceptions, a safety on the first snap, and a shutout that lasted until the final seconds of the third quarter. For Denver, the loss was a humbling end to a high scoring season, and Manning's second Super Bowl appearance ended very differently from his first. For Seattle, it was the franchise's first championship, and it confirmed Pete Carroll's young team as the league's new power. Malcolm Smith, a linebacker who was not the most famous player on his own defense, took home the MVP award for a game that included the pick six and a fumble recovery. The 35 point margin remains one of the largest in Super Bowl history.

Scoring timeline

  1. The safety on the first play, first quarter: a snap over Manning's head gives Seattle a 2-0 lead and signals that Denver's offense will not find its rhythm.
  2. Hauschka's two field goals, first quarter: Seattle leads 8-0 without a touchdown, and Denver's offense has already been stopped twice.
  3. Lynch's touchdown, second quarter: the 1 yard run makes it 15-0 and gives Seattle's running game a stake in the game.
  4. Smith's pick six, second quarter: the 69 yard interception return makes it 22-0 at a point when Denver still hopes to regroup at halftime.
  5. Harvin's kickoff return, third quarter: the 87 yard return to open the second half makes it 29-0 and ends any realistic comeback talk.
  6. Kearse's touchdown, third quarter: Wilson's 23 yard pass, with Kearse breaking tackles, makes it 36-0.
  7. Denver's only touchdown, third quarter: Manning finds Demaryius Thomas with three seconds left in the quarter, avoiding a shutout but changing nothing about the result.

Why this game mattered

Super Bowl XLVIII mattered for what it proved about where the league was headed. Seattle's win was a triumph for defense in an era increasingly built around passing, and it launched a young core, Wilson, Sherman, Thomas, Chancellor, Lynch, and coach Pete Carroll, into the center of the sport's conversation. The Seahawks would return to the Super Bowl the next year, and the Legion of Boom became a brand in itself, with Sherman's postgame interview style and the secondary's swagger shaping how defenses marketed themselves.

The game also changed how the league thought about cold weather Super Bowls. MetLife Stadium had been chosen as an experiment, the first outdoor Super Bowl in a cold weather city, and the weather cooperated, with game time temperatures far milder than feared. The experiment did not immediately lead to a flood of cold weather bids, but it removed the argument that a cold weather Super Bowl was impossible. And the halftime show proved a point of its own: Bruno Mars delivered a high energy set that drew a record halftime audience at the time, showing the league could hand its biggest stage to a younger pop star and not lose the room.
