Super Bowl LVI: Los Angeles Rams vs Cincinnati Bengals

By Editorial Team

Los Angeles won its second Super Bowl title, and its first since the 1999 season when the franchise was based in St. Louis. The Rams became the second straight team to win a Super Bowl in its own home stadium. Cincinnati fell to 0-3 in Super Bowls.

The game story

The stakes were about patience on one side and speed on the other. Los Angeles had gone all in: the Rams traded for Matthew Stafford, signed Odell Beckham Jr. midseason, and added Von Miller, building a roster designed to win immediately in their new home, SoFi Stadium. Cincinnati had arrived ahead of schedule: Joe Burrow, in his second season, had taken a team that won 4 games two years earlier to the Super Bowl, with the swagger of a team that did not know it was supposed to wait its turn. The Rams were favored, but the Bengals had already beaten Kansas City on the road to get here, so nobody in their locker room felt like an underdog.

The first half belonged to Los Angeles, but only just. Stafford found Beckham for a 17 yard touchdown with 6:26 left in the first quarter, and after Evan McPherson's 29 yard field goal for Cincinnati, Stafford hit Cooper Kupp for an 11 yard touchdown early in the second quarter. The Rams tried to stretch the lead with a trick play, a fake field goal on which punter Johnny Hekker threw to kicker Matt Gay for a two point conversion, but the pass fell incomplete, and the score stayed 13-3. Cincinnati answered before halftime when Mixon, the running back, threw a 6 yard touchdown pass to Tee Higgins, cutting the lead to 13-10. The more worrying development for Los Angeles was Beckham leaving with a knee injury in the second quarter. He did not return, and the Rams' best secondary weapon was gone.

The second half flipped in seconds. On the first play from scrimmage, Burrow threw deep to Higgins for a 75 yard touchdown, and Cincinnati led 17-13. Moments later, Stafford was intercepted by Chidobe Awuzie, and McPherson's 38 yard field goal stretched the lead to 20-13. The Bengals had scored 17 unanswered points, and the Rams, who had led for most of the night, suddenly looked stuck: their offense scored only a Matt Gay 41 yard field goal in the third quarter, and the fourth quarter began with Cincinnati still ahead 20-16.

What followed was the drive that decided the game, and it nearly did not happen. With the clock winding down, Stafford led a 15 play march that included a Kupp 7 yard run on fourth and 1, and survived a sequence near the goal line in which a Kupp touchdown was wiped out by offsetting penalties. With 1:25 left, Stafford found Kupp again, this time for a 1 yard touchdown, and Gay's extra point gave Los Angeles a 23-20 lead. Cincinnati had one last chance, but Burrow's fourth down pass fell incomplete under pressure from Aaron Donald with 39 seconds left, and the Rams ran out the clock.

The legacy of the game is split between a validation and a warning. For the Rams, it validated the all in strategy: Stafford, acquired to win exactly this kind of game, threw the winning touchdown to Kupp, who was named MVP with 8 catches for 92 yards and 2 touchdowns, capping a season in which he led the league in receptions, receiving yards, and receiving touchdowns. Head coach Sean McVay, at 36 years and 20 days old, became the youngest head coach ever to win a Super Bowl. For Cincinnati, the loss became a lesson about protection: Burrow was sacked 7 times, and the Bengals' inability to hold up against the Rams' front, especially on the final drive, was the difference between a first title and a third Super Bowl defeat. The halftime show, the first built entirely around hip hop, may have outlasted both storylines in popular memory, but on the field the game turned on one drive, one receiver, and one defensive stop.

Why this game mattered

Super Bowl LVI was the first Super Bowl halftime show built entirely around hip hop, and that fact arguably traveled further than the result. Dr. Dre, Snoop Dogg, Eminem, Mary J. Blige, and Kendrick Lamar performed on a set of houses modeled on Los Angeles, with 50 Cent appearing upside down as a surprise guest, and Eminem taking a knee after "Lose Yourself" in a gesture widely read as a nod to Colin Kaepernick. The show later won the Emmy for Outstanding Variety Special (Live), the first halftime show to do so.

On the field, the game mattered because it rewarded a strategy the league had long debated. The Rams traded draft picks for proven veterans, spent heavily, and won in their own building, the second straight home team Super Bowl win after Tampa Bay. That success made the all in approach respectable in a way it had not been before, and it gave Stafford, long cast as a talented quarterback on bad Detroit teams, a championship that changed how his career is remembered.

For Cincinnati, the game became the start of a window rather than the end of one. Burrow's poise under pressure, even while being sacked 7 times, and a young core built around Ja'Marr Chase and Tee Higgins, made the Bengals a team to expect back. They have not returned yet, which is part of why this loss still stings in Cincinnati: the chance was real, the lead was real, and it slipped away on one final drive.
