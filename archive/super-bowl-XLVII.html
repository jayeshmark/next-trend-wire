Super Bowl XLVII: Baltimore Ravens vs San Francisco 49ers

By Editorial Team

Baltimore won its second Super Bowl title. San Francisco, seeking a sixth title, lost a Super Bowl for the first time after five wins. The game is remembered as the Harbaugh Bowl, for brothers John Harbaugh of Baltimore and Jim Harbaugh of San Francisco coaching against each other, and as the Blackout Bowl, for a partial power outage that stopped play in the third quarter.

The game story

The stakes were personal before they were tactical. For the first time in Super Bowl history, brothers coached against each other, with John Harbaugh leading Baltimore and Jim Harbaugh leading San Francisco. Baltimore carried the story of Ray Lewis, its veteran linebacker, playing the final game of his career after announcing his retirement during the playoffs. San Francisco carried the story of Colin Kaepernick, the young quarterback who had taken over the starting job during the season and brought a running threat Baltimore had to account for on every snap. Baltimore entered at 10-6, San Francisco at 11-4-1, and San Francisco was the favorite. The 49ers were seeking a sixth Super Bowl title. The Ravens were seeking their second.

Baltimore controlled the first half. Joe Flacco threw a 13 yard touchdown pass to Anquan Boldin in the first quarter, and after a David Akers field goal for San Francisco, Flacco added a 1 yard touchdown pass to Dennis Pitta and a 56 yard touchdown pass to Jacoby Jones in the second quarter. Akers closed the half with a 27 yard field goal, but Baltimore led 21-6, and Flacco had thrown three touchdown passes without an interception. San Francisco had moved the ball in stretches, with Frank Gore running well and Vernon Davis catching passes over the middle, but the 49ers had settled for field goals while Baltimore scored touchdowns.

The second half began with the play that seemed to end the game. Jacoby Jones returned the opening kickoff 108 yards for a touchdown, and Baltimore led 28-6. Then the Superdome went partially dark. A power outage stopped play for 34 minutes, and when the lights came back, the game changed. Whether the delay cooled Baltimore down or simply gave San Francisco time to regroup is still debated, but what followed is not in doubt. Kaepernick threw a 31 yard touchdown pass to Michael Crabtree. Gore ran 6 yards for a touchdown. Akers kicked a 34 yard field goal. In a span of roughly four minutes of game time, a 22 point lead was down to five, 28-23, and the third quarter ended with San Francisco surging.

The fourth quarter became a test of nerve. Justin Tucker kicked a 19 yard field goal to push Baltimore ahead 31-23. Kaepernick answered with a 15 yard touchdown run, but San Francisco went for two points and failed, as Kaepernick's pass to Randy Moss fell incomplete, so Baltimore still led 31-29. Tucker kicked a 38 yard field goal with 4:23 left, making it 34-29. San Francisco then drove to the Baltimore goal line area in the final minutes, with a chance to take the lead. On four plays from close range, Baltimore's defense held. Kaepernick's fourth down pass to Crabtree in the end zone fell incomplete, with no penalty flag, a non call that remains argued about by 49ers fans. Baltimore took over, ran down the clock, and punter Sam Koch ran out of the back of the end zone for an intentional safety with 12 seconds left, giving San Francisco two points but letting Baltimore run out the remaining time on the free kick. The final score was 34-31.

The legacy of the game is a split screen. For Baltimore, it was a second championship and a farewell win for Lewis, with Flacco named MVP after a 287 yard, three touchdown game that capped a strong postseason run. For San Francisco, it was the franchise's first Super Bowl loss, and the start of a different kind of story: Kaepernick's electric running and throwing had brought the 49ers back from 22 points down, but the final drive fell short at the goal line. The blackout remains the game's defining image, a strange pause in the middle of the biggest night in American sports, after which a rout became a classic. And the Harbaugh Bowl remains unique. No brothers have coached against each other in a Super Bowl before or since.

Scoring timeline

  1. Boldin opens the scoring, first quarter: Flacco's 13 yard touchdown pass to Boldin gives Baltimore a 7-0 lead and sets the tone for a first half owned by the Ravens passing game.
  2. Flacco's third touchdown, second quarter: the 56 yard pass to Jones makes it 21-3 and leaves San Francisco chasing the game before halftime.
  3. Jones returns the second half kickoff 108 yards, third quarter: Baltimore leads 28-6, and the game looks finished.
  4. The blackout, third quarter: a partial power outage stops play for 34 minutes right after the Jones return, breaking the game's rhythm.
  5. San Francisco scores 17 unanswered points, third quarter: Crabtree's touchdown, Gore's touchdown, and Akers' field goal cut the lead to 28-23.
  6. The failed two point attempt, fourth quarter: after Kaepernick's touchdown run, San Francisco goes for two and fails, leaving Baltimore ahead 31-29 instead of tied.
  7. Tucker's second fourth quarter field goal, fourth quarter: the 38 yard kick makes it 34-29 with 4:23 left, so San Francisco needs a touchdown, not a field goal.
  8. The goal line stand, fourth quarter: Baltimore stops San Francisco on four downs inside the 10 yard line, and the intentional safety that follows only trims the final margin to 34-31.

Why this game mattered

Super Bowl XLVII is remembered for three things that had little to do with the final score. The first is the Harbaugh Bowl, the only Super Bowl in which brothers coached against each other, a storyline that dominated the two weeks before the game. The second is the blackout, a 34 minute partial power outage that turned a blowout into a comeback and gave the game its second nickname, the Blackout Bowl. The third is Ray Lewis, whose final game ended with a championship, closing one of the most decorated defensive careers of his era on the sport's biggest stage.

The game also marked a changing of the guard at quarterback. Kaepernick's second half, with 302 passing yards and a rushing touchdown, announced a new style of quarterback play on the biggest stage, even in defeat. Flacco's MVP award, meanwhile, capped a postseason run that turned a good quarterback into a franchise defining one in Baltimore, and it earned him a large new contract that offseason. For San Francisco, the loss began a long wait: the 49ers would not return to the Super Bowl for seven years.
