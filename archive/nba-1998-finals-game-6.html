1998 NBA Finals Game 6: Chicago Bulls vs Utah Jazz

By Editorial Team

Result in brief: Chicago won its sixth championship in eight seasons and completed a second three-peat. Utah was seeking the first title in franchise history. The game was decided by Michael Jordan's jump shot with 5.2 seconds left, and it was the final game together for Jordan, Scottie Pippen, and Phil Jackson.

The game story

Chicago arrived in Salt Lake City leading the series 3-2, one win from a second three-peat and a sixth title in eight years. Utah was the same opponent as the year before, and the Jazz held home court advantage after both teams finished the regular season 62-20 and Utah swept the two meetings between them. Utah had won Game 1 in overtime and Game 5 in Chicago, 83-81, after Jordan missed a potential winner at the buzzer, so a Game 7 was a real possibility. The night carried more weight than one game: this was the last run together for Jordan, Pippen, and coach Phil Jackson, and everyone around the team understood it.

Pippen scored the opening basket on a dunk and hurt his back on the play. He stayed in the game but moved with difficulty and finished with 8 points in 26 minutes, more decoy than threat for long stretches. Utah led after every break until the last one: 25-22 after the first quarter, 49-45 at halftime, and 66-61 after three quarters. Jordan took 35 of Chicago's 67 shots because there was no one else to take them. His 45 points were more than the other Bulls combined, who scored 42 between them.

Two shot-clock calls shaped the middle of the game. In the second quarter, Howard Eisley's three-pointer was waved off after referee Dick Bavetta ruled it came after the buzzer. Replays showed the ball left Eisley's hands with a second to spare, but the league did not use replay review until 2002, so the call stood. Later, Ron Harper hit a jumper as the shot clock expired that tied the game at 79, a call the broadcast crew itself debated on air. Neither call decided the game, but both fed the sense that the night would be settled by inches.

Utah still led by five entering the fourth quarter, and Chicago spent the period clawing it back, winning the quarter 26-20. In the final minute, Stockton hit a three-pointer with 41.9 seconds left for an 86-83 Utah lead. Jordan answered with a layup on the next possession. Utah then put the ball in Malone's hands in the low post, and Jordan slipped behind him, knocked the ball away, and took it the other way without a timeout. Guarded by Bryon Russell, Jordan crossed over, rose, and hit a 20-foot jumper with 5.2 seconds left for an 87-86 lead. Stockton's three-pointer at the buzzer missed, and Chicago had its sixth title.

The shot became known as the Last Shot, and it closed more than a game. Jordan was named Finals MVP for the sixth time, a record, after averaging 33.5 points in the series. It was the final game as Bulls for Jordan and Jackson, who both retired and later returned elsewhere. The game drew a 22.3 Nielsen rating with an average of 35.9 million viewers, the highest rated and most watched NBA game ever. The question of whether Jordan pushed off Russell never went away: Russell later said Jordan was making the shot either way, and Jordan denied pushing off when the play was retold in the documentary series The Last Dance. Jordan also wore the Air Jordan 14 for the first time that night, a shoe now known as the Last Shot. For Utah, it was a second straight Finals loss to the same team, and the franchise's wait for a first title continued.

Quarter swing

  • Utah led after the first quarter by 3, at halftime by 4, and after three quarters by 5.
  • Chicago won the fourth quarter 26-20, a six point edge that erased the deficit and produced the one point final margin.
  • The last 41.9 seconds flipped the game twice: Stockton's three-pointer put Utah up by 3, Jordan's layup cut it to 1, and Jordan's steal and jumper put Chicago up by 1.
  • Jordan scored 45 of Chicago's 87 points, and no other Bull scored more than 15.

Records broken or at stake

  • Chicago's sixth championship in eight seasons, and its second three-peat after 1991 through 1993 and 1996 through 1998.
  • Jordan's sixth Finals MVP award, a record at the time.
  • Jordan's 45 points set the Finals record for most points in a road championship-clinching game, matched only by Jalen Brunson in 2026.
  • The game drew the highest television rating of any NBA game ever, with a 22.3 Nielsen rating and an average of 35.9 million viewers.
  • Jordan's 45 points were more than the other Bulls combined, who scored 42.
  • At stake for Utah: the first title in franchise history, and a win would have forced a Game 7, something no Bulls Finals team had ever played.

Star performances

  • Michael Jordan, Chicago: 45 points on 15 of 35 shooting, plus the steal and the winning shot in the final 19 seconds. He averaged 33.5 points in the series and won his sixth Finals MVP award.
  • Karl Malone, Utah: 31 points, 11 rebounds, and 7 assists in defeat. He had scored 39 in Utah's Game 5 win.
  • Scottie Pippen, Chicago: 8 points in 26 minutes, limited by the back injury from the opening dunk.
  • Toni Kukoc, Chicago: 15 points, the second highest total for Chicago, with 4 assists.
  • John Stockton, Utah: 10 points and 5 assists, including the three-pointer that gave Utah an 86-83 lead with 41.9 seconds left.

Cultural context

The game is remembered as the Last Shot, the final act of the 1997-98 Bulls season later retold in the 2020 documentary series The Last Dance, which covered Jordan, Pippen, and Jackson's last run together.

Jordan wore the Air Jordan 14 for the first time that night. The black and red version of the shoe is now known as the Last Shot.

The push-off debate became part of the play's legacy. Russell later said Jordan was making the shot either way, and Jordan denied pushing off when the documentary revisited the play.

The game remains the highest rated NBA game ever, broadcast by NBC with Bob Costas on the call.

The shot was Jordan's final shot as a Bulls player, and his 25th game-winning shot for Chicago.
