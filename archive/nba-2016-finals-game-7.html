2016 NBA Finals Game 7: Cleveland Cavaliers vs Golden State Warriors

By Editorial Team

Result in brief: Cleveland became the first team to win the Finals after trailing 3-1, won the first championship in franchise history, and ended Cleveland's 52-year wait for a major sports title. Golden State's record-setting season ended without a title.

The game story

The stakes were lopsided in a way that made the result hard to believe even before tipoff. Golden State had finished the regular season 73-9, the best record in league history, and was defending its title at home. Cleveland trailed the series 3-1, and no team had ever recovered from that deficit in the Finals. LeBron James had kept Cleveland alive with 41 points in Game 5, when Draymond Green was suspended, and 41 again in Game 6. A Game 7 would decide whether the greatest regular season ever ended with a title, and whether Cleveland's 52-year wait for a major sports championship would finally end. The teams entered Game 7 dead even on total points across the series, 610 apiece, a first for a Finals Game 7.

The game itself was tight from the start. Cleveland led 23-22 after the first quarter, but Golden State won the second quarter 27-19 and took a 49-42 lead into halftime. Cleveland answered in the third quarter, winning it 33-27 to trail by a single point, 76-75. The fourth quarter became a defensive grind. For a stretch of 3 minutes and 46 seconds late in the game, neither team scored at all, and the score sat tied at 89. James played 47 minutes, and Green played 47 for Golden State.

The finish came down to three plays, each by a different Cavalier. With 1 minute and 50 seconds left and the score tied, Andre Iguodala broke free for a layup that would have given Golden State the lead, and James chased him down from behind for the block, with J.R. Smith's defense forcing Iguodala to adjust the attempt. With 53 seconds left, Kyrie Irving hit a three-pointer over Stephen Curry for a 92-89 Cleveland lead. Golden State then got the switch it wanted, Curry against Kevin Love, and Love stayed in front of him and forced a missed contested three-pointer. James was fouled by Green going for a dunk, and made one of two free throws with 10.6 seconds left for a 93-89 lead. Cleveland fouled Green with 6.5 seconds left. Curry took the inbounds pass, pump faked, and missed a three-pointer over Iman Shumpert, and Marreese Speights missed a final three-pointer as time expired. Cleveland had its title.

The legacy of the game is the comeback itself. Cleveland became the first team to win the Finals from 3-1 down, and the first champion in franchise history. The title was won on Golden State's floor. James was voted Finals MVP unanimously, the first player to lead all players in a Finals series in points, rebounds, assists, steals, and blocks, and only the third player with a triple-double in a Finals Game 7. It was his seventh career Finals triple-double. The game featured 20 lead changes and 11 ties, and it was the only game in the series decided by fewer than 10 points. For Cleveland, it ended a drought that stretched back to the Browns' title in 1964, and James's postgame line became the city's shorthand for the night: Cleveland, this is for you. For Golden State, the 73-win season, the best regular season ever played, finished without a championship.

Quarter swing

  • Cleveland led after the first quarter by 1, trailed by 7 at halftime, and trailed by 1 after three quarters.
  • Cleveland won the second half 51-40, including an 18-13 fourth quarter.
  • The final stretch turned on a scoreless run: with the score tied at 89, neither team scored for 3 minutes and 46 seconds before Irving's three-pointer.
  • The last three decisive plays all went Cleveland's way: the block at 1 minute and 50 seconds, the three-pointer at 53 seconds, and Love's defensive stop on Curry.

Records broken or at stake

  • Cleveland became the first team in NBA history to win the Finals after trailing 3-1.
  • James became the first player to lead all players in a Finals series in points, rebounds, assists, steals, and blocks.
  • James recorded a triple-double in a Finals Game 7, only the third such performance in league history.
  • James was voted Finals MVP unanimously.
  • Cleveland won the first championship in franchise history, and the city's first major sports title since the Browns in 1964, ending a 52-year drought.
  • At stake for Golden State: a second straight title to close a 73-9 season, the best regular season record in league history. The loss made it the first 73-win season to end without a championship.
  • The game featured 20 lead changes and 11 ties.

Star performances

  • LeBron James, Cleveland: 27 points, 11 rebounds, 11 assists, 2 steals, and 3 blocks in Game 7. He averaged 29.7 points, 11.3 rebounds, and 8.9 assists in the series, with 2.6 steals and 2.3 blocks, and was voted Finals MVP unanimously.
  • Kyrie Irving, Cleveland: 26 points, including the go-ahead three-pointer over Curry with 53 seconds left.
  • Kevin Love, Cleveland: 9 points and 14 rebounds, plus the defensive stop on Curry in the final minute.
  • Draymond Green, Golden State: 32 points, 15 rebounds, and 9 assists on 11 of 15 shooting in defeat, after missing Game 5 through suspension.
  • Stephen Curry and Klay Thompson, Golden State: a combined 31 points on 12 of 36 shooting from the field and 6 of 24 from three-point range, well below their season standard.

Cultural context

The finish is remembered as three plays: the Block, Irving's shot, and Love's stop. Together they form the shorthand for the game, replayed whenever the comeback is retold.

James had promised, on returning to Cleveland in 2014, to bring a championship to northeast Ohio. The title fulfilled that promise in his second season back.

The postgame line, Cleveland, this is for you, became the city's shorthand for the night and for the end of the 52-year drought.

The game is the counterpoint to the 73-win season: the best regular season ever played, against the only Finals comeback from 3-1 down.

Golden State's loss reshaped the league's next chapter, as the Warriors added Kevin Durant that summer and won the next two titles. The 2016 result is the reason that run is remembered as a response to a defeat rather than a continuation of a dynasty.
