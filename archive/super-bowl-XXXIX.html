Super Bowl XXXIX: New England Patriots vs Philadelphia Eagles

By Editorial Team

New England won its third Super Bowl title in four years. Philadelphia was seeking its first Super Bowl title. The Patriots led 24-14 late in the fourth quarter before Philadelphia scored once more and ran out of time.

The game story

The stakes were about history on one side and breakthrough on the other. New England arrived at 14-2 chasing a third Super Bowl title in four years, a run that would put the Patriots alongside the great dynasties of earlier eras. Philadelphia arrived at 13-3 chasing its first Super Bowl title, after years of falling short in the NFC Championship Game. The Eagles had the league's most talked about receiver in Terrell Owens, who had broken his leg late in the season and then returned for this game, a comeback story that dominated the week before kickoff.

The first quarter produced no points, and the game felt tense rather than open. Philadelphia struck first in the second quarter, when McNabb threw a six yard touchdown pass to L.J. Smith. New England answered before halftime, when Brady threw a four yard touchdown pass to David Givens, and the teams went to the break tied at 7. The halftime show, a solo set from Paul McCartney, was itself a statement: after the previous year's halftime controversy, the league had chosen the safest possible headliner, and the show passed without incident.

The third quarter traded touchdowns and kept the game level. Brady threw a two yard touchdown pass to Vrabel, a linebacker playing tight end near the goal line, and New England led 14-7. McNabb answered with a 10 yard touchdown pass to Brian Westbrook, and the game was tied at 14 going into the fourth quarter. At that point the game had been even in almost every way, and it felt like the team that made the next big play would win.

New England made it. Corey Dillon ran two yards for a touchdown early in the fourth quarter, and after Philadelphia's next drive stalled, Brady led a drive that ended with a 22 yard Adam Vinatieri field goal with 8:43 left. The lead was 24-14, and Philadelphia, which had used its timeouts, was running out of both time and options. McNabb did lead one more scoring drive, throwing a 30 yard touchdown pass to Greg Lewis with 1:55 left to cut the lead to 24-21, but the Eagles could not recover the onside kick, and New England ran out the clock.

The legacy of the game is the dynasty label. Three titles in four years made the Brady and Belichick Patriots the defining team of the decade, and Deion Branch's MVP award, won on 11 catches for 133 yards without a touchdown, was a reminder that the award can honor the player who moved the chains all night rather than the player who scored. For Philadelphia, the loss became part of a longer story: a franchise that had reached the top of its conference repeatedly but could not win the final game, a wait that lasted until the Eagles won Super Bowl LII thirteen years later. Owens finished with 9 catches for 122 yards on a broken leg, a performance that is still cited whenever players return from serious injury for a championship game. The game itself was not a classic finish, but it was a clean, hard fought championship, and it closed the first chapter of the Patriots dynasty with the team on top.

Why this game mattered

Super Bowl XXXIX is remembered as the night the Patriots dynasty became official and the night the Super Bowl halftime show went deliberately safe. After the previous year's controversy, Paul McCartney's four song set of Beatles and Wings classics was chosen to calm the event down, and it worked: the show was praised, uneventful, and set the template for single headliner rock shows in the years that followed. The commercials told a different story about the era: first time advertisers like GoDaddy, CareerBuilder, and Ameriquest used the game to introduce themselves to America, a sign that the Super Bowl ad break had become a launch pad for internet age brands. And on the field, Terrell Owens playing on a broken leg became a lasting image of toughness, even in a losing effort, while Branch's MVP win showed that a receiver could dominate a Super Bowl without scoring a touchdown.
