The game story

Oakland arrived at the Rose Bowl as the best team in football that season, and also as a team with a history of falling short. The Raiders had gone 13 and 1 in the regular season, beaten New England and Pittsburgh in the playoffs, and reached their second Super Bowl, nine years after losing Super Bowl II to Green Bay. Minnesota arrived as the familiar challenger: the Vikings had gone 11, 2, and 1, beaten Washington and Los Angeles in the playoffs, and reached their fourth Super Bowl, having lost the previous three. The question was whether Oakland's talent would finally break through, or whether Minnesota's experience would finally pay off. The first quarter gave no answer, because neither team scored. The second quarter gave a very clear one.

Oakland scored 16 points in the second quarter and never looked back. Errol Mann kicked a 24 yard field goal to open the scoring, then Ken Stabler threw a 1 yard touchdown pass to Dave Casper, then Pete Banaszak ran 1 yard for a touchdown, though Mann's extra point missed. The Raiders led 16 to 0 at halftime, and the numbers were even more one sided than the score: Oakland had gained 288 yards in the first half alone, a Super Bowl record, behind Clarence Davis, who finished with 137 rushing yards on just 16 carries, and Fred Biletnikoff, whose 4 catches for 79 yards repeatedly moved Oakland into scoring position and set up three Raiders touchdowns. Biletnikoff did not catch a touchdown pass and did not reach 100 yards, yet he was named MVP, because his catches came on the plays that mattered most, on third down and in scoring territory.

Minnesota's offense, meanwhile, could not get going against an Oakland defense that had no turnovers to give away and no patience for Minnesota's running game. Chuck Foreman, Minnesota's best offensive player, managed only 44 rushing yards, and Tarkenton, under constant pressure, completed 17 of 35 passes with 2 interceptions. In the third quarter, Mann added a 40 yard field goal to make it 19 to 0, and only then did Minnesota score, when Tarkenton threw an 8 yard touchdown pass to Sammy White. For a moment, at 19 to 7, Minnesota had a flicker of hope. Oakland extinguished it quickly. On the next drive, Stabler hit Biletnikoff for 48 yards to set up Banaszak's second touchdown, a 2 yard run that made it 26 to 7.

The lasting image of the game came next. In the fourth quarter, Willie Brown, a 36 year old cornerback in his 14th professional season, intercepted Tarkenton and ran 75 yards for a touchdown, in slow motion in the famous NFL Films version, with no Viking close enough to stop him. The extra point missed, but the score was 32 to 7, and the game was over. Minnesota scored once more, when backup quarterback Bob Lee threw a 13 yard touchdown pass to Stu Voigt with 25 seconds left, but it only made the final score 32 to 14.

The win gave Oakland its first Super Bowl championship and gave John Madden, in his eighth season as head coach, the title that had eluded him through six conference championship losses. It also gave the Raiders a place in history as one of the great single season teams: 13 and 1 in the regular season, unbeaten in the playoffs, and dominant in the championship game. For Minnesota, it was a fourth Super Bowl loss in four tries, and the pattern was now impossible to ignore: in all four losses, the Vikings had failed to score in the first half. Minnesota's fourth loss cemented a reputation the franchise would carry for decades, while Oakland's win cemented a different one: the Raiders as the team that finally, after years of near misses, finished the job.

Scoring timeline

  1. A scoreless first quarter. Both teams feel each other out, and neither scores.
  2. Oakland breaks through in the second quarter. Mann's field goal, Casper's touchdown catch, and Banaszak's touchdown run make it 16 to 0 at halftime.
  3. Oakland extends the lead. Mann's 40 yard field goal in the third quarter makes it 19 to 0.
  4. Minnesota finally scores. Tarkenton's touchdown pass to White cuts the lead to 19 to 7.
  5. Oakland answers immediately. Stabler's 48 yard pass to Biletnikoff sets up Banaszak's second touchdown, making it 26 to 7.
  6. Brown's pick six seals it. His 75 yard interception return makes it 32 to 7 in the fourth quarter.
  7. Minnesota's late consolation. Lee's touchdown pass to Voigt with 25 seconds left makes the final score 32 to 14.

Why this game mattered

Super Bowl XI was played on January 9, the earliest calendar date for a Super Bowl, and it drew 103,438 fans to the Rose Bowl, one of the largest crowds in Super Bowl history. It gave John Madden his only Super Bowl title as a coach, a fact that became more notable as Madden became the most famous broadcaster in football and the namesake of the best selling football video game series. It also gave the Raiders their first championship after years as the league's most colorful and controversial team, and it gave Willie Brown's interception return a permanent place in NFL Films lore. For Minnesota, the loss completed a run of four Super Bowl defeats that remains unmatched, and it marked the end of the Vikings' 1970s window: Bud Grant's team would not return to the Super Bowl again.
