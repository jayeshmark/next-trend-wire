Super Bowl LI: New England Patriots vs Atlanta Falcons

By Editorial Team

Result in brief: New England won its fifth Super Bowl title. Atlanta was seeking its first Super Bowl title in its second appearance. This was the first Super Bowl decided in overtime, and the largest comeback in Super Bowl history, from a 28-3 deficit.

The game story

The stakes were clear before kickoff, and they were not equal. New England arrived as the familiar power, a 14-2 team in its ninth Super Bowl appearance and its seventh with head coach Bill Belichick and quarterback Tom Brady together. Brady was 39 and playing his first full season back after a four game suspension to start the year, and a win would give him a fifth Super Bowl title as a starting quarterback. Atlanta arrived as the challenger with the league's most dangerous offense, an 11-5 team led by league MVP Matt Ryan, seeking the first Super Bowl title in franchise history in only its second appearance.

For most of the night, the challenger looked like the better team by a wide margin. The first quarter ended scoreless, but that was the last time the game felt even. In the second quarter, Atlanta scored three times. Devonta Freeman ran five yards for the first touchdown. Matt Ryan then found tight end Austin Hooper for a 19 yard touchdown. Then Robert Alford stepped in front of a Brady pass and returned the interception 82 yards for a touchdown, and the score was 21-0. New England finally reached the scoreboard on a 41 yard field goal by Stephen Gostkowski with two seconds left in the half, but a 21-3 halftime deficit already felt larger than the numbers suggested.

The third quarter made the result look settled. Ryan threw a six yard touchdown pass to Tevin Coleman with 8:31 left in the quarter, and Atlanta led 28-3. At that point, no team had ever overcome more than a 10 point deficit to win a Super Bowl. The gap was 25 points. New England's response was slow rather than spectacular. Brady led a 13 play, 75 yard drive that ended with a five yard touchdown pass to James White, but Gostkowski missed the extra point off the right upright, leaving the score at 28-9.

The fourth quarter turned on defense as much as on Brady. New England opened the quarter with a 33 yard Gostkowski field goal, cutting the lead to 28-12. On Atlanta's next drive, Dont'a Hightower came untouched off the edge on third and one, knocked the ball from Ryan, and Alan Branch recovered at the Atlanta 25. Five plays later, Brady threw a six yard touchdown pass to Danny Amendola, and White ran in the two point conversion. The lead was down to eight points, 28-20, with 5:56 left.

Atlanta then had its last real chance to close the game, and the sequence became as famous as the comeback itself. Freeman gained 39 yards on a screen pass, and Julio Jones made a remarkable sideline catch for 27 yards to the New England 22 with 4:40 left. A field goal there would have made it an 11 point game. Instead, Freeman lost a yard, Ryan was sacked for a 12 yard loss, and a holding penalty on Jake Matthews wiped out a completion and pushed Atlanta back to third and 33 from the New England 45. Atlanta punted, and New England took over at its own nine yard line with 3:30 left and two timeouts.

What followed was the drive that defined the night. Brady moved New England 91 yards in 10 plays in 2:33. The signature play came with 2:03 left, when Julian Edelman caught a deflected pass inches above the turf for 23 yards, giving New England first down at the Atlanta 41. Atlanta challenged the ruling, and the call stood. Brady then found Amendola for 20 yards, and two passes to White moved the ball to the one yard line. With 57 seconds left, White ran one yard for the touchdown, and Brady threw to Amendola for the two point conversion. The score was tied at 28, and a game that had looked like a rout was headed to the first overtime in Super Bowl history.

Overtime, under the rules then in use, gave New England the ball first, and a touchdown would win the game without Atlanta touching the ball. Brady led an eight play, 75 yard drive, and White scored on a two yard run. It was White's third touchdown of the night, and it gave New England a 34-28 win.

The legacy of the game is split in two, and both halves have lasted. For New England, it completed the largest comeback in Super Bowl history and gave Brady his fifth title and his fourth Super Bowl MVP award, both records at the time. Brady set Super Bowl records with 43 completions, 62 attempts, and 466 passing yards. White set a Super Bowl record with 14 receptions and scored 20 points, also a record for a single Super Bowl. For Atlanta, the game became shorthand for a collapse, reduced in popular memory to the score at its peak: 28-3. That is why Super Bowl LI remains the standard against which later comebacks are measured, and the warning every leading team now carries into the fourth quarter.

Scoring timeline

  1. Freeman opens the scoring, second quarter: a five yard run caps Atlanta's first sustained drive and gives the Falcons a 7-0 lead after a scoreless first quarter.
  2. Hooper extends the lead, second quarter: Ryan's 19 yard touchdown pass to Hooper makes it 14-0 and shows Atlanta can score through the air as well as on the ground.
  3. Alford's pick six, second quarter: Alford returns a Brady interception 82 yards for a touchdown. The lead is 21-0, and New England's mistakes are now directly producing Atlanta points.
  4. Coleman makes it 28-3, third quarter: Ryan's six yard touchdown pass to Coleman, with 8:31 left in the third quarter, creates the 25 point deficit that defines the game.
  5. White's first touchdown, third quarter: Brady's five yard pass to White makes it 28-9, but the missed extra point means New England will need two two point conversions later to tie.
  6. Hightower's strip sack, fourth quarter: Hightower forces a Ryan fumble, Branch recovers at the Atlanta 25, and New England scores five plays later to cut the lead to 28-20.
  7. Jones catches, then Atlanta stalls, fourth quarter: Jones makes a 27 yard sideline catch to the New England 22, but a sack and a holding penalty push Atlanta out of field goal range, and Atlanta punts with New England still down eight.
  8. Edelman's catch, fourth quarter: Edelman's deflected 23 yard catch, upheld on review, moves New England to the Atlanta 41 with 2:03 left on the tying drive.
  9. White ties it, then wins it: White's one yard touchdown run and Amendola's two point conversion tie the game at 28 with 57 seconds left, and White's two yard run in overtime wins it 34-28. New England's first lead of the game is the final score.

Why this game mattered

Super Bowl LI became a cultural reference point, not just a football result. The score "28-3" entered everyday language as shorthand for a lead that looks safe and is not, and it is still used in sports, business, and online discussion to describe a collapse. That shorthand stuck because the collapse happened on the largest American television stage, against the era's most famous quarterback, and in the first Super Bowl overtime.

The game also hardened two reputations in opposite directions. For Brady and Belichick, it became the strongest single piece of evidence in the argument that they were the greatest quarterback and coach pairing of their era, because the comeback required sustained execution rather than one lucky play. For Atlanta, it became a defining loss that followed the franchise and its coaches into later seasons, and it sharpened debate about late game play calling, clock management, and whether to run the ball and kick a field goal when a lead is already in hand.

The halftime show added to the night's reach. Lady Gaga performed solo from the stadium roof, with a drone light display behind her, and closed with her then current single "Million Reasons" before finishing on "Bad Romance". The performance was widely praised as a hits driven, solo showcase, and it helped the night feel like a pop culture event even before the comeback began.

Finally, the game changed how late leads are talked about. Before this night, a 10 point deficit was the largest ever overcome to win a Super Bowl. After it, no lead in a Super Bowl has felt safe in the same way, because the record is now 25 points, set in a game where the trailing team did not lead until the winning touchdown.
