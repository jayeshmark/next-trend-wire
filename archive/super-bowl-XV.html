Super Bowl XV: Oakland Raiders vs Philadelphia Eagles

By Editorial Team

Result in brief: Oakland became the first wild card team ever to win a Super Bowl. Jim Plunkett, a former first overall draft pick whose career had stalled, was named MVP, and Tom Flores became the first person to be part of a Super Bowl winning team as both a player and a head coach.

The game story

Philadelphia arrived in New Orleans as the favorite, and the reasoning was easy to follow. The Eagles had finished 12 and 4, had allowed the fewest points in the league in the regular season, and had already beaten Oakland 10 to 7 in November, sacking Jim Plunkett eight times in that game. This was Philadelphia's first Super Bowl, and the city was in the middle of a remarkable sporting year in which its four major teams had all reached their league championship round. Head coach Dick Vermeil had rebuilt a franchise that had not played for a league title since 1960, and quarterback Ron Jaworski had thrown 27 touchdown passes against only 12 interceptions in the regular season. The Eagles looked like the steadier, more complete team.

Oakland arrived with a stranger story, and a better one. The Raiders had finished 11 and 5 but lost the AFC West tiebreaker to San Diego, so they entered the playoffs as a wild card team. Their starting quarterback for most of the season had not even been the starter in September. Dan Pastorini broke his leg after a 2 and 3 start, and Plunkett, a former first overall draft pick whose career had stalled in New England and San Francisco, took over. Plunkett then led Oakland to nine wins in its final 11 regular season games, then through three road playoff wins at Houston, Cleveland, and San Diego. Head coach Tom Flores, in only his second season, had a team that had already proved it could win away from home in January. Nobody in the Raiders camp needed to be told they were underdogs. They had been underdogs since October.

The game turned almost immediately, and it never really turned back. On Philadelphia's first pass of the game, Rod Martin intercepted Jaworski and returned the ball to the Philadelphia 30 yard line. Seven plays later Plunkett hit Cliff Branch for a 2 yard touchdown, and Oakland led 7 to 0. Philadelphia's next drive stalled, Oakland punted the ball back, and then, with nine seconds left in the first quarter, Plunkett scrambled away from pressure and found Kenny King behind the secondary for an 80 yard touchdown, the longest scoring play in Super Bowl history to that point. Two Plunkett touchdown passes had produced a 14 to 0 lead, and the Eagles, a team built to play from in front behind its defense and its running game, were suddenly chasing the game against a defense that led the league in forced turnovers.

Philadelphia steadied itself enough to avoid a rout in the second quarter, kicking a 30 yard field goal through Tony Franklin, but Oakland never gave the lead back. The third quarter sealed the result. Plunkett led a 76 yard drive capped by a 29 yard touchdown pass to Branch, his second scoring catch of the night, and Chris Bahr added a 46 yard field goal to make it 24 to 3. Philadelphia finally reached the end zone in the fourth quarter, when Jaworski found Keith Krepfle for an 8 yard touchdown, but Bahr answered with a 35 yard field goal, and the final score was 27 to 10. The box score flattered Philadelphia in one respect: the Eagles outgained Oakland in passing yards and finished with more first downs, but three interceptions and a lost fumble meant those yards rarely became points.

The legacy of this game belongs to two firsts and one revival. Oakland became the first wild card team ever to win a Super Bowl, a feat that changed how the playoffs were understood, since a team that had not won its division could now be champion. Plunkett became only the second Heisman Trophy winner to be named Super Bowl MVP, and his performance completed one of the great second acts in league history, from released backup to champion in less than three seasons. Flores became the first person to be part of a Super Bowl winning team as both a player and a head coach. The game was also played five days after the end of the Iran hostage crisis, and the pregame ceremonies honored the released hostages, with yellow bows on the Superdome and yellow stripes on the players' helmets. For Philadelphia, the defeat began a long wait: the Eagles would not return to a Super Bowl until after the 2004 season, and would not win one until February 2018. For Oakland, this was the franchise's second championship, and its last title in the city of Oakland.

Why this game mattered

This game was played five days after the Iran hostage crisis ended, and that timing shaped the day. The pregame ceremonies honored the released hostages, a large yellow bow was attached to the outside of the Superdome, miniature bows were given to fans, and every player wore a yellow stripe on the back of his helmet. It was also the first Super Bowl broadcast with closed captioning for viewers who are deaf or hard of hearing. On the field, the result mattered because it broke the assumption that only a division winner could win the championship. The wild card route, created by the expanded playoff format, had produced a champion in only its third season of existence. Plunkett's revival also became a lasting story about second chances in professional sport: a quarterback discarded by two franchises, signed as a backup, thrust into the lineup by injury, and then unbeaten when it mattered most. The Raiders would soon leave Oakland for Los Angeles, winning another title there three years later, which makes this game the last championship of Oakland's first Raiders era.
