Super Bowl XXI: New York Giants vs Denver Broncos

By Editorial Team

Result in brief: New York won the franchise's first Super Bowl title and its first NFL championship since 1956, scoring 30 points in the second half, a Super Bowl record at the time. Phil Simms was named MVP after completing 22 of 25 passes.

The game story

The first half of this game was close, tense, and, for New York, quietly alarming. Denver, led by John Elway in his first Super Bowl, took a 10 to 7 lead in the first quarter on a 48 yard field goal by Rich Karlis and a 4 yard Elway touchdown run. New York answered with a 6 yard touchdown pass from Phil Simms to Zeke Mowatt, but the Giants' offense could not find a rhythm against Denver's defense, even though New York's defense had allowed only three points combined in its two playoff wins. The only score of the second quarter was a safety, when George Martin sacked Elway in the end zone, and Denver went to halftime ahead 10 to 9. It was the narrowest halftime margin in Super Bowl history to that point, and it flattered New York in one respect: the Giants had been outplayed for stretches and trailed, yet were within a point. Denver's coaches could feel good about the lead. New York's coaches, led by Bill Parcells, could feel good about something else: Simms had completed 12 of 14 passes in the half, and the Giants' defense had not yet broken.

The second half began with a trick that announced New York's intentions. After three plays gained nine yards, New York lined up to punt from its own 46 yard line, then shifted out of punt formation, and backup quarterback Jeff Rutledge took the snap and sneaked for a first down. Six plays later, Simms found tight end Mark Bavaro for a 13 yard touchdown, and New York led 16 to 10. The rout was on, and it never stopped. Raul Allegre kicked a 21 yard field goal, Joe Morris ran 1 yard for a touchdown as the third quarter expired, and Phil McConkey caught a 6 yard touchdown pass from Simms early in the fourth quarter. In less than twenty minutes of game time, a 10 to 9 deficit had become a 33 to 10 lead. Denver added a field goal and a late 47 yard touchdown pass from Elway to Vance Johnson, and Ottis Anderson added a 2 yard touchdown run for New York, but the final score, 39 to 20, barely captured how one sided the second half had been. New York scored 30 points after halftime, a Super Bowl record, and gained its yards in chunks while Denver's offense, which had moved the ball in the first half, managed only 2 net yards in the third quarter.

Simms was the story, and his numbers remain among the finest ever posted in a Super Bowl. He completed 22 of 25 passes for 268 yards and three touchdowns, with no interceptions, for a passer rating of 150.92, the highest for one game in Super Bowl history at the time. His 88 percent completion rate set both a Super Bowl and an NFL postseason record, and his 10 completions in 10 attempts in the second half included every pass he threw after halftime. Simms had been doubted for much of his career, drafted in 1979, injured repeatedly, and criticized in New York for inconsistency, but on this night he did not miss. He threw to Bavaro, to McConkey, to Mowatt, and to Morris with a calm that matched the moment, and when the game ended, head coach Bill Parcells received the now famous Gatorade shower from linebacker Harry Carson, a celebration that has since become a championship tradition. Simms, asked after the game what he would do next, said he was going to Disney World, launching another tradition that has lasted for decades.

The legacy of this game belongs to two franchises heading in opposite directions. For New York, it was the franchise's first Super Bowl title and its first NFL championship since 1956, ending a thirty year wait and validating Parcells, defensive coordinator Bill Belichick, and a defense nicknamed the Big Blue Wrecking Crew. The Giants would win another title four years later with much of the same core. For Denver, it was the first of three Super Bowl losses in four years, a run that would define Elway's early career as brilliant but unfulfilled, until he finally won championships more than a decade later. The game was played in front of 101,063 fans at the Rose Bowl, and it came three months after the New York Mets had won the World Series, giving New York City two championships in four months. Simms' performance, meanwhile, set a standard for Super Bowl quarterbacking that is still cited whenever a quarterback has a nearly perfect night on the largest stage.

Why this game mattered

This game gave American sports two traditions that have lasted for decades. The Gatorade shower, with Harry Carson sneaking up behind Bill Parcells in a security shirt and emptying a cooler over his coach, became the standard championship celebration across sports. Simms's postgame line about going to Disney World became the standard championship commercial, repeated by MVPs in every major sport since. The game also marked the arrival of the Giants as a modern franchise, ending a championship drought that stretched back to the days of Frank Gifford and the 1956 title team. For Denver, the loss was the first chapter in a painful trilogy, with two more Super Bowl defeats to follow, that would shape how Elway's greatness was debated until his late career championships. The Rose Bowl crowd of more than 101,000 also reflected the event's growth: the Super Bowl was no longer merely a football game, but a national occasion that filled the largest stadiums in the country and launched advertising and television traditions of its own.
