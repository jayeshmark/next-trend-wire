Super Bowl XXVIII: Dallas Cowboys vs Buffalo Bills

By Editorial Team

Result in brief: Dallas won its fourth Super Bowl title and its second straight, tying Pittsburgh and San Francisco for the most at the time. Buffalo suffered a fourth straight Super Bowl defeat, a record that still stands.

The game story

This was the first rematch in consecutive Super Bowls, and for one half it looked like a different result was possible. Buffalo had lost three straight Super Bowls, but the Bills led this one at halftime. Dallas had won the previous year by 35 points, yet the Cowboys trailed 13 to 6 at the break in Atlanta, and Buffalo had the ball and the momentum. Then the second half began, and in less than a minute the game turned.

Buffalo had built its lead the hard way. Eddie Murray kicked two field goals for Dallas, and Steve Christie answered with a 54 yard field goal for Buffalo, the longest in Super Bowl history at the time. Thurman Thomas ran 4 yards for a touchdown in the second quarter, and Christie added a 28 yard field goal on the final play of the half. Buffalo led 13 to 6, and the Bills had outplayed Dallas for thirty minutes. But Buffalo had also left points on the field, and Dallas had Emmitt Smith, who had already carried the ball well in the first half and was about to take the game over.

The turning point came 55 seconds into the second half. Thomas took a handoff, Lett knocked the ball loose, and Washington picked it up and ran for a touchdown. The score was tied at 13 to 13, and the stadium changed. Dallas forced Buffalo to punt on the next possession, took over at its own 36 yard line, and gave the ball to Smith seven times on an eight play drive. Smith finished the drive with a 15 yard touchdown run, and Dallas led 20 to 13. Buffalo never scored again.

Smith was the story of the second half. He carried the ball 30 times in the game for 132 yards, and 19 of those carries came after halftime. He scored again in the fourth quarter, on fourth down from the 1 yard line, after Washington had intercepted Kelly to set up the drive. Murray added a 20 yard field goal with 2 minutes and 50 seconds left, and the final score was 30 to 13. Dallas had scored 24 unanswered points in the second half.

Smith was named most valuable player, the second running back in a row to win the award after rushing for more than 100 yards in consecutive Super Bowls. The award recognized a second half in which Smith carried the offense almost alone, gaining 92 of his 132 yards after halftime and scoring both Dallas touchdowns after the break. It was a performance built on patience and repetition rather than big plays, and it wore Buffalo down exactly as Dallas had planned. For Dallas, the win was a fourth Super Bowl title, tying Pittsburgh and San Francisco for the most at the time, and a second straight title. For Buffalo, the loss was a fourth straight Super Bowl defeat, a record that still stands. The Bills have not returned to a Super Bowl since. This game is remembered in Buffalo not for the halftime lead, but for the minute that erased it: one fumble, one return, and a season of hope turned into another winter of questions.

Cultural context

The game was played in Atlanta, in the Georgia Dome, and it was the first Super Bowl in which the same two teams met in consecutive years. That fact alone made it historic, and the halftime show leaned into the host city with a country lineup. But the lasting cultural note is darker, and it belongs to Buffalo. Four straight Super Bowl appearances without a win is a record no team has matched, and the second half of this game is why the run feels so painful. Buffalo led at halftime of its fourth attempt, then lost the second half 24 to 0. For Dallas, the win confirmed a dynasty, but it was also the end of the Jimmy Johnson era in spirit: Johnson left after the season, and the Cowboys would win once more under Barry Switzer two years later, then never return to a Super Bowl. The two teams that met in Atlanta in 1994 have both spent the decades since chasing a return to this stage.
