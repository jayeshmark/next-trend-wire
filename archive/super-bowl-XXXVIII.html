Super Bowl XXXVIII: New England Patriots vs Carolina Panthers

By Editorial Team

New England won its second Super Bowl title in three years on a 41 yard Adam Vinatieri field goal with 9 seconds left. Carolina was in its first Super Bowl. The fourth quarter alone produced 37 points.

The game story

The stakes were clear, and they favored one team heavily on paper. New England arrived at 14-2, the defending champion from two years earlier, with Tom Brady and Bill Belichick chasing a second title together. Carolina arrived at 11-5 in its first Super Bowl, a team that had won close games all season and believed it could win one more. The Panthers were seven point underdogs, and for most of the night they played like a team that did not accept that number.

For a long stretch, the game refused to catch fire. The first quarter ended scoreless, the only scoreless opening quarter either team had played in weeks, and Vinatieri missed a 31 yard field goal. The second quarter finally produced points in a rush: Brady threw a five yard touchdown pass to Deion Branch, Delhomme answered with a 39 yard touchdown pass to Steve Smith, Brady threw a five yard touchdown pass to David Givens, and John Kasay kicked a 50 yard field goal as time expired in the half. New England led 14-10, and the game felt like it might stay a defensive struggle.

The third quarter stayed scoreless, which made what followed even harder to believe. The fourth quarter produced 37 points, and the lead changed hands three times. Antowain Smith ran two yards for a touchdown to make it 21-10 New England. DeShaun Foster ran 33 yards for a touchdown to cut it to 21-16, but the two point try failed. Then Delhomme threw an 85 yard touchdown pass to Muhsin Muhammad, and suddenly Carolina led 22-21, though again the two point try failed. Brady answered with a one yard touchdown pass to linebacker Mike Vrabel, and Kevin Faulk ran in the two point conversion for a 29-22 New England lead. Delhomme answered again, finding Ricky Proehl from 12 yards with 1:13 left to tie the game at 29.

That left Brady with 1:08 and all three timeouts, and that was enough. Brady completed passes to move New England into field goal range, and with 9 seconds left, Vinatieri kicked a 41 yard field goal for a 32-29 win. It was Vinatieri's second game winning Super Bowl kick in three years, and it confirmed New England as the team of the era. Brady finished with 354 yards and three touchdowns and was named MVP for the second time.

The legacy of the game is split, because the football and the halftime show both became permanent reference points. On the field, the fourth quarter is still cited as one of the greatest quarters in Super Bowl history, a 15 minute stretch in which two quarterbacks traded touchdowns and neither defense could get a stop when it mattered. Carolina earned respect that night that outlasted the loss: a first time finalist had pushed a champion to the final seconds and had led in the fourth quarter. Off the field, the halftime show produced the "wardrobe malfunction" involving Janet Jackson and Justin Timberlake, an incident that changed how live television was broadcast, with delays and stricter standards following in its wake. The game is therefore remembered twice, once for a classic finish and once for a few seconds at halftime that reshaped television.

Scoring timeline

  1. Branch opens the scoring, second quarter: Brady's five yard pass to Branch makes it 7-0 after a scoreless first quarter and a missed Vinatieri field goal.
  2. Smith answers, second quarter: Delhomme's 39 yard pass to Steve Smith ties the game and shows Carolina can strike quickly.
  3. Kasay's halftime field goal, second quarter: a 50 yard kick as time expires keeps Carolina within four points at 14-10.
  4. Smith extends the lead, fourth quarter: Antowain Smith's two yard run makes it 21-10 and looks like the start of a New England pull away.
  5. Foster and Muhammad flip the game, fourth quarter: Foster's 33 yard run and Muhammad's 85 yard catch give Carolina a 22-21 lead, though both two point tries fail, leaving the lead at one point instead of five.
  6. Vrabel and Faulk retake the lead, fourth quarter: Brady's touchdown pass to a linebacker, plus Faulk's two point run, makes it 29-22 New England.
  7. Proehl ties it, fourth quarter: Delhomme's 12 yard pass to Proehl with 1:13 left makes it 29-29.
  8. Vinatieri wins it, fourth quarter: Brady moves New England into range on the final drive, and Vinatieri's 41 yard kick with 9 seconds left makes the final score 32-29.

Why this game mattered

Super Bowl XXXVIII changed television more than it changed football. The halftime incident, in which part of Janet Jackson's costume was torn during the final song, drew a flood of complaints, a long FCC fight, and a new caution in live broadcasting that lasted for years. Broadcasters added delays to live events, and halftime shows moved toward safer, single headliner formats, which is why the next year's show was a solo Paul McCartney set. On the field, the game confirmed the Patriots as a dynasty in progress and gave the fourth quarter a permanent place in Super Bowl lore: 37 points, three lead changes, and a winning kick with 9 seconds left. Carolina, meanwhile, left with proof that a first time finalist could stand toe to toe with a champion, a reputation the franchise carried into the seasons that followed.
