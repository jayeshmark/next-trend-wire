Super Bowl XXXII: Denver Broncos vs Green Bay Packers

By Editorial Team

Result in brief: Denver won its first championship, ending 38 years without a title and ending the AFC's thirteen game Super Bowl losing streak. Green Bay became the third defending champion to lose a Super Bowl.

The game story

Denver arrived in San Diego carrying two kinds of history, both heavy. John Elway had lost three Super Bowls by wide margins, and the AFC had lost thirteen straight Super Bowls. Green Bay arrived as the defending champion, favored by 11 points, with Brett Favre coming off a third straight league most valuable player award. Almost no one outside Denver believed the Broncos could win. Denver believed it, and Terrell Davis carried that belief for 157 yards.

Green Bay scored first, on a 22 yard pass from Favre to Antonio Freeman. Denver answered with a 1 yard run by Davis, and the first quarter ended tied at 7 to 7. In the second quarter, Elway ran 1 yard for a touchdown, Jason Elam kicked a 51 yard field goal, and Denver led 17 to 7. Green Bay answered just before halftime, when Favre threw a 6 yard touchdown pass to Mark Chmura after a 17 play, 95 yard drive that used 7 minutes and 26 seconds. The halftime score was 17 to 14, and the game was already better than anyone had predicted.

The third quarter began with Green Bay tying the game on a 27 yard field goal by Ryan Longwell. Denver then drove 92 yards in 13 plays, using 7 minutes and 12 seconds, and Davis scored from the 1 yard line for a 24 to 17 lead. Green Bay answered in the fourth quarter, when Favre threw a 13 yard touchdown pass to Freeman, tying the game at 24 to 24 with 13 minutes and 32 seconds left. The game was now a test of nerve, and Denver had the ball and the running back to pass it.

The most famous play of the game came on the next Denver drive. On third down, Elway scrambled on a broken play, ran for the first down marker, and dove through the air. Two Green Bay defenders hit him in midair and spun him around, a play remembered ever since as the Helicopter. Elway got the first down, and Denver kept the ball. With 1 minute and 45 seconds left, Davis scored from the 1 yard line for his third touchdown of the game, giving Denver a 31 to 24 lead. Green Bay had one last chance, driving to the Denver 31 yard line, but Favre's fourth down pass was knocked away by Mobley, and Denver was a champion for the first time.

Davis was named most valuable player after rushing for 157 yards and 3 touchdowns, a Super Bowl record for rushing touchdowns at the time. Elway, at 37, had finally won a Super Bowl in his fourth try. Team owner Pat Bowlen held the trophy high and said, "This one's for John." For Denver, the win ended 38 years without a championship and ended the AFC's long losing streak. For Green Bay, the loss made the Packers the third defending champion to lose a Super Bowl, and it ended the NFC's thirteen game winning streak. The game is remembered for Davis running through a migraine, for Elway flying through the air, and for a final drive that gave Denver the title it had chased since the franchise began.

Cultural context

The game mattered because it broke two streaks at once. The AFC had not won a Super Bowl since January 1984, and Denver had never won one at all. Elway's three earlier losses, by a combined 76 points, had made him a symbol of the AFC's inferiority in the 1980s. Winning in San Diego, against the defending champion, changed that story in one night. The Helicopter play became an instant classic, voted one of the greatest plays in NFL history, and Bowlen's "This one's for John" line became part of Denver sports language. The halftime show, a Motown tribute in a stadium that would also host the World Series later that year, was a reminder of how much the Super Bowl had become a cultural event as well as a football game. Denver would win again the next year, but this first title, won as an 11 point underdog, is the one Denver fans still rank first.
