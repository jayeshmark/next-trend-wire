Super Bowl XLIII: Pittsburgh Steelers vs Arizona Cardinals

By Editorial Team

Pittsburgh won its sixth Super Bowl title, the first franchise to reach six, beating Arizona on a Santonio Holmes touchdown catch with 35 seconds left. Arizona was playing in its first Super Bowl and led 23-20 with 2:37 left.

The game story

The stakes were uneven in a way that made the finish more shocking, not less. Pittsburgh arrived as the standard bearer, a 12-4 team chasing a sixth Super Bowl title, which would put the Steelers alone at the top of the league's history. Arizona arrived as the outsider that had barely made the playoffs at 9-7, a franchise that had never played in a Super Bowl and had not won a championship of any kind since 1947. Kurt Warner, the Arizona quarterback, was playing in his third Super Bowl, but his first with the Cardinals, and Larry Fitzgerald had been the best player of the postseason. The question was whether Arizona's passing game could survive Pittsburgh's defense for four quarters.

For a half, it looked like the answer was no. Jeff Reed kicked an 18 yard field goal for Pittsburgh in the first quarter. Gary Russell scored from one yard out early in the second quarter, and Pittsburgh led 10-0. Arizona answered with a one yard touchdown pass from Warner to tight end Ben Patrick, cutting the lead to 10-7. Then came the play that defined the first half and nearly defined the game.

With 18 seconds left in the half, Arizona drove to the Pittsburgh one yard line, trailing 10-7, with a chance to take the lead into the locker room. Warner threw toward the end zone, and James Harrison intercepted the pass at the goal line and ran 100 yards the other way for a touchdown as time expired in the half. It was the longest interception return in Super Bowl history, and it turned a possible 14-10 Arizona lead into a 17-7 Pittsburgh lead. The swing was 14 points on a single play, and Arizona's sideline looked stunned.

The third quarter offered no relief for Arizona. Reed kicked a 21 yard field goal, and Pittsburgh led 20-7. The game looked settled, and the fourth quarter began with Arizona still down by thirteen. Then Fitzgerald took over. Warner found him for a one yard touchdown with 7:41 left, cutting the lead to 20-14. On Pittsburgh's next possession, a holding penalty on center Justin Hartwig was enforced in the end zone for a safety, making it 20-16. Arizona took the free kick, and on the next play from scrimmage, Warner hit Fitzgerald in stride for a 64 yard touchdown. With 2:37 left, Arizona led 23-20, and a team that had trailed by thirteen points in the fourth quarter was one stop from a championship.

Pittsburgh had one drive left, starting at its own 20, and Ben Roethlisberger made it count. He moved the Steelers down the field with short passes and one long completion to Holmes, reaching the Arizona six yard line with under a minute left. On second down, Roethlisberger threw to the right corner of the end zone, where Holmes caught the ball, dragged both toes in bounds, and fell out of bounds. The touchdown, with 35 seconds left, gave Pittsburgh a 27-23 lead after Reed's extra point. Arizona had one last chance, but Warner was sacked by LaMarr Woodley with 15 seconds left, fumbled, and Keisel recovered. Roethlisberger knelt out the clock.

The legacy of the game is that it is still argued about as the best Super Bowl ever played, and the argument rests on the final eighteen minutes. The Harrison return is the most famous defensive play in Super Bowl history, a 100 yard run by a linebacker who looked exhausted by the end of it. The Holmes catch is the most famous finish, a toe drag in the corner that survived a replay review. Between them sits Fitzgerald's 64 yard touchdown, the play that briefly made Arizona the champion elect. For Pittsburgh, the win made the Steelers the first franchise with six Super Bowl titles, a record that stood alone until New England matched it a decade later. For Arizona, the loss remains the closest the franchise has come to a title, and the game is remembered in Arizona not as a blowout survived but as a championship held for just over two minutes of game time.

  4. Harrison's 100 yard return, second quarter: Harrison intercepts Warner at the goal line with 18 seconds left in the half and returns it 100 yards for a touchdown. A possible Arizona lead becomes a 17-7 Pittsburgh lead.
  5. Reed's field goal, third quarter: Reed's 21 yard kick makes it 20-7, and the game looks settled.
  6. Fitzgerald's first touchdown, fourth quarter: Fitzgerald's one yard catch makes it 20-14 with 7:41 left and starts Arizona's comeback.

Why this game mattered

Super Bowl XLIII is the game people reach for when they argue about the greatest Super Bowl, and the reason is the shape of the fourth quarter. Most famous games have one iconic play. This one has three in eighteen minutes: a safety created by a penalty in the end zone, a 64 yard touchdown that gave the underdog the lead, and a toe drag touchdown with 35 seconds left that took it away. The Harrison return at the end of the first half would have been the defining play of almost any other game. Here it is almost a footnote to the finish.

The game also marked a changing of the guard in the commercial breaks. The Ad Meter winner was a fan made Doritos ad, made for under $2,000 by two brothers from Indiana, beating the professional campaigns that had dominated the ranking for a decade. That result, more than any single spot, signaled that the audience's favorite commercial no longer had to come from an agency. The halftime show pointed the other way, toward tradition: Springsteen and the E Street Band played four songs in twelve minutes, with no guest star and no controversy, a straight rock and roll set that closed with "Glory Days" and fireworks.

For Pittsburgh, the sixth title put the Steelers alone in first place among franchises, a position the franchise had been chasing since its 1970s dynasty. For Arizona, the loss hardened into the franchise's great what if: a first Super Bowl appearance, a fourth quarter lead, and a championship that lasted just over two minutes of game time.
