The game story

Baltimore arrived in Miami carrying a wound that had not healed. Two years earlier, the Colts had been heavy favorites against the New York Jets in Super Bowl III and had lost, a result that embarrassed the old NFL and gave the AFL its first championship. Dallas arrived with a different kind of pressure. The Cowboys had reached the NFL title game twice in the late 1960s and lost both times, and the label of a team that could not win the big game was already sticking. This was the first Super Bowl played after the AFL and NFL merger was completed, and fittingly it matched two clubs from the old NFL, because Baltimore had moved into the new AFC along with Cleveland and Pittsburgh.

The game itself was ugly from the start, and that ugliness is the reason it is still talked about. Dallas built a 6 to 0 lead on two short field goals by Mike Clark while Baltimore's offense sputtered. Then came the play that summed up the whole afternoon. Johnny Unitas threw high and behind Eddie Hinton. The ball glanced off Hinton's hands, was touched by Dallas defensive back Mel Renfro, and fell to John Mackey, who ran 75 yards for a touchdown. It was Baltimore's only touchdown pass of the day, and it should have tied the game, but Jim O'Brien's extra point was blocked, so the score stayed tied at 6 only in points, not in momentum, because neither side could hold the ball cleanly for long.

Dallas took a 13 to 6 lead into halftime when Craig Morton threw a 7 yard touchdown pass to Duane Thomas. Worse for Baltimore, Unitas had been knocked out of the game with a rib injury and would not return. Earl Morrall, the same quarterback who had started the Super Bowl III loss, came in to replace him. The third quarter produced no points at all, and early in the fourth quarter Howley intercepted Morrall, which looked like the play that would let Dallas run out the clock. Instead, the turnovers kept coming, and they kept coming at the worst possible moments for Dallas.

With Dallas trying to protect a seven point lead late in the fourth quarter, Volk intercepted Morton and gave Baltimore the ball at the Dallas 3. Two plays later, Tom Nowatzke ran 2 yards for a touchdown, and O'Brien's kick tied the game at 13. Dallas got the ball back with a chance to win it, but Morton, under pressure, threw a pass that slipped through Dan Reeves's hands and into the arms of Curtis. Curtis returned it to the Dallas 28. Two plays later, with five seconds left, O'Brien kicked a 32 yard field goal, and Baltimore won 16 to 13. It was the first time Baltimore had ever led in the game.

The result gave Baltimore its first Super Bowl title and a measure of redemption for the Super Bowl III defeat, though the performance itself offered little to celebrate. Don McCafferty became the first rookie head coach to win a Super Bowl. For Dallas, the loss deepened the reputation it would only shake off the following season. And for Howley, the day produced a strange piece of history: the best individual performance in the game, rewarded with the MVP award, in a game his team lost. The nickname stuck anyway. This was the Blunder Bowl, a championship decided not by the team that played best, but by the team that made its last mistake second to last.

Why this game mattered

Super Bowl V was the first championship game of the merged league, and it showed how large the event had already become: an estimated 46 million viewers watched on NBC. It was also the first Super Bowl played on artificial turf, at the Orange Bowl. The game gave the young Super Bowl era one of its lasting labels, the Blunder Bowl, and one of its lasting oddities, an MVP from the losing team. That oddity has never been repeated. The result also framed the next decade for both franchises. Baltimore, which had been humiliated in Super Bowl III, finally owned a Super Bowl title, even if the way it won it was shaky. Dallas, which had now lost its first Super Bowl, went on to win the next one, and the contrast between the two Januaries became part of the Cowboys story: the team that could not finish in 1971 learned how to finish in 1972.
