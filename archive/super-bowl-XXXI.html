Super Bowl XXXI: Green Bay Packers vs New England Patriots

By Editorial Team

Result in brief: Green Bay won its third Super Bowl title and its first since Super Bowl II, extending the franchise record for total NFL championships to 12. New England suffered a second Super Bowl defeat.

Decisive defensive and special teams plays

  • Green Bay return specialist Desmond Howard gained 244 total return yards: 154 kickoff return yards and 90 punt return yards, tying the Super Bowl record for total return yards at the time.
  • Howard returned the kickoff after New England's third quarter touchdown 99 yards for a touchdown, the longest kickoff return in Super Bowl history at the time. Favre threw a two point conversion pass to Mark Chmura after the return, making the score 35 to 21.
  • Green Bay defensive end Reggie White recorded 3 sacks, a Super Bowl record at the time.
  • New England never crossed midfield in four fourth quarter possessions, and Bledsoe was intercepted twice in the fourth quarter.
  • The 24 combined points in the first quarter were the most in Super Bowl history at the time.

The game story

Green Bay and New England arrived in New Orleans as two teams that had recently climbed out of long losing stretches. Green Bay had not won a Super Bowl since the Lombardi era, 29 years earlier, but the Packers had gone 13 and 3 behind Brett Favre and head coach Mike Holmgren. New England had gone 11 and 5 under Bill Parcells and Drew Bledsoe, and the Patriots were playing in only their second Super Bowl. Green Bay was favored by 14 points, the largest spread of the decade, and the game began as if the favorite would run away with it, then briefly looked like an upset, then ended with a single kickoff return.

Favre struck on the second play from scrimmage, throwing a 54 yard touchdown pass to Andre Rison for a 7 to 0 lead. Doug Evans intercepted Bledsoe on the next New England drive, and Chris Jacke kicked a 37 yard field goal for a 10 to 0 lead only 6 minutes and 18 seconds into the game. New England answered with touchdowns on its next two possessions. Keith Byars caught a 1 yard pass from Bledsoe, and Ben Coates caught a 4 yard pass from Bledsoe after a 44 yard completion to Terry Glenn. New England led 14 to 10, its first and only lead of the game, and the first quarter ended with 24 combined points, a Super Bowl record.

Green Bay scored 17 unanswered points in the second quarter. Favre threw an 81 yard touchdown pass to Antonio Freeman only 56 seconds into the quarter, a Super Bowl record for the longest touchdown pass at the time. Jacke kicked a 31 yard field goal, and Favre ran 2 yards for a touchdown after a long drive, diving for the pylon. Green Bay led 27 to 14 at halftime, and the game felt settled, but New England had one more push.

In the third quarter, Curtis Martin ran 18 yards for a touchdown with 3 minutes and 27 seconds left in the quarter, cutting the lead to 27 to 21. The Superdome noise rose, and a comeback felt possible. It lasted exactly one kickoff. Howard took the ensuing kickoff and returned it 99 yards for a touchdown, breaking through the New England coverage team and running untouched for much of the return. Favre found Chmura for the two point conversion, and the score was 35 to 21. Neither team scored again. Bledsoe was intercepted twice in the fourth quarter, New England never crossed midfield, and Green Bay ran out the clock.

Howard was named most valuable player, the first special teams player ever to win the award. Favre passed for 2 touchdowns and ran for another, but the voters chose the returner who had ended the game with one play. For Green Bay, the win was a third Super Bowl title and the first since Super Bowl II, and it extended the franchise record for total NFL championships to 12. For New England, the loss was a second Super Bowl defeat, but the team was only beginning a run that would later make it the dominant franchise of the next two decades. Parcells left after the game, and the Patriots would not return to a Super Bowl for five years. This game is remembered for Howard's return, for Favre's deep throws, and for a first quarter that promised a shootout and delivered a classic instead.

Scoring timeline

  1. Green Bay strikes in two plays. Favre finds Rison for 54 yards, and the Packers lead 7 to 0.
  2. Green Bay stretches it to 10 to 0. Evans intercepts Bledsoe, and Jacke kicks a field goal.
  3. New England answers twice. Byars and Coates catch touchdown passes from Bledsoe, and the Patriots lead 14 to 10.
  4. Green Bay answers with 17 unanswered points. Freeman catches an 81 yard touchdown, Jacke kicks a field goal, and Favre runs for a touchdown for a 27 to 14 halftime lead.
  5. New England cuts it to six. Martin runs 18 yards for a touchdown, making it 27 to 21.
  6. Howard ends the comeback. His 99 yard kickoff return, plus a two point conversion, makes it 35 to 21, and neither team scores again.

Cultural context

This was the first Super Bowl broadcast by Fox, and it became the highest rated program in Fox history at the time. The network had only recently won NFL rights, and the game helped establish Fox as a major sports broadcaster. The halftime show was also a sign of the times: a Blues Brothers revival, complete with a fake news bulletin that drew criticism for blurring news and entertainment, followed by James Brown and ZZ Top. On the field, the game marked the end of the NFC's long Super Bowl winning streak in style. Green Bay's win was the thirteenth straight Super Bowl victory for the NFC, a run that began in 1985 and would end the next year in San Diego. It also marked a passing of the torch in Green Bay, from the Lombardi legend to a new championship team led by Favre, and in New England, from Parcells to a franchise that would soon be rebuilt around a new coach and a new quarterback.
