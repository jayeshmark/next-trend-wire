Super Bowl LX: Seattle Seahawks vs New England Patriots

By Editorial Team

Seattle won its second Super Bowl title, and its first since Super Bowl XLVIII in the 2013 season. The win avenged Seattle's 28-24 loss to New England in Super Bowl XLIX, decided by a late end zone interception. Jason Myers set a Super Bowl record with 5 field goals.

The game story

The stakes were about two rebuilds arriving at the same place at the same time. Seattle, under head coach Mike Macdonald, had built the league's best defense around a punishing run game, and finished 14-3, tied for the league's best record. New England, under head coach Mike Vrabel, had gone from a long rebuild to the Super Bowl behind second year quarterback Drake Maye, who had become one of the league's young stars in a single season. The matchup also carried history: these teams had met in Super Bowl XLIX eleven years earlier, when New England won 28-24 on Malcolm Butler's goal line interception, one of the most famous plays in Super Bowl history. Seattle had waited a long time for a rematch.

The game itself was defined by one thing: Seattle's defense would not let New England play. The first quarter produced only a Jason Myers 33 yard field goal, and the second quarter produced two more Myers field goals, from 39 and 41 yards. New England's offense crossed midfield but could not finish, and Maye was sacked 3 times in the first half alone. The halftime score was 9-0, and the game already felt like it was slipping away from the Patriots, because their offense had shown no answer for Seattle's front.

The third quarter made the pattern permanent. Myers kicked his fourth field goal, from 41 yards, with 9:17 left in the quarter, making it 12-0. Late in the quarter, Maye was strip sacked by Derick Hall, and Byron Murphy II recovered at the New England 37, killing the Patriots' best chance to get back into the game before the fourth quarter. Seattle took a 12-0 lead into the final period, and New England had been shut out for three quarters.

The fourth quarter finally produced touchdowns, but it never produced drama. Darnold threw a 16 yard touchdown pass to AJ Barner with 13:29 left, making it 19-0 after Myers' extra point. New England answered 56 seconds later with a 35 yard Maye to Mack Hollins touchdown, making it 19-7. Myers kicked his fifth field goal, from 26 yards, to make it 22-7, and then the defense ended it: Nwosu intercepted Maye and returned it 45 yards for a touchdown with 4:37 left, making it 29-7 after the extra point. New England scored once more, a 7 yard Maye to Rhamondre Stevenson touchdown with 2:28 left, but the two point try failed, and the final score was 29-13.

The legacy of the game is about a defense winning a championship in an offensive era, and about a running back winning an award that almost never goes to running backs. Walker, with 135 yards on 27 carries and no touchdown, moved the chains all night while Seattle's defense did the rest. Myers' 5 field goals set a Super Bowl record, a fitting footnote for a game decided by field position and pressure rather than big plays. For New England, the loss was a reminder of how far the rebuild had come and how far it still had to go: Maye threw for 295 yards, but his 2 interceptions, 1 lost fumble, and 6 sacks told the real story of the night. Seattle, meanwhile, finally buried the ghost of Super Bowl XLIX, and did it by beating the same franchise in a game that was never as close as the old one.

Why this game mattered

Super Bowl LX mattered most for what happened at halftime. Bad Bunny, performing primarily in Spanish, became the first headliner to build a Super Bowl halftime show that way, staging a celebration of Puerto Rican culture complete with a recreated casita, sugar cane fields, and guest appearances from Lady Gaga and Ricky Martin. The choice had drawn political criticism for months before the game, and the response became its own event: Turning Point USA staged an alternative halftime show featuring Kid Rock, which drew millions of concurrent viewers on YouTube. The split audience became a symbol of how fragmented even the biggest shared television event in America has become.

The game itself averaged 124.9 million viewers in the United States, the second most watched Super Bowl ever, behind only the previous year's game. The halftime show averaged 128.2 million viewers, and the audience peaked at 137.8 million during the second quarter, an all time record for peak Super Bowl viewership. The numbers showed that even a one sided game, and a politically contested halftime show, could still command the largest audience on television.

On the field, the game mattered because it closed a loop. Seattle's loss in Super Bowl XLIX, on the goal line interception, had haunted the franchise for eleven years. Winning the rematch against the same opponent, with defense and a running game rather than a final drive, gave the franchise a second title and gave Walker, Darnold, and Macdonald a place in Seattle sports history that the old loss had denied their predecessors.
