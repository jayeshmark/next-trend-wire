How the ranking works

The ranking value is the lowest pre-play win probability recorded for the team that eventually won the game, across every play in the play by play record, including kicks. Rows in the file that are not plays are left out first: rows with no clock time, rows with no play type, and the end of game marker row, whose win probability values are placeholders rather than game states. Win probability here is the home team or away team estimate from the nflverse play by play data. The two estimates always sum to 100 percent on a play. The final score in the schedules data decides who won. Largest deficit is the biggest gap between the loser's and the winner's running total at any play in the game.

Regular season and postseason games are ranked together, with the type shown in the table. Ties are not ranked: with no winner there is no comeback. The ranking uses the single lowest pre-play value, so one play can decide a rank. The story pages show the full drive by drive path so each number can be checked in context.

What win probability is, and what it is not

Win probability is a model estimate, not a fact. It says how often teams in that game state went on to win in past games. It is not a prediction about any one game, and a low number does not mean the model was wrong. Near zero, small differences are the least certain part of the model: 0.3% and 0.5% both mean almost no chance, and the exact order of the closest games is model noise, not a real difference.

Win probability coverage was checked in the data before this section was built. It is present on between 100.00% and 100.00% of plays in every season from 2001 to 2026. The section starts in 2001 because the 1999 and 2000 win probability values are unreliable: in 246 games from those seasons the file gives a fixed floor value on the opening kickoff, at 0-0, in games the winner never trailed. Those values are file artifacts, not game states. The 2001 season was checked the same way and shows no such pattern. 17 games were left out because the scoring in the play by play file does not reconcile with the final score, so no trustworthy drive story can be told from them. Win probability values come from the nflverse data release pulled on November 16, 2026. A new data release can shift values slightly and change close ranks.
