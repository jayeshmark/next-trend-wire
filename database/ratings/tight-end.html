Tight end ratings

Open formula tight end ratings from play by play data, 1999 to 2026. Every input, weight, and pool average is published on this page. Hunter Henry leads the 2026 tight end ratings with 129.9; George Kittle leads the career ratings with 133.2.

How the rating works

This rating is an open formula. Every input is shown in the tables below, the weights are stated here, and the pool averages and spreads used to score each input are published with each table. Anyone with the same play by play data gets the same numbers. There is nothing hidden to trust.

Formula. Each input is turned into a component score: 100 plus 15 times (the player's value minus the pool average) divided by the pool spread. The pool is the set of qualifying players in that table, and the spread is the population standard deviation of the input across that pool. The rating is the weighted average of the component scores. 100 is the average qualifier in that pool. There is no fixed ceiling and no fixed floor.

Weights. EPA per target 50%, Target success rate 30%, Catch rate 20%.

Inputs. A target is a pass play where the player is the intended receiver. Kneels, spikes, and no plays are excluded.

Expected points added (EPA) measures how much a play improved the team's expected points, given down, distance, field position, and score. A positive number means the play helped; a negative number means it hurt. Success rate is the share of plays with positive EPA: plays that improved the team's expected points at all. Success rate and EPA come from the same play values. EPA measures how much value a play added on average; success rate measures how often a play added any value at all.

Seasons covered. 1999 to 2026. Play by play data starts in 1999, so players whose careers began earlier are shown with only their 1999 onward plays. No era adjustment is applied: a play in 2003 counts the same as a play in 2026. That choice is deliberate. Any era adjustment would be a second model on top of the formula, and the point of this page is that the reader can check every step. Career tables combine the regular season and the postseason.

Left out on purpose. Completion probability and air yards are not recorded in the play by play data before 2006, and yards after catch is not recorded before 2006 either. A formula that used them could not cover 1999 onward with one set of rules, so none of them is part of any rating here. Air yards appear as context on the 2026 tables only, where coverage is complete, and air yards do not affect the rating.

Data repair. In the 2003 to 2008 play by play files, the intended receiver field is missing on incomplete passes. The receiver key used here is that field filled from the backup receiver field where missing; the two fields agree wherever both are present. Without this repair, targets in those seasons would count completions only, and catch rates would read near 100 percent.

What this rating does not measure. It does not separate a player from teammates, coaching, or scheme: a quarterback's number reflects receivers and blocking as well as throws. It does not adjust for opponent strength or for era. It uses only what the play by play record shows, so it covers no blocking, no routes run, no coverage, and no pressure data. It describes past play value only. It is not a prediction about any future game.

Thresholds. The 2026 table requires at least 30 targets. The career table requires at least 200 targets. A player's position is the position listed most often in the season stats for that span. Small samples are excluded so one good game cannot top a table.
