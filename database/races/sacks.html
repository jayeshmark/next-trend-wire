How the projection works

Formula. Projected final total equals current total divided by games played, times 17.

Example. Will Anderson Jr. has 10.5 sacks in 9 games. 10.5 divided by 9 times 17 equals 19.8. Projected totals are rounded to one decimal place.

Games played counts the games with a weekly data row for that player. A bye week or a missed game does not count, so a player who missed time is judged on the games actually played, not on the league week number. Team games remaining is 17 minus the games the player's team has already played, from the schedules file. The regular season is 17 games per team. Postseason games are not included anywhere on this page.

What has to happen shows the rate a chaser needs per game, over that player's remaining team games, to finish ahead of the leader's projected final total, assuming the leader keeps the same per game pace. The bar is the leader's projected final total plus 0.5 sacks (sacks are recorded in halves), divided by the chaser's remaining games. The needed rate is rounded up, so the stated rate always clears the bar. The catch math uses the rounded projected total shown in the table, so it can be checked by hand from the numbers on this page.

Ties. Players tied on current total share a rank, and every player tied at the fifth place total is shown. That is why some tables hold six rows. A projection from fewer games moves more with one game: one big game or one quiet game changes the rate more when the sample is small.

A projection is arithmetic from past games. It is not a prediction, a pick, or odds, and it does not account for injuries, matchups, or playing time changes.
