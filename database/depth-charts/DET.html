How to read this page

The official depth chart says who is listed first. Snap counts say who actually plays. This page puts the two side by side, position by position, so the gap between the listing and the field is visible. A name at the top of the official chart with a small snap share is not playing as much as the listing suggests. A backup with a large and rising share is playing a starter role whatever the listing says.

Snap share. A player's snap share over a window is the player's snaps divided by the team's total snaps on that side of the ball over the same window. The team total for one game is the highest snap count by any player on that side in that game, which is the number of plays the unit was on the field. Shares are computed from snap counts, not from the percentages in any single game file, so multi game shares weight each game by how many plays it had.

Windows. The windows count games the team played: the last game, the last 3 games, and the last 5 games with snap data. A bye week is skipped, so the windows for a team coming off a bye reach back further in calendar weeks. The week labels on each table show exactly which weeks are in each window for this team.

Official spot. The official spot is the player's rank at that spot in the depth chart snapshot of November 16, 2026. "Not listed" means the player does not appear in that snapshot for this team. The snapshot is one point in time; the snap shares cover several weeks, so a recent signing or a player returning from injury can show a small share next to a high official spot.

Movers. The mover column compares snap share over the last 3 games with snap share over the 3 games before those. A rise of 10 points or more is flagged as a riser and a fall of 10 points or more as a faller. "New in rotation" means the player had no snaps in the earlier window and at least a 5 percent share in the last 3 games. "Out of rotation" means the reverse. Anything smaller is steady. A mover flag describes playing time only. It says nothing about how well the player played.

Who is included. Every player with snaps for this team in the last 5 games is listed on offense and defense, plus any player ranked first or second at a spot on the official chart even if he has no recent snaps. Players with no recent snaps who are ranked third or lower on the official chart are left out. The special teams table lists players with special teams snaps in the last 3 games, plus the top two at each kicking spot.

What this page does not claim. Snap share is playing time, not quality. A high share does not mean a player played well, and a low share does not mean a player played badly. Roles differ by position: a full time lineman should sit near 100 percent, while a running back in a rotation can lead his group at 60 percent. Compare shares within a position group, not across the whole roster. This page is not a prediction about any future game.
