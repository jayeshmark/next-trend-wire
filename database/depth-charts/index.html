How these pages work

Snap share. A player's snap share over a window is the player's snaps divided by the team's total snaps on that side of the ball over the same window. The team total for one game is the highest snap count by any player on that side in that game, which is the number of plays the unit was on the field.

Windows. The windows count games the team played: the last game, the last 3 games, and the last 5 games with snap data. A bye week is skipped, so the windows for a team coming off a bye reach back further in calendar weeks. Each team page labels its tables with the exact weeks in each window.

Official spot. The official spot is the player's rank at that spot in the depth chart snapshot of November 16, 2026. The snapshot is one point in time; the snap shares cover several weeks, so a recent signing or a player returning from injury can show a small share next to a high official spot.

Movers. The mover column compares snap share over the last 3 games with snap share over the 3 games before those. A rise of 10 points or more is flagged as a riser and a fall of 10 points or more as a faller.

What these pages do and do not claim

These pages claim one thing only: how much each player actually played, measured in snaps. They do not claim the most played player is the best player. Snap share is playing time, not quality, and roles differ by position: a full time lineman should sit near 100 percent, while a running back in a rotation can lead his group at 60 percent. Compare shares within a position group, not across the whole roster. Nothing here is a prediction about any future game.
